# BOOK FOUR

## LIBER QUARTUS

### 1. Of the death of Queen Chrodigildis

<a id="hist-04-en-001"></a>
[**[001]**](#hist-04-la-001) Queen Chrotchildis, full of days and endowed with good works, died in the city of Tours during the episcopate of Injuriosus. She was carried to Paris with great singing of psalms and was buried by her sons, Kings Childebert and Chlothar, in the sanctuary of the basilica of Saint Peter beside King Clovis. She herself had built that basilica; the most blessed Geneviève is also buried there.

### 2. How Chlothar wished to take the fruits of the churches

<a id="hist-04-en-002"></a>
[**[002]**](#hist-04-la-002) King Chlothar had ordered all the churches of his kingdom to pay a third of their revenues to the treasury. Although unwilling, all the other bishops consented and subscribed; but blessed Injuriosus resolutely rejected the demand and refused to subscribe, saying: “If you try to take God’s property, the Lord will swiftly take away your kingdom. It is unjust that the poor, whom you ought to feed from your own granary, should fill your granaries from their own meager resources.” Angry with the king, he departed without taking leave. The king was disturbed and also feared the power of blessed Martin. He sent gifts after Injuriosus, begged his pardon, condemned his own action, and asked him to entreat the power of the blessed bishop Martin on his behalf.

### 3. Of his wives and sons

<a id="hist-04-en-003"></a>
[**[003]**](#hist-04-la-003) This king had seven sons by different women. By Ingund he had Gunthar, Childeric, Charibert, Guntram, and Sigibert, as well as a daughter, Chlothsinda; by Aregund, Ingund’s sister, he had Chilperic; and by Chunsina he had Chramn. I will explain why he married his wife’s sister.

After he had married Ingund and loved her with singular devotion, she made a suggestion to him: “My lord has done as he pleased with his handmaid and has taken me to his bed. Now, to complete my reward, let my lord the king hear his handmaid’s proposal. I pray that you will deign to provide my sister, your servant, with a capable and well-endowed husband, so that I may not be humbled but rather exalted and may serve you more faithfully.” Being extremely licentious, he fell in love with Aregund when he heard this. He went to the estate where she lived and married her.

After the marriage, he returned to Ingund and said: “I have tried to fulfill the reward your sweetness requested. Looking for a rich and wise husband to give your sister, I found none better than myself. Know, therefore, that I have married her, which I trust does not displease you.” She replied: “Let my lord do what seems good in his eyes; only let your handmaid continue to live in the king’s favor.”

Gunthar, Chramn, and Childeric died while their father was still alive. I have described Chramn’s end later in this work. Alboin, king of the Lombards, married the king’s daughter Chlothsinda. Injuriosus, bishop of Tours, died in the seventeenth year of his episcopate. Baudinus, a member of King Chlothar’s household, succeeded him, the sixteenth bishop after the death of blessed Martin.

### 4. Of the counts of the Bretons

<a id="hist-04-en-004"></a>
[**[004]**](#hist-04-la-004) Chanao, a count of the Bretons, killed his three brothers. Wishing also to kill Macliavus, he captured him, loaded him with chains, and kept him in prison. Macliavus was saved from death by Felix, bishop of Nantes. He later swore to be faithful to his brother, but for some reason he tried to break the oath. When Chanao perceived this, he pursued him again. Seeing that he could not escape, Macliavus fled to another count of that region named Chonomor. When Chonomor perceived the pursuers approaching, he hid Macliavus underground in a small chamber. He heaped a burial mound over it in the customary manner, leaving a small breathing-hole through which Macliavus could draw air. When the pursuers arrived, they were told: “Look, Macliavus lies here dead and buried.” They believed it, rejoiced, and drank over the mound; then they reported to his brother that Macliavus was dead. On hearing this, Chanao took possession of the whole of his kingdom.

After the death of King Clovis, the Bretons were always subject to the power of the Franks, and their rulers were called counts, not kings. Macliavus emerged from underground, went to the city of Vannes, received the tonsure, and was ordained bishop. But when Chanao died, he apostatized, let his hair grow, and took back the wife he had left on entering the clergy, together with his brother’s kingdom. The bishops excommunicated him. I shall describe his end later. Bishop Baudinus died in the sixth year of his episcopate. Abbot Gunthar was appointed in his place, the seventeenth after the death of Saint Martin.

### 5. Of Saint Gallus the bishop

<a id="hist-04-en-005"></a>
[**[005]**](#hist-04-la-005) When blessed Quintianus had departed from this world, as we said above, Saint Gallus was appointed to his see with the king’s support. In his time the plague called the inguinal plague raged through various regions and was then devastating the province of Arles in particular. Saint Gallus was fearful not so much for himself as for his people. He prayed to the Lord day and night that he might not live to see his people destroyed. In a vision at night an angel of the Lord appeared to him, with hair and clothing as white as snow, and said: “You do well, priest, to pray to the Lord for your people. Your prayer has been heard. You and your people will be delivered from this disease, and no one in this region will perish from this destruction while you live. Do not be afraid now; but be afraid after eight years.” This made it clear that he departed from the world when those years had passed.

He awoke and thanked God for deigning to comfort him through a heavenly messenger. He instituted Rogations, ordering that in the middle of Lent the people should come on foot with psalmody to the basilica of the blessed martyr Julian. The journey is about 360 stades. At that time the walls of houses and churches also appeared in a sudden vision to be marked with signs; the rustics called the mark that was written “Tau.” While the plague was consuming those other regions, as we have said, it did not reach the city of Clermont because of Saint Gallus’s intercessory prayer. I consider it no small grace that he was found worthy, as their shepherd, not to see his sheep devoured because the Lord defended them.

When Gallus had departed from this world and his body had been washed and carried into the church, the priest Cato immediately received acclamations from the clergy as their bishop. As though he were already bishop, he brought all the church’s property under his own control, removed those who conducted ordinations, rejected the ministers, and arranged everything himself.

### 6. Of Cato the priest

<a id="hist-04-en-006"></a>
[**[006]**](#hist-04-la-006) After the bishops who had come to bury Saint Gallus had completed the burial, they said to the priest Cato: “We see that the greater part of the people loves you dearly. Come, accept our proposal, and we will bless and consecrate you as bishop. The king is only a child. If any charge is brought against you, we will take you under our protection and negotiate with the nobles and leading men of King Theudebald’s kingdom so that no harm is done to you. Trust us completely: we pledge everything on your behalf, and if any loss results, we promise to repay it from our own property.”

Puffed up with vainglory, Cato replied: “You know from common report that from my earliest years I have always lived religiously. I have devoted myself to fasting, delighted in almsgiving, frequently kept continuous vigils, and often persisted through the night in psalmody. The Lord my God, to whom I have rendered such service, will not allow me to be deprived of this ordination. I have always obtained the grades of the clergy according to canonical rule. I was a reader for ten years, served as subdeacon for five years, belonged to the diaconate for fifteen years, and have now held the honor of the priesthood for twenty years. What remains for me except to receive the episcopate, which faithful service deserves? Return therefore to your cities and attend to your own affairs, for I shall receive this honor canonically.” The bishops heard this, denounced his vainglory, and departed.

### 7. Of the episcopate of Cautinus

<a id="hist-04-en-007"></a>
[**[007]**](#hist-04-la-007) Cato had been elected to the episcopate with the clergy’s consent, and although he had not yet been ordained, he was already taking charge of everything. He began to utter various threats against the archdeacon Cautinus, saying: “I will remove you; I will humble you; I will order many deadly punishments to be inflicted on you.” Cautinus replied: “I desire your favor, most merciful lord. If I obtain it, I will do you one service. Without any effort on your part and without any deception, I will go to the king and obtain the episcopate for you. I ask for nothing except to earn your favor.” Cato suspected that Cautinus meant to mock him and treated the offer with contempt.

Seeing that he was being humiliated and subjected to false accusations, Cautinus feigned illness, left the city by night, and went to King Theudebald to announce Saint Gallus’s death. When the king and his companions heard this, they summoned the bishops to the city of Metz, and the archdeacon Cautinus was ordained bishop. By the time Cato’s messengers arrived, Cautinus was already bishop. By the king’s command the clergy were placed under him, together with all the church property that Cato’s party had taken. Bishops and chamberlains were appointed to accompany him, and he was sent to the Auvergne. He was gladly received by the citizens and clergy and given to the people of Clermont as bishop.

Great enmity subsequently arose between him and the priest Cato, because no one could ever induce Cato to submit to his bishop. The clergy were divided: some obeyed Bishop Cautinus and others the priest Cato, which caused them the greatest harm. Seeing that Cato could by no means be persuaded to submit, Cautinus took all church property away from him, his friends, and everyone who supported him, leaving them empty and destitute. But whenever any of them came over to Cautinus, they recovered what they had lost.

### 8. Of the kings of the Spaniards

<a id="hist-04-en-008"></a>
[**[008]**](#hist-04-la-008) While Agila was reigning in Spain and crushing the people beneath the grievous yoke of his rule, the emperor’s army entered Spain and seized several cities. After Agila was killed, Athanagild obtained his kingdom. Athanagild later fought many wars against that army and often defeated it, recovering some of the cities that it had wrongfully seized from its control.

### 9. Of the death of Theudebald

<a id="hist-04-en-009"></a>
[**[009]**](#hist-04-la-009) When Theudebald had grown up, he married Vuldetrada. They say that this Theudebald had an evil disposition. On one occasion, angry with a man whose property he coveted, he devised a fable: “A serpent found a flask full of wine. It entered through the flask’s mouth and greedily drank what was inside. Swollen with wine, it could not get out through the opening by which it had entered. When the owner of the wine came and saw the serpent struggling unsuccessfully to get out, he said: ‘First vomit up what you have swallowed, and then you will be able to leave free.’” This fable aroused great fear and hatred of the king.

During his reign Buccelin, after bringing all Italy under Frankish rule, was killed by Narses. Italy was taken by the emperor’s side, and there was no one to recover it thereafter. In Theudebald’s time we saw grapes growing on the tree we call the service tree without any vine being joined to it. The flowers of those trees, which normally produce the dark berries you know, yielded grapes in clusters. Also, when the moon was five days old, a star was seen approaching from the opposite direction and entering its circle. I believe that these signs foretold the king’s death.

He became so seriously ill that he could feel nothing below the waist. Declining little by little, he died in the seventh year of his reign. King Chlothar took his kingdom and brought Theudebald’s wife Vuldetrada to his own bed. But when the bishops rebuked him, he left her and gave her to Duke Garivald. He sent his son Chramn to the Auvergne.

### 10. Of the Rebellion of the Saxons

<a id="hist-04-en-010"></a>
[**[010]**](#hist-04-la-010) That year the Saxons rebelled. King Chlothar called out an army against them and destroyed the greater part of them. He also ranged through all Thuringia and devastated it because the Thuringians had assisted the Saxons.

### 11. How they sought Cato for the bishopric of Tours

<a id="hist-04-en-011"></a>
[**[011]**](#hist-04-la-011) When Bishop Gunthar died in the city of Tours, the priest Cato was sought to govern the church of Tours, at the instigation, it is said, of Bishop Cautinus. The clergy therefore joined with Leubastes, keeper of the martyrs’ shrine and abbot, and hurried to the Auvergne with a large retinue. When they had made the king’s wishes known to Cato, he delayed his answer for several days. Wishing to return, they said: “Tell us your decision, so that we may know what course to follow; otherwise we shall go home. We did not seek you by our own choice, but by the king’s command.”

Desiring vainglory, Cato assembled a crowd of poor people and ordered them to cry out: “Why are you abandoning us, good father? We are your sons, whom you have taught until now. Who will give us food and drink if you go away? We beg you not to leave us, whom you have been accustomed to feed.” Turning to the clergy of Tours, he said: “You see, dearest brothers, how this multitude of poor people loves me. I cannot abandon them and go with you.” They received this answer and returned to Tours.

Cato had formed a friendship with Chramn and had received a promise from him that, if King Chlothar happened to die at that time, he would immediately expel Cautinus from the episcopate and put Cato in charge of the church. But Cato, who regarded the see of blessed Martin with contempt, did not obtain what he wanted. In him was fulfilled what David sang: “He refused blessing, and it shall be far from him.” He was carried away by vain pride and thought that no one surpassed him in holiness. On one occasion he hired a woman to cry out in church as though possessed, declaring that he was a great saint beloved by God, while Bishop Cautinus was guilty of every crime and unworthy to have obtained the priesthood.

### 12. Of Anastasius the priest

<a id="hist-04-en-012"></a>
[**[012]**](#hist-04-la-012) After obtaining the episcopate, Cautinus conducted himself in such a way that everyone execrated him. He was immoderately addicted to wine and was often so filled with drink that four men could scarcely carry him from a banquet. As a result, he later became epileptic, which was frequently evident to the people. He was also so avaricious that, if anyone’s property adjoined his own boundaries, he thought it would be his death not to take some part of it. He took from the powerful with quarrels and scandal, and violently plundered the weak. As our Sallust says of such people, he scornfully refused to pay the price and despairingly refused to accept title-deeds.

At that time there was a priest named Anastasius, freeborn, who possessed certain property by deeds issued by Queen Chrotchildis of glorious memory. The bishop repeatedly met with him and humbly asked him to surrender the queen’s deeds and place the property under his control. Anastasius put off complying with his bishop’s wish. Cautinus alternately enticed him with flattery and frightened him with threats. At last he ordered Anastasius to be brought to the city and shamelessly detained, commanding that, unless he surrendered the deeds, he should be mistreated and starved. Anastasius resisted courageously and never surrendered the deeds, saying that it was better to waste away from hunger for a time than to leave his descendants in misery.

By the bishop’s command he was handed over to guards to be starved to death unless he produced the documents. At the basilica of the martyr Saint Cassius there was a very ancient and secluded crypt containing a great tomb of Parian marble, in which the body of an aged man appeared to lie. The living priest was buried in this tomb above the corpse, and the stone that had formerly covered the sarcophagus was placed over him. Guards were stationed at the entrance. Although they were supposed to be faithful, they knew that he was weighed down by the stone. Since it was winter, they lit a fire, drank warm wine, and fell asleep.

The priest, like a new Jonah, implored the Lord’s mercy from the enclosure of the tomb as though from the belly of hell. As we have said, the sarcophagus was spacious. Although he could not turn his whole body over, he could freely stretch out his hands in whatever direction he wished. He used to relate that a deadly stench flowed from the dead man’s bones, shaking not only his outward senses but his innermost organs. When he held his cloak over the entrance to his nostrils, he felt nothing deadly so long as he could hold his breath. But when he thought he was suffocating and moved the cloak a little away from his face, he drew in the pestilential odor not only through his mouth and nostrils but even, so to speak, through his ears.

Why say more? When, as I believe, the Divine Mercy took pity on him, he stretched his right hand toward the edge of the sarcophagus and found a crowbar that had remained between the lid and the rim when the cover fell. Moving it little by little, he felt the stone shift with God’s help. When it had moved far enough for him to put his head out, he opened a wider gap and climbed completely free.

Night was now covering the earth, but daylight had not yet fully faded. He went to another door of the crypt. It was secured with strong bars and solid keys, but the woodwork was not so closely fitted that a man could not look between the boards. The priest put his head to the opening and saw a man passing along the road. He called to him in a faint voice. The man heard him and immediately took an axe, cut through the wooden supports holding the bars, and opened a way out for the priest. Passing through the night, Anastasius went home, earnestly begging the man to tell no one what had happened.

On entering his house, he found the deeds that the queen had given him and carried them to King Chlothar. He explained how his bishop had consigned him alive to burial. Everyone was astonished and said that neither Nero nor Herod had ever committed such a crime as shutting a living man in a tomb. Bishop Cautinus also came before King Chlothar, but when the priest brought his accusation, Cautinus departed defeated and covered with confusion. The priest obtained royal orders, defended and possessed his property as he wished, and left it to his descendants.

There was nothing holy and nothing serious in Cautinus. He was entirely ignorant of all writings, both ecclesiastical and secular. He was very dear and subservient to the Jews, not from a pastor’s proper concern for their salvation, but in order to purchase goods. He flattered them, and they openly flattered him in return, selling him their wares for more than they were worth.

### 13. Of the levity and malice of Chramn

<a id="hist-04-en-013"></a>
[**[013]**](#hist-04-la-013) Chramn was residing in the Auvergne in those days. He conducted many affairs unreasonably, and for this his removal from the world was hastened; the people cursed him bitterly. He loved no one from whom he could receive good and useful advice. He gathered about himself only disreputable young men of unstable character and followed their counsel, even issuing orders for senators’ daughters to be dragged away by force for them. He grievously wronged Firminus and drove him from the countship of the city, replacing him with Sallustius, son of Euodius. Firminus and his mother-in-law took refuge in a church.

It was the season of Lent. Bishop Cautinus had arranged to go with psalmody to the district of Brioude according to Saint Gallus’s institution, as we wrote above. He left the city weeping bitterly, fearing that he might suffer some harm on the journey, for Chramn was also uttering threats. While Cautinus was traveling, Chramn sent Imnacharius and Scaptharius, two of his chief attendants, saying: “Go and drag Firminus and his mother-in-law Caesaria out of the church by force.”

After the bishop had departed with the psalm-singers, Chramn’s emissaries entered the church and tried to soothe Firminus and Caesaria with various deceptive speeches. For a long time they walked through the church talking of one thing after another. While the refugees were giving their full attention to what was being said, the emissaries approached the royal doors of the sacred building, which were then open. Imnacharius seized Firminus and Scaptharius seized Caesaria in their arms and threw them out of the church, where servants stood ready to receive them. They were immediately sent into exile. But on the following day their guards were overcome by sleep. Finding themselves free, Firminus and Caesaria fled to the basilica of blessed Julian and thus escaped exile. Their property, however, was confiscated for the treasury.

Bishop Cautinus suspected that Chramn intended to injure him too. While he was continuing his journey, with his horse saddled, he saw men riding up behind him to meet him. He cried: “Woe is me! These are the men Chramn has sent to seize me.” Abandoning the psalmody, he mounted his horse and rode alone, spurring it with both heels and nearly fainting, until he reached the portico of the basilica of Saint Julian.

In relating these things, I recall the remark that Sallust made about the detractors of historians: “It is difficult to write history: first, because the words must be equal to the deeds; and then because most people think that any misdeeds you criticize are described out of malice and envy.” But let us continue the work we have begun.

### 14. How Chlothar went against the Saxons a second time

<a id="hist-04-en-014"></a>
[**[014]**](#hist-04-la-014) After Theudebald’s death, Chlothar took over the kingdom of the Franks. While traveling through it, he heard from his men that the Saxons were again raging in their accustomed madness, rebelling against him and refusing to pay the tribute they had customarily rendered each year. Incited by this report, he marched against them.

When he was already near their border, the Saxons sent envoys to him, saying: “We do not despise you. We do not refuse the payments we have customarily made to your brothers and nephews, and we will pay even more if you ask. We ask only for peace, so that your army and our people may not clash.” King Chlothar said to his men: “These men speak well. Let us not attack them, lest we sin against God.” But they replied: “We know that they are liars and will not fulfill their promises. Let us attack them.”

The Saxons next offered half their property in return for peace. Chlothar said to his men: “I beg you, leave these people alone, lest God’s anger be aroused against us.” They refused. The Saxons then offered clothing, cattle, and all the rest of their property, saying: “Take all these things together with half our land. Only leave our wives and little children free, and let there be no war between us.” The Franks refused even this.

King Chlothar said to them: “I beg you, abandon this plan. We have no just cause. Do not go into a battle in which you will be destroyed. If you insist on going, I will not follow you of my own free will.” Enraged at King Chlothar, they rushed upon him, tore his tent, abused him with insults, and dragged him by force. They were ready to kill him if he delayed going with them. Seeing this, Chlothar went with them unwillingly.

When battle was joined, the Franks were cut down by their enemies in a tremendous slaughter. So great a multitude fell on both sides that it was impossible to estimate or count. Greatly humiliated, Chlothar sued for peace, declaring that he had not attacked the Saxons of his own free will. Peace was obtained, and he returned home.

### 15. Of the episcopate of the holy Eufronius

<a id="hist-04-en-015"></a>
[**[015]**](#hist-04-la-015) When the people of Tours heard that the king had returned from the slaughter of the Saxons, they agreed upon the priest Eufronius and went to the king. After they presented their petition, the king replied: “I had commanded that the priest Cato be ordained there. Why has my command been disregarded?” They answered: “We sought him, but he refused to come.”

While they were speaking, the priest Cato suddenly arrived and begged the king to expel Cautinus and appoint him to the see of Clermont. The king laughed at him. Cato then asked to be ordained at Tours, the see he had previously despised. The king said: “I first commanded that you be consecrated bishop at Tours, but from what I hear, you held that church in contempt. Therefore you shall be kept away from its government.” Cato departed in confusion.

When the king asked about Saint Eufronius, they told him that Eufronius was a kinsman of the blessed Gregory whom we mentioned above. The king replied: “That is a foremost and noble lineage. Let the will of God and blessed Martin be done; let the election be completed.” A royal order was issued, and Saint Eufronius was ordained bishop, the eighteenth after blessed Martin.

### 16. Of Chramn

<a id="hist-04-en-016"></a>
[**[016]**](#hist-04-la-016) Chramn committed various evils in the Auvergne, as we have said, and always cherished hostility toward Bishop Cautinus. At that time he became so gravely ill that his hair fell out because of the violent fever. He had with him a distinguished and entirely good citizen of Clermont named Ascovindus, who tried earnestly to turn him away from his wickedness but could not. He also had Leo of Poitiers with him, a severe incitement to every evil deed. True to his name, Leo was as savage as a lion in every kind of greed. He is said once to have declared that Martin and Martial, confessors of the Lord, had left nothing useful to the resources of the treasury. Immediately struck by the power of the confessors, he became deaf and dumb and died out of his mind. The wretched man went to the basilica of Saint Martin at Tours, kept vigils, and gave gifts, but the saint’s accustomed power did not regard him. He returned with the same affliction he had brought.

Chramn left the Auvergne and came to the city of Poitiers. While residing there in great power, he was led astray by evil counsel and wished to defect to his uncle Childebert, planning treachery against his father. Childebert deceitfully promised to receive him, though he ought instead to have given him spiritual warning not to become his father’s enemy. They exchanged secret messengers, swore a conspiracy, and agreed together against Chlothar. Childebert forgot that whenever he had acted against his brother, he had always departed in confusion.

After making this pact, Chramn returned to the Limousin and brought under his own control the region through which he had previously traveled in his father’s kingdom. Meanwhile the people of Clermont were shut inside their walls and were dying miserably from various diseases.

King Chlothar sent his two sons Charibert and Guntram against Chramn. Passing through the Auvergne and hearing that Chramn was in the Limousin, they advanced as far as the mountain called Black Mountain and found him. They pitched their tents opposite him and sent envoys demanding that he restore his father’s property, which he had wrongfully seized; otherwise he should prepare a battlefield. Chramn pretended to be subject to his father and said: “I cannot give up all that I have acquired, but I wish to retain it under my own authority with my father’s favor.” His brothers demanded that battle decide the matter. Both armies advanced with a great array of weapons, but a sudden storm with violent lightning and thunder prevented them from fighting.

After they returned to camp, Chramn deceitfully sent an outsider to tell his brothers that their father was dead. At that time the war against the Saxons mentioned above was still being waged. Alarmed, the brothers returned to Burgundy with all speed. Chramn sent his army after them, advanced to the city of Chalon, besieged it, and captured it. From there he proceeded to the fortress of Dijon, where he arrived on a Sunday. I shall now relate what happened.

Saint Tetricus, whom we mentioned in an earlier book, was bishop there. The clergy placed three books on the altar—the Prophets, the Apostle, and the Gospels—and prayed to the Lord to reveal what would happen to Chramn and whether divine power would grant him success or the ability to reign. They agreed that each would read at Mass the first passage he found on opening a book. When the book of the Prophets was opened first, they found: “I will take away its hedge, and it shall be laid waste; because instead of producing grapes, it produced wild grapes.” When the book of the Apostle was opened, they found: “You yourselves know well, brothers, that the day of the Lord will come like a thief in the night. When they say, ‘Peace and security,’ sudden destruction will come upon them like the pains of a woman in labor, and they will not escape.” And the Lord said through the Gospel: “Whoever does not hear my words will be like a foolish man who built his house upon sand. The rain fell, the floods came, the winds blew and beat upon that house, and it fell; and great was its fall.”

Chramn was received by the bishop at the basilicas and ate bread there, then proceeded toward Childebert. He was not permitted to enter within the walls of Dijon. At that time King Chlothar was fighting vigorously against the Saxons. They say that the Saxons had been incited by Childebert and had been angry with the Franks in the previous year. They had left their own region, entered Frankish territory, plundered as far as the city of Divitia, and committed extremely grave crimes.

### 17. How Chramn passed over to Childebert

<a id="hist-04-en-017"></a>
[**[017]**](#hist-04-la-017) Chramn had by then married Willichar’s daughter. He went to Paris and bound King Childebert to himself by pledges of loyalty and affection, swearing that he was his father’s implacable enemy. While Chlothar was fighting the Saxons, King Childebert entered the Champagne region and hastened as far as the city of Reims, devastating everything with plunder and fire. He had heard that his brother had been killed by the Saxons. Thinking to bring everything under his own rule, he seized all that he could reach.

### 18. Of the duke Austrapius

<a id="hist-04-en-018"></a>
[**[018]**](#hist-04-la-018) Duke Austrapius also feared Chramn and took refuge in the basilica of Saint Martin. Divine assistance did not fail him in this distress. Chramn ordered that he be confined so strictly that no one dared give him food and that he was not even permitted to draw water. He hoped that hunger would compel Austrapius to leave the holy basilica of his own accord and go to his death. A man brought him a small vessel of water mixed with wine to drink. Austrapius accepted it, but the judge of the place immediately rushed up, snatched it from his hand, and poured it on the ground. God’s swift vengeance and the blessed bishop’s power followed. On the very day he committed this act, the judge was seized by fever and died at midnight. He did not live until the same hour the next day, when he had dashed the cup from the fugitive’s hand in the saint’s basilica. After this miracle, everyone brought Austrapius abundant supplies.

When King Chlothar returned to his kingdom, Austrapius was held in high esteem by him. During Chlothar’s reign Austrapius entered the clergy and was ordained bishop at the fortress of Selles in the diocese of Poitiers, with the intention that, when Bishop Pientius, who then governed the church of Poitiers, died, Austrapius should succeed him. But King Charibert altered the arrangement. When Bishop Pientius departed from this life, Pascentius, then abbot of the basilica of Saint Hilary, succeeded him at Paris by King Charibert’s command. Austrapius protested that the see was owed to him, but his words accomplished little. He returned to his own fortress, where the Thuringians, whom he had often oppressed, rose against him. Wounded by a lance, he died a cruel death. His diocese returned to the church of Poitiers.

### 19. Of the death of Saint Medard the bishop

<a id="hist-04-en-019"></a>
[**[019]**](#hist-04-la-019) In King Chlothar’s time Saint Medard, God’s bishop, completed his course of good works and died full of days and outstanding in holiness. King Chlothar buried him with the highest honor in the city of Soissons and began to build a basilica over him. His son Sigibert later completed and adorned it. At his blessed tomb we have seen prisoners’ fetters and chains lying broken and shattered. They are preserved at the blessed tomb to this day as testimony to his power. But let us return to our earlier narrative.

### 20. Of the death of Childebert and the end of Chramn

<a id="hist-04-en-020"></a>
[**[020]**](#hist-04-la-020) King Childebert fell ill. After lying sick in Paris for a long time, he died and was buried in the basilica of blessed Vincent, which he himself had built. King Chlothar took his kingdom and treasures and sent Vulthrogotha and her two daughters into exile. Chramn was brought before his father but later proved unfaithful again. Seeing that he could not escape, he went to Brittany, where he, his wife, and his daughters hid with Chonoober, count of the Bretons. His father-in-law Willichar took refuge in the basilica of Saint Martin.

The holy basilica was then set on fire by Willichar and his wife, because of the people’s sins and the mockeries committed there; I do not record this without a heavy sigh. The city of Tours had also been consumed by fire a year earlier, and all the churches built there had been left desolate. By King Chlothar’s order, the basilica of blessed Martin was immediately roofed with tin and restored to its former elegance.

Two swarms of locusts then appeared. They passed through the Auvergne and the Limousin and came, it is said, to the field of Romagnat, where they fought one another and were destroyed in great numbers. King Chlothar, enraged with Chramn, marched with an army against him into Brittany. Chramn was not afraid to go out against his father. When both armies were massed in one field and Chramn had drawn up his battle line with the Bretons against his father, night fell and fighting ceased. During the night Chonoober, count of the Bretons, said to Chramn: “I consider it wrong for you to go out against your father. Let me attack him tonight and overthrow him and his whole army.” Chramn, restrained, I believe, by God’s power, would not permit it.

At daybreak both armies advanced to battle against each other. King Chlothar went like a new David to fight against his son Absalom, lamenting and saying: “Look down from heaven, Lord, and judge my cause, for I suffer unjust injuries from my son. Look down, Lord, and judge justly; impose the judgment that you once imposed between Absalom and his father David.” When battle was joined, the count of the Bretons turned and fled and fell there. Chramn then fled toward ships he had prepared at sea. But while he was trying to rescue his wife and daughters, his father’s army overwhelmed him, and he was captured and bound. When this was reported to King Chlothar, he ordered Chramn to be burned together with his wife and daughters. They were shut inside a poor woman’s hut. Chramn was stretched out on a bench and strangled with a cloth. The hut was then set on fire over them, and he perished with his wife and daughters.

### 21. Of the death of King Chlothar

<a id="hist-04-en-021"></a>
[**[021]**](#hist-04-la-021) In the fifty-first year of his reign King Chlothar went with many gifts to the shrine of blessed Martin. Coming to the bishop’s tomb at Tours, he reviewed all the actions that he might have performed negligently. With deep groaning he prayed that the Lord’s blessed confessor would entreat mercy for his sins and wash away by his intercession the things Chlothar had unreasonably committed. After returning from there, still in the fifty-first year of his reign, he was hunting in the forest of Cuise when he was seized by fever. He returned from there to the estate of Compiègne. Grievously tormented by fever, he said: “Alas! What kind of king do you think the heavenly King is, who thus kills such mighty kings?” Worn out by illness, he breathed out his spirit. His four sons carried him with great honor to Soissons and buried him in the basilica of blessed Medard. He died one year less a day after Chramn was killed.

### 22. The division of the kingdom among his sons

<a id="hist-04-en-022"></a>
[**[022]**](#hist-04-la-022) After his father’s funeral, Chilperic took the treasures gathered at the estate of Brannacum. He sought out the more influential Franks, won them over with gifts, and brought them under his authority. He soon entered Paris and occupied King Childebert’s seat. He was not allowed to hold it for long: his brothers joined forces and drove him out. The four—Charibert, Guntram, Chilperic, and Sigibert—then made a lawful division among themselves. The lot assigned Childebert’s kingdom to Charibert, with his seat at Paris; Chlodomer’s kingdom to Guntram, with his seat at Orléans; their father Chlothar’s kingdom to Chilperic, with his seat at Soissons; and Theuderic’s kingdom to Sigibert, with his seat at Reims.

### 23. How Sigibert went against the Huns

<a id="hist-04-en-023"></a>
[**[023]**](#hist-04-la-023) After King Chlothar’s death the Huns attacked Gaul. Sigibert led an army against them, fought a battle, defeated them, and put them to flight. Their king later obtained friendly relations with Sigibert through envoys. While Sigibert was occupied with the Huns, his brother Chilperic seized Reims and took the other cities belonging to him. Civil war, worse than the foreign conflict, arose between the brothers.

Sigibert returned victorious over the Huns and occupied the city of Soissons. Finding Theudebert, King Chilperic’s son, there, he seized him and sent him into exile. He then advanced against Chilperic and made war on him. Chilperic was defeated and put to flight, and Sigibert restored the cities to his own control. He ordered Chilperic’s son Theudebert to be kept under guard at the estate of Ponticon for a full year. Later, being merciful, he enriched him with gifts and returned him safe and sound to his father, after receiving his oath never to act against Sigibert again. That oath was later broken because of sin.

### 24. Of the patriciate of Celsus

<a id="hist-04-en-024"></a>
[**[024]**](#hist-04-la-024) When King Guntram had obtained his share of the kingdom like his brothers, he removed Patrician Agroecola and conferred the honor of the patriciate on Celsus. Celsus was tall in stature, strong in the shoulders, robust in the arms, inflated in speech, ready in reply, and skilled in reading law. His desire for possessions later became so great that he frequently seized church property and brought it under his own control. Once, when he heard a reading from the prophet Isaiah in church—“Woe to those who join house to house and couple field to field until there is no room left”—he is said to have cried out: “That is ill-timed! Woe to me and my sons!” He left a son who died childless and bequeathed the greater part of his property to the churches his father had despoiled.

### 25. Of the wives of Guntram

<a id="hist-04-en-025"></a>
[**[025]**](#hist-04-la-025) King Guntram was a good man. He first took Veneranda, a servant woman belonging to one of his men, as his concubine and had a son by her named Gundobad. He later married Marcatrud, daughter of Magnarius, and sent his son Gundobad to Orléans. After bearing a son herself, Marcatrud became arrogant and plotted Gundobad’s death. They say that she sent him poison in a drink and killed him. After his death, by God’s judgment she lost her own son, incurred the king’s hatred, and was repudiated by him. She died not long afterward. Guntram next married Austrechild, surnamed Bobilla, by whom he again had two sons. The elder was called Chlothar and the younger Chlodomer.

### 26. Of the wives of Charibert

<a id="hist-04-en-026"></a>
[**[026]**](#hist-04-la-026) King Charibert married Ingoberga and had a daughter by her who later went to Kent to take a husband. At that time Ingoberga had in her service two girls, daughters of a poor man. The elder was called Marcovefa and wore religious clothing; the other was Merofledis. The king was deeply in love with them. As we have said, they were the daughters of a woolworker. Ingoberga was jealous because the king loved them. She secretly had their father set to work, hoping that when the king saw this he would despise his daughters. While the man was working, she summoned the king. Expecting to see something new, he looked from a distance and saw the man preparing the royal wool. Angered by the sight, he left Ingoberga and married Merofledis.

He also had another girl named Theudegild, daughter of a shepherd—that is, a keeper of sheep—by whom he is said to have had a son who was carried to the tomb as soon as he came from the womb.

During this king’s reign Leontius assembled the bishops of his province at the city of Saintes and expelled Emerius from the episcopate, asserting that he had not received the office canonically. Emerius had obtained a decree from King Chlothar allowing him to be consecrated without the metropolitan’s consent because the metropolitan was absent. After expelling Emerius, the bishops agreed upon Heraclius, then a priest of the city of Bordeaux. They subscribed the decision in their own hands and sent it to King Charibert through the same priest. Heraclius came to Tours and explained the matter to blessed Eufronius, asking him to deign to subscribe their decision. The man of God openly refused.

When the priest entered the gates of Paris and came into the king’s presence, he addressed him: “Hail, glorious king. The apostolic see sends your eminence abundant greetings.” Charibert asked: “Have you been to the city of Rome, that you bring us its pope’s greeting?” The priest replied: “Your father Leontius and his provincial bishops send you greeting. They report that Cymulus”—for so they used to call Emerius in his childhood—“has been expelled from the episcopate because he sought the bishopric of Saintes in violation of the sanction of the canons. They have therefore sent their decision to you so that another may be appointed in his place. Thus, while transgressors of the canons are properly rebuked, the power of your kingdom will be extended through long ages.”

The king was enraged by this speech. He ordered the priest to be dragged from his sight, placed on a cart heaped with thorns, and driven into exile. He said: “Do you think there is no son of King Chlothar left to uphold his father’s acts, that these bishops have expelled without my judgment a bishop whom his will chose?” He immediately sent religious men to restore Emerius to his see. He also sent chamberlains, who fined Bishop Leontius one thousand gold pieces and condemned the other bishops to fines according to their means. In this way the injury to the prince was avenged.

Charibert afterward married Marcovefa, Merofledis’s sister. Bishop Saint Germanus excommunicated both the king and Marcovefa for this. When the king refused to leave her, she was struck by God’s judgment and died. The king himself died not long after her.

After his death Theudegild, one of his queens, sent messengers to King Guntram and offered herself to him in marriage. The king replied: “Let her not hesitate to come to me with her treasures. I will receive her and make her great among the people, so that she enjoys greater honor with me than with my recently deceased brother.” Rejoicing, she gathered all her possessions and set out to him. Seeing this, the king said: “It is more fitting for these treasures to remain with me than with a woman who unworthily entered my brother’s bed.” He took most of her property, left her a little, and confined her in a monastery at Arles.

She could hardly endure the fasting and vigils imposed on her. Through secret messengers she approached a certain Goth and promised that, if he would take her to Spain and marry her, she would leave the monastery with her treasures and gladly follow him. He agreed without hesitation. She gathered her belongings and prepared bundles in readiness to leave the monastery. But the abbess’s diligence anticipated her plan. The deception was discovered, and the abbess ordered Theudegild to be severely beaten and placed in custody. Worn down by severe sufferings, she remained there until the end of her life.

### 27. How Sigibert took Brunhilda

<a id="hist-04-en-027"></a>
[**[027]**](#hist-04-la-027) King Sigibert saw that his brothers were taking wives unworthy of themselves and, in their baseness, were even marrying servant women. He sent an embassy to Spain and asked with many gifts for Brunhilda, daughter of King Athanagild. The girl was skilled in handiwork, beautiful in appearance, honorable and graceful in conduct, prudent in counsel, and pleasant in speech. Her father did not refuse, but sent her with great treasures to the king. Sigibert assembled his leading men, prepared banquets, and received her as his wife with immense joy and delight. She had been subject to Arian doctrine, but she was converted by the bishops’ preaching and the king’s own exhortation. Confessing and believing in the blessed Trinity in unity, she was anointed with chrism. She remains Catholic in the name of Christ.

### 28. Of the wives of Chilperic

<a id="hist-04-en-028"></a>
[**[028]**](#hist-04-la-028) King Chilperic saw this and, although he already had several wives, sought Brunhilda’s sister Galswintha. He promised through envoys to put away the others if he might obtain a wife worthy of himself and of royal lineage. Her father accepted these promises and sent his daughter to him with great wealth, as he had sent her sister. Galswintha was older than Brunhilda. When she came to King Chilperic, she was received with great honor and joined to him in marriage. He loved her deeply because she had brought great treasures with her. But his love for Fredegund, whom he had previously kept, caused great scandal between them.

Galswintha had by then been converted to the Catholic faith and anointed with chrism. She repeatedly complained to the king that she suffered constant injuries and had no honor in his household. She asked to be allowed to leave behind the treasures she had brought and return freely to her own country. He concealed his intentions and soothed her with gentle words. Finally he ordered a servant to strangle her, and she was found dead in her bed.

After her death God displayed a great miracle. A wooden torch hanging by a cord and burning before her tomb fell to the pavement without anyone touching it when the cord broke. The hardness of the pavement yielded before it as though it were sinking into some soft substance. It was buried halfway into the floor without being broken at all, a great miracle to those who saw it. The king mourned her death, but a few days later he married Fredegund. His brothers judged that he had ordered the queen’s murder and drove him from the kingdom.

Chilperic then had three sons by his former queen Audovera: Theudebert, whom we mentioned above, Merovech, and Chlodovech. But let us return to our narrative.

### 29. Of Sigibert's second war against the Huns

<a id="hist-04-en-029"></a>
[**[029]**](#hist-04-la-029) The Huns attempted to invade Gaul again. Sigibert marched against them with an army and a great multitude of brave men. When battle was about to be joined, the Huns, skilled in magical arts, produced various phantoms and utterly routed them. Sigibert’s army fled, and he himself was surrounded and held by the Huns. But being skillful and resourceful, he later overcame by the art of gift-giving those he could not overcome by force in battle. He gave gifts and made a treaty with their king that they would never wage war against each other for the rest of their lives. Just reason judges this more to his credit than to his reproach. The king of the Huns also gave many gifts to King Sigibert. He was called the khagan; all the kings of that people are called by this title.

### 30. How the Arverni went to take Arles

<a id="hist-04-en-030"></a>
[**[030]**](#hist-04-la-030) King Sigibert wished to capture the city of Arles and ordered the Auvergnats to mobilize. Firminus was then count of their city, and he went at their head. Adovarius approached from another direction with an army. They entered Arles and exacted oaths on behalf of King Sigibert. When King Guntram learned of this, he sent Patrician Celsus there with an army. Celsus went and captured Avignon, then advanced on Arles and surrounded it. He began to attack Sigibert’s army, which was confined within the walls.

Bishop Sabaudus said to them: “Go outside and join battle. You cannot defend us or this city’s inhabitants by remaining enclosed within the walls. If you defeat them with God’s favor, we will keep the faith we promised. If they prevail against you, you will find the gates open; come in so that you do not perish.” Deceived by this trick, they went out and prepared for battle. Celsus’s army defeated them, and they fled. When they reached the city, they found the gates barred. Attacked from behind by the army’s javelins and pelted with stones by the townspeople, they made for the river Rhône. Supported by their shields, they tried to reach the farther bank. The river’s violence swept many of them away and drowned them. The Rhône then did to the Auvergnats what the Simois is said once to have done to the Trojans: “It rolls beneath the waves the snatched shields of men, their helmets, and their strong bodies. A few swimmers appear in the vast whirlpool.” Only with great difficulty, swimming and aided by the support of their shields, were they able to reach the level ground of the opposite shore. Stripped of their possessions and deprived of their horses, they returned to their country in great disgrace. Firminus and Adovarius, however, were granted leave to depart. Many noble Auvergnats died there, not only swept away by the torrent’s force but also struck down by swords. King Guntram recovered Arles and, with his customary goodness, restored Avignon to his brother’s control.

### 31. Of the castle of Tauredunum and other signs

<a id="hist-04-en-031"></a>
[**[031]**](#hist-04-la-031) A great prodigy occurred in Gaul at the fortress of Tauredunum, which stood above the river Rhône. For more than sixty days it emitted a strange roaring sound. At last the mountain split and separated from a neighboring mountain. It plunged into the river with its men, churches, property, and houses. The river’s channel was blocked, and the water flowed backward. That place was enclosed on both sides by mountains, and the river flowed through the narrow passage between them. The flood covered and destroyed everything situated on the upper bank. The accumulated water then burst downward, caught people unawares, and drowned them as it had done above. It overturned houses, destroyed cattle, and swept away and overthrew everything on those banks as far as the city of Geneva in a violent and sudden inundation. Many relate that the mass of water was so great that it entered Geneva over its walls. This is not difficult to believe, because, as we have said, the Rhône flows there through a narrow passage between mountains; when it was blocked, it had nowhere to turn aside. The dislodged mountain moved and collapsed all at once, destroying everything.

After this happened, thirty monks came to the place where the fortress had fallen. Digging in the ground left behind by the mountain’s collapse, they found bronze and iron. While doing this, they heard the mountain roaring as before. Savage greed held them there. The part that had not yet fallen came down on them, covered them, and killed them; they were never found again.

Great prodigies likewise terrified the Auvergne before its disaster. Often three or four great lights appeared around the sun. The rustics called them suns and said: “Look, there are three or four suns in the sky!” On one occasion, on the kalends of October, the sun appeared so darkened that not even a quarter of it remained shining. Foul and discolored, it looked like a sack. A star that some call a comet, with a beam like a sword, appeared over that region for a whole year. The sky seemed to burn, and many other signs appeared.

In the church at Clermont, while the morning vigils were being celebrated on a certain feast, a lark entered. It extinguished all the burning lights by putting its wings over them with such speed that one would have thought a single hand had plunged them into water. Passing beneath the veil into the sanctuary, it tried to extinguish the lamp there, but the doorkeepers stopped and killed it. Another bird did the same to the burning lamps in the basilica of blessed Andrew.

When the disaster itself arrived, so great a destruction of the people occurred throughout the region that the multitudes who fell there could not be counted. When sarcophagi and planks ran out, ten or more bodies were buried in a single trench in the ground. On one Sunday three hundred corpses were counted in the basilica of blessed Peter alone. Death was sudden. When a sore like a serpent appeared in the groin or armpit, the poison killed the person so quickly that he breathed out his spirit on the second or third day. The force of the poison also deprived him of his senses.

The priest Cato died at that time. Many fled from the plague, but he never left the place: he buried the people and celebrated Masses for them individually. This priest was very humane and greatly loved by the poor. I believe that this charity served as a remedy for any pride he may have had. Bishop Cautinus, fearing the disaster, traveled through various places but returned to the city. He caught the disease and died on the day of preparation for the Lord’s Passion. His cousin Tetradius died at the same hour. Lyon, Bourges, Chalon, and Dijon were also severely depopulated by this sickness.

### 32. Of Julian the monk

<a id="hist-04-en-032"></a>
[**[032]**](#hist-04-la-032) At that time there was at the monastery of Randan in the territory of Clermont a priest of outstanding virtue named Julian. He was a man of great abstinence, taking neither wine nor any other relish, always wearing a hair shirt beneath his tunic, prompt in vigils, and constant in prayer. By invoking the Lord’s name and making the sign of the holy cross, he could readily heal the possessed, give sight to the blind, and drive away other infirmities. Standing continually caused his feet to become infected with sores. When asked why he always stood beyond his body’s capacity, he used to answer with a spiritual jest: “They do my work while life is kind, and with the Lord’s help their support does not abandon me.” We once saw him heal a possessed man with only a word in the basilica of the blessed martyr Julian. He also often brought relief through prayer to those suffering from quartan and other fevers. During this time of plague, full of days and virtues, he was taken from this world into rest.

### 33. Of the abbot Sunniulfus

<a id="hist-04-en-033"></a>
[**[033]**](#hist-04-la-033) The abbot of that monastery also died at that time, and Sunniulf succeeded him. Sunniulf was a man of complete simplicity and charity. He often washed the guests’ feet himself and dried them with his own hands. He governed the flock entrusted to him not by fear but by entreaty. He used to relate that he had been led in a vision to a fiery river. Crowds rushing together from one bank were plunged into it like bees entering hives. Some stood in it up to the waist, others up to the armpits, and some up to the chin, crying out with tears that they were being fiercely burned. A bridge over the river was so narrow that it could scarcely accommodate the width of a single footstep. On the other bank appeared a great house, whitened on the outside. Sunniulf asked those with him what these things meant. They replied: “Whoever is found slothful in correcting the flock entrusted to him will be thrown from this bridge. But whoever is diligent will cross without danger and be joyfully led into the house you see beyond.” On hearing this, he awoke and thereafter appeared much sterner to the monks.

### 34. Of the monk of Bordeaux

<a id="hist-04-en-034"></a>
[**[034]**](#hist-04-la-034) I will also relate what happened at a certain monastery at that time. I will not name the monk because he is still alive, lest he fall into vainglory and relapse when this writing reaches him. A young man came to a monastery and commended himself to the abbot in order to live in God’s service. The abbot raised many objections, telling him that the service of the place was hard and that he could not possibly fulfill all that would be required of him. Invoking the Lord’s name, the young man promised to fulfill everything, and so the abbot received him.

A few days later, while he was conducting himself with complete humility and holiness, the monks brought grain out of the granary and spread about three heaps in the sun to dry. They assigned him to guard it. While the others were taking refreshment and he remained watching the grain, the sky suddenly clouded over. A heavy rainstorm, driven by a rushing wind, rapidly approached the heap of grain. The monk did not know what to do. Considering that if he called the others they could not gather so much grain into the granaries before the rain, he set everything else aside and turned to prayer. He begged the Lord that not a drop of that rain might fall on the wheat. He threw himself on the ground and prayed. The cloud divided, and rain poured heavily all around the grain without wetting a single grain of wheat, if I may say so.

When the other monks and the abbot realized what was happening, they hurried to gather the grain. Seeing the miracle, they looked for the guard and found him lying on the sand not far away, praying. At the sight the abbot prostrated himself behind him. When the rain had passed and the prayer was finished, he called the monk to rise. He then seized him and ordered him to be beaten, saying: “You must grow humbly in the fear and service of God, my son, not glory in prodigies and miracles.” He shut him in a cell and ordered him to fast for seven days like a guilty man, to turn vainglory away from him so that it would not become an obstacle.

We have learned from trustworthy men that this monk now practices such abstinence that during the days of Lent he eats no bread, but only every third day drinks a full cup of barley water. With your prayers, may the Lord deign to preserve him until the completion of his life so that he may please him.

### 35. Of the episcopate of Avitus of the Auvergne

<a id="hist-04-en-035"></a>
[**[035]**](#hist-04-la-035) After Bishop Cautinus died at Clermont, as we have said, many sought the episcopate, offering much and promising still more. The priest Eufrasius, son of the former senator Euodius, sent valuable merchandise obtained from Jews to the king through his kinsman Beregisil, hoping to obtain by rewards what he could not obtain by merit. He was elegant in manner but unchaste in conduct. He often gave barbarians drink but seldom refreshed the needy. I believe that he failed because he sought this honor through men rather than through God. Nor could the word spoken by the Lord through the mouth of Saint Quintianus be altered: “No one shall arise from the stock of Hortensius to rule the church of God.”

The archdeacon Avitus assembled the clergy in the church at Clermont. He made no promises, but after receiving their consent he went to the king. Firminus, who had obtained the countship in that city, wished to obstruct him but did not go himself. The friends sent on his behalf begged the king at least to let one Sunday pass without Avitus being consecrated there. They promised the king one thousand gold pieces if this were made known, but the king did not consent.

The citizens of Clermont assembled together, and blessed Avitus, who was then archdeacon, was elected by clergy and people and received the episcopal see. The king honored and loved him so greatly that, departing somewhat from canonical strictness, he ordered Avitus to be consecrated in his own presence, saying: “May I be found worthy to receive blessed bread from his hand.” This favor caused him to be consecrated in the city of Metz.

After receiving the episcopate, Avitus showed himself great in every respect. He rendered justice to the people, aid to the poor, comfort to widows, and the greatest assistance to orphans. A stranger who came to him was treated with such love that he felt he had found both a father and a homeland in him. Avitus flourished in great virtues and kept with his whole heart everything pleasing to God, rooting out wrongful luxury everywhere and planting the chastity just before God.

### 36. Of Saint Nicetius of Lyon

<a id="hist-04-en-036"></a>
[**[036]**](#hist-04-la-036) When Sacerdos, bishop of Lyon, died at Paris after the synod that expelled Saffaracus, Saint Nicetius was elected by him and received the episcopate, as we wrote in the book of his Life. He was outstanding in complete holiness and chaste conduct. The apostle commanded us to preserve charity with everyone if possible; Nicetius practiced it toward all as far as possible, so that the Lord himself, who is true charity, could be discerned in his heart. If he was angered by someone’s negligence, he received the person back as soon as he was corrected, as though he had never been offended. He corrected offenders and forgave penitents. He gave alms and worked diligently. He took the greatest care in building churches, putting houses in order, sowing fields, and planting vineyards, but these activities did not distract him from prayer.

After serving in the priesthood for twenty-two years, he departed to the Lord. He now performs great miracles at his tomb for those who pray. Through the oil of the lamp burned daily at his tomb, he restores sight to blind eyes, drives demons from possessed bodies, restores health to contracted limbs, and is a great protection to all the sick at this time.

Bishop Priscus, who succeeded him, and his wife Susanna began to persecute and kill many who had been close to the man of God. They had not been overcome by any fault, convicted of any crime, or caught in any theft. Envy alone inflamed Priscus because they had been faithful to Nicetius. He and his wife uttered many blasphemies against the saint of God. Earlier bishops had long observed the rule that a woman should not enter the house belonging to the church, but Susanna and her maids even entered the chamber in which the blessed man had rested.

Divine majesty was at last aroused by these acts and took vengeance on Bishop Priscus’s household. His wife was seized by a demon. With her hair loose, she was driven madly through the whole city. She proclaimed the saint of God, whose friendship with Christ she had denied while sane, and begged him to spare her. The bishop was seized by a quartan fever and developed a tremor. Even when the fever subsided, he remained trembling and dull. His son and whole household appeared pale and stupefied. No one can doubt that they were struck by the holy man’s power. Bishop Priscus and his household constantly barked impious words against the saint of God and declared anyone their friend who poured out insults against him.

At the beginning of his episcopate Priscus had ordered the church house to be raised. A deacon whom Saint Nicetius, while alive, had repeatedly punished for adultery—not only excluding him from communion but often ordering him to be beaten, without ever being able to bring him to amendment—climbed onto the roof of that house. When he began to uncover it, he said: “I thank you, Jesus Christ, because after the death of that most wicked Nicetius I have been found worthy to walk upon this roof.” The words were still on his lips when the surface on which he stood was pulled from beneath his feet. He fell to the ground, burst, and died.

While the bishop and his wife were committing many unreasonable acts, the saint appeared in a dream to a certain man and said: “Go and tell Priscus to amend his evil works and make his works good. Also tell the priest Martin: ‘Because you consent to these works, you will be chastised; if you refuse to amend your perversity, you will die.’” On waking, the man spoke to a deacon: “Since you are a friend in the bishop’s house, please go and say these things to the bishop and to the priest Martin.” The deacon promised to speak, but then drew back and refused. That night, when he had gone to sleep, the saint appeared to him and said: “Why did you not repeat what the abbot told you?” He closed his fists and began beating the deacon’s throat. In the morning the deacon’s throat was swollen and very painful. He went to the men and reported everything he had heard. They paid little attention and said it was merely a dream vision. The priest Martin immediately fell into a fever and was ill, but recovered. Yet he continued to speak flatteringly to the bishop and consent to his evil acts and the blasphemies they poured out against the saint. He was again seized by fever and breathed out his spirit.

### 37. Of Saint Friard the recluse

<a id="hist-04-en-037"></a>
[**[037]**](#hist-04-la-037) Saint Friard also died at the same time as Saint Nicetius. He was full of days, outstanding in holiness, sublime in action, and noble in life. We recorded some of his miracles in the book we wrote about his Life. At his passing, as Bishop Felix arrived, the whole cell shook. I do not doubt that an angelic presence was there and made the place tremble as he departed. The bishop washed him, wrapped him in fitting garments, and committed him to burial.

### 38. Of the kings of the Spaniards

<a id="hist-04-en-038"></a>
[**[038]**](#hist-04-la-038) To return to our history: when King Athanagild died in Spain, Liuva received the kingdom together with his brother Liuvigild. After Liuva died, his brother Liuvigild took possession of the whole kingdom. Following his own wife’s death, he married Goiswintha, Queen Brunhilda’s mother. He had two sons by his first wife; one was betrothed to Sigibert’s daughter and the other to Chilperic’s daughter. He divided the kingdom equally between them. He also killed all who had been accustomed to murder their kings, leaving not a single one of them alive to piss against a wall.

### 39. Of the death of Palladius of the Auvergne

<a id="hist-04-en-039"></a>
[**[039]**](#hist-04-la-039) Palladius, son of the former count Britianus and Caesaria, obtained the countship in the city of Javols through King Sigibert’s grant. A dispute arose between him and Bishop Parthenius that greatly injured the people. Palladius frequently overwhelmed the bishop with insults, various reproaches, and accusations, seizing church property and despoiling his men. As the dispute grew, they hastened into the prince’s presence and exchanged many accusations. Palladius called the bishop soft and effeminate, saying: “Where are your husbands, with whom you live disgracefully and shamefully?” Divine vengeance soon followed and blotted out these words spoken against the bishop.

The following year Palladius was removed from the countship and returned to the Auvergne, while Romanus sought the office. One day in the city of Clermont the two met. While they were quarreling over the countship, Palladius heard that King Sigibert intended to have him killed. The report was found to be false and to have originated chiefly with Romanus. Terrified, Palladius was reduced to such severe distress that he threatened to kill himself with his own hand. His mother and his kinsman Firminus watched him closely to prevent him from carrying out his bitter intention. But after some hours he escaped his mother’s sight and entered his chamber. Taking advantage of being alone, he drew his sword, pressed its hilt beneath his feet, and raised the point toward his breast. He threw himself upon it: entering at one nipple, the sword came out through his back near the shoulder blade. He rose again, pierced himself similarly through the other breast, fell, and died.

I marvel that this crime was not accomplished without the devil’s work. The first wound could have killed him if the devil had not supported him in carrying out this impious act. His mother ran up half-dead and fell in bereavement upon her son’s body, while the whole household uttered cries of lamentation. He was carried to the monastery of Chronon and buried, but not beside Christian corpses, nor did he receive the solemnity of Masses. It is believed that this befell him for no other reason than the injury he had done to the bishop.

### 40. Of the empire of Justin

<a id="hist-04-en-040"></a>
[**[040]**](#hist-04-la-040) After Emperor Justinian died at Constantinople, Justin obtained the empire. He was devoted to every kind of avarice, despised the poor, and despoiled the senators. His greed was so great that he ordered iron chests to be made in which he could heap up talents of gold coin. They also say that he fell into the Pelagian heresy. Not long afterward he lost his reason and appointed Tiberius Caesar to defend his provinces. Tiberius was a just man, an almsgiver, equitable in judgment, victorious in war, and—what surpasses all other virtues—a most Christian man.

King Sigibert sent envoys to Emperor Justin to seek peace, namely Warnachar the Frank and Firminus the Auvergnat. Traveling by ship, they entered Constantinople, spoke with the emperor, and obtained what they sought. They returned to Gaul in the following year.

After this the Persians captured Antioch in Egypt and Apamea in Syria, very great cities, and carried the people away captive. The basilica of Saint Julian, martyr of Antioch, was then destroyed by a severe fire. The Armenians under Persian rule came to Emperor Justin with a great weight of woven silk. They sought his friendship and explained that they were hostile to the Persian emperor. The Persian emperor’s envoys had come to them, saying: “The emperor’s concern requires us to ask whether you are keeping intact the treaty made with him.” When the Armenians replied that they were keeping all their promises inviolate, the envoys said: “It will be clear that you preserve his friendship if you worship fire as he does.” The people answered that they would never do so. The bishop who was present said: “What divinity is there in fire, that it should be worshipped? God created it for human use. It is kindled by fuel, extinguished by water, burns when applied, and grows cool when neglected.” As the bishop continued with these and similar words, the envoys were inflamed with fury and beat him with fists and clubs. Seeing their bishop stained with blood, the people rushed upon the envoys, laid hands on them, and killed them. Then, as we have said, the Armenians sought this emperor’s friendship.

### 41. How Alboin with the Lombards occupied Italy

<a id="hist-04-en-041"></a>
[**[041]**](#hist-04-la-041) Alboin, king of the Lombards, who had married Chlothsinda, King Chlothar’s daughter, left his own region and went to Italy with the entire Lombard people. Calling up their army, they departed with their wives and children, intending to settle there. They entered the region and ranged through it for about seven years, plundering churches and killing priests, and brought it under their own power. After Alboin’s wife Chlothsinda died, he married another woman whose father he had killed shortly before. She always hated her husband and waited for an opportunity to avenge her father’s wrong. Desiring one of the servants, she poisoned her husband. After his death she went away with the servant, but they were captured together and killed. The Lombards then set another king over themselves.

### 42. Of the wars of Mummolus against the Lombards

<a id="hist-04-en-042"></a>
[**[042]**](#hist-04-la-042) Eunius, surnamed Mummolus, obtained the patriciate from King Guntram. I think I should go back further to explain the origin of his military career. He was born to his father Peonius and was an inhabitant of the city of Auxerre. Peonius held the countship of that city. When he sent gifts to the king through his son to renew his tenure, the son received his father’s property, sought his father’s countship, and supplanted the father whom he ought to have supported. Advancing gradually from this beginning, he rose to a higher office.

When the Lombards broke into Gaul, Patrician Amatus, who had recently succeeded Celsus, went against them. Battle was joined; he fled and fell there. The Lombards are said to have slaughtered so many Burgundians that the number of the slain could not be gathered. Laden with booty, they returned to Italy. After their departure Eunius, also called Mummolus, was summoned by the king and received the dignity of the patriciate.

The Lombards again broke into Gaul and advanced as far as Mustiae Calmes, near the city of Embrun. Mummolus called out his army and went there with the Burgundians. He surrounded the Lombards with his army, laid ambushes, and rushed upon them through trackless parts of the forest. He killed many, captured some, and sent them to the king. The king ordered the captives to be dispersed and guarded in various places. Only a few escaped by flight to carry the news home. The brothers Salonius and Sagittarius, both bishops, took part in this battle. Not guided by the heavenly cross but armed with helmet and secular breastplate, they are said—what is worse—to have killed many men with their own hands. This was Mummolus’s first victory in battle.

Afterward the Saxons who had come with the Lombards into Italy again broke into Gaul. They pitched camp within the territory of Riez, at the estate of Stablo. They ranged through the estates of the neighboring cities, seizing plunder, carrying off captives, and laying everything waste. When Mummolus learned of this, he called out his army and rushed upon them. He killed many thousands and did not cease slaughtering until evening, when night put an end to it. He had caught the men off their guard, suspecting nothing of his approach. At daybreak the Saxons drew up their army and prepared for battle, but messengers intervened and they made peace. They gave gifts to Mummolus, left all the region’s plunder and the captives, and departed. First they swore to return to Gaul in submission to the kings and in support of the Franks.

On returning to Italy, the Saxons took their wives, little children, and all the furnishings of their property, intending to go back to Gaul. They meant to be gathered by King Sigibert and settled in the place from which they had originally departed. They formed two divisions, as is said. One came through the city of Nice and the other through Embrun, following the same route as in the previous year. They joined forces in the territory of Avignon. It was harvest time, and that place had abundant crops standing in the open; the inhabitants had stored none of them at home. The Saxons divided the crops among themselves, gathered and threshed the grain, and ate it, leaving nothing for those who had done the work.

After consuming the produce, they reached the bank of the river Rhône, intending to cross the torrent and place themselves under King Sigibert’s rule. Mummolus met them and said: “You shall not cross this torrent. You have devastated the region of my lord the king, gathered the crops, destroyed the cattle, burned the houses, and cut down the olive groves and vineyards. You shall not proceed unless you first compensate those whom you have left destitute. Otherwise you will not escape my hands: I will set the sword upon you, your wives, and your little children, and avenge the injury done to my lord King Guntram.” Greatly afraid, they gave many thousands of gold coins as their ransom and were permitted to cross. Thus they reached the Auvergne.

It was then springtime. They produced stamped bars of bronze and passed them off as gold. Everyone who saw them believed them to be tested and proven gold, so cleverly had they been colored by some device. Deceived by this trick, many people handed over gold, received bronze, and became poor. The Saxons then crossed over to King Sigibert and were settled in the place from which they had originally departed.

### 43. Of the archdeacon of Marseille

<a id="hist-04-en-043"></a>
[**[043]**](#hist-04-la-043) In King Sigibert’s kingdom Jovinus, governor of Provence, was removed from office, and Albinus was appointed in his place. This caused great enmity between them. When overseas ships arrived at the harbor of Marseille, seventy men belonging to Archdeacon Vigilius stole casks, commonly called orcae, containing oil and garum, without their master’s knowledge. When the merchant discovered that his property had been stolen, he began a careful inquiry into where the stolen goods had been hidden. Someone told him that Archdeacon Vigilius’s men had committed the theft. The report reached the archdeacon. He investigated and discovered the truth, but instead of making it public he began to excuse his men, saying: “No one has ever left my house who would dare commit such a deed.”

Since the archdeacon defended them in this way, the merchant went to Albinus, laid out his case, and accused the archdeacon of complicity in the theft. On the holy day of the Lord’s Nativity the bishop entered the church. The archdeacon, clothed in an alb, stood ready as custom required to invite the bishop to proceed to the altar and celebrate the solemnity at the proper time. Without delay Albinus leaped from his seat, seized the archdeacon, dragged him away, beat him with fists and kicks, and confined him in prison. Neither the bishop, nor the citizens, nor any elder, nor the cry of the whole people could persuade him to release the archdeacon on sureties so that he might celebrate the holy day with the others and have the accusation heard later. Albinus did not even fear the sacred solemnity enough to refrain from seizing a minister of the Lord’s altar on such a day. He condemned the archdeacon to a fine of four thousand solidi. Albinus later came before King Sigibert and, with Jovinus pressing the case, was obliged to pay fourfold compensation.

### 44. Of the Lombards and Mummolus

<a id="hist-04-en-044"></a>
[**[044]**](#hist-04-la-044) Afterward three Lombard leaders—Amo, Zaban, and Rodanus—invaded Gaul. Amo took the road through Embrun and advanced as far as the estate of Machao in the territory of Avignon, which Mummolus had obtained by royal gift; there he pitched his tents. Zaban descended through the city of Die and came as far as Valence, where he also established camp. Rodanus attacked the city of Grenoble and spread out his tents there. Amo overran the province of Arles and the surrounding cities. Advancing as far as the Stony Plain near Marseille, he stripped it of cattle and people. He prepared to besiege Aix but withdrew after receiving twenty-two pounds of silver from it. Rodanus and Zaban did likewise in the places they attacked.

When these events were reported to Mummolus, he called out his army and went to meet Rodanus, who was attacking Grenoble. The army was crossing the river Isère with difficulty when, by God’s will, an animal entered the river, revealed a ford, and enabled the people to reach the farther bank. The Lombards saw this and immediately attacked with drawn swords. In the ensuing battle they were cut down so severely that Rodanus, wounded by a lance, fled to the mountain heights. Breaking through trackless forests with the five hundred men remaining to him, he reached Zaban, who was then besieging Valence, and reported everything that had happened. After collecting all their plunder together, they returned toward Embrun. Mummolus met them there face to face with an immense army. Battle was joined, and the Lombard ranks were cut down almost to annihilation. Their leaders returned to Italy with only a few men.

When they reached the city of Susa, the inhabitants received them harshly, especially because Sisinnius, master of the soldiers on the emperor’s side, was residing there. A servant pretended to belong to Mummolus and produced letters for Sisinnius in Zaban’s sight. He delivered greetings in Mummolus’s name and said: “Here he is close at hand!” Hearing this, Zaban immediately left and hurried past the city. When Amo heard this news, he collected all his plunder on the march and set out. But the snows blocked his way; abandoning the plunder, he was barely able to escape with a few men. They were terrified of Mummolus’s power.

### 45. How Mummolus came to Tours

<a id="hist-04-en-045"></a>
[**[045]**](#hist-04-la-045) Mummolus fought many wars and emerged victorious. After Charibert’s death Chilperic seized Tours and Poitiers, which had been assigned to King Sigibert by agreement. Sigibert joined with his brother Guntram, and they chose Mummolus to recall these cities to their rightful ruler. Mummolus came to Tours and drove out Chilperic’s son Chlodovech. After exacting oaths from the people on King Sigibert’s behalf, he advanced to Poitiers. Basilius and Sigarius, citizens of Poitiers, assembled a multitude and tried to resist. Mummolus surrounded them from different directions, crushed, overwhelmed, and killed them. He then entered Poitiers and exacted oaths. Let this account of Mummolus suffice for now; the rest will be related later.

### 46. Of the death of Andarcius

<a id="hist-04-en-046"></a>
[**[046]**](#hist-04-la-046) Before describing Andarchius’s death, I wish first to set out his origin and homeland. They say that he was a slave of Senator Felix. Assigned to attend his lord, he was educated alongside him and distinguished himself by his excellent training. He was thoroughly instructed in Virgil’s works, the books of the Theodosian Code, and arithmetic. Puffed up by this knowledge, he began to despise his masters. When Duke Lupus went to Marseille by King Sigibert’s command, Andarchius commended himself to Lupus’s protection. On returning, Lupus took Andarchius with him, carefully recommended him to King Sigibert, and placed him in the king’s service. Sigibert sent him to various places and gave him the opportunity of military service. Regarded as a man of honor because of this, Andarchius came to the Auvergne and formed a friendship with Ursus, a citizen of Clermont.

Being sharp-witted, he wished to betroth Ursus’s daughter. They say that he hid a breastplate in a document chest in which deeds were usually kept. He told Ursus’s wife: “I entrust to you more than sixteen thousand of my gold pieces hidden in this chest. They can be yours if you promise your daughter to me in betrothal.” But “accursed hunger for gold, to what do you not compel mortal hearts?” The woman believed him without suspicion and promised the girl to him in her husband’s absence.

Andarchius returned to the king and presented an order to the local judge directing that the girl be joined to him in marriage, saying: “I have given earnest money for her betrothal.” Ursus denied this, saying: “I do not know who you are or where you come from, and I have none of your property.” As the dispute grew more violent, Andarchius sought to summon Ursus before the king. When Ursus came to the estate of Brinnacum, Andarchius found another man named Ursus. He secretly brought this other man to an altar and made him swear: “By this holy place and the relics of the blessed martyrs, if I do not give you my daughter in marriage, I will pay you sixteen thousand solidi without delay.” Witnesses stood in the sanctuary, secretly listening to the words spoken but entirely unable to see the person who spoke.

Andarchius then soothed the real Ursus with gentle words and made him return home without appearing before the king. He had a written record of the oath prepared and presented it to the king as he departed, saying: “Ursus made this written promise to me. I therefore request an order from your glory directing him to give me his daughter in marriage. Otherwise, let me possess his property until I have received the sixteen thousand solidi and can withdraw from the case.” Having obtained the order, he returned to the Auvergne and showed the king’s command to the judge.

Ursus withdrew to the territory of Villavo. While his property was being consigned to Andarchius, Andarchius came to Villavo. Entering one of Ursus’s houses, he ordered supper to be prepared and water heated for his bath. When the servants did not appear sufficiently prompt to their rude master, he beat one with a club and another with rods, striking several on the head until he drew blood. The terrified household prepared supper. He bathed in warm water, became drunk on wine, and lay down on a couch. Only seven servants were with him. Buried in wine and sleep alike, they slept deeply.

The household assembled and shut the doors of the house, which was built of wooden planks. Taking the keys, they broke down the stacks of grain standing nearby and heaped the grain around and over the house. The grain was then in sheaves, and the house appeared entirely covered with it. They set fire to it in different places. When the burning material of the building was already falling on the unhappy men inside, they awoke and cried out, but no one came to help them. The whole house was burned down, and the fire consumed them with it. Ursus, fearing the consequences, sought refuge in the basilica of Saint Julian. He gave gifts to the king and recovered his property intact.

### 47. How Theudebert seized the cities

<a id="hist-04-en-047"></a>
[**[047]**](#hist-04-la-047) Chilperic’s son Chlodovech was expelled from Tours and went to Bordeaux. While he was residing peacefully in Bordeaux, a certain Sigulf on Sigibert’s side attacked him. Sigulf pursued him with trumpets and horns as though hunting a fleeing stag. Chlodovech was scarcely able to return safely to his father, but he eventually reached him by way of Angers.

A dispute arose between Kings Guntram and Sigibert. King Guntram assembled all the bishops of his kingdom at Paris so that they might determine where the truth lay between the two kings. But, because of sin, the kings delayed hearing them, allowing the civil war to grow more destructive. Chilperic was enraged. Through his elder son Theudebert—who had once been captured by Sigibert and had sworn to be faithful to him—he seized Sigibert’s cities: Tours, Poitiers, and the others situated on this side of the Loire. Theudebert came to Poitiers and fought Duke Gundovald. Gundovald’s army fled, and Theudebert inflicted great slaughter on its men. He also burned the greater part of the region of Tours and would immediately have subdued all of it if the inhabitants had not submitted for the time being.

Calling out his army, he overran, wasted, and overthrew the Limousin, Cahors, and the neighboring regions. He burned churches, carried off their furnishings, killed clergy, destroyed men’s monasteries, desecrated women’s convents, and devastated everything. At that time there was greater lamentation in the churches than during Diocletian’s persecution.

### 48. Of the monastery of Latte

<a id="hist-04-en-048"></a>
[**[048]**](#hist-04-la-048) We still stand astonished and wonder why such great blows fell upon them. But let us compare what their parents did with what these men are doing. Their ancestors were converted from pagan shrines to churches by the preaching of bishops; these men daily drag plunder out of churches. Their ancestors venerated and obeyed the Lord’s bishops with their whole heart; these men not only refuse to obey them but persecute them. Their ancestors enriched monasteries and churches; these men destroy and overthrow them.

What shall I say about the monastery of Latte, where relics of blessed Martin are kept? A troop of enemies approached it and prepared to cross the nearby river to plunder the monastery. The monks cried out: “Do not cross here, barbarians! Do not! This is blessed Martin’s monastery.” Many heard them and, pierced by the fear of God, turned back. But twenty of them, who neither feared God nor honored the blessed confessor, boarded a boat and crossed. At the enemy’s prompting they beat the monks, destroyed the monastery, and plundered its property. They made the goods into bundles and loaded them onto the boat.

As soon as they entered the river, the boat began to toss and carried them this way and that. Having lost the use of their oars, they fixed their spear shafts into the riverbed and tried to push themselves back. The boat split open beneath their feet. Each man’s weapon, which he was holding in front of himself, was driven into his breast, and all were pierced and killed by their own javelins. Only one remained unharmed—the man who had rebuked them to prevent them from committing these deeds. If anyone judges that this happened by chance, let him observe that one innocent man escaped while many guilty men perished. After they were killed, the monks drew the bodies and their property out of the river. They buried the men and restored the goods to the monastery.

### 49. How Sigibert came to Paris

<a id="hist-04-en-049"></a>
[**[049]**](#hist-04-la-049) While these things were happening, King Sigibert called out the peoples living beyond the Rhine and resolved to begin civil war against his brother Chilperic. Hearing this, Chilperic sent envoys to his brother Guntram. They joined together and made a treaty that neither would allow his brother to be destroyed. Sigibert arrived with those peoples, while Chilperic remained on the opposite side with his army. Sigibert wished to advance against his brother but had no place to cross the river Seine. He sent a command to his brother Guntram: “Unless you allow me to cross this river through your share, I will march against you with my whole army.” Fearing this, Guntram made a treaty with him and allowed him to cross.

When Chilperic perceived that Guntram had abandoned him and gone over to Sigibert, he moved camp and went as far as the village of Ablis near Chartres. Sigibert pursued him and demanded that he prepare a battlefield. Chilperic feared that, if both armies clashed, their kingdom would also collapse. He sued for peace and restored the cities that Theudebert had wrongfully seized. He begged that their inhabitants not be punished, since his son had acquired them unjustly by oppression with fire and sword.

The villages around Paris were largely consumed by fire. Houses and other property were plundered by the enemy, and captives were carried away. The king adjured them not to do these things, but he could not restrain the fury of the peoples who had come from beyond the Rhine. He endured everything patiently until they could return to their homeland. Some of those peoples then murmured against him for withdrawing from battle. Being fearless, he mounted his horse, rode to them, and soothed them with gentle words; afterward he ordered many of them to be stoned.

No one doubts that blessed Martin’s power enabled these men to make peace without battle. On the very day they made peace, three paralytics were brought to the blessed man’s basilica. With the Lord’s help we shall describe this in later books.

### 50. How Chilperic made a pact with Guntram, and of the death of Theudebert his son

<a id="hist-04-en-050"></a>
[**[050]**](#hist-04-la-050) It grieves the mind to relate these civil wars. A year later Chilperic again sent envoys to his brother Guntram, saying: “Let my brother come so that we may meet, make peace, and pursue our enemy Sigibert together.” This was done: they met and honored each other with gifts. Chilperic called out his army and advanced as far as Reims, burning and overrunning everything. Hearing this, Sigibert again summoned the peoples mentioned above and came to Paris, preparing to march against his brother. He sent word to the men of Douai and Tours to attack Theudebert. When they evaded the order, the king sent Dukes Godegisel and Guntram to lead them. They called out their army and advanced against Theudebert. Abandoned by his own men, Theudebert remained with only a few, but he did not hesitate to go into battle. When battle was joined, Theudebert was defeated and laid low on the field. His enemies stripped his lifeless body, which is grievous to relate. A certain Aunulf gathered him up, washed him, clothed him in fitting garments, and buried him in the city of Soissons. When Chilperic learned that Guntram had again made peace with Sigibert, he fortified himself with his wife and sons within the walls of Tournai.

### 51. Of the death of King Sigibert

<a id="hist-04-en-051"></a>
[**[051]**](#hist-04-la-051) That year a flash of light was seen running through the sky, as we once saw before Chlothar’s death. After obtaining the cities situated around Paris, Sigibert advanced as far as the city of Rouen, intending to cede those cities to his enemies. His own men prevented him from doing so. He returned from there and entered Paris, where Brunhilda came to him with her sons.

The Franks who had formerly looked to the elder Childebert sent an embassy to Sigibert, asking him to come to them, abandon Chilperic, and establish himself as king over them. On hearing this, Sigibert sent men to besiege his brother in the city mentioned above and prepared to hasten there himself. Bishop Saint Germanus said to him: “If you go but do not kill your brother, you will return alive and victorious. If you think otherwise, you will die. For thus the Lord said through Solomon: ‘You will fall into the pit that you prepare for your brother.’” Because of sin, Sigibert neglected this warning.

When he came to the estate called Vitry, the whole army gathered around him. Raising him on a shield, they established him as king over them. Then two servants, corrupted by Queen Fredegund and carrying strong knives commonly called scramasaxes smeared with poison, pretended to present another petition and struck him in both sides. He cried out and collapsed, and not long afterward breathed out his spirit. His chamberlain Charegisel fell there too. Sigila, who had formerly come from Gothia, was severely wounded there. King Chilperic later captured him, burned all his joints with glowing cauteries, and tore him apart limb by limb, so that he died cruelly.

Charegisel was as frivolous in conduct as he was burdensome in greed. Rising from the lowest rank, he became great with the king through flattery. He was a coveter of other people’s property and a breaker of wills. His end was such that, when death was imminent, he could not carry out his own will, though he had often destroyed the wills of others.

Chilperic remained fixed in uncertainty, doubting whether he would escape or perish, until messengers came to announce his brother’s death. He then left Tournai with his wife and sons and buried Sigibert, clothed, at the village of Lambrus. Sigibert was later translated from there to Soissons, to the basilica of Saint Medard that he himself had built, and buried beside his father Chlothar. He died in the fourteenth year of his reign, at the age of forty. From the death of the elder Theudebert to the death of Sigibert, twenty-nine years are reckoned. Between Sigibert’s death and that of his nephew Theudebert there were eighteen days. After Sigibert’s death his son Childebert reigned in his place.

From the beginning to the Flood: 2,242 years. From the Flood to Abraham: 942 years. From Abraham to the exodus of the children of Israel from Egypt: 462 years. From the exodus of the children of Israel from Egypt to the building of Solomon’s Temple: 480 years. From the building of the Temple to its destruction and the exile to Babylon: 390 years. From the exile to the Lord’s Passion: 668 years. From the Lord’s Passion to the death of Saint Martin: 412 years. From the death of Saint Martin to the death of King Clovis: 112 years. From the death of King Clovis to the death of Theudebert: 37 years. From the death of Theudebert to the death of Sigibert: 29 years. Altogether these amount to 5,774 years.

## 1. De obito Chrodigildis regina.

<a id="hist-04-la-001"></a>
[**[001]**](#hist-04-en-001) Igitur Chrodigildis regina, plena dierum bonisque operibus praedita, apud urbem Toronicam obiit tempore Iniuriosi episcopi. Quae Parisius cum magno psallentio deportata, in sacrario basilicae sancti Petri ad latus Chlodovechi regis sepulta est a filiis suis, Childebertho atque Chlothachario regibus. Nam basilicam illam ipsa construxerat; in qua et Genuveifa beatissima est sepulta.

## 2. Quod Chlothacharius fructuum eclesiis auferre voluit.

<a id="hist-04-la-002"></a>
[**[002]**](#hist-04-en-002) Denique Chlothacharius rex indixerat, ut omnes eclesiae regni sui tertiam partem fructuum fisco dissolverent. Quod, licet inviti, cum omnes episcopi consensissent atque subscripsissent, viriliter hoc beatus Iniuriosus respuens, subscribere dedignatus est, dicens: 'Si volueris res Dei tollere, Dominus regnum tuum velociter aufret, quia iniquum est, ut pauperes, quos tuo debes alere horreo, ab eorum stipe tua horrea repleantur'. Et iratus contra regem nec valedicens abscessit. Tunc commotus rex, timens etiam virtutem beati Martini, misit post eum cum muneribus, veniam praecans et hoc quod fecerat damnans, simulque et rogans, ut pro se virtutem beati Martini antestites exoraret.

## 3. De uxoribus ac filiis eius.

<a id="hist-04-la-003"></a>
[**[003]**](#hist-04-en-003) Denique ipse rex de diversis mulieribus septim filius habuit, id est de Ingunde Guntharium, Childericum, Chariberthum, Gunthchramnum, Sigyberthum et Chlothsindam filiam; de Aregundem vero, sororem Ingundis, Chilpericum; de Chunsinam habuit Chramnum. Quae autem causa fuerit, ut uxoris suae sororem acciperet, dicam. Cum iam Ingundem in coniugio accipisset et eam unico amore diligeret, suggestionem ab ea accepit, dicentes: 'Fecit dominus meus de ancilla sua quod libuit et suo me stratui adscivit. Nunc ad conplendam mercide, quid famula tua suggerat, audiat dominus meus rex. Praecor, ut sorore meae, servae vestrae, utilem atque habentem virum ordinare dignimini, unde non humilier, sed potius exaltata servire fidelius possem'. Quod ille audiens, cum esset nimium luxoriosus, in amore Aregundis incedit et ad villam, in qua ipsa resedebat, dirigit eamque sibi in matrimonio sociavit. Quae accepta, ad Ingundem rediens, ait: 'Tractavi mercidem illam inplere, quam me tua dulcitudo expetiit. Et requirens virum divitem atque sapientem, quem tuae sorori deberem adiungere, nihil melius quam me ipsum inveni. Itaque noveris, quia eam coniugem aceepi, quod tibi displicere non credo'. At illa: 'Quod bonum', inquid, Videtur in oculis domini mei, faciat; tantum ancilla tua cum gratia regis vivat'. Guntharius vero, Chramnus atque Childericus vivente patre mortui sunt. Exitum vero Chramni in posterum scripsimus. Alboenus quoque rex Langobardorum Chlothsindam, filiam regis, accepit. Obiit autem Iniuriosus episcopus urbis Turonicae decimo et septimo episcopatus sui anno; cui Baudinus ex domestico Chlothachari regis successit, decimus sextus post obitum beati Martini.

## 4. De Brinctanorum comitibus.

<a id="hist-04-la-004"></a>
[**[004]**](#hist-04-en-004) Chanao quoque Brittanorum comes tres fratres suos interfecit. Volens autem adhuc Macliavum interfecere, conpraehensum atque catenis oneratum in carcere retinebat. Qui per Filicem Namneticum episcopum a morte liberatus est. Post haec iuravit fratri suo, ut ei fidelis esset; sed nescio quo casu sacramentum inrumpere voluit. Quod Chanao sentiens, iterum eum persequebatur. At ille, cum se evadere non posse videret, post alium comitem regiones illius fugit, nomen Chonomorem. His cum sentiret persecutores eius adpropinquare, sub terra eum in loculo abscondit, conponens desuper ex more tumulum parvumque ei spiraculum reservans, unde alitum resumere possit. Advenientibus autem persecutoribus eius, dixerunt: 'Ecce! hic Macliavus mortuos atque sepultus iacet'. Quod illi audientes atque gaudentes et super tumulum illum bibentes, renuntiaverunt fratri, eum mortuum esse. Quod ille audiens, regnum eius integrum accepit. Nam semper Brittani sub Francorum potestatem post obitum regis Chlodovechi fuerunt, et comites, non regis appellati sunt. Macliavus autem de sub terra consurgens, Veneticam urbem expetiit ibique tonsoratus et episcopus ordinatus est. Mortuo autem Chanaone, hic apostatavit, et dimissis capillis, uxorem, quam post clericatum reliquerat, cum regno fratris simul accepit, sed ab episcopis excommunicatus est. Cui qualis fuerit interitus, sequenter scribemus. Obiit autem Baudinus episcopus anno sexto episcopatus sui. In cuius loco Guntharius abba subrogatur, XVII. post transitum sancti Martini.

## 5. De sancto Gallo episcopo.

<a id="hist-04-la-005"></a>
[**[005]**](#hist-04-en-005) Denique cum beatus Quintianus, sicut supra diximus, ab hoc mundo migratus est, sanctus Gallus in eius cathedram, rege opitulante, substitutus est. Huius tempore cum lues illa quam inguinariam vocant per diversas regiones desaeviret et maxime tunc Arelatensim provinciam depopularet, sanctus Gallus non tantum pro se quantum pro populo suo trepidus erat. Cumque die noctuque Dominum deprecaretur, ut vivens plebem suam vastari non cernerit, per visum noctis apparuit ei angelus Domini, qui tam caesariem quam vestem in similitudinem nivis candidam efferebat, et ait ad eum: 'Bene enim facis, o sacerdos, quod sic Dominum pro populo tuo supplicas. Exaudita est enim oratia tua, et ecce! eris cum populo tuo ab hac infirmitate liberatus, nullusque te vivente in regione ista ab hac strage deperiet. Nunc autem ne timeas; post octo vero annos time'. Unde manifestum fuit, transactis his annis eum a saeculo discessisse. Expergefactus autem et Deo gratias pro hac consolatione agens, quod eum per caelestem nuntium confortare dignatus est, rogationes illas instituit, ut media quadragesima psallendo ad basilicam beati Iuliani martyris itinere pedestri venirent. Sunt autem in hoc itinere quasi stadia 360. Tunc etiam in subita contemplatione parietes vel domorum vel ecclesiarum signari videbantur, unde a rusticis hic scriptos Thau vocabatur. Cum autem regiones illas, ut diximus, lues illa consumeret, ad civitatem Arvernam, sancti Galli intercedente oratione, non attigit. Unde ego non parvam censeo gratiam, qui hoc meruit, ut pastor positus oves suas devorari defendente Domino non videret. Cum autem ab hoc mundo migrasset et ablutus in ecclesia deportatus fuisset, Cato presbiter continuo a clericis de episcopatu laudes accepit; et omnem rem ecclesiae, tamquam si iam esset episcopus, in sua redegit potestate, ordinatores removet, ministros respuit, cuncta per se ordinat.

## 6. De Catone presbytero.

<a id="hist-04-la-006"></a>
[**[006]**](#hist-04-en-006) Episcopi tamen qui advenerant ad sanctum Gallum sepeliendum, postquam eum sepelierant, dixerunt Catoni presbitero: 'Videmus, quia te valde diligit pars maxima populorum; veni, consenti nobis, et benedicentes consecremus te ad episcopatum. Rex vero parvulus est, et si qua tibi adscribitur culpa, nos suscipientes te sub defensione nostra, cum proceribus et primis regni Theodovaldi regis agemus, ne tibi ulla excitetur iniuria. Nobis quoque in tantum fideliter crede, ut spondeamus pro te omnia, etiamsi damni aliquid supervenerit, de nostris propriis facultatibus id reddituros'. Ad haec ille coturno vanae conflatus gloriae, ait: 'Nostis enim fama currente, me ab initio aetatis meae semper religiose vixisse, vacasse ieiuniis, elemosinis delectatum fuisse, continuatas saepius exercuisse vigilias, psallentio vero iugi crebra perstitisse statione nocturna. Nec me dominus Deus meus patitur ab hac ordinatione privari, cui tantum famulatum exibui. Nam et ipsos clericati grados canonica sum semper institutione sortitus. Lector decim annis fui, subdiaconatus officium quinque annis ministravi, diaconatui vero quindecim annis mancipatus fui, presbiterii, inquam, honorem viginti annis potior. Quid enim mihi nunc restat, nisi ut episcopatum, quem fidelis servitus promeretur, accipiam? Vos igitur revertimini ad civitates vestras, et si quid utilitati vestrae conpetit, exercete; nam ego canonice adsumpturus sum hunc honorem'. Haec audientes episcopi et in eum vanam gloriam exsecrantes, discesserunt.

## 7. De episcopatu Cautini.

<a id="hist-04-la-007"></a>
[**[007]**](#hist-04-en-007) Igitur cum consensu clericorum ad episcopatum electus, cum adhuc non ordinatus cunctis ipse praeesset, Cautino archidiacono diversas minas intendere coepit, dicens: 'Ego te removebo, ego te humiliabo, ego tibi multas neces impendi praecipiam'. Cui ille: 'Gratiam', inquid, 'tuam, domne piissime, habere desidero; quam si mereor, unum tibi beneficium praestabo. Sine ullo enim labore tuo et absque ullo dolo ego ad regem pergam et episcopatum tibi obtineam, nihil petens, nisi promerear gratiam tuam'. At ille suspicans, eum sibi velle inludere, haec valde despexit. Hic vero cum se cerneret humiliari atque calumniae subieci, languore simulato et per noctem civitatem egrediens, ad Theodovaldum regem petiit, adnuntians transitum sancti Galli. Quod ille audiens vel qui cum eo erant, convocatis sacerdotibus apud Metensem civitatem, Cautinus archidiaconus episcopus ordinatur. Cum autem venissent nuntii Catonis presbiteri, hic iam episcopus erat. Tunc ex iussu regis traditis ei clericis et omnia, quae hi de rebus ecclesiae exhibuerant, ordinatisque qui cum eodem pergere deberent episcopis et camerariis, Arverno eum direxerunt. Qui a civibus et clericis libenter exceptus, episcopus Arvernis est datus. Grandis postea inter ipsum et Catonem presbiterum inimicitiae ortae sunt, quia nullus umquam potuit flectere Catonem, ut episcopo suo subditus esset. Nam et divisio clericorum facta est, et alii Cautino episcopo erant subditi, alii Catoni presbitero; quod eis fuit maximum detrimentum. Cautinus autem episcopus videns, eum nulla ratione posse flecti, ut sibi esset subditus, tam ei quam amicis eius vel quicumque ei consentiebant omnes res ecclesiae abstulit reliquidque eos inanes ac vacuos. Quicumque tamen ex ipsis ad eum convertebantur, iterum quod perdiderant recipiebant.

## 8. De Hispanorum regibus.

<a id="hist-04-la-008"></a>
[**[008]**](#hist-04-en-008) Regnante vero Agilane apud Hispaniam, eum populum gravissimo dominationis suae iugo adterriret, exercitus imperatoris Hispanias est ingressus et civitates aliquas pervasit. Interfecto autem Agilane, Athanagildus regnum eius accepit. Qui multa bella contra ipsum exercitum postea egit et eos plerumque devicit, civitatisque, quas male pervaserant, ex parte auferens de potestate eorum.

## 9. De obitu Theudovaldo.

<a id="hist-04-la-009"></a>
[**[009]**](#hist-04-en-009) Theodovaldus vero cum iam adultus esset, Vuldetradam duxit uxorem. Hunc Theodovaldum ferunt mali fuisse ingenii, ita ut iratus cuidam, quem suspectum de rebus suis habebat, fabulam fingeret, dicens: 'Serpens ampullam vino plenam repperit. Per huius enim os ingressus, quod intus habebatur avidus hausit. A quo inflatus vino, exire per aditum, quo ingressus fuerat, non valebat. Veniens vero vini dominus, cum ille exire niteretui nec possit, ait ad serpentem: „Evome prius quod ingluttisti, et tunc poteris abscidere liber". Quae fabula magnum ei timorem atque odium praeparavit. Sub eo enim et Buccelenus, cum totam Italiam in Francorum regno redigisset, a Narsitae interfectus est, Italiam ad partem imperatoris captam, nec fuit qui eam ultra reciperet. Sub huius tempore uvas in arbore quem savucum vocamus absque vitis coniunctione natas vidimus, et flores ipsorum arbo rum, quae nigra, ut nostis, grana proferre solitae sunt, racimorum grana dederunt. Tunc et in circulum lunae quintae stella ex adverso veniens introisse visa est. Credo, haec signa mortem ipsius regis adnuntiasse. Ipse vero valde infirmatus, a cinctura deorsum se iudecare non poterat. Qui paulatim decidens, septimo regni sui anno mortuos est, regnumque eius Chlothacharius rex accepit, copulans Vuldotradam, uxorem eius, stratui suo. Sed increpitus a sacerdotibus, reliquit eam, dans ei Garivaldum ducem, dirigensque Arvernus Chramnum, filium suum.

## 10. De rebellione Saxenum.

<a id="hist-04-la-010"></a>
[**[010]**](#hist-04-en-010) Eo anno rebellantibus Saxonibus, Chlothacharius rex, commoto contra eos exercito, maximam eorum partem delevit, pervagans totam Thoringiam ac devastans, pro eo quod Saxonibus solatium praebuissent.

## 11. Quod Catonem ad episcopatum Turonici petierunt.

<a id="hist-04-la-011"></a>
[**[011]**](#hist-04-en-011) Decedente vero apud urbem Turonicam Guntharium episcopum, per emissionem, ut ferunt, Cautini episcopi Cato presbiter ad gubernandam Turonicae urbis ecclesiam petebatur. Unde factum est, ut coniuncti clerici cum Leubaste martyrario et abbate cum magno apparatu Arvernum properarent. Cumque Catoni regis voluntatem patefecissent, suspendit eos a responso paucis diebus. Hi vero regredi cupientes, dicunt: 'Pande nobis voluntatem tuam, ut sciamus, quid debeamus sequi; alioquin revertimur ad propria. Non enim nostra te voluntate expetivimus, sed regis praeceptione'. At ille, ut erat vanae gloriae cupidus, adunata pauperum caterva, clamorem dari praecepit his verbis: 'Cur nos deseris, bone pater, filios, quos usque nunc edocasti? Quis nos cibo potuque reficiet, si tu abieris? Rogamus, ne nos relinquas, quos alere consuesti'. Tunc ille conversus ad clerum Turonicum, ait: 'Videtis nunc, fratres dilectissimi, qualiter me haec multitudo pauperum diligit; non possum eos relinquere et ire vobiscum'. Istud hi responsum accipientes, regressi sunt Turonus. Cato autem amicitias cum Chramno nexuerat, promissionem ab eo accipiens, ut, si contigerit in articulo temporis illius regem mori Chlotharium, statim eiecto Cautino ab episcopatu, iste praeponeretur ecclesiae. Sed qui cathedram beati Martini contemptui habuit, quam voluit non accepit; impletumque est in eo quod David cecinit, dicens: Noluit benedictionem, et prolongabitur ab eo. Erat enim vanitatis coturno elatus, nullum sibi putans in sanctitate haberi praestantiorem. Nam quadam vice conductam pecuniam mulierem clamare fecit in ecclesia quasi per inergiam et se sanctum magnum Deoque carum confiteri, Cautinum autem episcopum omnibus sceleribus criminosum indignumque, qui sacerdotium debuisset adipisci.

## 12. De Anastasio presbytero.

<a id="hist-04-la-012"></a>
[**[012]**](#hist-04-en-012) Denique Cautinus, adsumpto episcopatu, talem se reddidit, ut ab omnibus execraretur, vino ultra modum deditus. Nam plerumque in tantum infundebatur potu, ut de convivio vix a quattuor portaretur. Unde factum est, ut epylenticus fieret in sequenti. Quod saepius populis manifestum fuit. Erat enim et avaritiae in tantum incumbens, ut, cuiuscumque possessionis fines eius termino adhaesissent, interitum sibi putaret, si ab eisdem aliquid non minuisset. Et maioribus quidem cum rixa et scandalo auferebat, a minoribus autem violenter diripiebat. Quibus et a quibus, ut Sollius noster ait, nec dabat pretia contemnens nec accipiebat instrumenta desperans. Erat enim tunc temporis Anastasius presbiter, ingenuus genere, qui per chartas gloriosae memoriae Chrodigildis reginae proprietatem aliquam possidebat. Quem plerumque conventum episcopus rogat suppliciter, ut ei chartas supradictae reginae daret sibique possessionem hanc subderet. Sed ille cum voluntatem sacerdotis sui implere differret eumque episcopus nunc blanditiis provocaret, nunc minis terreret, ad ultimum invitum urbi exhiberi praecepit ibique inpudenter teneri et, nisi instrumenta daret, iniuriis adfici et fame negari iussit. Sed ille virili repugnans spiritu, numquam praebuit instrumenta, dicens, satius sibi esse ad tempus inaedia tabescere quam sobolem in posterum miseram derelinqui. Tunc ex iussu episcopi traditur custodibus, ut, nisi has chartulas proderet, fame necaretur. Erat enim ad basilicam sancti Cassii martyris cripta antiquissima abditissimaque, ubi erat sepulchrum magnum ex marmore Phario, in quo grandaevi cuiusdam hominis corpus positum videbatur. In hoc sepulchro super sepultum vivens presbiter sepelitur operiturque lapide, quo prius sarchofagum fuit obtectum, datis ante ostium custodibus. Sed custodes fidi, quod lapide premeretur, cum esset hiems, accenso igne, vino sopiti calido, obdormiunt. At presbiter, tamquam novus Ionas, velut de ventre inferi, ita de conclusione tumuli Domini misericordiam flagitabat. Et quia spatiosum, ut diximus, erat sarchofagum, etsi se integrum vertere non poterat, manus tamen in parte qua voluisset libere extendebat. Manabat enim ex ossibus mortui, ut ipse erat solitus referre, fetor letalis, qui non solum externa, verum etiam interna viscerum quatiebat. Cumque pallium aditus narium obseraret, quamdiu flatum continere poterat, nihil pessimum sentiebat; ubi autem se quasi suffocari potabat, remoto paululum ab ore pallio, non modo per os aut nares, verum etiam per ipsas, ut ita dicam, aures odorem pestiferum hauriebat. Quid plura? Quando Divinitati, ut credo, condoluit, manum dexteram ad spondam sarchofagi tendit, repperitque vectem, qui, decidente opertorio, inter ipsum ac labium sepulchri remanserat. Quem paulatim commovens, sensit, cooperante Dei adiutorio, lapidem amoveri. Verum ubi ita remotum fuit, ut presbiter caput foris educeret, maiorem quo totus egreditur aditum liberius patefecit. Interea operientibus nocturnis tenebris diem nec adhuc usquequaque diffusis, ad alium criptae ostium petit. Erat enim seris fortissimis clavisque firmissimis obseratum, verumtamen non erat ita levigatum, ut inter tabulas aspicere homo non possit. Ad hos aditus presbiter caput reclinat advertitque hominem viam praetereuntem. Hunc, licet voce tenui, vocat. Exaudit ille; nec mora, secure manu tenens, sudes ligneos, quibus serae continebantur, incidit aditumque presbitero patefecit. At ille de nocte praeteriens, ad domum pergit, satis virum obsecrans, ne de hoc cuiquam aliquid enarraret. Domum igitur suam ingressus, inquisitis chartis, quas ei memorata regina tradiderat, ad Chlotharium regem defert, indicans, qualiter ab episcopo suo vivens sepulturae fuerat mancipatus, stupescentibus autem omnibus et dicentibus, numquam vel Neronem vel Herodem tale facinus perpetrasse, ut homo vivens sepulchro reconderetur. Advenit autem ad Chlotharium regem Cautinus episcopus, sed accusante presbitero, victus confususque discessit. Presbiter autem, acceptis a rege praeceptionibus, res suas ut libuit defensavit posseditque ac suis posteris dereliquit. In Cautino autem nihil sancti, nihil pensi fuit. De omnibus enim scripturis, tam ecclesiasticis quam saecularibus, adplene immunis fuit. Iudaeis valde carus ac subditus erat, non pro salute, ut pastoris cura debet esse sollicita, sed pro comparandis speciebus, quas, cum hic blandiretur et illi se adulatores manifestissime declararent, maiori quam constabant pretio venundabant.

## 13. De levitate ac malitia Chramni.

<a id="hist-04-la-013"></a>
[**[013]**](#hist-04-en-013) Chramnus vero his diebus apud Arvernus resedebat. Multae enim causae tunc per eum inrationabiliter gerebantur, et ob hoc acceleratus est de mundo; multum enim maledicebatur a populo. Nullum autem hominem diligebat, a quo consilium bonum utilemque possit accipere, nisi collectis vilibus personis aetate iuvenele fluctuantibus, eosdem tantummodo diligebat, eorumque consilium audiens, ita ut filias senatorum, datis praeceptionibus, eisdem vi detrahi iuberet. Firminum a comitatum urbis graviter iniuriatum abegit et Salustium, Euvodi filium, subrogavit. Sed Firminus cum socro sua eclesiam petiit. Erant autem quadraginsimae dies, et Cautinus episcopus Brivatensim diocisim psallendo adire disposuerat iuxta institutione sancti Galli, sicut supra scripsimus. Egressus est igitur episcopus ab urbe cum magno fletu, metuens, ne alequid in itinere adversi perferret. Intendebat enim et ipse rex Chramnus moenas. Qui dum iter ageret, misit rex Imnacharium et Scaptharium primus de latere suo, dicens: 'Ite et vi abstrahite Firminum Caesariamque socrum eius, de eclesia'. Discendente vero episcopo cum psallentio, sicut supra memoravimus, hi qui missi fuerant a Chramno ingrediuntur eclesiam ac Pirminum Caesariamquae variis collucutionum dolis mulcere temptant. Verum ubi diutissime alia ex aliis deambulantes per eclesiam conlocuntur et in hoc qui confugerant intenderent ex animo quae dicebantur, ad regias aedis sacrae, quae tunc reseratae fuerant, adpropinquant. Tunc Imnacharius Firminum, Scaptharius Caesariam adpraehensis inter brachia ab aeclesia eieciunt, paratis pueris, qui susciperent. Quos statim in exilio direxerunt. Sed die altera depraessis somno costodibus, ipsi se liberos sentientes, ad beati Iuliani basilicam confugiunt, et sic ab exilio liberantur. Res tamen eorum fisco conlatae sunt. Cautinus autem episcopus, cum suspectus esset, quod et ipsi iniuriaretur, ac memoratum iter terens, equum haberet stratum, vidit postergum hominis venientes cum caballis, qui ad occursumeius veniebant, et ait: 'Vae mihi, quia hi sunt quos Chramnus direxit ad me conpraehendendum'. Et ascenso equite, relicto psallentio, solus usque in porticum basilicae sancti Iuliani, ambis urguens calcaneis cornipedem, pene exanimis percurrit. Sed nos haec narrantis, Salustii sententiam, quam in detractaturibus historiografforum protulit, memoramus. Ait enim: Arduum videtur res gestas scribere: primum quod facto, dictis exaequanda sunt; deinde quia plerique quae delecta repraehenderis malevolentia et invidia dicta putant. Sed coepta sequamur.

## 14. Quod Chlothacharius contra Saxones abiit iterata vice.

<a id="hist-04-la-014"></a>
[**[014]**](#hist-04-en-014) Igitur Chlothacharius post mortem Theodovaldi cum regno Franciae suscepisset atque eum circuiret, audivit a suis in iterata insania efferviscere Saxonis sibique esse rebelles, et quod tributa, quae annis singulis consueverant ministrare, contemnerent reddere. His incitatus verbis, ad eos dirigit. Cumque iam prope terminum illorum esset, Saxones legatus ad eum mittunt, dicentes: 'Non enim sumus contemptoris tui, et ea quae fratribus ac neputibus tuis reddere consuevimus non negamus, et maiora adhuc, si quaesieris, reddimus. Unum tantum exposcimus, ut sit pax, ne tuus exercitus et noster populus conlidatur'. Haec audiens Chlothacharius rex, ait suis: 'Bene locuntur hii homines. Non incedamus super eos, ne forte peccemus in Deum'. At ille dixerunt: 'Scimus enim eos mendacis nec omnino quod promiserint impleturus. Eamus super eos'. Rursum Saxones obtulerunt medietatem facultatis suae, pacem petentes. Et Chlotharius ait suis: 'Dissistete, quaeso, ab his hominibus, ne super nos Dei ira concitetur'. Quod illi non adquieverunt. Rursum Saxones obtulerunt vestimenta, pecora vel omni corpus facultatis suae, dicentes: 'Haec omnia tollite cum medietatem terrae nostrae, tantum uxoris et parvolus nostros relinquete liberos, et bellum inter nos non committatur'. Franci autem nec hoc adquiescere voluerunt. Quibus ait Chlothacharius rex: 'Desistite, quaeso, desistite ab hac intentione. Verbum enim derictum non habemus; nolite ad bellum ire, in quo disperdamini. Tamen si abire volueritis, spontania voluntate ego non sequar'. Tunc illi ira commoti contra Chlotharium regem, super eum inruunt, et scindentes tenturium eius ipsumque convitiis exasperantes ac vi detrahentes, interficere voluerunt, si cum illis abire deferret. Haec videns Chlotharius, invitus abiit cum eis. At ille, inito certamine, maxima ab adversariis internitione caeduntur, tantaque ab utroque exercitu multitudo caecidit, ut nec aestimare nec numerare paenitus possit. Tunc Chlotharius valde confusus pacem petiit, dicens, se non sua voluntate super eos venisse. Qua obtenta, ad propriam rediit.

## 15. De episcopatu sancti Eufroni.

<a id="hist-04-la-015"></a>
[**[015]**](#hist-04-en-015) Turonici autem audientes, regressum fuisse regem de caede Saxonum, facto consensu in Eufronio presbitero, ad eum pergunt. Data quoque suggestionem, respondit rex: 'Praeciperam enim, ut Cato presbiter illuc ordinaretur; et cur est spreta iussio nostra?' Responderuntque: 'Petivimus enim eum, sed venire noluit'. Haec illis dicentibus, advenit subito Cato presbiter, depraecans regem, ut, eiecto Cautino, ipsum Arverno iuberet institui. Quod rege inridente, petiit iterum, ut Turonus ordinaretur, quod ante dispexerat. Cui rex ait: 'Ego primum praecipi, ut Turonus te ad episcopatum consecrarent, sed quantum audio, despectui habuisti ecclesiam illam; ideoque elongaveris a dominatione eius'. Et sic confusus abscessit. De sancto vero Eufronio interrogans, dixerunt, eum nepotem esse beati Gregorii, cui supra meminimus. Respondit rex: 'Prima taec est et magna generatio. Fiat voluntas Dei et beati Martini, electio compleatur'. Et data praeceptione, octavos decimus post beatum Martinum sanctus Eufronius ordinatur episcopus.

## 16. De Chramno.

<a id="hist-04-la-016"></a>
[**[016]**](#hist-04-en-016) Chramnus vero apud Arvernus diversa, ut diximus, exercebat mala, semper adversus Cautinum episcopum invidiam tenens. Eo tempore graviter egrotavit, ita ut capilli eius a nimia febre decederunt. Habebat autem tunc secum virum magnificum et in omni bonitate perspicuum civem Arvernum Ascovindum nomen, qui eum vi ab hac malitia quaerebat avertere, sed non poterat. Habebat enim et Leonem Pectavinsim ad omnia mala perpetranda gravem stimulum, qui nominis sui tamquam leo erat in omni cupiditate saevissimus. Hic fertur quadam vice dixisse, quod Martinus et Marcialis confessoris Domini nihil fisci viribus utile reliquissent. Sed statim percussus a virtute confessorum, surdus et mutus effectus, amens est mortuos. Venit enim miser ad basilicam sancti Martini Toronus celebravitque vigilias, dedit munera, sed non eum respexit virtus consueta. Cum ipsa enim qua venerat infirmitateP regressus est. Chramnus ab Arverno regressus, Pectavus civitatem venit. Ubi cum magna potentia resederet, seductus per malorum consilium, ad Childeberthum patruum suum transire cupit, patri insidias parare disponens. Ille vero dolosae quidem, sed suscipere eum promittit, quem monere spiritaliter debuerat, ne patri exsisterit inimicus. Tunc per occultus nuntius inter se coniurati, contra Chlotharium unanimiter conspirant. Sed nec memor fuit Childeberthus quod, quotienscumque adversus fratrem suum egit, semper confusus abscessit. Chramnus vero, hoc foedere inito, Limovicino rediit et illud, per quod prius ambulaverat in regno patris sui, in sua dominatione redigit. Tunc Arvernus populos infra murus tenebatur inclusus, et diversis infirmitatibus oppraessus, graviter interibat. Porro Chlotharius rex duos filios suos, id est Chariberthum et Gunthramnum, ad eum diriget. Qui per Arvernum venientes audientesque, quod in Lemovicino esset, usque ad montem quem Nigrum nomen dicunt accedunt eumque repperiunt, figentesque tenturia, contra se resederunt, mittentes legationem, ut res paternas, quas male pervaserat, reddere deberet; sin autem aliut, campum praepararet ad bellum. Cumque ille patre subditum se esse confingeret diceretque: 'Omne quod circuivi laxare non potero, sed sub mea hoc potestate cum gratia patris mei cupio retenere, ille, ut proelium hoc inter ipsus deiudicaret, postulant. Cumque moto utrique exercitu cum magno armorum apparatu ad bellum convenissent, subito exorta tempestas cum gravi curuscatione atque tonitruo eos, ne pugnarent, inibuit. Redeuntes autem ad castra, Chramnus dolosae per extraneam personam patris mortem fratribus pronuntiat. Eo enim tempore bellum contra Saxones, quod supra diximus, gerebatur. At ille timentes, cum summa velocitate Burgundiam redierunt. Chramnus vero cum exercitu post eos dirigens, usque civitatem Cavillonensim venit eamque obsedens adquisivit. Exinde usque Divionensem castrum pertendit, ibique cum die dominico advenisset, quid gestum fuerit, dicamus. Erat ibi tunc sanctus Tetricus episcopus, cui in superiori libellum memoriam fecimus. Positis clerici tribus libris super altarium, id est prophetiae, apostoli atque euangeliorum, oraverunt ad Dominum, ut Chramnum quid evenirit ostenderit, aut, si ei felicitas succiderit aut certe se regnare possit, divina potentia declararet; simulque unam habentes conibentiam, ut unusquisque in libro quod primum aperiebat hoc ad missas et legeret. Aperto ergo primo omnium prophetarum libro, repperiunt: Auferam maceriam eius, et erit in dissolationem; pro eo quod debuit facere uvam, fecit autem lambruscam. Reseratumque apostoli librum, inveniunt: Ipsi enim diligenter scitis, fratres, quia dies Domini sicut für in nocte ita veniet. Cum dixerint: 'Pax et securitas', tunc repentinus superveniet illis interitus, sicut dolores parturientes, et non effugient. Dominus autem per euangelium ait: Qui non audit verba mea, adsimilabitur viro stulto, qui aedificavit domum suam super arenam; discendit pluvia, advenerunt flumina, flaverunt venti et inruerunt in domum illam, et cecidit, et facta est ruina eius magna. Chramnus vero ad basilicas ab antedicto sacerdote susceptus est, ibique panem comedens, ad Childeberthum pertendit. Infra murus tamen Divionensis non est permissus intrare. Fortiter tunc rex Chlotharius contra Saxones decertabat. Saxones enim, ut adserunt, per Childeberthum commoti atque indignantes contra Francos superiore anno, exeuntesque de regione sua in Francia venerant et usque Divitiam civitatem praedas egerunt nimiumque grave scelus perpetrati sunt.

## 17. Quod Chramnus ad Childeberthum transiit.

<a id="hist-04-la-017"></a>
[**[017]**](#hist-04-en-017) Tunc Chramnus, iam accepta Wiliacharii filia, Parisius accedens, secum Childeberthum regem constringit in fide atque caritate, iurans, se patri esse certissimum inimicum. Childeberthus autem rex, dum Chlotharius contra Saxones decertaret, in campaniam Remensim accedit, et usque Remus civitatem properans, cuncta predis atque incendio devastavit. Audierat enim, fratrem suum a Saxonibus fuisse perimtum, et regno suo cuncta subici aestimans, quae adire potuit universa pervasit.

## 18. De Austrapio duce.

<a id="hist-04-la-018"></a>
[**[018]**](#hist-04-en-018) Tunc et Austrapius dux Chramnum metuens, in basilica sancti Martini confugit. Cui tali in tribulatione posito non defuit divinum auxilium. Nam cum Chramnus ita eum constringi iussit, ut nullus illi alimenta praebere praesumerit, et ita arcius custodiretur, ut nec aquam quidem ei aurire liceret, quo facilius conpulsus inaedia ipse sponte sua de basilicam sancta periturus exiret, accedens quidam vasculum illi cum aqua simevivo detulit ad putandum. Quo accepto, velociter iudex loci advolavit ereptumque de manu eius terrae diffudit. Quod velox Dei ultio et beati antestetis virtus est subsecuta. Eam namque die iudex qua ista gesserat correptus a febre, nocte media expiravit, nec pervenit in crastino ad illam horam, qua in basilica sancti poculum de manu excusserat fugitivi. Post istud miraculum omnes ei opolentissime quae erant necessaria detulerunt. Redeunte autem in regnum suum rege Chlothario, magnus cum eo est habitus. Tempore vero eius ad clericatum accedens, apud Sellensim castrum, quod in Pectava habitur diocisi, episcopus ordenaturto; futurum, ut, decedente Pientio antestite, qui tunc Pectavam regebat eclesiam, ipse succederet. Sed rex Chariberthus in aliut vertit sententiam. Denique cum Pientius episcopus ab hac luce migrasset, apud Parisius civitatem Pascentius, qui tunc abba erat basilicae sancti Helari, ei succedit ex iussu regis Chariberthi, clamante Austrapio, sibi hunc redebere locum. Sed parum ei iactati profuere sermones. Ipse quoque regressus ad castrum suum, mota super se Theifalorum seditione, quos saepe gravaverat, lancea sauciatus crudiliter vitam finivit. Dioceses vero suas eclesia Pectava recipit.

## 19. De obitu sancti Medardi episcopi.

<a id="hist-04-la-019"></a>
[**[019]**](#hist-04-en-019) Tempore queque Chlothari regis sanctus Dei Medardus episcopus, consummato boni operis cursu et plenus dierum, sanctitate praecipuus, diem obiit. Quem Chlotharius rex cum summo honore apud Sessionas civitatem sepelivit et basilicam super eum fabricare coepit, quam postea Sigiberthus, filius eius, explevit atque conposuit. Ad cuius beatum sepulchrum vidimus vinctorum conpedes atque catenas disruptas confractasque iacere; quae usque hodie in testimonium virtutis eius ad ipsum beati sepulchrum reservantur. Sed ad superiora redeamus.

## 20. De obitu Childeberthi et interitu Chramni.

<a id="hist-04-la-020"></a>
[**[020]**](#hist-04-en-020) Childeberthus igitur rex aegrotare coepit, et cum diutissime apud Parisius lectulo decubasset, obiit et ad basilicam beati Vincenti, quam ipse construxerat, est sepultus. Cuius regnum et thesauros Chlotharius rex accepit; Vulthrogotham vero et filias eius duas in exilium posuit. Chramnus autem patri repraesentatur, sed postea infidelis extitit. Cumque se cerneret evadere non posse, Brittanias petiit, ibique cum Chonoobro Brittanorum comite ipse vel uxor eius ac filiae latuerunt. Wiliacharius autem, socer eius, ad basilica sancti Martini confugit. Tunc sancta basilica a peccatis populi ac ludibria, quae in ea fiebant, per Wiliacharium coniugemque eius succensa est; quod non sine gravi suspirio memoramus. Sed et civitas Toronica ante annum iam igne consumpta fuerat, et totae eclesiae in eadem constructae desertae relictae sunt. Protinus beati Martini basilica, ordinante Chlothario rege, ab stagno cooperta est et in illa ut prius fuerat elegantia reparata. Tunc duae acies locustarum apparuerunts quae per Arvernum atque Limovicinum transeuntes, ut ferunt, Romaniacum campum venerunt, in quo, proelium inter se actum, maxime sunt conlisae. Chlotharius autem rex contra Chramnum frendens, cum exercitu adversus eum in Brittanias dirigit. Sed nec ille contra patrem egredi timuit. Cumque in unum campum conglobatus uterque resederet exercitus et Chramnus cum Brittanis contra patrem aciem instruxisset, incumbente nocte a bello cessatum est. Ea quoque nocte Chonoober Brittanorum comes dicit ad Chramnum: 'lniustum censeo, te contra patrem tuum debere egredi. Permitte me hac nocte, ut inruam super eum ipsumque cum toto exercitu prosternam'. Quod Chramnus, ut credo virtute Dei praeventus, fieri non permisit. Mane autem facto, uterque commoto exercitu ad bellum contra se properant. Ibatque Chlotharius rex tamquam novus David contra Absolonem filium pugnaturus, plangens atque dicens: 'Respice, Domine, de caelo et iudica causam meam, quia iniuste a filio iniurias patior. Respice, Domine, et iudica iuste, illudque inpone iudicium, quod quondam inter Absalonem et patrem eius David posuisti'. Confligentes etenim pariter, Brittanorum comes terga vertit ibique et caecidit. Denique Chramnus fugam iniit, naves in mare paratas habens; sed dum uxorem vel filias liberare voluit, ab exercitu patris oppraessus, captus atque legatus est. Quod cum Chlothario regi nuntiatum fuisset, iussit eum cum uxore et filiabus igni consumi. Inclusique in tugurium cuiusdam pauperculae, Chramnus super scamnum extensus orario sugillatus est; et sic postea super eos incensa casula, cum uxore et filiabus interiit.

## 21. De obitu Chlothachari regis.

<a id="hist-04-la-021"></a>
[**[021]**](#hist-04-en-021) Rex vero Chlotharius anno quinquaginsimo primo regni sui cum multis muneribus limina beati Martini expetiit, et adveniens Toronus ad sepulchrum antedicti antestetis, cunctas actiones, quas fortassis neglegenter egerat, replicans et orans cum grande gemitu, ut pro suis culpis beatus confessor Domini misericordiam exoraret et ea quae inrationabiliter conmiserat suo obtentu dilueret, exinde regressus, quinquaginsimo primo regni sui anno, dum in Cotiam silvam venationem exerceret, a febre corripitur, et exinde Conpendio villa rediit. In qua cum graviter vexaretur a febre, aiebat:'Wa! Quid potatis, qualis est illi rex caelestis, qui sic tam magnos regis interfecit?' In hoc enim taedio positus, spiritum exalavit. Quem quattuor filii sui cum magno honore Sessionas deferentes, in basilica beati Medardi sepelierunt. Obiit autem post unum decurrentes anni diem, quod Chramnus fuerat interfectus.

## 22. Divisio regni inter filios eius.

<a id="hist-04-la-022"></a>
[**[022]**](#hist-04-en-022) Chilpericus vero post patris funera thesaurus, qui in villa Brannacum erant congregati, accepit et ad Francos utiliores petiit ipsusque muneribus mollitus sibi subdidit. Et mox Parisius ingreditur sedemque Childeberthi regis occupat; sed non diu ei hoc licuit possedere; nam coniuncti fratres eius eum exinde repulerunt, et sic inter se hii quattuor, id est Chariberthus, Gunthramnus, Chilpericus atque Sigiberthus, divisionem legitimam faciunt. Deditque sors Charibertho regnum Childeberthi sedemque habere Parisius, Gunthramno vero regnum Chlodomeris ac tenere sedem Aurilianensem, Chilperico vero regnum Chlothari, patriseius, cathedramque Sessionas habere, Sygibertho quoque regnum Theuderici sedemque habere Remensim.

## 23. Quod Sigiberthus contra Chunus abiit.

<a id="hist-04-la-023"></a>
[**[023]**](#hist-04-en-023) Nam post mortem Chlothari regis Chuni Gallias appetunt, contra quos Sigyberthus exercitum dirigit, et gestum contra eos bellum, vicit atque fugavit. Sed postea rex eorum amicitias cum eodem per legatus meruit. Dum autem cum eis esset turbatus Sigyberthus, Chilpericus, frater eius, Remus pervadit et alias civitates, quae ad eum pertenebant, abstulit. Ex hoc enim inter eos, quod peius est, bellum civile surrexit. Rediens autem Sigyberthus victur a Chunis, Sessionas civitatem occupat, ibique inventum Theodoberthum, Chilperici regis filium, adpraehendit et in exilio transmittit. Accedens autem contra Chilpericum, bellum commovit; quo victo atque fugato, civitatis suas in sua dominatione restituit. Theodoberthum vero, filium eius, apud Ponticonem villam custodire iussit per annum integrum; quem postea, ut erat clemens, muneribus ditatum patri reddidit sanum, data tamen sibi sacramenta, ne umquam contra eum agere deberet. Quod postea, peccatis facientibus, est inruptum.

## 24. De patriciato Celsi.

<a id="hist-04-la-024"></a>
[**[024]**](#hist-04-en-024) Cum autem Gunthchramnus rex regnum partem, sicut fratris sui, obtenuisset, amoto Agroecola patricio, Celsum patriciatus honori donavit, virum procerum statu, in scapulis validum, lacertu robustum, in verbis tumidum, in responsis oportunum, iuris lectione peritum; cui tanta deinceps habendi cupiditas extitit, ut saepius aeclesiarum res auferens suis ditionibus subiugaret. Nam cum audisset quadam vice Isaiae prophetae lectionem in aeclesia legi, in qua ait: Vae his qui coniungunt domum ad domum et agrum ad agrum copolant usque ad terminum loci, exclamasse fertur: 'Incongruae hoc; vae mihi et filiis meis!' Sed reliquit filium, quiabsque liberis functus, maximam partem facultatis aeclesiis, quas pater expoliaverat, derelinquit.

## 25. De uxoribus Gunthchramni.

<a id="hist-04-la-025"></a>
[**[025]**](#hist-04-en-025) Gunthchramnus autem rex bonus primo Venerandam, cuiusdam suorum ancillam, pro concubina toro subiunxit; de qua Gundobadum filium suscepit. Postea vero Marcatrudem, filiam Magnarii, in matrimonium accepit. Gundobadum vero filium suum Aurilianis transmisit. Aemula autem Marcatrudis post habitum filium in huius morte crassatur; transmissum, ut aiunt, venenum in potu maedificavit. Quo mortuo, ipsa iudicio Dei filium y, quem habebat, perdidit et odium regis incurrit, demissaque ab eodem, ne multo post tempore mortua est. Post quam Austerchilde cognomento Bobillam accepit, de qua iterum duos filios habuit, quorum senior Chlotharius, minor Chlodomeris dicebatur.

## 26. De uxoribus Chariberthi.

<a id="hist-04-la-026"></a>
[**[026]**](#hist-04-en-026) Porro Chariberthus rex Ingobergam accepit uxorem, de qua filiam habuit, quae postea in Ganthia virum accipiens est deducta. Habebat tunc temporis Ingoberga in servitium suum duas puellas pauperis cuiusdam filias, quorum prima vocabatur Marcovefa, relegiosa veste habens, alia vero Merofledis; in quarum amore rex valde detenebatur. Erant enim, ut diximus, artificis lanariae filiae. Aemula ex hoc Ingoberga, quod a rege diligerentur, patrem secretius operare fecit, futurum, ut, dum haec rex cerneret, odio filias eius haberet. Quo operante, vocavit regem. Ille autem sperans aliquid novi videre, aspicit hunc eminus lanas regiasP conponentem. Quod videns, commotus in ira, reliquid Ingobergam et Merofledem accepit. Habuit et aliam puellam opilionis, id est pastoris ovium, filiam, nomen Theudogildem, de qua et filium fertur habuisse, qui ut processit ex alvo, protinus delatusest ad sepulchrum. Huius regis tempore apud urbem SanctonicamLeontius, congregatis provinciae suae episcopis, Emerium ab episcopatu depulit, adserens, non canonice eum fuisse huic honore donatum. Decretum enim regis Chlotharii habuerat, ut absque metropolis consilium benediceretur, quia non erat praesens. Quo eiecto, consensum fecere in Heraclium tunc Burdigalensis urbis presbiterum; quod regi Charibertum subscriptum propriis manibus per nuncupatum presbiterum transmiserunt. Quiveniens Toronus, rem gestam beato Eufronio pandit, depraecans, ut hoc consensum subscribere dignaretur; quod vir Dei manifeste respuit. Igitur postquam presbiter Parisiacae urbis portas ingressus regis praesentiam adiit, haec affatus est: 'Salvae, rex gloriosae. Sedis enim apostolica eminentiae tuae salutem mittit uberrimam'. Cui ille: 'Numquid', ait, 'tu Romanam adisti urbem, ut papae illius nobis salutem deferas?' 'Patris', inquid presbiter, 'tui Leontius cum provincialibus suis tibi salutem mittit, indicans Cymulum', – sic enim vocitare consueverant Emerium in infantia sua – 'eiectum ab episcopatu, pro eo quod, praetermissa canonum sanctione, urbis Sanctonicae episcopatum ambivit. Ideoque consensum ad te direxerunt, ut alius in loco eius substituatur; quo fiat, ut, dum transgressores canonum regulariter arguuntur, regni vestri potentia aevis prolixioribus propagitur'. Haec eo dicente, frendens rex, eum a suis conspectibus extrahi iussit, et plaustro spinis opplete inponi desuper et in exilium trudi praecipit, dicens: 'Potasne, quia non est super quisquam de filiis Chlothari regis, qui patris facta custodiat, quod hi episcopum, quem eius volontas elegit, absque nostrum iuditic proiecerunt?' Et statim directis viris relegiosis, episcopum in loco restituit, dirigens etiam quosdam de camarariis suis, qui, exactis Leontio episcopo mille aureis, reliquos iuxta possibilitatem condempnavit episcopos. Et sic principie est ulta iniuria. Post haec Marcoveifa, Merofledis scilicet sororem, coniugio copulavit. Pro qua causa a sancto Germano episcopo excomunicatus uterque est. Sed cum eam rex relinquere nollit, percussa iuditio Dei obiit. Ne multo post et ipse rex post eam decessit. Cuius post obitum Theodogildis, una reginarum eius, nuntius ad Gunthchramnum regem dirigit se ultro offerens matrimonio eius. Quibus rex hoc reddidit in responsis: 'Accedere ad me ei non pigeat cum thesauris suis. Ego enim accipiam eam faciamque magnam in populis, ut scilicet maiorem mecum honorem quam cum germano meo, qui nuper defunctus est, potiatur'. At illa gavisa, collectis omnibus, ad eum profecta est. Quod cernens rex, ait: 'Rectius est enim, ut hi thesauri penes me habeantur, quam post hanc, quae indigne germani mei torum adivit' Tunc, ablatis multis, paucis relictis, Arelatinsi eam monasthirio distinavit. Haec vero aegre adquiescens ieiuniis ac vigiliis adfici, per occultus nuntios Gothum quendam adivit; promittens, si se in Hispaniis deductam coniugio copularet, cum thesauris suis de monasthirio egrediens, libenti eum animo sequeretur. Quod ille, nihil dubitans, repromisit. Cumque haec, collictis rebus factisque volucris, a cenobio pararet egredi, antecepavit voluntatem eius industria abbatissae, depraehensamque fraudem, eam graviter caesam custodiae mancipare praecipit; in qua usque ad exitum vitae praesentis, non mediocribus adtrita passionibus. perduravit.

## 27. Quod Sigiberthus Brunichildem accepit.

<a id="hist-04-la-027"></a>
[**[027]**](#hist-04-en-027) Porro Sigyberthus rex cum videret, quod fratres eius indignas sibimet uxores acciperent et per vilitatem suam etiam ancillas in matrimonio scciarents. legationem in Hispaniam mittit et cum multis muneribus Brunichildem, Athanagilde regis filiam, petiit. ## 31. De Tauredune castro et aliis signis.

<a id="hist-04-la-031"></a>
[**[031]**](#hist-04-en-031) Igitur in Galliis magnum prodigium de Taureduno castro apparuit. Super Rhodanum enim fluvium collocatum erat. Qui cum per dies amplius sexaginta nescio quem mugitum daret, tandem scissus atque separatus mons ille ab alio monte sibi propinquo, cum hominibus, eclesiisopibusqueac domibusin fluvium ruit, exclusaque amnis illius litora, aqua retrorsum petiit. Similiter et ante cladem Arvernam magna regionem illam prodigia terruerunt. Nam plerumque tres et quattuor splendores magni circa solem apparuerunt, quod rustici soles vocabant, dicentes: 'Ecce tres vel quattuor soles in caelum!' Quadam tamen vice in Kalendis Octobribus ita sol obscuratus apparuit, ut nec quarta quidem pars in eodem lucens remaneret, sed teter atque decolor apparens, quasi saccus videbatur. Nam et stilla, quam quidam comiten vocant, radium tamquam gladium habens, super regionem illam per annum integrum apparuit, et caelum ardere visum est, et multa alia signa apparuerunt. In eclesia vero Arverna, dum matutinae caelebrarentur vigiliae in quadam festivitate, aves coredallus, quam alaudam vocamus, ingressa, omnia luminaria quae lucebant, alis superpositis in tanta velocitate extinguit, ut putaris, ea in unius hominis manu posita aquae fuisse submersa; in sacrarium autem sub velo transiens, cicindelum extinguere voluit; sed ab ostiariis prohibita atque occisa est. Simili et in basilica beati Andreae de lichinis lucentibus avis alia fecit. Iam vero adveniente ipsa clade, tanta stragesde populo per totam regionem illam facta est, ut nec numerare possit, quantae ibidem ceciderunt legiones. Nam cum iam sarcofagi aut tabulae defecissent, decim aut eo amplius in unam humi fossam sepeliebantur. Numerati sunt autem quadam dominica in una beati Petri basilicam tricenta defunctorum corpora. Erat enim et ipsa mors subita. Nam nascente in inguene aut in ascella vulnus in modum serpentis, ita interficiebatur homo ille a veneno, ut die altera aut tercia spiritum exalaret. Nam cum de liac lue multi fugissent, ille tamen populum sepeliens et missas viritim dicens, numquam ab eo loco discessit. Hic autem presbiter multae humanitatis et satis dilectur pauperum fuit; et credo, haec causa ei, si quid superbiae habuit, medicamentum fuit. Nam ipsa hora et Tetradius, consubrinus eius, mortuus est. Tunc et Lugdunum, Bitorex, Cabillonum atque Divione ab hac infirmitate valde depopulatae sunt.

## 34. De Burdigalense monacho.

<a id="hist-04-la-034"></a>
[**[034]**](#hist-04-en-034) Quid etiam apud quendam monasterium eo tempore actum sit, pandam; nomen autem monachi, quia superest, nominare nolo, ne, cum haec scripta ad eum pervenerit, vanam incurrens gloriam reviliseat. Quidam iuvenis ad monasterium veniens, abbati se commendavit, ut in Dei servitium degeret. Cui ille cum multa obiceret, dicens, durum esse servitium illius loci, nec omnino tanta possit implere, quanta ei iungebantur: se omnia impleturum, invocato nomine Domini, pollicetur. Sicque collectus est ab abbate. Factum est autem post paucos dies, dum in humilitate atque sanctitate se in omnibus exiberet, ut expellentes monachi de horrea anonas quasi choros III ad solem siccare ponerent, quas huic custodire praecipiunt. Dum autem, reficientibus aliis, hic ad custodiam resideret anonae, subito nubilatum est caelum, et ecce! imber validus cum rumore venti festinus ad anonae congeriem propinquabat. Quod cernens monachus, quid ageret, quid faceret, nesciebat. Tractans autem, quod, si ceteros vocaret, prae multitudine hoc recondire ante pluviam in horrea non valerent, cuncta postposita, ad orationem convertitur, Dominum deprecans, ne super triticum illud imbris illius gutta descenderet. Quod cum se terrae deiciens exoraret, divisa est nubis, et circa anonam pluvia valde diffusa est, nullum granum tritici, si dici las est, humectans. Cumque reliqui monachi cum abbate haec consentientes, velociter ut anonam collegerent advenissent, cernunt hoc miraculum, requirentesque custodem, inveniunt haut procul harene deiectum orantem. Quod videns abbas, se post eum prosternit, et pertranseunte pluvia, consumata oratione, vocat, ut surgeret; quem apprehensum verberibus agi praecepit, dicens: 'Oportet enim te, fili, in timore et servitio Dei humiliter crescere, non prodigiis atque virtutibus gloriari'. Reclusumque in cellulam septem dies eum sicut culpabilem ieiunare praecepit, quo ab eo vanam gloriam, ne ei aliquid impedimentum generaret, averteret. Nunc autem idem monachus, ut a fidelibus viris cognovimus, in tanta abstinentia est devotus, ut in diebus quadragesimae nullum alimentum panis accipiat, nisi tantum die tertia plenum calicem thisinae hauriat. Quem Dominus, orantibus vobis, usque vitae consumationem, ut sibi placeat, custodire dignetur.

## 35. De episcopatu Abiti Arverni.

<a id="hist-04-la-035"></a>
[**[035]**](#hist-04-en-035) Defuncto igitur, ut diximus, apud Arvernum Cautino episcopo, plerique intendebant propter episcopatum, offerentes multa, plurima promittentes. Nam Eufrasius presbiter, filius quondam senatoris Euvodi, susceptas a Iudaeis species magnas regi per cognatum suum Beregisilum misit, ut scilicet, quod meritis optinere non poterat, praemiis optineret. E rat quidem elegans in conversatione, sed non erat castus in opere, et plerumque inebriabat barbaros, sed rare reficiebat egenos. Et credo, haec causa obstitit, ut non optineret, quia non per Deum, sed per homines adipisci voluit hos honores. Sed nec illud potuit immutari, quod Dominus per os sancti Quintiani locutus est, quia: 'Non surgit de stirpe Hortinsi, qui regat ecclesiam Dei'. Congregatos igitur Abitus archidiaconus clericis in ecclesia Arverna nulla quidem promisit, sed tamen accepto consensu ad regem petiit; voluitqne ei tunc Firminus, qui in hac civitate comitatum potitus fuerat, impedire; sed ipse non abiit. Amici autem eius, qui in hac causa directi fuerant, rogabant regem, ut saltim una dominica praeteriret, ut hic non benediceretur; quod si propalaretui, mille aureos regi darent; sed rex his non adnuit. Factum est ergo, ut, congregatis in unum civibus Arvernis, beatus Abitus, qui tunc temporis, ut diximus, erat archidiaconus, a clero et populo electus cathedram pontificatus acciperet; quem rex in tanto honore dilexit, ut parumper rigorem canonicum praeteriens, in sua eum praesentia benedici iuberet, dicens: 'Merear de manu eius eulogias accipere'. Haec enim gratia fecit, ut apud Metensim urbem benediceretur. Idem, accepto episcopatu, magnum se in omnibus praebuit, iustitiam populis tribuens, pauperibus opem, viduis solacium pupillisque maximum adiumentum. Iam si peregrinus ad eum advenerit, ita diligitur, ut in eodem se habere et patrem recognoscat et patriam; qui eum magnis virtutibus floreat et omnia quae Deo sunt placita ex toto corde custodiat, iniquam in omnibus extirpans luxuriam, iustam Dei inserit castitatem.

## 36. De sancto Nicetio Lugdunense.

<a id="hist-04-la-036"></a>
[**[036]**](#hist-04-en-036) Decedente vero apud Parisios post sinodum illam quae Saffaracum expulit Sacerdote Lugdunense episcopo, sanctus Nicetius ab ipso, sicut in libro vitae eius scripsimus, electus suscepit episcopatum, vir totius sanctitatis egregius, castae conversationis. Caritatem vero, quam apostolus cum omnibus, si possibile esset, observare praecepit, hic possibiliter ita in cunctis exercuit, ut in eius pectore ipse Dominus, qui est vera caritas, cerneretur. Nam si et commotus contra aliquem pro neglegentia fuit, ita protinus emendatum recepit, tanquam si non fuisset offensus. Erat enim castigator delinquentium poenitentumque remissor, elemosinarius atque strenuus in labore; ecclesias erigere, domos componere, serere agros, vineas pastinare diligentissime studebat. Sed non eum hae res ab oratione turbabant. Hic, 22 annis sacerdotio ministrato, migravit ad Dominum; qui nunc magna miracula ad suum tumulum exorantibus praestat. Nam de oleo cicindelis, qui ad ipsum sepulchrum cotidie accenditur, caecorum oculis lumen reddit, daemones de obsessis corporibus fugat, contractis membris restituit sanitatem et omnibus infirmis magnum in hoc tempore habetur praesidium. Igitur Priscus episcopus, qui ei successerat, cum coniuge sua Susanna coepit persequi ac interficere multos de his quos vir Dei familiares habuerat, non culpa aliqua victos, non in crimine comprobatos, non furto deprehensos, tantum inflammante malitia invidus, cur ei fideles fuissent. Declamabat multa blasphemia ipse cum coniuge de sancto Dei; et cum diu multoque tempore observatum fuisset ab anterioribus pontificibus, ut mulier domum non ingrederetur ecclesiae, haec cum puellis etiam in cellula, in qua vir beatus quieverat, introibat. Sed pro his commota tandem divina maiestas ulta est in familia Prisci episcopi. Nam coniux eius daemone arrepta, dimissis crinibus per totam urbem insana vexabatur, et sanctum Dei, quem sana negaverat, amicum Christi confessa, ut sibi parceret, declamabat. Episcopus ille a tipo quartano correptus, tremorem incurrit. Nam cum typus ille recessisset, hic semper tremens habebatur ac stupidus. Filius quoque omnisque familia decolor esse videbatur ac stupida, ut nulli sit dubium, eos a sancti viri virtute percussos. Semper enim Priscus episcopus eiusque familia contra sanctum Dei nefariis vocibus oblatrabant, ipsumque sibi amicum esse dicentes, quicumque de eo inproperia evomuisset. Iusserat enim in primordio episcopatus sui aedificium domus ecclesiasticae exaltari; et diaconus, quem saepe pro facinus adulterii sanctus Dei, dum esset in corpore, non solum a communione removerat, sed etiam saepius caedi praeceperat et numquam eum ad emendationem reducere potuit, hic ascendens super tectum domus illius, cum detegere coepisset, ait: 'Gratias tibi ago, Iesu Christe, quod post mortem iniquissimi Nicetii super hunc tectum calcare promerui'. Adhuc verba in ore pendebant, et statim subductus a pedibus eius rubor in quo stabat, cecidit ad terram crepuitque et mortuus est. Cum autem episcopus vel coniux eius multa contra rationem agerent, apparuit cuidam sanctus per somnium, dicens: 'Vade et die Prisco, ut emendetur ab operibus malis, et fiant opera eius bona. Martino quoque presbitero dicis: „Quia consentis his operibus, castigandus eris; et si emendare perversitatem tuam nolueris, morieris"'. At ille evigilans, locutus est diacono cuidam, dicens: 'Vade, quaeso, eo quod sis amicus in domo episcopi, et haec loquere sive episcopo sive Martino presbitero'. Promisit se diaconus locuturum, sed retractans, noluit ea fari. Nocte autem cum se sopori dedisset, apparuit ei sanctus, dicens: 'Cur non dixisti quae tibi abba locutus est?' Et clausis pugnis coepit guttur eius caedere. Mane autem facto, inflatis faucibus cum magno dolore, accessit ad viros et omnia quae audierat intimavit. At illi parvi pendentes ea quae audierant, fantasiam somniorum esse dixerunt. Martinus vero presbiter statim inruit in febre et aegrotans convaluit; sed cum semper adolatorie episcopo loqueretur et consentiret in malis actibus ac blasphemiis, quae in sanctum evomebant, iterum in febre redactus, spiritum exalavit.

## 39. De interitu Palladi Arverni.

<a id="hist-04-la-039"></a>
[**[039]**](#hist-04-en-039) Palladius autem, Britiani quondam comites ac Caesariae filius, comitatum in urbe Gabalitana, Sigibertho rege inpertiente, promeruit, sed orta intentio inter ipsum Partheniumque episcopum valde populum conlidebat. Nam plerumque conviciis ac diversis oppropriis criminibusque obruebat episcopum, pervadens res eclesiae spoliansque homines eius. Unde factum est, ut, hac intentione crescente, cum ad praesentiam iam dicti principes properassent et diversa sibi invicem obiectarent, möllem episcopum, effeminatum Palladius vocitaret: 'Ubi sunt mariti tui, cum quibus stoprose ac turpiter vivis?' Sed haec in sacerdote verba prolata divina confestim ultio subsequens abolevit. Nam anno sequenti remotus a comitatu Palladius, Arvernum regressus est; Romanus vero comitatum ambivit. Factum est autem, ut quadam die in urbe Arverna uterque coniungerint, et altercantibus inter se pro hac actione comitatus, audivit Palladius se a Sigybertho rege debere interfeci. Sed falsa haec et maxime a Romano emissa depraehensa sunt. Tunc ille timore perterritus, ita in angustia gravi redactus est, ut minaretur se propria dextera peremere, Cumque a matre vel a cognato suo Firmino intente adtenderetur, ne perficeret quod mente amara conciperat, per intervalla horarum elapsus a matris aspectu, ingressus cubiculum, accepto spatio solitudinis, evaginato gladium cornuaque ensis pedibus calcans, acumen ad pectus erexit, inpraessusque desuper gladius, ab una ingressus mammilla, in spadolam dorsi egressus est, erectusque iterum, similiter in alia mammilla perfossus, cecidit et mortuus est. Quod non sine diabuli opere scelus perfectum mirati sumus; nam prima eum plaga interficere potuit, si non diabulus sustentaculum praebuisset, quod haec nefanda peragerit. Currit mater exanimis et supra filii corpusculum orbata conlabitur, atque omnes familia voces planctus emittit. Verumtamen ad monasthirium Chrononensim delatus, sepulturae mandatus, sed non iuxta christianorum cadavera positus, sed nec missarum solemnia meruit; quod non ob alia causa nisi ob iniuriam episcopi haec ei evenisse probantur.

## 40. De imperio Iustini.

<a id="hist-04-la-040"></a>
[**[040]**](#hist-04-en-040) Defuncto igitur apud urbem Constantinopolitanam Iustiniano imperatore, Iustinus ambivit imperio, vir in omni avaritia deditus, contemptor pauperorum, senatorum spoliatur; cui tanta fuit cupiditas, ut arcas iuberet fieri ferreas, in quibus numismati auri talenta congererit. Quem etiam feruntin heresiPelagiana dilapsum. Nam non post multum tempus ex sensu effectus, Tiberium caesarem sibi adscivit ad defensandas provintias suas, hominem iustum, elimosinarium, aequiter discernentem obtenentemque victorias et, quod omnibus supereminit bonis, esse virissimum christianum. Denique Sigyberthus rex legatusad Iustinumimperatorem misit, pacem petens, id est Warmarium Francum et Firminum Arvernum. Qui euntis evectu navali, Constantinopolitanam sunt urbem ingressi, locutique tamen cum imperatore, quae petierant obtenuerunt. In alium tamen annum in Galliis sunt regressi. Post haec autem Anthiocia Egypti et Apamiae Siriae maximae civitatis a Persis captae sunt, et populus captivus abductus. Basilica tunc sancti Iuliani Anthiocensis martyris gravi incendio concremata est. Ad Iustinum autem imperatorem Persi-Armeni cum magno syrici intexti pondere venerunt, petentes amicitias eius atque narrantis, se imperatori Persarum esse infensus. Venerant enim ad eos legati eius, dicentes: 'Sollicitudo imperialis sciscitatur, si foedus initum cum eo custodiatis intactum'. Respondentibus illis, omnia ab his pollicita inlibata servari, dixerunt legati: 'In hoc apparebit, vos eius amicitias custodire, si ignem, ut ille veneratur, adoraveritis'. Respondente populo, nequaquam se hoc facturum, ait episcopus, qui coram erat: 'Quae est in igne deitas, ut venerari quaeat? Quem Deus ad usus hominum procreavit, qui fomentis accenditur, aqua restinguitur, adhibitus urit, neglectus tepiscit'. Haec et his similia episcopo prosequente, legati furore succensi, actum convitiis fustibus caedunt. Cernens autem populus sacerdotem suum sanguine cruentatum, super legatus inruunt, manus iniciunt interemuntque et, sicut diximus, huius imperatoris amicitias petierunt.

## 41. Quod Alboenus cum Langobardis Italiam occupavit.

<a id="hist-04-la-041"></a>
[**[041]**](#hist-04-en-041) Alboenus vero Langobardorum rex, qui Chlothosindam, regis Chlothari filiam, habebat, relecta regione sua, Italiam cum omni illa Langobardorum gente petiit. Nam, commoto exercitu, cum uxoribus et liberis abierunt, illuc commanere deliberantes. Quam regionem ingressi, maxime per annos septem pervagantes, spoliatis eclesiis, sacerdotibus interfectis, in suam redigunt potestatem. Mortua autem Chlothosinda, uxore Alboeni, aliam duxit coniugem, cuius patrem ante paucum tempus interfecerat. Qua de causa mulier in odio semper virum habens, locum opperiebat, in quo possit iniurias patris ulcisci; unde factum est, utunum ex famulis concupiscens, virum veninu medificaret. Quo defuncto, cum famulo iit, sed adpraehensi pariter interfecti sunt. Langobardi deinceps alium super se regem statuunt.

## 42. De bellis Mummoli cum eisdem.

<a id="hist-04-la-042"></a>
[**[042]**](#hist-04-en-042) Eunius quoque cognomento Mummolus a rege Gunthchramno patriciatum promeruit. De cuius militiae origine altius quaedam repetenda potavi. Hic etenim Peonio patre ortus, Audisiodorensis urbis incola fuit. Peonius vero huius municipii comitatum regebat. Cumque ad renovandam actionem munera regi per filium transmisisset, illi, datis rebus paternis, comitatum patris ambivit subplantavitque genetorem, quem sublevare debuerat. Ex hoc vero gradatim proficiens, ad maius culmen evectus est. Igitur prorumpentibus Langobardis in Galliis, Amatus patricius, qui nuper Celsi successor extiterat, contra eos abiit, cummissumque bellum, terga vertit ceciditque ibi. Tantumque tunc stragem Langobardi feruntur fecisse de Burgundionibus, ut non possit colligi numerus occisorum; oneratique praeda, discesserunt iterum in Italiam. Quibus discedentibus, Eunius, qui et Mummolus, arcessitus a rege, patriciatus culmine meruit. Inruentibus iterum Langobardis in Gallias et usque Mustias Calmes accedentibus, quod adiacit civitati Ebredonense, Mummolus exercitum movit et cum Burgundionibus illuc proficiscetur. Circumdatisque Langobardis cum exercitu, factis etiam concidibus, per divia silvarum, inruit super eos, multus interfecit, nonnullus coepit et rege direxit. Quos ille per loca dispersos custodire praecepit, paucis quodadmodo per fugam lapsis, qui patriae nunciarent. Fueruntque in hoc proelio Salonius et Sagittarius fratres atque episcopi, qui non cruce caelesti moniti, sed galea ac lurica saeculari armati, multos manibus propriis, quod peius est, interfecisse referuntur. Haec prima Mummoli in certamine victuria fuit. Post haec Saxones, qui cum Langobardis in Italiam venerant, iterum prorumpunt in Galliis et infra territurium Regensim, id est apud Stablonem villam castra ponunt, discurrentes per villas urbium vicinarum, diripientes praedas, captivos abducentes vel etiam cuncta vastantes. Quod cum Mummolus conperisset, exercitum movet, inruensque super eos, multa ex his milia interficit et usque ad vesperum caedere non distitit, donec nox finem faceret. Ignarus enim reppererat homines et nihil de his quae accesserunt autumantes. Mane autem facto, statuunt Saxones exercitum, praeparantes se ad bellum; sed, intercurrentibus nuntiis, pacem fecerunt, datisque muneribus Mummolo, relicta universa regionis praeda cum captivis, discesserunt, iurantes prius, quod ad subiectionem regum solaciumque Francorum redire deberent in Galliis. Igitur regressi Saxones in Italiam, adsumptis secum uxoribus atque parvolis vel omni suppellectile facultatis, redire in Galliis distinant, scilicet ut a Sigybertho rege collecti in loco unde egressi fuerant stabilirentur. Feceruntque ex se duos, ut aiunt, cunios, et unus quidem per Niceam urbem, alius vero per Ebredunensim venit, illam re vera tenentes viam, quam anno superiore tenuerant; coniunctique sunt in Avennico terreturio. Erat enim tunc tempus messium, et locus ille maxime fructus terrae sub divo habebat, nec quicquam ex his domi incolae reconderant. Denique accedentes hi, segetes inter se dividunt; colligentesque ac triturantes frumenta comedebant, nihil ex his eis qui laboraverant relinquentes. Verum postquam, expensis fructibus, ad litus Rhodani amnis accesserunt, ut, transacto turrente, regno se regis Sigyberthi conferrent, occurrit eis Mummolus, dicens: 'Non transibitis turrentem hunc. Ecce! regionem domini mei regis depopulati estis, collegistis segetis, pecora devastastis, tradedistis domus incendiis, olivita ac vinita succidistis! Non ascenditis, nisi satisfaciatis prius his quos exiguos reliquistis; alioquin non effugitis manus meas, nisi ponam gladium super vos et uxores ac parvolus vestrus et ulciscar iniuriam domini mei Gunthchramni regis'. Tunc illi timentes valde, dantes multa nummismati auri milia pro redemptione sua, transire permissi sunt; et sic Arvernum pervenerunt. Erat tunc vernum tempus. Proferebant ibi regulas aeris incisas pro auro; quas quisque videns, non dubitabat aliud, nisi quod aurum probatum atque examinatum esset; sic enim coloratum ingenio nescio quo fuit. Unde nonnulli hoc dolo siducti, aurum dantes et aes accipientes, pauperis facti sunt. Hi vero ad Sygiberthum regem transeuntes, in loco, unde prius egressi fuerant, stabiliti sunt.

## 43. De archidiacono Massiliense.

<a id="hist-04-la-043"></a>
[**[043]**](#hist-04-en-043) In regno autem Sigyberthi regis, remoto ab honore Iovino rectore Provinciae, Albinus in loco eius subrogatur. Magnam enim inter eos inimicitiam haec causa congessit. Igitur advenientibus ad cataplum Massiliensim navibus transmarinis, Vigili archidiaconis homines septuaginta vasa quas vulgo orcas vocant olei liquaminisque furati sunt, nesciente domino. Negotiator autem cum cognovisset sibi rem furto ablatam fuisse, inquirere diligenter coepit, quo furtum in loco fuisset reconditum. Haec inquirens audit a quodam, quia hoc homines Vigili archidiaconis perpetrassent. Perveniunt haec ad archidiaconem, qui inquirens et inveniens, nequaquam publicare, sed excusare suos coepit, dicens: 'Numquam de domo mea egressus est, qui talia auderet admittere'. Taliter denique excusante archidiacono, negotiator ad Albinum pergit; causam exerit et archidiaconem in crimine fraudis huius mixtum accusat. Die autem sancto natalis dominici episcopo in ecclesia adveniente, archidiaconus indutus albam adest, episcopum, ut mos est, invitans ad altarem debere procedere ac solemnitate diei sancti oportuno debere tempore celebrare. Nec mora, Albinus de sede exiliens, adpraehensum archidiaconem detrahit, pugnis calcibusque caedit et custodia carcerali coartat. Pro quo numquam obtinere potuerunt non episcopus, non cives, non ullus maior natu, non ipsa vox totius populi adclamantes, ut datis fideiussoribus diem sanctum archidiacono liceat cum reliquis celebrare atque accusatio causae in posterum deberet audiri. Sed nec de ipsa sacrosancta solemnia metum habuit, ut ministrum dominici altaris tali in die auderet adripere. Quid plura? Quattuor milia solidorum archidiaconem condemnavit; quod in praesentia regis Sigyberthi veniens, quadrupla satisfactione, insequente Iovino, conposuit.

## 44. De Langobardis et Mummolo.

<a id="hist-04-la-044"></a>
[**[044]**](#hist-04-en-044) Post haec tres Langobardorum duces, id est Amo, Zaban ac Rodanus, Gallias inruperunt. Et Amo quidem Ebredunensim carpens viam, usque Machao villam Avennici territurii, quam Mummolus munere meruerat regio, accessit; ibique fixit tenturia. Zaban vero per Deinsim discendens urbem, usque Valentiam venit, ibique et castra posuit. Rodanus enim Gratianopolitanam urbem adgressus est, ibique papilionis extendit. Et Amo quoque Arelatensim debellavit provinciam cum urbibus qui circumsitae sunt, et usque ipsum Lapideum campum, quod adiacit urbi Masiliensi, accedens, tam de pecoribus quam de hominibus denudavit. Aquinsibus autem obsidionem paravit, de quibus, viginti duabus libris argenti acceptis, abscessit. Sicque et Rodanus ac Zaban in locis quibus accesserant fecerunt. Quae cum Mummolo perlata fuissent, exercitum movit et Rodano, qui Gratianopolitanam urbem debellabat, occurrit. Sed cum Eseram fluvium exercitus laboriose transiret, nutu Dei animal amnem ingreditur, vadum ostendit, et sic populus [liberi] in ulteriorem ripam egreditur. Quod videntes Langobardi nec morati, evaginatis gladiis hos adpetunt, cummissoque bello, in tantum caesi sunt, ut Rhodanus sauciatus lancia ad montium excelsa confugiret. Exinde cum quingentis viris, qui ei remanserant, per divia silvarum prorumpens, ad Zabanem pervenit, qui tunc urbem Valentiam obsedebat, narravitque omnia quae acta fuerat. Tunc datis pariter cunctis in praeda, ad Ebredunensem urbem regressi sunt, ibique eis cum innumero exercitu Mummolus in faciem venit. Commissoque proelio, Langobardorum phalangae usque ad internitionem caesae, cum paucis duces in Italiam sunt regressi. Cumque usque Sigusium urbem perlati fuissent et eos incolae loci durae susciperent, praesertim cum Sisinnius magister militum a parte inperatoris in hac urbe resident, simolatus Mummoli puer in conspectu Zabanis Sisinnio litteras protulit salutemque ex nomine Mummoli dedit, dicens: 'En ipsum in proximo!' Quod audiens Zaban, curso veloci ab urbe ipsa digressus praeteriit. His auditis Amo, collecta omni praeda in itenere, proficiscitur; sed resistentibus nivibus, relicta praeda, vix cum paucis erumpere potuit. Exterriti enim erant virtute Mummoli.

## 45. Quod Mummolus Turonus venit.

<a id="hist-04-la-045"></a>
[**[045]**](#hist-04-en-045) Multa enim Mummolus bella gessit, in quibus victur extetit. Nam post mortem Chariberthi, cum Chilpericus Toronus ac Pectavis pervasissit, quae Sigybertho regi per pactum in partem venerant, coniunctus rex ipse cum Gunthchramno fratre suo, Mummolum elegunt, quihas urbes ad verum dominium revocare deberet. Qui Toronus veniens, fugato exinde Chlodovecho, Chilperici filium, exacta populo ad partem regis Sigyberthi sacramenta, Pectavum accessit. Sed Basilius ac Sigarius Pectavi civis, collecta multitudine, resistere voluerunt; quos de diversis partibus circumdatus oppressit, obruit, interimit, et sic Pectavum accedens, sacramenta exigit. Haec interim de Mummolo dicta sufficiant; reliqua in posterum sunt digerenda.

## 46. De interitu Andarci.

<a id="hist-04-la-046"></a>
[**[046]**](#hist-04-en-046) De Andarchi vero interitu locuturus, prius genus ordire placet et patriam. Hic igitur, ut adserunt, Filices senatoris servus fuit; qui ad obsequium domini depotatus, ad studia litterarum cum eo positus, bene institutus emicuit. Nam de operibus Virgilii, legis Theodosianae libris artemque calculi aplene eruditus est. Hac igitur scientiam tumens, dispicere dominos coepit et se patrocinio Lupi ducis, cum ad urbem Massiliensim ex iusso regis Sigyberthi accederet, commendavit. De qua regressus, hunc secum habire praecepit insinuavitque eum diligenter Sigybertho regi atque ad serviendum tradedit. Quem ille per loca diversa dirigens, locum praebuit militandi. Ex hoc quasi honoratus habitus, Arvernum venit ibique cum Urso, civi urbis eius, amicitias inlegat. Interim, ut erat acris ingenii, filiam eius disponsare desiderans, luricam, ut ferunt, in libellare, quo charte abdi soleti sunt, recondit, dicens mulieri, coniugi utique Ursi, quia: 'Multitudinem aureorum meorum amplius quam sedecim milia in hoc libellare reconditam tibi conmendo, quod tuum esse poterit, si mihi filiam tuam praestiteris disponsari'. Sed Quid non mortalia pectora cogis, Auri sacra famis? Promisit mulier haec simpliciter credens, absente viro, huic disponsare puellam. Ad ille regressus ad regem, praeceptionem ad iudicem loci exibuit, ut puellam hanc suo matrimonio sociaret, dicens, quia: 'Dedi arram in disponsatione eius'. Negavit autem vir ille, dicens, quia: 'Neque te novi, unde sis, neque alequid de rebus tuis habeo'. Qua intentione pollulante ac vihementius increscente, Andarchius expetiit Ursum regis praesentiamarcessire. Cumque venisset apud villam Brinnacum, requiret hominem alium Ursum nomen, quem ad altarium clam adductumiurare fecit ac dicere: 'Per hoc locum sanctum et reliquias martyrum beatorum, quia, si filiam meam tibi in matrimonium non tradidero, sidecem milia soledorum tibi satisfacere non morabor'. Stabant enim testes in sacrario, abscultantes occultae Verba loquentes, sed personam qui loqueretur paenitus non videntis. Tunc Andarchius demulsit verbis lenibus Urso, fecitque eum sine regis praesentiam reverti ad patriam. Factoque ex hoc iuramento breve sacramentorum, rege illo protulit discidente, dicens: 'Haec et haec mihi Ursus scripsit; et ideo gloriae vestrae praeceptionem deposco, ut filiam suam mihi tradat in matrimonio. Alioquin liceat mihi res eius possedere, donec, sedicem milibus solidorum acceptis, me ab hac causa removeam'. Tunc, adeptis praeceptionibus, Arvernum redit, ostendit iudici iussionem regis. Ursus autem se in Villavo territurio contulit. Cumque res eius huic consignarentur, et hic in Villavum accessit. Ingressusque unam Ursi domum, caenam sibi praecepit praeparare atque aquas quibus ablueretur calefieri iubet. Sed cum servi domus minime rudi domino apparerent, alius fustibus, alius virgis caedit, nonnullus capita percutiens sanguinem elicuit. Turbata ergo familia, praeparatur caena, abluetur hic aquis calidis, inebriatur vino et se collocat super stratum. Erant autem cum eo septem pueri tantum. Cumque non minus somno quam vino sepulti altius obdormissent, congregata familia, ostia domus, quae erat ex ligneis fabricata tabulis, claudit; acceptisque clavibus, metas annonae, quae aderant, elidit hac circa domum et supra domum adgregat acervos annonae, quae tunc in manibulis erat, ita ut operta ex his domus cernetur omnino. Tunc inmisso per partes diversas igne, cum iam super hos infelices materia aedificii exusta deruerent, excitantur, emissas voces, sed non fuit qui exaudiret, donec, totam domum adustam, ipsus pariter ignis absorberit. Ursus vero metuens basilicam sancti Iuliani expetiit; datisque rege muneribus, res suas in soliditate recipit.

## 47. Quod Theudoberthus civitatis pervasit.

<a id="hist-04-la-047"></a>
[**[047]**](#hist-04-en-047) Chlodovechus vero, Chilperici filius, de Toronico eiectus, Burdegala abiit. Denique cum apud Burdigalinsim civitatem, nullum prursus inquietante, resederet, Sigulfus quidam a parte Sigyberthi se super eum obiecit. Quem fugientem cum tubis et bucinis quasi labentem cervum fugans, insequebatur. Qui vix ad patrem regredi liberum habuit aditum. Tamen per Andigavus regressus, ad eum rediit. Cum autem intentio inter Gunthchramnum et Sigyberthum regis verteretur, Gunthchramnus rex apud Parisius omnes episcopus regni sui congregat, ut inter utrusque quid veritas haberit edicerent. Sed ut bellum civili in maiore pernicitate crescerit, eos audire, peccatis facientibus, distulerunt. Chilpericus autem in ira commotus, per Theodoberthum filium suum seniorem, qui a Sigybertho quondam adpraehensus sacramentum dederat, ut ei fidelis esset, civitates eius pervadit, id est Toronus, Pectavus vel reliquas citra Legere sitas. Qui Pectavus veniens, contra Gundovaldum ducem pugnavit. Terga autem vertente exercitu partis Gundovaldi, magnam ibi stragem de populo illo fecit. Sed et de Toronicam regionem maximam partem incendit et, nisi ad tempus manus dedissent, totam continuo debellasset. Cummotu autem exercitu, Lemovicinum, Cadurcinum vel reliquas illarum propinquas pervadit, vastat, evertit; eclesias incendit, ministeria detrahit, clericus interlicit, monastiria virorum deicit, puellarum deludit et cuncta devastat. Fuitque tempore illo peior in eclesiis gemitus quam tempore persecutionis Diocliciani.

## 48. De Latta monasterio.

<a id="hist-04-la-048"></a>
[**[048]**](#hist-04-en-048) Et adhuc obstupiscimus et admiramur, cur tantae super eos plagae inruerint. Sed recurramus ad illud quod parentes eorum egerunt et isti perpetrant. Illi post praedicationem sacerdotum de fanis ad ecclesias sunt conversi; isti cotidie de ecclesiis praedas detrahunt. Illi sacerdotes Domini ex toto corde venerati sunt et audierunt; isti non solum non audiunt, sed etiam persecuntur. Illi monasteria et ecclesias ditaverunt; isti eas diruunt ac subvertunt. Quid de Latta monasterio referam, in quo beati Martini habentur reliquiae? Cum ad eum unus cuneus hostium adventaret et fluvium, qui propinquus est, transire disponeret, ut monastirium spoliaret, clamaverunt monachi dicentes: 'Nolite, o barbari, nolite huc transire; beati enim Martini istud est monasterium'. Haec audientes multi, conpuncti a Dei timore, regressi sunt. Viginti tamen ex ipsis, qui non metuebant Deum neque beatum confessorem honorabant, ascendentes navem, illuc transgrediuntur et, inimico stimulante, monachos caedunt, monasterium evertunt resque diripiunt; de quibus facientes sarcinas, navi inponunt. Ingressique fluvium, protinus vibrante carina, huc illucque feruntur. Cumque amisso solatio remorum, hastilia lancearum in fundum alvei defixa, remeare conarentur, navis sub pedibus eorum dehiscit, et uniuscuiusque ferrum, quod contra se tenebat, pectori defigitur, transverberatique cuncti a propriis iaculis interimuntur. Unus tantum ex ipsis, qui eos increpabat ne ista committerent, remansit inlaesus. Quodsi hoc quis fortuitu evenisse iudicat, cernat, unum insontem plurimis evasisse de noxiis. Quibus interfectis, monachi ipsos et res suas ex alveo detrahentes, illos sepelientes, res domi restituunt.

## 49. Quod Sigiberthus Parisius venit.

<a id="hist-04-la-049"></a>
[**[049]**](#hist-04-en-049) Dum haec ageretur, Sigyberthus rex gentes illas quae ultra Renum habentur commovit, et bellum civili ordiens, contra fratrem suum Chilpericum ire distinat. Quod audiens Chilpericus ad fratrem suum Gunthchramnum legatus mittit. Qui coniuncti pariter foedus iniunt, ut nullus fratrem suum perire sinerit. Sed cum Sigyberthus gentes illas adducens venisset et Chilpericus de alia parte cum suo exercitu resederet, nec haberet rex Sigyberthus super fratrem iturus, ubi Sequanam fluvium transmearet, fratre suo Gunthchramno mandatum mittit, dicens: 'Nisi me permiseris per tuam sortem hunc fluvium transire, cum omni exercitu meo super te pergam'. Quod ille timens, foeduscum eodem iniit eumque transire permisit. Denique sentiens Chilpericus, quod scilicit Gunthchramnus, relicto eo, ad Sigyberthum transisset, castra movet et usque Avalocium Carnotensim vicum abiit. Quem Sigyberthus insecutus, campum sibi praeparare petiit. Illi vero timens, ne, conliso utroque exercitu, etiam regnum eorum conruerit, pacem petiit civitatesque eius, quas Theodoberthus male pervaserat, reddidit, depraecans, ut nullo caso culparentur earum habitatores, quos ille iniuste igne ferroque obpremens adquisierat. Vicos quoque qui circa Parisius erant maxime tunc flamma consumpsit; et tam domus quam res reliquae ab hoste direpti sunt, ut etiam et captivi ducerentur. Obtestabat enim rex, ne haec fierent; sed furorem gentium, quae de ulteriore Rheni amnis parte venerant, superare non poterat; sed omnia patienter ferebat, donec redire possit ad patriam. Tunc ex gentibus illis contra eum quidam murmoraverunt, cur se a certamine subtraxisset. Sed ille, ut erat intrepedus, ascenso equo, ad eos dirigit eosque verbis lenibus demulsit, multos ex eis postea a lapidibus obrui praecipiens. Sed nec hoc sine beati Martini fuisse vertutemambigitur, ut hi sine bello pacificarentur; nam in ipsa die, qua hi pacem fecerunt, tres paralitici ad beati basilicam sunt directi. Quod in sequentibus libris, Domino iubente, desseruemus.

## 50. Quod Chilpericus cum Guntchramno foedus iniit.

<a id="hist-04-la-050"></a>
[**[050]**](#hist-04-en-050) Dolorem enim ingerit animo ista civilia bella referre. Nam post annum iterum Chilpericus ad Guntchramnum fratrem suum legatus mittit, dicens: 'Veniat frater meus, et videamus nos et pacificati persequamur Sigyberthum inimicum nostrum'. Quod cum fuisset factum seque vidissent ac muneribus honorassent, commoto Chilpericus exercitu usque Remus accessit, cuncta incendens atque debellans. Quod audiens Sigyberthus, iterum convocatis gentibus illis, quarum supra memoriam fecimus, Parisius venit et contra fratrem suum Ire disponit, mittens nuntius Dunensibus vel Toronicis, ut contra Theodoberthum ire deberent. Quod ille dissimolantes, rex Godegiselum et Gunthchramnum duces in capite dirigit. Qui commoventes exercitum, adversus eum pergunt. Ad ille derelictus a suis, cum paucis remansit, sed tamen ad bellum exire non dubitat. Ineuntes autem proelium, Theodoberthus evictus in campo prosternitur, et ab hostibus exanime corpus, quod dici dolor est, spoliatur. Tunc ab Aunulfo quodam collectus ablutusque ac dignis vestibus est indutus et ad Ecolisinensim civitatem sepultus. Chilpericus vero cognuscens, quod iterum se Gunthchramnus cum Sigybertho pacificasset, se infra Thornacinsis murus cum uxore et filiis communivit. B 1.

## 51. De obitu Sigiberthi regis.

<a id="hist-04-la-051"></a>
[**[051]**](#hist-04-en-051) In eo anno fulgor per caelum discurrisse visus est, sicut quondam ante mortem Chlothari factum vidimus. Sigyberthus vero, obtentis civitatibus illis, quae circa Parisius sunt positae, usque Rhotomaginsem urbem accessit, volens easdem urbes hostibus cedere. Quod ne faceret, a suis prohibitus est. Regressus inde, Parisius est ingressus, ibique ad eum Brunichildis cum filiis venit. Tunc Franci, qui quondam ad Childeberthum aspexerant seniorem, ad Sigyberthum legationem mittunt, ut ad eos veniens, derelicto Chilperico, super se ipsum regem stabilirent. Illi vero haec audiens, misitquequi fratrem suum in supra memoratam civitatem obsederent, ipsi illuc properare deliberans. Cui sanctus Germanus episcopus dixit: 'Si abieris et fratrem tuum interficere nolueris, vivus et victur redis; sin autem aliut1 cogitaveris, morieris. Sic enim Dominus per Salomonem dixit: Foveam quae fratri tuo parabis, in ea conrues. Quod ille, peccatis facientibus, audire neglexit. Veniente autem illo ad villam cui nomen est Victuriaco, collectus est ad eum omnis exercitus, inpositumque super clypeum sibi regem statuunt. Tunc duo pueri cum cultris validis, quos vulgo scramasaxos vocant, infectis vinino, malificati a Fredegundae regina, cum aliam causam suggerire simularent, utraque ei latera feriunt. At ille vociferans atque conruens, nbn post multo spatio emisit spiritum. Ibique et Charegyselus cubicularius eius conruit; ibi et Sigila, qui quondam ex Ghotia venerat, multum laceratus est, et postea ab Chilperico rege conpraehensus, incensis cum cauteriis candentibus omnibus iuncturis ac membratim separatus, crudiliter vitam finivit. Fuit autem Charegyselus ille tam levis opere quam gravis cupiditate. De minimis consurgens, magnus per adulationes cum rege effectus est; conpetitur rerum alienarum testamentorumque effractur; cui talis fuit vitae exitus, ut non meriritur voluntatem propriam mortem inminente conplere, qui aliorum voluntates saepe distruxerat. Chilpericus autem in ancipite casu defixus, in dubium habebat, an evaderet an periret, donec ad eum missi veniunt de fratris obitu nuntiantes. Tunc egressus a Thornaco cum uxore et filiis, eum vestitum apud Lambrus vicum sepelivit. Unde postea Sessionas in basilica sancti Medardi, quam ipse aedificaverat, translatus, secus Chlotharium patrem suum sepultus est. Obiit autem quarto decimo regni sui anno, aetate quadraginaria. A transito ergo Theodoberthi seniores usque ad exitum Sigyberthi supputantur anni 29. Inter exitum veroeius et nepotis sui Theodoberthi fuere dies 18. Mortuo autem Sigybertho, regnavit Childeberthus, filius eius, pro eo. A. principio usque ad diluvium anni 2242. A diluvium autem usque ad Abraham anni 942. Ab Abraham vero usque ad egressum filiorum Israhel ex Aegipto anni 462. Ab egressu filiorum Israhel ex Aegipto usque ad aedificationem templi Salomonis anni 480. Ab aedificationem ergo templi usque ad dissolationem eius et transmigratione in Babilonia anni 390. A transmigratione igitur usque ad passionem Domini anni 668. A passione Domini usque ad transitum sancti Martini anni 412. A transitum sancti Martini usque ad transitum Chlodovechi regis anni 112. A transitum Chlodovechi regis usque ad transitum Theodoberthi anni 37. A transitum Theodoberthi usque ad exitum Sigyberthi anni 29. Quod sunt simul anni 5774 tantum. EXPLICIT LIBER QUARTUS.
