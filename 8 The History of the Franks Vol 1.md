# BOOK EIGHT

## LIBER OCTAVUS

### 1. How the king came to Orléans

<a id="hist-08-en-001"></a>
[**[001]**](#hist-08-la-001) In the twenty-fourth year of his reign, King Guntram left Chalon and approached the city of Nevers. He was on his way to Paris by invitation, to receive from the sacred font of regeneration Chilperic’s son, whom they had already named Chlothar. Leaving Nevers, he came to Orléans, showing himself gracious to its citizens. He visited their houses by invitation and partook of the meals they offered; they gave him many gifts, and he bestowed gifts upon them with generous kindness. When he arrived at Orléans, it was the feast of blessed Martin, the fourth day before the Nones of the fifth month. An immense crowd went out to meet him with standards and banners, singing his praises. The languages of the Syrians, the Latins, and even the Jews themselves rang out in various acclamations, saying: ‘Long live the king! May his kingdom among the nations be extended for countless years!’ The Jews, who seemed to share in these praises, said: ‘May all nations adore you, bend the knee before you, and be subject to you!’ After Mass had been celebrated and the king had taken his place at the banquet, he said: ‘Woe to the Jewish people, wicked, faithless, and always guided by deceit. They shouted flattering praises to me today so that all nations might adore me as lord, in order that I should command their synagogue, which the Christians destroyed some time ago, to be rebuilt at public expense. God willing, I will never do that.’ O king distinguished by admirable prudence! He understood the heretics’ deceit so well that they were quite unable to steal upon him with the suggestion they intended to make later. Even during the banquet the king addressed the bishops who were present, saying: ‘I ask that tomorrow I may receive your blessing in my house. May your entrance bring me salvation, so that I may be saved when the words of your blessings descend upon my humble person.’ When he had spoken, we all gave thanks and rose after the banquet was finished.

### 2. How the bishops were presented to him, and how he prepared the feast

<a id="hist-08-en-002"></a>
[**[002]**](#hist-08-la-002) In the morning, while the king was visiting holy places to pray, he came to my lodging, where the basilica of the abbot Saint Avitus, whom I mentioned in the Book of Miracles, stood. I confess that I rose joyfully to meet him and, after a prayer, begged him to accept blessed Martin’s gift in my lodging. He did not refuse, entered graciously, drank a cup, invited me to a banquet, and departed cheerfully. Now Bertram, bishop of Bordeaux, and Palladius, bishop of Saintes, were greatly displeasing to the king because of their reception of Gundovald, whom I mentioned above. Palladius had also incurred the king’s anger chiefly because he had repeatedly acted deceitfully towards him. A little earlier the other bishops and the king’s nobles had questioned them about why they had received Gundovald and why they had ordained Faustianus bishop of Agen at his frivolous command. Bishop Palladius took this responsibility for the ordination away from his metropolitan Bertram and rolled it upon himself, saying: ‘My metropolitan’s eyes were painfully afflicted, and I was plundered, despised, and brought to that place against my will. I could do nothing except what the man who declared that he would take control of all Gaul commanded.’ When this was reported to the king, he was so angry that he could scarcely be persuaded to invite to the banquet men whom he had not previously seen. When Bertram entered, the king asked: ‘Who is this?’ for it had been a long time since he had seen him. They replied: ‘This is Bertram, bishop of Bordeaux.’ The king said to him: ‘I thank you for thus keeping faith with your own family. You ought to have known, most beloved father, that you were my kinsman through my mother, and you ought not to have brought a foreign pestilence upon your own people.’ After Bertram had heard these and similar words, the king turned to Palladius and said: ‘Nor do I owe you excessive thanks, Bishop Palladius. You have perjured yourself to me three times—which it is shameful to say of a bishop—sending messengers full of deceit. You excused yourself to me by letter while inviting my brother with other letters. God has judged my cause, though I always sought to summon you as fathers of the Church, while you continually acted deceitfully towards me.’ He said to Bishops Nicasius and Antidius: ‘Tell me, most holy fathers, what you have done for the welfare of this region and the safety of my kingdom.’ When they too remained silent, the king washed his hands, received the bishops’ blessing, and sat at table with a joyful countenance and cheerful expression, as though he had said nothing about the contempt shown to him.

### 3. Of the singers and of Mummolus's silver

<a id="hist-08-en-003"></a>
[**[003]**](#hist-08-la-003) Meanwhile, when the meal was half finished, the king ordered me to tell my deacon, who had sung the responsorial psalm at Mass the previous day, to sing. While he sang, the king again ordered me to instruct all the bishops present, at my direction, to each provide one of their clerics to sing before the king. Admonished by me according to the king’s command, each bishop, as best he could, had a responsorial psalm sung in the king’s presence. When the dishes were brought in, the king said: ‘All the silver you see belonged to that perjurer Mummolus, but now, by the Lord’s grace, it has passed into my possession. I have already broken up fifteen dishes made from it, including that larger one you see, and I have kept nothing more than this one and another weighing one hundred and seventy pounds. What more does one need than daily use? I have no other son except Childebert, who already has enough from the treasures his father left him and from the possessions of that wretched man which were found at Avignon and which I have already sent on to him. The rest shall be assigned to the needs of the poor and the churches.’

### 4. The praise of King Childebert

<a id="hist-08-en-004"></a>
[**[004]**](#hist-08-la-004) ‘I ask only one thing of you, priests of the Lord: pray for the Lord’s mercy for my son Childebert. He is a wise and useful man; scarcely anyone so prudent and energetic could be found among men of much greater age. If God has deigned to grant him to this land of Gaul, there is hope that my people, now greatly diminished, may rise again through him. I do not doubt that this will happen according to His mercy, because such was the omen at the boy’s birth. On holy Easter Day, while my brother Sigibert stood in church and the deacon came forward with the holy Gospel book, a messenger arrived for the king. The deacon reading the Gospel and the messenger saying, “A son has been born to you,” spoke with one voice. At both announcements the whole people cried out together: “Glory to almighty God!” He also received baptism on holy Pentecost, and the king was likewise enthroned on the holy day of the Lord’s Nativity. Therefore, if your prayers accompany him, he will be able to reign with the Lord’s favour.’ When the king said this, everyone poured out prayers to the Lord that His mercy would preserve both kings. The king added: ‘His mother Brunhild threatens to kill me, but I have no fear of that. The Lord who rescued me from my enemies’ hands will also free me from her snares.’

### 5. Of the visions which the king or we saw concerning Chilperic

<a id="hist-08-en-005"></a>
[**[005]**](#hist-08-la-005) He then spoke at length against Theodore, declaring that if he came to the synod he would be driven into exile again, and saying: ‘I know that he caused my brother Chilperic to be killed. I should not deserve to be considered men if I cannot avenge his death this year.’ I replied: ‘Who killed Chilperic except his own malice and your prayer? He laid many unjust snares for you, and they brought him to destruction. I clearly saw this in a dream: his head was first tonsured and he seemed to be ordained a bishop; then he was placed upon a chair covered only with soot and carried away, while lamps and candles shone before him.’ As I related this, the king said: ‘I too saw another vision which foretold his death. He was brought before me bound in chains by three bishops: Tetricus, Agricola, and Nicetius of Lyons. Two of them said: “Release him, I beg you, and let him depart after being punished.” Bishop Tetricus, on the contrary, answered bitterly: “That shall not happen; he shall be burned with fire for his crimes.” After they had argued at length, I saw a bronze cauldron boiling violently over a fire in the distance. While I wept, they seized the wretched Chilperic, broke his limbs, and threw him into the cauldron. Amid the steaming water he was immediately so dissolved and liquefied that no trace of him remained.’ As the king spoke, I marvelled, and when the banquet was finished I rose.

### 6. Of those whom we presented

<a id="hist-08-en-006"></a>
[**[006]**](#hist-08-la-006) On the following day the king went hunting. When he returned, Garacharius, count of Bordeaux, and Bladast were presented by me because, as I said above, they had taken refuge in Saint Martin’s basilica after joining Gundovald. Previously I had pleaded for them without obtaining anything, but now I said: ‘Let your majesty hear me, O king. I have been sent to you as an envoy by my lord. What shall I report to him who sent me if you give me no answer?’ Astonished, he asked: ‘Who is your lord who sent you?’ Smiling, I replied: ‘Blessed Martin sent me.’ He then ordered the men to be brought before him. When they came into his presence, he reproached them with many acts of treachery and perjury, repeatedly calling them cunning foxes, but he restored them to his favour and returned what had been taken from them.

### 7. Of Bishop Palladius, how he said mass

<a id="hist-08-en-007"></a>
[**[007]**](#hist-08-la-007) When Sunday came, the king went to church to attend the solemn Mass. The brother bishops present yielded to Bishop Palladius the duty of conducting the feast. When he began the prophecy, the king asked who he was. Told that Bishop Palladius had begun, the king was immediately angered and said: ‘Shall the man who has always been faithless and treacherous to me now preach the sacred words? I will leave this church at once rather than hear my enemy preach.’ Saying this, he began to leave. The bishops, troubled by their brother’s humiliation, said to the king: ‘I saw him at your banquet and saw you receive a blessing from his hand; why does the king now reject him? If I had known that he was hateful to you, I would have turned to someone else to perform this office. Now, if you permit, let him complete what he has begun; afterwards, if you make any charge against him, let it be determined by canonical censure.’ Bishop Palladius had already withdrawn into the sacristy in great humiliation. The king ordered him recalled, and he completed what he had begun. When Palladius and Bertram were later invited to the king’s banquet, they became angry and reproached one another at length with adultery and fornication, and also with perjury. Many laughed at these matters, but some of keener understanding lamented that the devil’s tares should sprout thus among the Lord’s priests. On leaving the king’s presence, they gave guarantees and sureties that they would attend the synod on the tenth day before the Kalends of the ninth month.

### 8. Of the Signs Shown

<a id="hist-08-en-008"></a>
[**[008]**](#hist-08-la-008) Then signs appeared: rays in the northern sky, as have often appeared, and a flash seemed to run across the heavens. Flowers also appeared on the trees, although it was the fifth month.

### 9. Of the oath given for Chilperic's son

<a id="hist-08-en-009"></a>
[**[009]**](#hist-08-la-009) Afterwards the king came to Paris and began to speak before everyone, saying: ‘My brother Chilperic is said to have left a son at his death. At his mother’s request his guardians asked me to receive him from the holy font at the feast of the Lord’s Nativity, but they did not come. They next asked that he be baptized at holy Easter, but the infant was not brought then either. They made a third request that he be presented at Saint John’s feast, but again he did not come. They have thus made me leave my dwelling at an unproductive time. I have come, and behold, the boy is hidden and not shown to me. As far as I can tell, none of these promises means anything; I believe he may be the son of one of my leudes. If he were of my stock, he would surely have been brought to me. Know therefore that I will not receive him unless I obtain certain proofs about him.’ Hearing this, Queen Fredegund assembled the leading men of her kingdom—three bishops and three hundred noblemen—and they swore that the child had been begotten by King Chilperic. Thus the suspicion was removed from the king’s mind.

### 10. Of the bodies of Merovech and Chlodovech

<a id="hist-08-en-010"></a>
[**[010]**](#hist-08-la-010) The king often lamented the deaths of Merovech and Clovis and did not know where their bodies had been thrown after they were killed. A man came to him and said: ‘If no harm will be done to me afterwards, I will reveal where Clovis’s body lies.’ The king swore that he would suffer no harm but would instead receive gifts. The man said: ‘The circumstances themselves, O king, will prove that I speak the truth. When Clovis was killed and buried beneath the eaves of an oratory, the queen feared that he might someday be found and buried with honour, so she ordered him thrown into the bed of the River Marne. I found him in the trap I had made to catch fish. Not knowing who he was, I recognized Clovis by his long hair, lifted him onto my shoulders, carried him to the bank, and buried him beneath a covering of turf. His limbs are preserved; do with him what you wish.’ When the king learned this, he pretended to go hunting, opened the tomb, and found the body whole and unharmed. Only the hair underneath had fallen away; the rest remained intact, including the locks themselves. He knew that this was the man he had been earnestly seeking. He summoned the city’s bishop and had the body carried for burial to Saint Vincent’s basilica with clergy, people, and innumerable candles, mourning his dead nephews no less than he had mourned his own sons when he saw them buried. Afterwards he sent Pappolus, bishop of Chartres, who found Merovech’s body and buried it near Clovis’s tomb.

### 11. Of the doorkeepers and the death of Boantus

<a id="hist-08-en-011"></a>
[**[011]**](#hist-08-la-011) One doorkeeper said of another: ‘Lord king, this man accepted a bribe and agreed that you should be killed.’ The accused doorkeeper was arrested, beaten, and subjected to many tortures, but revealed nothing about the charge on which he was questioned. Many then said that this had been done through malice and envy because the accused doorkeeper was greatly loved by the king. Ansovald, affected by some suspicion, departed from the king without saying farewell. The king returned to Chalon and ordered Boantus, who had always been disloyal to him, to be put to the sword. Surrounded in his own house, Boantus was killed by the king’s men, and his property was brought under the control of the fisc.

### 12. Of Bishop Theodore and the plague upon Ratharius

<a id="hist-08-en-012"></a>
[**[012]**](#hist-08-la-012) When the king was again making a determined effort to persecute Bishop Theodore, Marseilles had already returned to King Childeberht’s control, and Ratharius was sent there as duke on Childebert’s behalf to investigate these matters. Neglecting the task assigned by his king, Ratharius surrounded the bishop, demanded sureties, and sent him to King Guntram so that he might attend the synod to be held at Mâcon and apparently be condemned by the bishops. Divine vengeance, which is accustomed to defend its servants from the mouths of rabid dogs, was not absent. As soon as the bishop left the city, Ratharius plundered the Church’s property, appropriating some and sealing up the rest. Immediately after he had done this, a most savage disease attacked his steward, who died consumed by fever. Ratharius’s own son died of the same affliction, and he buried him amid loud lamentation in a suburb of Marseilles itself. So great was the blow to his household that when he left the city he scarcely expected to return to his own country. King Guntram detained Bishop Theodore but did him no harm. He is a man of outstanding holiness and constant in prayer. Magneric, bishop of Trier, told me this about him: Some years earlier, when Theodore was being brought before King Childebert under such strict guard that whenever he entered a city he was permitted to see neither the bishop nor any citizen, Magneric arrived at Trier and was told that Theodore had already been placed on a ship and was being secretly taken away. The bishop rose sorrowfully, hurried after him, and found him at the riverbank. He protested to the guards against the impiety of not allowing one brother bishop to see another. When at last he saw Theodore, he kissed him, gave him an item of clothing, and departed. He then went to Saint Maximinus’s basilica and prostrated himself at the tomb, recalling the Apostle James’s words: ‘Pray for one another, that you may be saved.’ He prayed long with tears that the Lord would deign to help his brother, and then went outside. A woman agitated by a spirit of error began to cry out to the bishop: ‘O wicked and ancient man, you pour out prayer to the Lord for Theodore, our enemy! I daily seek how he may be driven from this Gaul, for he scorches us with daily fires, yet you never cease praying for him. You would do better to look carefully after your Church’s property so that nothing belonging to the poor is lost, rather than plead so earnestly for him.’ She added: ‘Woe to us, who cannot overcome him!’ Although demons should not be believed, the bishop’s character was evident from what the demon lamented and cried out about him. But let us return to my subject.

### 13. Of the legation of Guntram sent to Childebert

<a id="hist-08-en-013"></a>
[**[013]**](#hist-08-la-013) The king sent an envoy to his nephew Childebert, who was then staying at the fortress of Coblenz, so named because the Moselle and Rhine flow together and join at that place. It had been agreed that they should bring the bishops of both kingdoms together at Troyes in Champagne, but it was not convenient for the bishops of Childebert’s kingdom to do so. After greeting him and presenting his letters, the envoy Felix said: ‘Your uncle earnestly asks, O king, who has drawn you back from your promise, so that the bishops of your kingdom delay coming to the council which you both decreed. Have evil men perhaps caused some root of discord to spring up between you?’ While the king remained silent, I answered: ‘Doubtless tares may be sown among the people, but no root can immediately be found among these men. No one is unaware that King Childebert has no father except his uncle, and that Guntram intends to have no son except him, as I heard him say this year. God forbid that a root of discord should grow between them, when they ought equally to protect and love one another.’ King Childebert then called the envoy Felix aside privately and asked him, saying: ‘Beg my lord and father to do no injury to Bishop Theodore. If he does, a scandal will immediately arise between us, and discord will divide us, though we ought to preserve peace by maintaining our love.’ Having received answers on other matters as well, the envoy departed.

### 14. Of the peril in the river

<a id="hist-08-en-014"></a>
[**[014]**](#hist-08-la-014) While I was staying with the king in the fortress just mentioned, I was detained at his banquet until dark. When the meal was over I rose, came to the river, and found on the bank the boat prepared for me. When I boarded, a crowd of various people rushed in, and the boat was filled both with people and water. But the Lord’s power was present with a great miracle: although the boat was filled to its rim, it could not sink. I carried blessed Martin’s relics and those of other saints with me, and I believe that I was saved by their power. When the boat returned to the bank from which I had set out, it was emptied of people and water; the strangers were sent away, and I crossed without difficulty. On the following day I said farewell to the king and departed.

### 15. Of the conversion of the deacon Vulfelaicus

<a id="hist-08-en-015"></a>
[**[015]**](#hist-08-la-015) Continuing my journey, I came to the fortress of Eposius and, meeting the deacon Wulfilaic there, was conducted to his monastery and received most kindly. This monastery stands on a mountain summit about eight miles from the fortress just mentioned. On the mountain he built a great basilica and enriched it with relics of blessed Martin and other saints. While staying there, I began to ask him to tell me something about the blessing of his conversion and how he had entered the clerical office, for he was a Lombard by birth. He could not be persuaded to explain, wishing with his whole mind to avoid vainglory. I adjured him with solemn oaths and first promised that I would reveal to no one what he related; then I begged him to conceal nothing that I asked. After resisting for a very long time, he was finally overcome by my prayers and entreaties and said: ‘When I was a small boy and heard blessed Martin’s name, I did not yet know whether he was a martyr or confessor, what good he had done in the world, or what region had been privileged to receive his blessed limbs in a tomb. Yet I already kept vigils in his honour and gave alms whenever a coin came into my hands. As I grew older, I sought to learn letters, and I could write before I knew the proper order of the written letters. Then I joined Abbot Aridius and was taught by him, and visited blessed Martin’s basilica. When I returned with him, he took a little dust from the blessed tomb as a blessing, and I hung it around my neck in a small capsule. When I arrived at his monastery in the territory of Limoges, I took the capsule so that he might place it in his oratory. The dust had increased so much that it not only filled the whole capsule but even poured out through the joints wherever it could find an opening. My mind was further kindled by this miraculous sign to fix all my hope upon Martin’s power. I then sought the territory of Trier and built with my own labour the dwelling you see on this mountain. I found here an image of Diana whom the unbelieving local people worshipped as a god. I also set up a column on which I stood with great suffering, my feet without any covering. When winter came in its usual course, I was so burned by the icy cold that its force often tore off my toenails, and frozen water hung from my beard like candles. That region is indeed said to endure very severe winters.’ When I eagerly asked what food and drink he had and how he had destroyed the images on that mountain, he said: ‘My food and drink were a little bread and vegetables and a small amount of water. When multitudes from the neighbouring villages began to flock to me, I preached continually that Diana was nothing, images were nothing, and the worship they seemed to practise was nothing. The songs they sang amid drinking and dissolute luxury were unworthy; rather, they should offer a sacrifice of praise to almighty God, who made heaven and earth. I also prayed often that the Lord would deign to destroy the image and free that people from error. The Lord’s mercy bent the rustic mind to listen to my words, so that they abandoned their idols and followed the Lord. Then I summoned some of them and, with their help, was able to overthrow this immense image which I could not break by my own strength; I had already broken the other images myself, since they were easier. Many assembled at Diana’s statue, attached ropes, and began to pull, but their labour accomplished nothing. I hurried to the basilica, prostrated myself on the ground, and tearfully implored divine mercy to destroy by divine power what human effort could not overthrow. After praying I went out to the workmen and grasped the rope. At our first pull the image immediately fell to the ground, and I broke it with iron hammers and reduced it to powder. At that very hour, when I came to take food, my whole body from head to foot was covered with malignant pustules, so that no empty space large enough for one finger could be found. I entered the basilica alone and stripped before the holy altar. I had there a flask full of oil which I had brought from Saint Martin’s basilica. With my own hands I anointed all my limbs from it and soon lay down to sleep. Awaking about midnight and rising to perform the office, I found my whole body unharmed, as though no ulcer had ever appeared on me. I knew that these wounds had been inflicted through the enemy’s envy. And because the envious one always tries to harm those who seek God, bishops came who ought rather to have encouraged me to complete the work I had begun. Instead they said: “The way you follow is not right, and you, a man of no distinction, cannot compare yourself with Simeon of Antioch who sat upon a column. Nor does the situation of this place permit you to endure such suffering. Come down instead and live with the brothers you have gathered around you.” Because disobedience to bishops is accounted a fault, I confess that I came down, walked with them, and ate with them. One day a bishop took me farther away to an estate and sent workmen with picks, hammers, and axes, who demolished the column on which I used to stand. Returning the next day, I found everything destroyed. I wept bitterly, but I could not rebuild what they had demolished, lest I be called disobedient to the bishops’ commands. From then on, as I do now, I have been content to live with the brothers.’

### 16. And what he related of the virtues of Saint Martin

<a id="hist-08-en-016"></a>
[**[016]**](#hist-08-la-016) When I asked him to describe some of blessed Martin’s miracles performed in that place, he related the following: ‘The son of a certain Frank, a most noble man among his people, was mute and deaf. His parents brought him to this basilica, and I ordered him to rest on a small bed in the holy church itself with my deacon and another minister. During the day he devoted himself to prayer, and at night he slept in the church as I have said. At last God took pity, and blessed Martin appeared to me in a vision, saying: “Put the lamb out of the basilica, for he has now been healed.” In the morning, while I was wondering what this dream meant, the boy came to me, uttered sounds, and began to thank God. Turning to me, he said: “I thank almighty God, who has restored both speech and hearing to me.” Healed, he returned home. Another man, often involved in thefts and various crimes and accustomed to perjure himself, was once accused of theft and said: “I will go to blessed Martin’s basilica, clear myself by oaths, and be declared innocent.” As he entered, the axe slipped from his hand and he fell at the doorway, struck by a grievous pain in his heart. The wretched man confessed in his own words what he had come to deny by perjury. Another man, similarly accused of burning his neighbour’s house, said: “I will go to Saint Martin’s church and give my oath, and I shall be cleared of this charge.” It was well known that he had burned the house. When he went to swear, I turned to him and said: “According to your neighbours’ statements, you are not innocent of this crime. God is everywhere, however, and His power outside is the same as within. But if vain confidence has possessed you, so that you think God and His saints do not avenge perjury, behold the holy church opposite; swear as you wish. You will not be permitted to tread upon its sacred threshold.” He raised his hands and said: “By almighty God and the power of His bishop blessed Martin, I did not commit this arson.” Having sworn, as he withdrew he seemed to himself to be surrounded by fire. He immediately fell to the ground and began to cry out that the blessed bishop was fiercely burning him. The wretched man said: “I call God to witness that I saw fire fall from heaven; it surrounds me and scorches me with mighty heat.” As he spoke, he breathed his last. This event taught many never again to dare perjure themselves in this place.’ The deacon related many more miracles, but I have thought it too long to recount them all.

### 17. Of the signs which appeared

<a id="hist-08-en-017"></a>
[**[017]**](#hist-08-la-017) While I was staying in that place, for two nights I saw signs in the sky: rays in the north shining so brightly that none had ever before appeared like them, and blood-red clouds on both sides, east and west. On the third night these rays appeared about the second hour. While I was gazing at them in amazement, other similar rays arose from the four quarters of the world, and I saw the whole sky covered by them. In the centre of the sky was a shining cloud, towards which these rays gathered like a tent which, beginning with broader bands below and narrowing above, is often drawn together into a single peaked hood. Between the rays were other clouds flashing like bright lightning. This great sign filled me with fear, for I expected some calamity to be sent upon me from heaven.

### 18. How Childebert sent an army into Italy

<a id="hist-08-en-018"></a>
[**[018]**](#hist-08-la-018) Urged on by imperial envoys who demanded the gold given the previous year, King Childebert sent an army into Italy. There was a report that his sister Ingund had already been taken to Constantinople. But the commanders quarrelled among themselves and returned without gaining any profit. Duke Wintrjo was expelled by his own people and deprived of his dukedom; he would have lost his life had flight not saved him. Afterwards, when the people had been pacified, he recovered the dukedom. Nicetius, removed from the countship of Clermont through Eulalius’s scheming, sought a dukedom from the king and gave immense gifts for it. He was appointed duke over Clermont, Rodez, and Uzès. Though very young, he was shrewd and established peace in Auvergne and the other places under his authority. Chulderic the Saxon, having offended King Guntram for the reason mentioned above, sought refuge in blessed Martin’s basilica, leaving his wife in that king’s kingdom. Guntram had adjured her not to presume to see her husband until he had been reconciled to royal favour. I repeatedly sent envoys on his behalf and finally obtained permission for him to receive his wife and live on this side of the Loire, though he was not to presume to cross over to King Childebert. But once he received liberty to recover his wife, he secretly crossed to Childebert, obtained a ducal appointment over cities beyond the Garonne under that king’s power, and went there. King Guntram, wishing to govern the kingdom of his nephew Chlothar, Chilperic’s son, appointed Theodulf count of Angers. When he entered the city, the citizens, especially Domigisel, humiliatingly rejected him. He returned to the king, received another command, was admitted by Duke Sigulf, and governed the countship of that city. Gundovald contended with Werpinus for the countship of Meaux, entered the city, and began to conduct judicial business. While touring the city’s district in this office, he was killed by Werpinus at an estate. Gundovald’s relatives assembled, attacked Werpinus, trapped him in an upper room, and killed him. Thus both departed from the countship through imminent death.

### 19. Of the death of the abbot Dagulfus

<a id="hist-08-en-019"></a>
[**[019]**](#hist-08-la-019) Abbot Dagulf was frequently accused of crimes, for he often committed theft and murder and was exceedingly dissolute in adultery. At one time he coveted his neighbour’s wife and had intercourse with her. Seeking various opportunities to destroy the adulteress’s husband, who lived on the monastery’s land, he finally threatened him, saying that he would be punished if he went to his wife. When the husband left his lodging, Dagulf came at night with a cleric and entered the harlot’s house. After drinking until they were very drunk, they lay down in one bed. While they slept, the husband arrived, lit some straw, raised a double axe, and killed them both. Let this event therefore teach clerics not to enjoy the company of unrelated women contrary to the canons, since both canonical law and all the Holy Scriptures forbid it, except with those women about whom no suspicion can arise.

### 20. What was done in the synod of Mâcon

<a id="hist-08-en-020"></a>
[**[020]**](#hist-08-la-020) Meanwhile the appointed day arrived, and the bishops assembled at Mâcon by King Guntram’s command. Faustianus, who had been ordained bishop of Agen at Gundovald’s command, was removed on condition that Bertram, Orestes, and Palladius, who had consecrated him, support him in turn and provide him with one hundred gold pieces each year. Nicetius, however, though a layman, obtained the bishopric in that city, having previously secured a command from King Chilperic. Ursicinus, bishop of Cahors, was excommunicated because he publicly confessed that he had received Gundovald. A settlement was made that he should do penance for three years, neither cut his hair nor beard, abstain from wine and meat, and not presume to celebrate Mass, ordain clerics, consecrate churches or chrism, or distribute blessed bread; nevertheless, the administration of the Church was to be carried out entirely under his direction as before. At this synod one bishop asserted that a woman could not be called a human being. He was pacified when the bishops explained that the sacred book of the Old Testament teaches that at the beginning, when God created humanity, it says: ‘Male and female He created them, and He called their name Adam,’ which means earthly human being, thus calling woman as well as man human; He called both human. The Lord Jesus Christ is likewise called the Son of Man because He is the Son of the Virgin, that is, of a woman. When He was about to turn water into wine, He said to her: ‘Woman, what have I to do with you?’ and so on. This contention was refuted by these and many other testimonies and came to rest. Praetextatus, bishop of Rouen, recited before the bishops the prayers he had composed in exile. Some approved of them, while others criticized them because he had not followed the rules of composition; nevertheless, their style was in places ecclesiastical and reasonable. There was also a great slaughter between the servants of Bishop Priscus and Duke Leudegisel, though Bishop Priscus offered a large sum to purchase peace. In those days King Guntram became so gravely ill that some thought he could not possibly survive. I believe divine providence caused this, for he had been planning to drive many bishops into exile. Bishop Theodore returned to his own city and was received with praise by the whole favourable population.

### 21. Of the pact at Belsonacum and of the violated tomb

<a id="hist-08-en-021"></a>
[**[021]**](#hist-08-la-021) While this synod was being held, King Childebert met his men at the estate of Belsenan, situated in the middle of the Ardennes forest. Queen Brunhild complained to all the leading men about her daughter Ingund, still held in Africa, but obtained little consolation. Then a charge arose against Guntram Boso. A few days earlier a childless relative of his wife had died and been buried in a basilica at Metz with costly ornaments and much gold. A few days later came the feast of blessed Remedius, celebrated at the beginning of the eighth month. Many people left the city with the bishop, especially the city’s leading men with the duke. Guntram Boso’s servants came to the basilica where the woman was buried, entered, locked the doors behind them, opened the tomb, and took all the ornaments they could find from the corpse. The basilica’s monks perceived what was happening and came to the door but were not admitted. Seeing this, they reported it to their bishop and the duke. Meanwhile the servants took the goods, mounted their horses, and began to flee. Fearing that they would be caught on the road and subjected to various punishments, they returned to the basilica. They placed the goods upon the altar but did not dare go outside, crying out: ‘We were sent by Guntram Boso!’ When Childebert and his nobles assembled at the estate mentioned above, Guntram Boso was questioned about this but gave no answer and fled. All the property he had acquired in Auvergne as a gift from the fisc was subsequently taken from him, and he also left behind in disgrace various possessions which he had wrongfully seized.

### 22. Of the death of the bishops and of Wandelenus

<a id="hist-08-en-022"></a>
[**[022]**](#hist-08-la-022) Laban, bishop of Elusa, died this year, and the layman Desiderius succeeded him, although the king had promised on oath that he would never appoint a bishop from among laymen. But what does the accursed hunger for gold not compel human hearts to do? Returning from the synod, Bertram was seized by fever. He summoned the deacon Waldo, who had also been named Bertram at baptism, and entrusted to him the government of the bishopric together with all the provisions of his will and the disposal of his goods. After the deacon departed, Bertram died. The deacon returned and hastened to the king with gifts and the citizens’ consent, but obtained nothing. The king issued a command for Gundegisil, count of Saintes, surnamed Dodo, to be ordained bishop, and this was done. Before the synod many clerics of Saintes, with Bishop Bertram’s consent, had written accusations against their bishop Palladius to bring humiliation upon him. After Bertram’s death, Palladius arrested them and had them severely beaten and stripped. At this time Wandelenus, King Childebert’s foster-father, also died. No one was appointed in his place because the queen mother wished to take personal charge of her son. Whatever he had acquired from the fisc was restored to the fisc. Duke Bodygisel also died in those days, full of years, but his sons’ inheritance was not diminished. The priest Saius was appointed in place of Faustus, bishop of Auch. After holy Salvius’s death, Desideratus was given this year as bishop to the people of Albi.

### 23. Of the Floods

<a id="hist-08-en-023"></a>
[**[023]**](#hist-08-la-023) There were great rains this year, and the rivers rose so high that shipwrecks frequently occurred. Overflowing their banks and covering nearby crops and meadows, they caused severe destruction. The spring and summer months were so wet that the season seemed winter rather than summer.

### 24. Of the islands of the sea

<a id="hist-08-en-024"></a>
[**[024]**](#hist-08-la-024) Two islands in the sea were burned this year by a divinely sent fire. For seven days they were consumed and overwhelmed together with their people and cattle. Those who fled into the sea and plunged into deep water were consumed in the very water into which they sank; those who did not immediately die were burned with still greater torment. When everything had been reduced to ashes, the whole sea was covered with them. Many also said that the signs which I described seeing in the eighth month, as though the sky were burning, came from the glare of this fire.

### 25. Of the Island on Which Blood Appeared

<a id="hist-08-en-025"></a>
[**[025]**](#hist-08-la-025) On another island near the city of Vannes there was a deep lake full of fish, which turned to blood to a depth of one cubit. An incalculable multitude of dogs and birds gathered for many days, lapped up the blood, and departed satiated in the evening.

### 26. Of Berulfus who had been duke

<a id="hist-08-en-026"></a>
[**[026]**](#hist-08-la-026) Ennodius was appointed duke over Tours and Poitiers. Berulf, who had previously governed these cities, was suspected together with his associate Arnegisel of secretly stealing King Sigibert’s treasures. When he sought the dukedom over these cities, Duke Rauching captured him and his followers by a trick. Servants were immediately sent to their houses and plundered everything. Much of their own property and some of the treasures mentioned above were found there, all of which were brought to King Childebert. When matters had reached the point where the sword was about to fall upon their necks, the bishops intervened and their lives were spared, but they recovered none of the property taken from them.

### 27. How Desiderius went to the king

<a id="hist-08-en-027"></a>
[**[027]**](#hist-08-la-027) Duke Desiderius hastened to King Guntram with several bishops, Abbot Aridius, and Antestius. Although the king was reluctant to receive him, he was overcome by the bishops’ prayers and restored Desiderius to favour. Eulalius was also there, intending to make a complaint concerning his wife, who had rejected him and gone over to Desiderius; but he was reduced to ridicule and humiliation and fell silent. Desiderius was rewarded by the king and returned in his favour.

### 28. Of Hermenegild and Ingund

<a id="hist-08-en-028"></a>
[**[028]**](#hist-08-la-028) As I have often said, Ingund was abandoned by her husband with the imperial army and died and was buried in Africa while being conducted to the emperor with her small son. Leovigild put to death his son Hermenegild, whom the woman just named had borne. Angered by these events, King Guntram resolved to send an army into Spain, intending first to bring Septimania, still within the borders of Gaul, under his control and then advance farther. While this army was on the move, a letter was found with some rustic men. They sent it to King Guntram to be read. It was written as though Leovigild were addressing Fredegund, asking her to prevent the army’s advance by any means and saying: ‘Quickly kill our enemies Childebert and his mother, and make peace with King Guntram, purchasing it with many gifts. If you lack money, I will secretly send it, provided only that you carry out my request. When I have avenged myself upon my enemies, reward Bishop Amelius and the matron Leuba, through whom my envoys gain access to you.’ Leuba was Duke Bladast’s mother-in-law.

### 29. How Fredegund sent men to kill Childebert

<a id="hist-08-en-029"></a>
[**[029]**](#hist-08-la-029) Although this letter was brought to King Guntram and communicated to his nephew Childebert, Fredegund nevertheless ordered two iron knives to be made, deeply engraved and smeared with poison, so that if a mortal thrust did not destroy the vital organs, the poison itself might more quickly extinguish life. She handed these knives to two clerics with these instructions: ‘Take these swords and go as quickly as possible to King Childebert, pretending to be beggars. When you have prostrated yourselves at his feet as though asking alms, pierce both his sides, so that Brunhild, who derives her arrogance from him, may fall with him and become subject to me. If the boy is so closely guarded that you cannot approach him, kill my enemy Brunhild herself. Your reward will be that, if you die in this work, I will bestow goods upon your relatives, enrich them with gifts, and make them the foremost men in my kingdom. Meanwhile cast off all fear and let there be no dread of death in your hearts. Remember that death awaits all men. Strengthen your minds with courage and consider how often brave men fall in battle, while their relatives are afterwards ennobled and raised above all others by immense wealth.’ As the woman spoke, the clerics began to tremble, thinking her orders difficult to carry out. Seeing their uncertainty, she gave them a drugged potion and directed them where to go. Their courage immediately increased, and they promised to fulfil all her commands. She also ordered them to take a flask filled with this potion, saying: ‘On the day when you do what I command, take this drink in the morning before beginning the work. It will give you great resolution.’ Thus equipped, she sent them off. As they travelled and approached Soissons, Duke Rauching captured and questioned them; they revealed everything and were imprisoned. A few days later Fredegund, now convinced that her orders had been carried out, sent a servant to learn what the people were reporting or to find anyone who would say that Childebert had been killed. The servant left her and came to Soissons. Hearing that the men were held in prison, he approached the door, but when he began speaking to the guards he too was captured and detained. All were then sent together to King Childebert. Questioned, they revealed the truth, stating that Fredegund had sent them to kill him, and saying: ‘At the queen’s command we were to disguise ourselves as paupers. When we prostrated ourselves at your feet seeking alms, we intended to stab you with these swords. If the sword did not penetrate with sufficient force, the poison smeared upon the iron would quickly destroy your life.’ As they spoke, they were subjected to various tortures; their hands, ears, and noses were cut off, and they were put to death in various ways.

### 30. How the army went into Septimania

<a id="hist-08-en-030"></a>
[**[030]**](#hist-08-la-030) King Guntram ordered the army to march into Spain, saying: ‘First subject the province of Septimania, which is adjacent to Gaul, to my authority, for it is disgraceful that the territory of the dreadful Goths should extend into Gaul.’ He assembled the whole army of his kingdom and sent it there. But the peoples living beyond the Saône, Rhône, and Seine joined the Burgundians and severely devastated the banks of the Saône and Rhône, taking both crops and cattle. They committed many murders, burnings, and robberies in their own region, stripped churches, and killed clerics, bishops, and the rest of the people at the very altars consecrated to God, advancing as far as Nîmes. Likewise the men of Bourges, Saintes, Périgueux, and the other cities then subject to the king advanced as far as Carcassonne and committed similar evils. When they reached that city, the inhabitants voluntarily opened the gates and they entered unopposed, but after some quarrel with the people of Carcassonne they left the city. Terentiolus, formerly count of Limoges, was struck by a stone thrown from the wall and killed. His head was cut off and carried into the city as vengeance against his enemies. The whole army was terrified and resolved to return home, abandoning everything it had acquired along the way or brought with it. The Goths ambushed and killed many after robbing them. Then they fell into the hands of the people of Toulouse, whom they had greatly injured on their march; robbed and beaten, they scarcely reached their homes. Those who had attacked Nîmes devastated the whole region, burned houses and crops, cut down olive trees and vines, and, unable to harm those shut inside, moved on to other cities. These were strongly fortified and fully stocked with food and other necessities, so the army devastated their outskirts but could not break into them. Duke Nicetius, marching with the men of Clermont on this expedition, also took part in the siege. When he could accomplish nothing, he came to a fortress. Its inhabitants opened the gates voluntarily after receiving an oath and trustingly admitted the men as peaceful visitors. But once inside, the soldiers disregarded their oath, plundered all the stores, and took the people captive. Then by common counsel everyone returned home. Along the way they committed such crimes, murders, robberies, and plunder in their own region that it would take too long to recount them all. Because they had burned the crops of Provence, they themselves died of hunger and starvation along the road; some drowned in rivers and many were killed in disturbances. It was said that more than five thousand perished in these disasters, yet the deaths of others did not restrain the survivors. Churches in Auvergne near the public roads were stripped of their furnishings, and there was no end to their wrongdoing until each man reached home. Their return filled King Guntram with bitter grief. The army’s commanders sought refuge in the basilica of the martyr Saint Symphorian. When the king came for the saint’s feast, they were brought before him on condition of a future hearing. Four days later the king assembled the bishops and the senior laymen and began to question the commanders, saying: ‘How can I obtain victory now, when I do not keep the ways my fathers followed? They built churches, placed all their hope in God, honoured the martyrs, and revered the bishops; thus they gained victories and often subjected hostile peoples with sword and shield through divine aid. I not only fail to fear God but devastate His sanctuaries, kill His ministers, and tear apart and destroy the saints’ very relics in mockery. Victory cannot be gained where such things are done. Therefore my hands are weak, the sword grows dull, and the shield no longer defends and protects me as it used to. If this is attributed to my fault, let God exact it from my own head. But if you despise royal commands and delay fulfilling my orders, the axe ought to fall upon your necks. The execution of one commander will teach the whole army. I must now determine what ought to be done. Let anyone who intends to follow justice do so; if anyone refuses, let public vengeance hang over his neck. It is better that a few obstinate men perish than that God’s anger descend upon an entire innocent region.’ The commanders replied: ‘Your great goodness cannot easily be described, best of kings: your fear of God, love for the churches, reverence for bishops, compassion for the poor, and provision for the needy. But since everything your majesty says is judged right and true, what shall we do when the whole people has sunk into vice and everyone delights in wrongdoing? No one fears the king, no one fears the duke, no one respects the count. If anyone disapproves and tries to correct these things for the preservation of your life, sedition and tumult immediately arise among the people. Everyone is so fiercely enraged against his superior that he scarcely believes he can escape if he does not withdraw quickly enough.’ The king answered: ‘If anyone follows justice, let him live; if anyone rejects the law and my command, let him perish, so that I may no longer continue this blasphemy.’ As he spoke, a messenger arrived and said: ‘Reccared, Leovigild’s son, has come out of Spain, captured the fortress of Cabaret, devastated most of the district of Toulouse, and carried off captives. He has broken into the Arles fortress of Ugernum, taken all its property and people, and shut himself within the walls of Nîmes.’ Hearing this, the king appointed Leudegisel duke in place of Calumniosus, surnamed Aegyla, entrusted to him the whole province of Arles, and stationed more than four thousand men as guards along the frontier. Nicetius, duke of Clermont, likewise went with guards and patrolled the region’s borders.

### 31. Of the slaying of Bishop Praetextatus

<a id="hist-08-en-031"></a>
[**[031]**](#hist-08-la-031) While these events were taking place and Fredegund was staying at Rouen, she exchanged bitter words with Bishop Praetextatus, saying that a time would come when he would return to the exile in which he had been detained. He replied: ‘I have always been, am, and shall be a bishop, whether in exile or out of it; but you will not always enjoy royal power. By God’s gift I am promoted from exile to a kingdom, while you shall be plunged from this kingdom into the abyss. You would do better to abandon folly and malice, turn to better things, and withdraw from the pride which continually inflames you, so that you may obtain eternal life and bring the little son you bore to lawful maturity.’ When he had spoken, the woman took his words badly and withdrew from his presence boiling with gall. On the day of the Lord’s Resurrection, the bishop hurried early to church to perform the ecclesiastical offices and began the antiphons in order according to custom. While he reclined on a bench during the psalmody, a cruel murderer approached, drew a knife from his belt, and struck the bishop beneath the armpit as he rested on the bench. Praetextatus cried out for the clergy present to help him, but none of the many bystanders assisted him. Stretching his blood-filled hands over the altar, pouring out prayer, and thanking God, he was carried in the hands of the faithful to his chamber and laid upon his bed. Fredegund immediately arrived with Duke Beppolen and Ansovald, saying: ‘It was not right, holy bishop, that this should happen to you while we and the rest of your people were present. If only the man who dared to do this were identified, so that he might suffer fitting punishment for the crime!’ Knowing that she spoke deceitfully, the bishop said: ‘Who did this except the one who has destroyed the laws, often shed innocent blood, and committed various evils in this kingdom?’ The woman answered: ‘We have most skilful physicians who can treat this wound. Allow them to come to you.’ He said: ‘God has already commanded me to be called from this world. You, who are found to be the author of these crimes, shall be cursed in this world, and God shall avenge my blood upon your head.’ After she departed, the bishop set his household in order and died. Romacharius, bishop of Coutances, came to bury him. Great grief then seized all the citizens of Rouen, especially the senior Franks of that place. One of these leading men came to Fredegund and said: ‘You have committed many evils in this world, but you had never yet done worse than ordering a priest of God to be killed. May God swiftly avenge innocent blood! We shall all seek out the author of this crime, so that you may no longer be permitted to practise such cruelty.’ When he said this and left the queen’s presence, she sent someone to invite him to a banquet. He refused, and she asked that, if he would not dine with her, he at least drink a cup and not leave the royal house fasting. He waited, accepted the cup, and drank absinthe mixed with wine and honey, as is the barbarian custom; but the drink was poisoned. As soon as he drank, he felt violent pain assail his chest and cried out to his companions as though he were being cut open inside: ‘Flee, wretched men, flee this evil, lest you perish with me!’ They did not drink but hurried away. He was immediately blinded, mounted a horse, and fell dead three furlongs from that place. Afterwards Bishop Leudovald sent letters to all the bishops and, by common counsel, closed the churches of Rouen so that the people could not celebrate the divine offices there until the author of the crime was discovered by a common investigation. He also arrested some men and extorted by torture the truth that these things had been done on Fredegund’s advice, but he could not avenge them because she protected the guilty. It was also said that assassins came against Leudovald himself because he was determined to investigate vigorously, but his men surrounded and protected him so that they could do him no harm. When these events were reported to King Guntram and the crime was imputed to the woman, he sent three bishops to the child said to be Chilperic’s son, whom I mentioned above as Chlothar: Artemius of Sens, Veranus of Cavaillon, and Agricius of Troyes. They were to investigate with the child’s guardians who was responsible for the crime and bring the person before him. After the bishops had spoken, the leading men replied: ‘I entirely disapprove of this deed and increasingly desire to avenge it. But if anyone among us is found guilty, he cannot be brought before your king, since I can pay for our offences according to royal decree.’ The bishops said: ‘Know that if the person who committed this is not produced, our king will come here with an army and devastate this whole region with sword and fire, because it is evident that the woman who ordered a Frank to be killed by witchcraft has also had the bishop killed by the sword.’ With these words they departed without receiving a reasonable answer, adjuring them solemnly that Melantius, who had previously been appointed in Praetextatus’s place, should never perform the episcopal office in that church.

### 32. Of the death of Domnola, wife of Nectarius

<a id="hist-08-en-032"></a>
[**[032]**](#hist-08-la-032) Many evils were committed at this time. Domnola, widow of the late Burgulinus and daughter of Victorius, bishop of Rennes, whom Nectarius had married, had a dispute over vineyards with Bobolenus, Fredegund’s referendary. Hearing that she had entered these vineyards, he sent messengers adjuring her not to presume to set foot in the property. She disregarded this, declared that it had been her father’s property, and entered. He then raised a disturbance and attacked her with armed men. He killed her, recovered the vineyards, plundered the property, and put to the sword both the men and women with her; only those able to escape survived.

### 33. Of the burning of the city of Paris

<a id="hist-08-en-033"></a>
[**[033]**](#hist-08-la-033) In those days a woman at Paris said to the inhabitants: ‘Flee from the city, and know that it shall be burned with fire.’ Many ridiculed her, thinking she spoke from some omen of lots, had dreamed something vain, or was certainly speaking at the instigation of the noonday demon. She replied: ‘It is not as you say. I speak the truth, for in a dream I saw a shining man come from Saint Vincent’s basilica holding a candle and setting the merchants’ houses on fire one after another.’ On the third night after the woman spoke, at twilight, a citizen lit a lamp and entered his storeroom. He took oil and other necessities and departed, leaving the lamp beside a small cask of oil. His house was the first beside the gate opening to the south. The house caught fire from the lamp and burned, and others began to catch fire from it. As the flames fell upon the prisoners’ chains, blessed Germanus appeared to them, broke the beam and the chains binding them, opened the prison door, and allowed them to depart unharmed. They went out and took refuge in Saint Vincent’s basilica, where the blessed bishop’s tomb is. While the wind drove flames throughout the city and the fire raged with full force, it began to approach another gate containing an oratory of blessed Martin. This had been built because Martin had once driven away a leprous man’s disease there with a kiss. The man who had built the oratory high with interwoven branches trusted in the Lord and did not doubt blessed Martin’s power. He enclosed himself and his possessions within its walls, saying: ‘I believe and am confident that he who often commanded fires and cleansed a leper’s skin here with a healing kiss will drive the fire away from this place.’ As the fire approached, great balls of flame were carried against the oratory wall but immediately died down upon striking it. Men and women among the people cried out: ‘Flee, wretched people, so that you may escape! A mass of fire is already falling upon you; ashes and burning coals are spreading towards you like heavy rain! Leave the oratory, lest you be burned with it!’ But they prayed and were never moved by these cries. Nor did the woman ever leave the window through which flames occasionally entered, for she was fortified with firmest hope in the blessed bishop’s power. So great was the blessed bishop’s power that he not only saved this oratory with his own foster-child’s house, but did not permit the raging flames to harm the surrounding houses either. The fire which had begun raging from one bridge subsided there. On the other side everything burned so fiercely that only the river stopped it. Yet the churches and their houses were not burned. People said that this city had been consecrated in ancient times so that fire could not prevail there and no snake or dormouse would appear. Recently, however, when a conduit of the bridge was cleaned and the mud filling it removed, they found a bronze serpent and dormouse. After these were removed, countless dormice and snakes subsequently appeared there, and the city began to suffer fires.

### 34. Of the recluses tempted

<a id="hist-08-en-034"></a>
[**[034]**](#hist-08-la-034) Because the prince of darkness has a thousand ways of doing harm, I will relate what recently happened to recluses devoted to God. Vennocus the Breton, honoured with the priesthood and mentioned in another book, devoted himself to such abstinence that he wore only skins, ate uncooked wild herbs, and merely raised the wine vessel to his mouth, seeming rather to touch it with a kiss than drink. But when devout people often generously offered him vessels full of this liquor, he learned—what is worse—to drink immoderately and became so overcome that he was frequently seen drunk. As time passed and his drunkenness increased, he was seized by a demon and tormented with madness, so that he would seize a knife or any weapon, stone, or club he could grasp and chase people in insane fury. It became necessary to bind him with chains and guard him in a cell. Raving under this affliction for two years, he died. Another man, Anatolius of Bordeaux, was, it is said, a twelve-year-old boy serving a merchant when he asked permission to become a recluse. His master long resisted, thinking that he was too weak at that age to accomplish what he sought, but finally overcome by the servant’s prayers he permitted him to fulfil his desire. There was an ancient crypt there, beautifully vaulted and elegantly constructed, in one corner of which a small cell had been enclosed with squared stones, scarcely large enough to hold one standing man. The boy entered this cell and lived there for eight years or more, content with scant food and drink and devoting himself to vigils and prayers. Afterwards he suffered a violent terror and began to cry out that he was being tormented within. Then, with the aid, I believe, of the devil’s soldiers, he removed the squared stones enclosing him, broke the wall to the ground, clapped his hands, and cried that God’s saints were burning him. He remained in this madness for a very long time, often invoking Saint Martin’s name and saying that Martin was tormenting him more than the other saints. He was brought to Tours, but the evil spirit, suppressed, I believe, by the saint’s power and greatness, did not harm the man there. After spending a year in that place without suffering anything, he returned, but again incurred the afflictions from which he had been free.

### 35. Of the legates of the Spaniards

<a id="hist-08-en-035"></a>
[**[035]**](#hist-08-la-035) Envoys from Spain came to King Guntram with many gifts seeking peace, but received no definite answer. In the previous year, when the army attacked Septimania, ships which had gone from Gaul to Galicia were ravaged at King Leovigild’s command; their goods were taken, men were beaten and killed, and some were carried off captive. A few escaped in skiffs and reported to their homeland what had happened.

### 36. Of the death of Magnovaldus

<a id="hist-08-en-036"></a>
[**[036]**](#hist-08-la-036) Magnovald was then killed at King Childebert’s command for secret reasons in the following manner. While the king stood in the palace at Metz watching a spectacle in which an animal surrounded by a pack of dogs was being exhausted, Magnovald was summoned. He came unaware of what was intended and, laughing freely with the others, began to watch the beast. When the man assigned the task saw him intent upon the spectacle, he swung an axe and struck his head. Magnovald fell dead, was thrown through a window of the house, and was buried by his own people. His property was immediately plundered and, as far as it could be found, brought into the public treasury. Some asserted that the reason for his death was that after his brother’s death he had beaten his wife to death with various blows and taken his brother’s wife to his own bed.

### 37. That a son was born to Childebert

<a id="hist-08-en-037"></a>
[**[037]**](#hist-08-la-037) Afterwards a son was born to King Childebert. Magneric, bishop of Trier, received him from the sacred font, and he was named Theudebert. King Guntram was so delighted that he immediately sent envoys with many gifts, saying: ‘Through him God in the mercy of His own majesty will deign to raise up the kingdom of the Franks, if his father or he himself lives.’

### 38. How the Spaniards Burst into Gaul

<a id="hist-08-en-038"></a>
[**[038]**](#hist-08-la-038) In the eleventh year of King Childebert’s reign, envoys again came from Spain seeking peace, but returned without obtaining any definite answer. Reccared, Leovigild’s son, came as far as Narbonne, took plunder within the territory of Gaul, and secretly withdrew.

### 39. Of the death of the bishops

<a id="hist-08-en-039"></a>
[**[039]**](#hist-08-la-039) Many bishops died that year, including Badegisel, bishop of Le Mans, a very savage man towards the people, who wrongfully took and plundered others’ property. His still more savage wife had accommodated herself to his harsh and pitiless mind and goaded him with counsels to commit the most wicked crimes. No day or moment passed without his raging in plundering citizens or in various quarrels. He daily conducted cases with judges, exercised secular offices, raged against some, pursued others with slaughter, beat many with his own hands, trampled them, and said: ‘Because I have become a cleric, shall I therefore not be the avenger of my own injuries?’ What shall I say of others when he did not spare even his own brothers but rather plundered them most of all? They could never obtain justice from him concerning their paternal or maternal property. Having completed the fifth year of his episcopate and entered the sixth, he prepared a banquet for the citizens with immense rejoicing, but was seized by fever and immediately ended by death the year he had begun. Bertram, archdeacon of Paris, was appointed in his place. He is known to have had many disputes with the dead bishop’s widow because she retained as her own property given to the Church in Bishop Badegisel’s time, saying: ‘This was my husband’s service.’ Although unwilling, she restored everything. Badegisel had been of unspeakable malice: he often cut men’s genitals together with the skin of their bellies and burned women’s private parts with heated metal plates, and committed many other iniquities which I have thought better to leave unmentioned. Sabaudus, bishop of Arles, also died, and Licerius, King Guntram’s referendary, was chosen in his place. A severe plague then ravaged Provence itself. Evantius, bishop of Vienne, died too, and Virus, a priest of senatorial family, was appointed to his see at the king’s choice. Many bishops departed from this world that year; I have wished to pass over most of them because each has left his own memorial in his own city.

### 40. Of Pelagius of Tours

<a id="hist-08-en-040"></a>
[**[040]**](#hist-08-la-040) There was also in Tours a certain Pelagius, practised in every wickedness, who feared no judge because the keepers of the fiscal horses were under his authority. He therefore continually committed theft, assault, violent seizure, murder, and various crimes on rivers as well as on land. I often summoned him and tried with threatening and gentle words to deter him from this wickedness, but received from him hatred rather than any fruit of justice, according to Solomon’s proverb in Wisdom: ‘Rebuke a fool, and he will hate you more.’ The wretched man hated me so much that he often robbed and beat the holy Church’s men and left them half dead, seeking opportunities to injure the Church and Saint Martin’s basilica. On one occasion, when our men came bringing church vessels, he beat and trampled them and stole the vessels. When I learned of this, I suspended him from communion, not to avenge my own injury but to correct him more easily and free him from this madness. He came with twelve chosen men to clear himself of the crime by oath. I wished to accept no oath, but, pressed by him and my citizens, I set aside the others and accepted his oath alone, ordering him restored to communion. It was then the first month. When the fifth month came, in which meadows are usually mown, he seized a meadow belonging to nuns which adjoined his own. As soon as he put his sickle into it, he was seized by fever and died on the third day. He had prepared a tomb for himself in Saint Martin’s basilica at the village of Candé, but his men found it broken open and shattered. He was therefore buried afterwards in the basilica’s portico. The church vessels over which he had perjured himself were brought from his storeroom after his death. Thus was manifested the power of blessed Mary, in whose basilica the wretched man uttered his false oath.

### 41. Of those who slew Bishop Praetextatus

<a id="hist-08-en-041"></a>
[**[041]**](#hist-08-la-041) When the report spread throughout the land that Bishop Praetextatus had been killed by Fredegund, she ordered a servant arrested and severely beaten to clear herself more easily of the crime, saying: ‘You brought this accusation against me by attacking Praetextatus, bishop of Rouen, with a sword.’ She handed him over to the bishop’s nephew. When the nephew put him to torture, he revealed the whole affair clearly and said: ‘I received one hundred solidi from Queen Fredegund to do this, fifty from Bishop Melantius, and another fifty from the city’s archdeacon. I was also promised freedom for myself and my wife.’ At these words the man drew his sword and cut the accused to pieces. Fredegund then established Melantius, whom she had previously appointed bishop, over the church.

### 42. How Bippolinus was given as duke

<a id="hist-08-en-042"></a>
[**[042]**](#hist-08-la-042) Because Duke Beppolen was greatly harassed by Fredegund and not shown the honour due to his rank, seeing himself despised, he went to King Guntram. Receiving from him ducal authority over the cities belonging to Chlothar, King Chilperic’s son, he proceeded with great power, but the people of Rennes did not receive him. Coming to Angers, he committed many evils, devastating the grain, hay, wine, and whatever else he could find in citizens’ houses without waiting for keys but breaking down the doors. He beat and trampled many inhabitants. He also frightened Domigisel but was afterwards reconciled with him. While feasting with various people in a tavern near the city, the raised floor of the house suddenly collapsed; he escaped barely alive and many were crippled, yet he persisted in the same evils as before. Fredegund then confiscated much of his property in her son’s kingdom. Returning to Rennes and wishing to subject its people to King Guntram, he left his son there. Not long afterwards the people of Rennes attacked and killed the son together with many distinguished men. Many signs appeared this year: in the seventh month trees were seen to flower, and many which had already borne fruit produced new fruit which remained on the trees until the Lord’s Nativity. Lightning seemed to run across the sky like a serpent.

### 43. How Nicetius was ordained rector of Provence

<a id="hist-08-en-043"></a>
[**[043]**](#hist-08-la-043) In King Childebert’s twelfth year, Nicetius of Clermont was appointed governor of the province of Marseilles and the other cities in those parts belonging to Childebert’s kingdom. Antestius was sent to Angers by King Guntram and inflicted many penalties upon those involved in the death of Domnola, Nectarius’s wife. Bobolenus’s property was confiscated by the fisc because he had been the author of the crime. Antestius then came to Nantes and began to attack Bishop Nonnichius, saying: ‘Justice demands that your son, who was involved in this crime, suffer the punishment his offence deserves.’ Terrified by his accusing conscience, the young man fled to Chlothar, Chilperic’s son. Antestius accepted sureties from the bishop that he would appear before the king and went to Saintes. A report had circulated in those days that Fredegund had secretly sent envoys to Spain and that Palladius, bishop of Saintes, had secretly received them and sent them onward. It was holy Lent, and the bishop had withdrawn to an island in the sea to pray. According to custom he was returning to his church for the feast of the Lord’s Supper while the people awaited him, but Antestius intercepted him on the road. Without examining the truth of the matter, Antestius said: ‘You shall not enter the city but shall be condemned to exile, because you received envoys of our lord king’s enemy.’ Palladius replied: ‘I do not know what you mean. But since the holy days are approaching, let us go to the city; after the holy feasts have been celebrated, bring whatever charges you wish and I will answer, for your suspicion is baseless.’ Antestius said: ‘No. You shall not approach the threshold of your church, because you have proved disloyal to our lord king.’ In short, the bishop was detained on the road, the church house was seized, and its property plundered. The citizens could not obtain from Antestius even permission for the bishop’s case to be heard after Easter. While they pleaded and he refused, he finally revealed the desire hidden in his breast: ‘If he conveys to me by sale the house he is known to possess in the territory of Bourges, I will do what you ask; otherwise he shall not escape my hands but shall be driven into exile.’ The bishop did not dare refuse; he wrote and signed a deed and transferred the land. Then, after giving sureties to appear before the king, he was permitted to enter the city. When the holy days were over he went to the king. Antestius was present but could prove none of his charges against the bishop. The bishop was ordered to return to his city, with the case deferred until a future synod in case any of the charges could be more clearly established. Bishop Nonnichius was also present and departed after giving many gifts.

### 44. Of him who wished to kill King Guntram

<a id="hist-08-en-044"></a>
[**[044]**](#hist-08-la-044) Fredegund sent envoys to King Guntram ostensibly in her son’s name. Having presented their petition and received an answer, they said farewell and departed, but for some reason lingered a little at their lodging. In the morning the king went to matins preceded by a candle. A man was seen sleeping like a drunkard in a corner of the oratory, girt with a sword whose hilt rested against the wall. Seeing him, the king exclaimed that it was not innocent for a man to rest in such a place in the dread of night. The man was seized, bound with straps, and questioned about his purpose and actions. Subjected to torture without delay, he said that the newly arrived envoys had sent him to kill the king. Fredegund’s envoys were then arrested but confessed nothing when questioned, saying: ‘We were sent for no purpose except to deliver the message we presented.’ The king ordered the man to be beaten in various ways and imprisoned, and condemned the envoys to exile in different places. It was quite evident that they had been sent by Fredegund in this plot to kill the king, which the Lord’s mercy did not permit. Baddo was the eldest among them.

### 45. Of the death of Duke Desiderius

<a id="hist-08-en-045"></a>
[**[045]**](#hist-08-la-045) Although envoys from Spain repeatedly came to King Guntram, they could obtain no favour of peace and hostility only increased. King Guntram therefore restored the city of Albi to his nephew Childebert. Duke Desiderius, who had established the greater part of his property in that city’s territory, saw this and feared that vengeance would be exacted from him because of an old enmity: he had once severely defeated an army of King Sigibert of glorious memory in that city. With his wife Tetradi, whom he had taken from Eulalius, now count of Clermont, he crossed into the territory of Toulouse with all his possessions. He assembled an army and prepared to march against the Goths, having first, it is said, divided his property among his sons and wife. Taking Count Austrovald with him, he made for Carcassonne. The citizens had heard of his approach and prepared to resist. When battle was joined, the Goths began to flee, and Desiderius and Austrovald pursued the enemy from behind. After their flight Desiderius approached the city with only a few men, for his companions’ horses were exhausted. Coming to the gate, he was surrounded by the citizens within the walls and killed with all who had followed him; scarcely a few escaped to report what had happened. Hearing that Desiderius was dead, Austrovald turned back and went to the king, who soon appointed him duke in Desiderius’s place.

### 46. Of the death of King Leuvigild

<a id="hist-08-en-046"></a>
[**[046]**](#hist-08-la-046) Afterwards Leovigild, king of the Spains, fell ill. As some assert, he did penance for his heretical error, adjured everyone not to agree with that heresy, and passed over to the Catholic faith. Continuing in tears for seven days over the iniquities he had devised against God, he died. His son Reccared reigned in his place.

*IN CHRISTI NOMEN INCIPIT LIBER VIII.*

## 1. De eo quod rex Aurilianis venit.

<a id="hist-08-la-001"></a>
[**[001]**](#hist-08-en-001) Igitur Gunthchramnus rex anno XXIIII. regni sui de Cavillonno progressus, Nevernensem urbem adgreditur. Invitatus enim Parisius veniebat, ut Chilperici filium, quem iam Chlotliacharium vocitabant, a sacro regenerationes fonte deberet excipere. Digressus vero a Neverno ad Aurilianensem urbem venit, magnum se tunc civibus suis praebens. Nam per domibus eorum invitatus abibat et prandia data libabat; multum ab his muneratus muneraque ipsis proflua benignitate largitus est. Sed cum ad urbem Aurilianensem venisset, erat ea die solemnitas beati Martini, id est quarto Nonas mensis quinti. Prccessitque in obviam eius inmensa populi turba cum signis adque vixillis, canentes laudes. Et hinc lingua Syrorum, hinc Latinorum, hinc etiam ipsorum Iudaeorum in diversis laudibus variae concrepabat, dicens: 'Vivat rex, regnumque eius in populis annis innumeris dilatetur'. Iudaei vero, qui in his laudibus videbantur esse participes, dicebant: 'Omnes gentes te adorent tibique genu flectant adque tibi sint subditi'. Unde factum est, ut, celebratis missis, cum rex ad convivium resederet, diceret: 'Vae genti Iudaicae malae et perfidae ac subdolo semper sensu viventi. Ob hoc enim mihi', inquid, 'hodie laudes adulaturias adclamabant, ut me cunctae gentes quasi dominum adorarent, ut synagoga eorum, quae dudum a christianis deruta est, iuberem ope publica sublevare; quod, iubente Domino, numquam ero facturus'. O regem admirabili prudentia clarum! Sic intellexit dolositatem hereticorum, ut ei paenitus non valerent subripere, quae erant postmodum suggesturi. Iam enim mediante epulo rex loeutus est sacerdotibus qui aderant, dicens: Rogo, ut in domo mea crastina die vestram promerear benedictionem, fiatque mihi salus in ingressu vestro, ut ex hoc salvus fiam, cum super me humilem vestrarum benedictionum verba defluxerint'. Haec eo dicente, omnes gratias agentes, epulo expleto, surreximus.

## 2. Qualiter ei episcopi praesentati sunt, et qualiter ipse convivium praeparavit.

<a id="hist-08-la-002"></a>
[**[002]**](#hist-08-en-002) Mane autem facto, dum rex loca sanctorum orationis gratia visitaret, ad metatum nostrum advenit. Erat enim ibi basilica sancti Aviti abbatis, cui in libro Miraculorum meminimus. Surrexi gavisus, fateor, ad occursum eius et, data oratione, depraecor, ut in mansione mea euglogias beati Martini dignaretur accipere. Quod ille non respuens, benigno animo ingressus, hausto poculo, admonitis nobis ad convivium, laetus abscessit. Tunc Berthchramnus Burdegalensis episcopus cum Palladio Santonico valde regi infensus erat pro susceptione Gundovaldi, cui supra meminimus. Sed et Palladius episcopus ob hoc maxime regem incurrerat, quod ei saepius fallacias intulisset. Discussi enim ante paulolum fuerant a reliquis episcopis et optimatibus regis, cur Gundovaldum suscepissent, cur Faustianum Aquis episcopum ad praeceptionem eius levissimam ordinassent. Sed hanc causam ordinationis Palladius episcopus a Bertechramno metropole suo auferens, super se divolvit, dicens: 'Oculi metropolis mei valde doloribus artabantur, et ego spoliatus et contemptus, invitus in eo loco adductus sum. Non potui aliud facere, nisi quae ille, qui omnem principatum Galliarum se testabatur accipere, imperabat'. Cum haec rege nuntiata fuissent, valde commotus est, ita ut vix obtineri possit, ut eos ad convivium provocaret, quos antea non viderat. Introeunte itaque Bertliramno, interrogat rex: 'Quis', ait, 'est iste?' Diu enim erat, quod ab eo visus non fuerat. Dixeruntque: 'Hic est Berthchramnus Burdegalinsis urbis episcopus'. Cui ille: 'Gratias', inquid, agimus, quod sic custodisti fidem generationi tuae. Scire enim te oportuerat, dilectissime pater, quod parens eras nobis ex matre nostra, et super gentem tuam non debueras inducere pestem extraneam'. Cumque talia et his similia Berthchramnus audisset, conversus ad Palladium rex ait: 'Nec tibi, o Palladi episcope, nimium sunt gratiae referendae. Tertio enim mihi, quod de episcopo dici iniquum est, periurasti, mittens indicolos dolositate plenus. A me excusabaris per epistolas, et germanum meum cum scriptis aliis invitabas. Iudicavit enim Deus causam meam, cum ego provocare vcs semper tamquam aeclesiae patres studui et vos circa me semper egistis dolose'. Nicasio autem et Antidio episcopis dixit: 'Quid vos, o sanctissimi patres, pro regiones utilitate vel regni nostri sospitate tractastis, edicite'. Illis quoque tacentibus, ablutis rex manibus, accepta a sacerdotibus benedictione, ad mensam resedit laeto vulto et hilare faciae, quasi nihil de contempto suo fuisset effatus.

## 3. De cantoribus et argento Mummoli.

<a id="hist-08-la-003"></a>
[**[003]**](#hist-08-en-003) Interea iam medium prandii peractum, iubet rex, ut diaconem nostrum, qui ante die ad missas psalmum responsurium dixerat, canere iuberem. Quo canente, iubet iterum mihi, ut omnes sacerdotes qui aderant per meam commonitionem, datis ex officio suo singulis clerecis, coram rege iuberentur cantare. Per me enim secundum regis imperium admoniti, quisque, ut potuit, in regis praesentia psalmum responsurium decantavit. Cum autem fercula profererentur, dixit rex: 'Argentum omne, quod cernitis, Mummoli illius periuris fuit; sed nunc, gratia Domini tribuente, in nostra dominatione translatum est. Nam quindecim ex eo catinos, ut istum maiorem cernitis, iam concidi, et non exinde amplius quam hunc et alium de cento septuaginta libris reservavi. Et quid amplius quam opus cotidianum? Non ego, quod peius est, alium filium praeter Childeberthum habeo, cui satis sit de thesauris, quos ei pater reliquid', et quae iam de huius miserrimi rebus, quae Avennione inventae sunt, transmittere curavi. Reliqua vero pauperum et aeclesiarum erunt necessitatibus tribuenda.

## 4. Laus Childeberthi regis.

<a id="hist-08-la-004"></a>
[**[004]**](#hist-08-en-004) Unum vos tantummodo, sacerdotes Domini, depraecor, ut pro filio meo Childebertho Domini misericordiam exoretis. Est enim vir sapiens adque utilis, ut de multorum annorum aevo vix ita tam cautus homo repperire possit ac strinuus. Quia, si hunc Deus his Galliis concedere dignabatur, fortassis spes erat, de eodem gentem nostram, quae valde exinanita est, posse consurgere. Quod fieri iuxta eius misericordiam non diffido, eo quod tale fuerit pueri nativitates praesagium. Nam in diem sanctum paschae, stante fratre meo Sigybertho in aeclesia, procedente diacono cum sancto euangeliorum libro, nuntius regi advenit, unaque vox fuit pronuntiantes lectionem euangelicam ac nuntii dicentis: „Filius natus est tibi". Unde factum est, ut omnis populus in utraque adnuntiatione pariter proclamaret: „Gloria Deo omnipotenti". Sed et baptismum in die sanctum pentecosten accepit, et rex nihilominus in diem sanctum dominicae nativitatis est elevatus. Unde, si oratio vestra prosequitur, poterit hic, Domino annuente, regnare'. Haec rege dicente, omnes orationem fuderunt ad Dominum, ut utrumque regem eius misericordia conservaret. Adiecitque rex: 'Verum quia mater eius Brunichildis me minatur interimere, sed nihil mihi ex hoc formidinis est. Dominus enim, qui me eripuit de manibus inimicorum meorum, et de huius insidiis liberavit me'.

## 5. De visiones, quas rex vel nos de Chilperico vidimus.

<a id="hist-08-la-005"></a>
[**[005]**](#hist-08-en-005) Multa tunc et in Theodorum adversa locutus est, protestans, quod, si ad synodum veniret, iterum exilio truderetur, dicens: 'Scio enim, quod horum causa germanum meum Chilpericum interfeci fecit. Denique nec nos pro viris habere debemur, si eius necem ulciscere non valemus hoc anno. Cui ego respondi: 'Et quis Chilpericum interemit, nisi malitia sua tuaque oratio? Multas enim tibi contra iustitiam tetendit insidias, quae ei mortis exitium intulerunt. Quod ut dicam, valde hoc per visionem somnii inspexi, cum viderem eum, ante tonsorato capite, quasi episcopum ordinari; deinde super cathedram puram, sola fuligine tectam, inpositum ferri, praelucentibus coram eo lyghnis ac cereis'. Me haec narrante, rex ait: 'Vidi et ego aliam visionem, quae huius interitum nuntiavit. Adducebatur enim in conspectu meo a tribus episcopis vinctus catenis, quorum unus Tetricus, alius Agroecula, tertius vero Nicetius Lugduninsis erat. E quibus dicebant duo: „Solvite, quaesumus, eum et castigatum abire permittite". Quibus e contrario cum amaritudine Tetricus episcopus respondebat: „Non fiet ita, sed igni concremabitur pro sceleribus suis". Et cum diu multumque quasi altercantes haec inter se verba proferrent, conspicio eminus aeneum super ignem positum fervere vehementer. Tunc me flente, adpraehensum infilicem Chilpericum, confractis membris, proiciunt in aeneum. Nec mora, inter undarum vapores ita dissolutus ac liquefactus est, ut nullum ex eo paenitus indicium remaneret'. Haec rege dicente, admirantibus nobis, epulo expleto, surreximus.

## 6. De his quos praesentavimus.

<a id="hist-08-la-006"></a>
[**[006]**](#hist-08-en-006) Rex igitur in crastinum in venatione progressus est. Quo redeunte, Garacharius comis Burdigalensis adque Bladastis a nobis repraesentat sunt, quia, ut superius diximus, in basilica sancti Martini confugium fecerant, pro eo quod Gundovaldo coniuncti fuissent. Nam cum prius, pro his depraecatus, nihil obtinere potuissem, haec in sequenti locutus sum: 'Audiat, o rex, potestas tua. Ecce! a domino meo in legatione ad te directus sum. Vel quid renuntiabo ei qui me misit, cum nihil mihi responsi reddere vellis?' At ille obstupefactus ait: 'Et quis est dominus tuus, qui te misit?' Cui ego subridens: 'Beatus Martinus', inquio, 'misit me'. Tunc ille iussit sibi repraesentari viros. Sed cum in eius conspectu venissent, multas eis perfidias ac periurias exprobravit, vocans eos saepius vulpis ingeniosas, sed restituit eos gratiae suae, reddens quae illis ablata fuerant.

## 7. De Palladio episcopo, qualiter missas dixit.

<a id="hist-08-la-007"></a>
[**[007]**](#hist-08-en-007) Adveniente quoque die dominico, rex ad aeclesiam ad spectanda missarum solemnia petit. Fratres vero consacerdotesque, qui aderant, locum Palladio episcopo ad agenda festa praebuerunt. Quo incipiente prophetiam, rex interrogat, quis esset. Cumque Palladium episcopum initiasse pronuntiassent, statim commotus rex ait: 'Qui mihi semper infidelis et perfidus fuit, ille nunc sacrata verba praedicavit? Egrediar prursus ab haec aeclesia, ne inimicum meum audiam praedicantem'. Et haec dicens, egredi coepit aeclesiam. Tunc conturbati sacerdotes de fratres humilitate, dixerunt regi: Tidimus enim eum convivio tuo adesse ac de eius manu te benedictionem accipere; et cur eum nunc rex aspernatur? Si enim scissemus tibi exosum, declinassemus utique ad alium, qui haec agere debuisset. Nunc, si permittis, caelebret, quae coepit; in posterum autem, si aliquid opposueris, canonicae sanctionis censura finiatur'. lam enim Palladius episcopus in sacrario cum grande humilitate discesserat. Tunc rex iussit eum revocare, et sic quae agere coeperat expedivit. Nam cum iterato ad convivium regis Palladius atque Berthchramnus acciti fuissent, commoti in invicem multa sibi de adulteriis ac fornicatione exprobraverunt, nonnulla etiam de periuriis. Quibus de rebus multi ridebant, nonnulli vero, qui alacriores erant scientiae, lamentabant, cur inter sacerdotes Domini taliter zezania diabuli pollularet. Discedentes itaque a regis praesentia, cautiones et fideiussores dederunt, ut decimo Kalendas mensis noni ad sinodum convenirent.

## 8. De signis ostensis.

<a id="hist-08-la-008"></a>
[**[008]**](#hist-08-en-008) Tunc apparuerunt signa, id est radii a parte aquilonis, sicut saepius apparere solent. Fulgor per caelum cucurrisse visus est, floresque in arboribus ostensi sunt. Erat enim mensis quintus.

## 9. De sacramento pro filio Chilperici dato.

<a id="hist-08-la-009"></a>
[**[009]**](#hist-08-en-009) Post haec rex Parisius venit et coram omnibus loqui coepit, dicens: ' Germanus meus Chilpericus moriens dicitur filium reliquisse, cuius nutritores, matre depraecante, petierunt, ut eum de sancto lavacro in dominici natalis solemnitate deberem excipere, et non venerunt. Rogaverunt deinceps, ut ad sanctum pascha baptizaretur, sed nec tunc adlatus est infans. Depraecati sunt autem tertio, ut ad festivitatem sancti Iohannis exhiberetur, sed nec tunc venit. Moverunt itaque me per tempus sterile de loco ubi abitabam. Veni igitur, et ecce! absconditur nec ostenditur mihi puer. Unde, quantum intellego, nihil est quod promittitur, sed, ut credo, alicuius ex leudibus nostris sit filius. Nam si de stirpe nostra fuisset, ad me utique fuerat deportatus. Ideoque noveritis, quia a me non suscipitur, nisi certa de eo cognuscam indicia'. Haec audiens Fredegundis regina, coniunctis prioribus regni sui, id est cum tribus episcopis et tricentis viris optimis, sacramenta dederunt, hunc ab Chilperico rege generatum fuisse; et sic suspicio ab animis regis ablata est.

## 10. De corporibus Merovechi et Chlodovechi.

<a id="hist-08-la-010"></a>
[**[010]**](#hist-08-en-010) Denique cum interitum Merovechi adque Chlodovechi saepius lamentaret nesciretque, ubi eos postquam inteificerant proiecissent, venit ad regem homo, qui diceret: 'Si mihi contrarium in posterum non habetur, indicabo, in quo loco Chlodovechi cadaver sit positum'. Iuravit rex, nihil ei molestum fieri, sed potius muneribus ampliari. Tunc ille: 'Veritatem', inquid, 'me loqui, o rex, ipsa ratio quae acta est conprobabit. Nam quando Chlodovechus interfectus est ac sub stillicidio oraturii cuiusdam sepultus, metuens regina, ne aliquando inventus cum honore sepeliretur, iussit eum in alveum Matronae fluminis proici. Tunc intra lapsum, quod opere meo ad capiendorum piscium necessitatem praeparaveram, repperi. Sed cum ignorarem, quisnam esset, a caesariae prolixa cognovi Chlodovechum esse, adpraehensumque in humeris ad litus detuli ibique eum cespite superposito tumulavi. Ecce, salvatis artubus, quod volueris effice!' Quod cum rex conperisset, confingens se ad venationem procedere, detectoque tumulo, repperit corpusculum integrum et inlaesum. Una tantum pars capillorum, quae subter fuerat, iam defluxerat, alia vero cum ipsis crinium flagellis intacta durabat. Cognitumque est, hunc esse, quem rex intento animo requirebat. Convocato igitur episcopo civitatis, cum clero et populo ac cereorum innumerabilium ornato ad basilicam sancti Vincenti detulit tumulandum, non minus plangens nepotes mortuos, quam cum vidit filios proprios iam sepultus. Post haec misit Pappolum Carnotenae urbis episcopum, qui Merovechi cadaver requirens, iuxta Chlodovechi tumulum sepelivit.

## 11. De ostiariis et interito Boanti.

<a id="hist-08-la-011"></a>
[**[011]**](#hist-08-en-011) Ostiarius vero quidam de alio ostiario dixit: 'Domine rex, hic, accepto praemio, consinsit, ut tu interficiaris'. Adpraehensusque ostiarius, de quo dixerat, caesus suppliciisque multis adfectus, nihil de causa, qua interrogabatur, aperuit. Loquebantur enim tunc multi hoc in insidiis et invidia factum, quod ostiarius ille, cui hoc crimen inpactum fuerat, plurimum a reges diligeretur. Ansovaldus autem, nescio qua suspicione tactus, nec vale dicens, a rege discessit. Rex vero Cavillonum regressus, iussit Boantum, qui sibi semper fuerat infidelis, gladio percuti. Qui vallatus in domo sua, ab hominibus regis peremptus interiit, resque suae fisci dicionibus subiugatae sunt.

## 12. De Theudoro episcopo et plaga super Ratharium.

<a id="hist-08-la-012"></a>
[**[012]**](#hist-08-en-012) Denique cum rex maxima intentione Theodorum episcopum iterum persequi conaretur et Massilia iam in Childeberthi regis dominatione revocata fuisset, ad discutiendas causas Ratharius illuc quasi dux a parte regis Childeberthi diregitur. Sed postposita actione, quae ei a rege iniuncta fuerant, episcopum vallat, fideiussores requirit et ad praesentiam regis Gunthchramni direxit, ut scilicet ad synodum, quod Matiscone futurum erat, quasi ab episcopis damnandus adesset. Nec defuit ultio divina, quae servos suos ab ore canum rabidorum defensare consuevit. Nam egrediente episcopo a civitate, statim res aeclesiae direpit et alia quidem sibi vindicat, alia sub sigillorum munitione concludit. Cumque hoc fecisset, protenus famulus eius saevissimus invadit morbus exhaustusque febre peremit; filius eius ab hcc incommodo defecit, quem suburbano Massiliae ipsius cum gravi gemitu sepelivit. Fuitque talis domui eius plaga, ut, cum ab urbe illa est digressus, vix ad patriam suam regredi putaretur. Theodorus vero episcopus a Gunthchramno rege detentus est, sed nihil ei rex nocuit. Est enim vir egregiae sanctitatis et in oratione assiduus, de quo mihi Magnericus Treverensis episcopus haec retulit: Ante hos annos cum ad praesentia Childeberthi regis ita sub ardua custodia duceretur, ut, quandoque ad urbem aliquam venisset, neque episcopum neque quemquam de civibus videre permitteretur, adveniens Treverus, nuntiatum est episcopo, hunc iam in navi positum clam abduci. Surrexitque sacerdus tristis, ac velociter prosecutus, repperit eum ad litus; causatusque cum custodibus, cur tanta esset impietas, ut non liceret fratri fratrem aspicere, visoque tandem, osculatus eum, indulgens aliquid vestimenti, discessit. Veniens itaque ad basilicam sancti Maximini, prosternitur sepulchro, illud apostoli Iacobi retinens: Orate pro invicem, ut salvimini. Fusaque diu oratione cum lacrimis, ut fratre dignaretur Dominus adiuvare, egressus est foris. Et ecce! mulier, quam spiritus erroris agitabat, clamare sacerdoti coepit ac dicere: 'O sceleste et inveterate dierum, qui pro inimico nostro Theodore orationem fundis ad Dominum, ecce! nos cotidie querimus, qualiter ab his Galliis extrudatur, qui nos cotidianis incendiis conflat, et tu pro eo rogare non desinis! Satius enim tibi erat, res aeclesiae tuae diligenter inquirere, ne pauperibus aliquid deperiret, quam pro hoc tam intente deposcere'. Et agebat: 'Vae nobis, qui eum non possumus expugnare'. Et licet daemoniis credi non debeat, tamen qualis esset sacerdus, de quo haec daemon condolens declamabat, apparuit. Sed ad coepta redeamus.

## 13. De legationem Gunthchramni ad Childeberthum directa.

<a id="hist-08-la-013"></a>
[**[013]**](#hist-08-en-013) Igitur legatus ad nepotem suum Childeberthum rex diregit, qui morabatur tunc ad castrum Confluentis, qui ob hoc nomen accepit, pro eo quod Musella Rhenusque amnes pariter confluentes in eodem loco iungantur. Et quia placitum fuerat, ut Trecas Campaniae urbem de utrumque regnum coniungerent, sacerdotibusque de regno Childeberthi congruum non fuit, Felix legatus, salutatione praemissa, ostensis litteris, ait: 'Patruus tuus, o rex, diligenter interrogat, quis te ab hac promissione retraxit, ut sacerdotes regni vestri ad concilium, quod simul decreveratis, venire differrent. An forsitan mali homines aliquam inter vos discordiae radicem faciunt pullulare?' Tunc ego, rege tacente, respondi: 'Nimirum, si zizania seratur in populus; nam inter hos quo radicem obligit protenus non potest repperiri. Nulli enim latet, quod Childeberthus rex alium patrem nisi patruum non habet, neque ille alium filium nisi hunc habere disponit, iuxta id quod eum anno praesenti audivimus loqui. Absit ergo, ut inter eos radix discordiae germinet, cum se pariter et tuere debeant et amare'. Tunc vocato secretius Felice legato, Childeberthus rex rogavit, dicens: 'Depraecor dominum et patrem meum, ut Theodoro episcopo nihil iniuriae inferat; quod si fecerit, confestim inter nos scandalum germinavit, erimusque, discordia inpediente, disiuncti, qui debemus amorem tuendo esse pacifici'. Acceptoque et de aliis causis responso, legatus discessit.

## 14. De periculo in flumine.

<a id="hist-08-la-014"></a>
[**[014]**](#hist-08-en-014) Nobis itaque in antedicto castro cum regem commorantibus, dum ad convivium principis usque obscura nocte reteneremur, epulo expleto, surreximus, venientesque ad fluvium, offendimus navem in litus, quae nobis fuerat praeparata. Ascendentibusque nobis, inruit turba hominum diversorum, impletaque est navis tam hominibus quam aquis. Sed virtus Domini adfuit non sine grande miraculo, ut, cum usque labium impleta fuisset, mergi non possit. Habebamus enim nobiscum beati Martini reliquias cum aliorum sanctorum, quorum virtutem nos credimus fuisse salvatos. Ad vero nave ad litus unde egressi fuimus redeunte, evacuata vel ab hominibus vel a lymphis, repulsis extraneis, sine inpedimento transivimus. In crastino autem vale regi dicentes, abscessimus.

## 15. De conversione Vulfelaici diaconi.

<a id="hist-08-la-015"></a>
[**[015]**](#hist-08-en-015) Profecti igitur in itenere, ad Eposium castrum accessimus, ibique a Vulfilaico diacono nancti, ad monasterium eius deducti, benignissime suscepti sumus. Est enim hoc monasterium quasi milibus octo ab antedicto castro in montis cacumine collocatum. In quo monte magnam basilicam aedificavit, quam beati Martini vel reliquorum sanctorum reliquiis inlustravit. Commorantes autem ibi, petere ab eo ccepimus, ut nobis aliqua de conversionis suae bono narraret, vel qualiter ad clericatus officium advenissit, quia erat genere Langobardus. Sed nequibat exponere, vanam tcta intentione cupiens gloriam evitare. Quem ego terribilibus sacramentis coniurans, pollicitus primo, ut nulli quae referebat expanderem, rogare coepi, ut nihil mihi de his quae interrogabam cccoleret. Cumque diutissime reluctasset, victus tandem tam praecibus quam obsecrationibus meis, haec effatus est: 'Dum essem', inquid, 'puer parvolus, audito beati Martini nomine, nesciens adhuc, utrum martyr an confessor esset, vel quid boni in mundo gessisset, vel quae regio beatus artus tumulo meruisset accipere, iam in eius honore vigilias celebrabam ac, si aliquid inter manus numismatis advenisset, aelimosinas faciebam. Iamque in maiore aetate proficiens, litteras discere studui; ex quibus prius scribere potui, quam ordinem scriptarum litterarum scirem. Deinde Aridio abbati coniunctus ab eoque edoctus, beati Martini basilicam adii. Revertensque cum eo, ille parumper pulveris beati sepulchri pro benedictione sustulit, quod in capsulam positum ad collum meum dependit. Devectique ad monasterium eius Limovicino in termino, accepta capsula, ut eam in oraturio suo locaret, in tantum pulvis adcrevit, ut non solum totam capsam repleret, verum etiam foris inter iuncturas, ubi aditum repperire potuit, scatiret. Ex hoc mihi miraculi lumine animus magis accendit totam spem meam in eius virtute defigere. Deinde territurium Trevericae urbis expetii, et in quo nunc estis monte habitaculum quod cernitis proprio labore construxi. Repperi tamen hic Dianae simulacrum, quod populus hic incredulus quasi deum adorabat. Colomnam etiam statui, in qua cum grandi cruciatu sine ullo pedum perstabam tegmine. Itaque cum hiemis tempus solite advenisset, ita rigore glaciali urebar, ut ungues pedum meorum saepius vis rigoris excuteret et in barbis meis aqua gelu conexa candelarum more dependeret. Magnam enim hiemem regio illa persaepe dicitur sustenere '. Sed cum nos sollicite interrogaremus, qui ei cybus aut potus esset, vel qualiter simulacra montis illius subvertisset, ait: 'Potus cybusque meus erat parumper panis et oleris ac modicum aquae. Verum ubi ad me multitudo vicinarum villarum confluere coepit, praedicabam iugiter, nihil esse Dianam, nihil simulacra nihilque quae eis videbatur exercere cultura; indigna etiam esse ipsa, quae inter pocula luxuriasque profluas cantica proferebant; sed potius Deo omnipotenti, qui caelum fecit ac terram, dignum sit sacrificium laudis inpendere. Orabam etiam saepius, ut simulacrum Dominus dirutum dignaretur populum illum ab hoc errore discutere. Flexit Domini misericordia mentem rusticam, ut inclinaret aurem suam in verba oris mei, ut scilicet, relictis idolis, Dominum sequeretur. Tunc convocatis quibusdam ex eis, simulacrum hoc inmensum, quod elidere propria virtute non poteram, cum eorum adiutorio possim eruere; iam enim reliqua sigillorum, quae faciliora fuerant, ipse confringeram. Convenientibus autem multis ad hanc Dianae statuam, missis funibus, trahere coeperunt; sed nihil labor eorum proficere poterat. Tunc ego ad basilicam propero, prostratusque solo, divinam misericordiam cum lacrimis flagitabam, ut, quia id humana industria evertere non valebat, virtus illud divina destrueret. Egressusque post orationem, ad operarius veni, adprehensumque funem, ut primo ictu trahere coepimus, protenus simulacrum ruit in terra, confractumque cum malleis ferreis in pulverem redegi. Ipsa quoque hora, cum ad cibum capiendum venissem, ita omne corpus meum a vertice usque ad plantam pusulis malis repletum est, ut locus, quem unus digitus tegerit, vacuus invenire non possit. Ingressusque basilicam solus, denudavi me coram sancto altario. Habebam enim ibi ampullam cum oleo plenam, quam de sancti Martini basilicam detuleram; ex qua propriis manibus omnes artus perunxi, moxque sopori locatus sum. Expergefactus vero circa medium noctis, cum ad cursum reddendum surgerem, ita corpus totum incolomem repperi, acsi nullum super me ulcus apparuisset. Quae vulnera non aliter nisi per invidiam inimici emissa cognovi. Et quia semper ipse invidus Deum quaerentibus nccere conatur, advenientibus episcopis, qui me magis ad hoc cohortare debuerant, ut coeptum opus sagaciter explicare deberem, dixerunt mihi: „Non est aequa haec via, quam sequeris, nec tu ignobilis Symeoni Anthiochino, qui colomnae insedit, poteris conparare. Sed nec cruciatum hoc te sustenere patitur loci positio. Discende potius et cum fratribus, quos adgregasti tecum, inhabita". Ad quorum verba, quia sacerdotes non obaudire adscribitur crimini, discendebam, fateor, et ambulabam cum eisdem ac cybum pariter capiebam. Quadam vero die, provocans me episcopus longius ad villam, emisit operarius cum scutis et malleis ac securibus, et eliserunt colomnam, in qua stare solitus eram. In crastinum autem veniens, inveni omnia dissipata. Flevi vehementer, sed erigere nequivi quae distruxerant, ne dicerer contrarius iussionibus sacerdotum; et ex hoc, sicut nunc habito, cum fratribus habitare contentus sum'.

## 16. Et quae de virtutibus sancti Martini retulit.

<a id="hist-08-la-016"></a>
[**[016]**](#hist-08-en-016) Cui cum de virtutibus beati Martini, quas in eo loco operatus est, aliquam ut declararet exposcirem, haec retulit: 'Franci cuiusdam et nobilissimi in gente sua viri filius mutus surdusque erat; adductusque a parentibus ad hanc basilicam, iussi eum cum diacono meo et alio ministro in ipsum templum sanctum in lectulo requiescere. Et per diem quidem orationi vacabat, nocte autem in ipsa, ut diximus, aede dormiebat. Quandoque misertus Deus, apparuit mihi in visione beatus Martinus, dicens: „Eiece agnum de basilica, quia iam sanus factus est". Mane autem facto, cogitanti mihi, quod esset hoc somnium, venit ad me puer, et emittens vocem, gratias agere Deo coepit, conversusque ad me, ait: „Gratias ago Deo omnipotenti, qui mihi et eloquium reddedit et auditum". Ex hoc sanus redditus, ad domum rediit. Alius vero, qui plerumque in furtis diversisque sceleribus conmixtus, periurare consueverat, cum aliquando a quibusdam pro furtu argueritur, ait: „Ibo ad basilicam beati Martini, et sacramentis me exuens, innocens reddar". Quo ingrediente, elapsa secure de manu eius, ad usteum ruit, gravi cordis dolore perculsus. Confessusque est miser verbis propriis quae venerat excusare periuriis. Alius simili modo cum de incendiis domus vicini sui argueritur, ait: „Vadam ad templum sancti Martini et, fide data, insons redditurus ero ab hoc crimine". Manifestum erat enim, hunc domum illam incendio concremasse. Abiens autem ad sacramenta danda, conversus ad eum, dixi ei: „Quantum vicinorum tuorum dictat assertio, non eris innocens ab hoc scelere; sed tamen Deus ubique est, et virtus eius ipsa est forinsecus, quae habetur intrinsecus. Tamen si ita te vana fiducia coepit, quod Deus vel sancti eius in periuribus non ulciscantur, ecce! templum sanctum e contra, iura, ut libet. Nam calcare limen sacrum non permitteris". Ad ille, elevatis manibus, ait: „Per omnipotentem Deum et virtutem beati Martini antestitis eius, quia hoc incendium non admisi". Data itaque sacramenta, dum recederet, visum est ei quasi ab igne circumdare. Et statim ruens in terra, clamare coepit, se a beato antestite vehementer exuri. Aiebat enim miser: „Testor Deum, quia ego vidi ignem de caelo cadere, qui me circumdans validis vaporibus conflat". Et dum haec diceret, spiritum exalavit. Multis haec causa documentum fuit, ne in hoc loco auderent ulterius periurare'. Plurima quidem de his virtutibus hic diaconus retulit, quae sequi longum potavi.

## 17. De signis quae apparuerunt.

<a id="hist-08-la-017"></a>
[**[017]**](#hist-08-en-017) Dum autem in loco illo commoraremur, vidimus per duas noctes signa in caelo, id est radius a parte aquilonis tam clare splendidus, ut prius sic apparuisse non fuerent visi; et ab utraque quidem parte, id est ab euro et zephero, nubes sanguineae. Tertia vero nocte quasi hora secunda apparuerunt hii radii. Et ecce! dum eos miraremur attoniti, surrexerunt a quatuor plagis mundi alii horum similes; vidimusque totum caelum ab his operire. Et erat nubes in medio caeli splendida, ad quam se hi radii collegebant in modum tenturii, quod ab imo ex amplioribus incoeptum fasceis, angustatis in altum, in uno cuculli capite saepe collegitur. Erantque in medio radiorum et aliae nubes, ceu coruscum valide fulgorantes. Quod signum magnum nobis ingessit metum. Operiebamur enim, super nos aliquam plagam de caelo transmitti.

## 18. Quod Childeberthus in Italiam direxit exercitum.

<a id="hist-08-la-018"></a>
[**[018]**](#hist-08-en-018) Childebertus vero rex, inpellentibus missis imperialibus, qui aurum, quod anno superiore datum fuerat, requirebat, exercitum in Italia diregit. Sonus enim erat, sororem suam Ingundem iam Constantinopoli fuisset translata. Sed cum duces inter se altercarentur, regressi sunt sine ullius lucri conquisitione. Nam Wintrio dux, a paginsibus suis depulsus, ducatum caruit; finissitque vitam, nisi fuga auxilium praebuisset. Sed postea, pacatum populum, ducatum recepit. Itaque Nicetius per emissionem Eulalii a comitatu Arverno submotus, ducatum a rege expetiit, datis pro eo inmensis muneribus. Et sic in urbe Arverna, Rutena atque Ucetica dux ordinatus est, vir valde aetate iuvenis, sed acutus in sensu, fecitque pacem in regionem Arverna vel in reliqua ordinationis suae loca. Chuldericus vero Saxo in offensam regis Guntchramni incedens, pro causa, qua superius diximus alius confugisse, beati Martini basilicam expetiit, uxorem in regno regis antedicti relinquens. Cui obtestaverat rex, ne virum videre praesumeret, nisi prius ille regali gratiae reconciliaretur; ad quem cum pro eo saepius legationem misissimus, tandem obtenuemus, ut uxorem reciperet et citra Legerem fluvium commoraretur, non tamen ad regem Childeberthum transire praesumeret. Sed ille, accepta libertate recipiendae uxoris, clam ad eum transiit, adeptaque ordinatione ducatus in civitatebus ultra Garonnam, quae in potestatem supradicti regis habebantur, accessit. Guntchramnus vero rex volens regnum nepotis sui Chlotchari, fili scilicet Chilperici, regere, Theodulfum Andegavis comitem esse decrevit. Introductusque in urbe, a civibus et praesertim a Domighisilo cum humilitate repulsus est. Recurrensque ad regem, iterum praeceptum accipiens, a Sigulfo duci intromissus, comitatum urbis illius rexit. Gundovaldus autem comitatum Meldensim super Werpinum conpetiit, ingressusque urbem, causarum actionem agere coepit. Exinde dum pagum urbis in hoc officio circuiret, in quadam villa a Werpino interficitur. Cuius parentes congregati super hunc inruunt, inclusumque in pensilem domus interemunt. Sicque uterque a comitatu morte inminente discessit.

## 19. De interitu Daulfi abbatis.

<a id="hist-08-la-019"></a>
[**[019]**](#hist-08-en-019) Cum autem saepius Dagulfus abba pro sceleribus suis argueritur, quia furta et homicidia plerumque faciebat, sed et in adulteriis nimium dissolutus erat, quodam tempore uxorem vicini sui concupiscens, miscebatur cum ea. Requirens occasiones diversas, qualiter virum adulterae, qui in terra huius monasterii conmanebat, deberet oppremere, ad extremum contestatus est ei, dicens, quod, si ad uxorem suam accederet, puniretur. Illo quoque discedente ab hospitiolo suo, hic nocte cum uno clerico veniens, domum meretricis ingreditur. Postquam autem diutissime bibentes inebriati sunt, in uno strato locantur. Quibus dormientibus, adveniens vir ille, accenso stramine, elevata bipenne utrumque peremit. Ideoque documentum sit haec causa clericis, ne contra canonum statuta extranearum mulierum consortium potiantur, cum haec et ipsa lex canonica et omnes scripturae sanctae prohibeant, praeter his feminis, de quibus crimen non potest aestimari.

## 20. Quae in sinodo Matescense acta sunt.

<a id="hist-08-la-020"></a>
[**[020]**](#hist-08-en-020) Interim dies placiti advenit, et episcopi ex iusso regis Guntchramni apud Matiscensim urbem collecti sunt. Faustianus autem, qui ex iusso Gundovaldi Aquinsi urbi episcopus ordinatus fuerat, ea condicione removitur, ut eum Bertchramnus Orestesque sive Palladius, qui eum benedixerant, vicibus pascerent centinusque ei aureus annis singulis ministrarent. Nicetius tamen ex laico, qui prius ab Chilperico regi praeceptum elicuerat, in ipsa urbe episcopatum adeptus est. Ursicinus Cadurcinsis episcopus excommunicatur, pro eo quod Gundovaldum excipisse publice est confessus, accepto huiusmodi placito, ut, paenitentiam tribus annis agens, neque capillum neque barbam tonderit, vino et carnibus absteniret, missas celebrare, clericus ordinare aeclesiasque et crisma benedicere, eulogias dare paenitus non anderet, utilitas tamen aeclesiae per eius ordinationem, sicut solita erat, omnino exerceretur. Extetit enim in hac synodo quidam ex episcopis, qui dicebat, mulierem hominem non posse vocitare. Sed tamen ab episcopis ratione accepta quievit, eo quod sacer Veteris Testamenti liber edoceat, quod in principio, Dec hominem creante, ait: Masculum et feminam creavit eos, vocavitque nomen eorum Adam, quod est homo terrenus, sic utique vocans mulierem ceu virum; utrumque enim hominem dixit. Sed et dominus Iesus Christus ob hoc vocitatur filius hominis, quod sit filius virginis, id est mulieris. Ad quam, cum aquas in vina transferre pararet, ait: Quid mihi et tibi est, mulier? et reliqua. Multisque et aliis testimoniis haec causa convicta quievit. Praetextatus vero Rotomagensk episcopus orationis, quas in exsilio positus scalpsit, coram episcopis recitavit. Quae quibusdam quidem placuerunt, a quibusdam vero, quia artem secutus minime fuerat, repraehendebantur. Stilus tamen per lcca aeclesiasticus et rationabilis erat. Caedis enim magna tunc inter famulus Prisci episcopi et Leudeghisili ducis fuit. Priscus tamen episcopus ad coemendam pacem multum paecuniae obtulit. His etenim diebus Guntchramnus rex graviter aegrotavit, ita ut potaretur a quibusdam non posse prorsus evadere. Quod, credo, providentia Dei fecisset. Cogitabat enim multus episcoporum exsilio detrudere. Theodorus itaque episcopus ad urbem suam regressus, favente omni populo, cum laude susceptus est.

## 21. De placito Belsonanco et de sepulchro violato.

<a id="hist-08-la-021"></a>
[**[021]**](#hist-08-en-021) Itaque cum hoc synodum ageretur, Childeberthus rex aput Belsonancum villa, quae in medio Ardoennensis silvae sita est, cum suis coniungitur. Ibique Brunichildis regina pro Ingunde filia, quae adhuc in Africa tenebatur, omnibus prioribus questa est, sed parum consolationis emeruit. Tunc contra Bosonem Guntchramnum causa exoritur. Ante paucus autem dies mortua propinqua uxoris eius sine filiis, in basilicam urbis Metinsis sepulta est cum grandibus crnamentis et multo auro. Factum est autem, ut post dies paucus adesset festivitas beati Remedii, quae in initio mensis octavi caelebratur. Discedentibus autem multis ei civitate cum episcopo et praesertim senioris urbis cum duci, venerunt pueri Bosonis Guntchramni ad basilica, in qua mulier erat sepulta. Et ingressi, conclusis super se osteis, detexerunt sepulchrum, tollentes omnia ornamenta corporis defuncti, quae reperire potuerant. Sentientes autem haec monachi basilicae illius, venerunt ad cstium; sed ingredi non sunt permissi. Quod videntes, nuntiaverunt haec episcopo suo ac duci. Interea pueri, acceptis rebus ascensisque equitibus, fugire coeperunt. Sed timentes, ne, adprehensi in via, diversis subegerentur poenis, regressi sunt ad basilicam. Posueruntque quidem res super altarium, sed foris egredi non sunt ausi, clamantes atque dicentes, quia: 'A Gunthchramno Bosone transmissi sumus'. Sed cum ad placitum in villam quam diximus Childeberthus cum proceribus suis convenisset et Gunthchramnus de his interpellatus nullum responsum dedisset, dam aufugit. Ablataeque sunt ei deinceps omnes res, quae in Arverno de fisci munere promeruerat. Sed et diversorum res, quas male pervaserat, cum confusionem reliquid.

## 22. De obito episcoporum et Wandalini.

<a id="hist-08-la-022"></a>
[**[022]**](#hist-08-en-022) Laban Helosinsis episcopus hoc anno obiit; cui Desiderius ex laico successit. Cum iusiurando enim rex pollicitus fuerat, se numquam ex laicis episcopum ordinaturum. Sed quid pectora humana non cogat auri sacra famis? Berthchramnus vero regressus ex synodo, a febre corripitur; arcessitumque Waldonem diaconem, qui et ipse in baptismo Berthchramnus vocitatus est, summam ei sacerdotii depotat omnesque condicionis tam testamenti quam benemeritorum suorum ipsi committit. Quo discedente, hic spiritum exalavit. Regressus diaconus, cum muneribus et consensu civium ad regem properat, set nihil obtenuit. Tunc rex, data praeceptione, iussit Gundegisilum Sanctonicum comitem cognomento Dodonem episcopum ordinare gestumque est ita. Et quia multi clericorum Sanctonicorum ante synodum, consentientes Berthchramno episcopo, in Palladium sacerdotem suum aliqua adversa conscripserant, quae ei humilitatem ingererent, post eius obitum adprehensi a sacerdote, graviter caesi adque expoliati sunt. Hoc tempore et Wandelenus, nutritor Childeberti regis, obiit, sed in loco eius nullus est subrogatus, eo quod regina mater curam vellit propriam habere de filio. Quaecumque de fisco meruit, fisci iuribus sunt relata. Obiit his diebus Bodygisilus dux plenus dierum, sed nihil de facultate eius filiis minuatum est. In loco Fausti Auscensis episcopi Saius presbiter subrogatur. Post obitum sancti Salvii hoc anno Desideratus Albiginsibus episcopus datus est.

## 23. De diluviis.

<a id="hist-08-la-023"></a>
[**[023]**](#hist-08-en-023) Magnae hoc anno pluviae fuerunt, amnesque in tantum convaluerunt, ut plerumque naufragia evenirent. Ipsique litora excedentes, propinquas segetis ac prata operientes graviter eliserunt, fueruntque vernalis aestivique mensis tam inrigui, ut hiems magis potaretur esse quam aestas.

## 24. De insolis maris.

<a id="hist-08-la-024"></a>
[**[024]**](#hist-08-en-024) Duae hoc anno insolae in mare divinitus incendio concrematae sunt, quae per dies septim cum hominibus pecoribusque consumptae subvertebantur. Nam qui in mari confugerant et se in profunde praecipitabant, in ipsa qua mergebantur aqua consumebantur graviorique supplicio, qui non confestim emittebant spiritum, urebantur. Redactis quoque omnibus in favilla, cuncta maris operuit. Ferebant etiam multi, signa, quae superius nos vidisse octavo mense narravimus, quasi ardere caelum, ex huius incendii splendore fuisse.

## 25. De insola, in qua sanguis apparuit.

<a id="hist-08-la-025"></a>
[**[025]**](#hist-08-en-025) In alia vero insola, quae est proxima civitate Veneticae, erat stagnum validum piscibusque refertum, quod in unius ulnae altitudine conversum est in cruore; ita per dies multus congregata canum atque avium inaestimabilis multitudo, sanguinem hoc lambens, satiata redibat in vesperum.

## 26. De Berulfo, qui dux fuit.

<a id="hist-08-la-026"></a>
[**[026]**](#hist-08-en-026) Toronicis vero atque Pectavis Ennodius dux datus est. Berulfus autem, qui his civitatibus ante praefuerat, pro thesauris Syghiberti regis, quos clam abstulerat, cum Arnegysilo socio suspectus habetur. Qui cum hoc ducatum in supradictis urbibus expeterit, a Rauchingo duce, facto ingenio, cum satellite allegatur. Nec mora, missi ad domus eorum pueri expilant omnia; multa ibi de proprio, nonnulla de antedictis thesauris sunt reperta, quae omnia ad Childeberthum regem delata sunt. Cumque in hoc res ageretur, ut gladius cervicem decideret, interventu episcoporum obtenta vita laxati sunt, nihil tamen de his quae eis ablata fuerant recipientes.

## 27. Quod Desiderius ad regem abiit.

<a id="hist-08-la-027"></a>
[**[027]**](#hist-08-en-027) Desiderius vero dux cum aliquibus episcopis et Aridio abbate vel Antestio ad regem Gunthchramno properavit. Sed cum eum rex aegre vellit accipere, victus precibus sacerdotum, in gratia sua recepit. Tunc ibi Eulalius adfuit, quasi pro coniuge, quae eum spreverat et ad Desiderium transierat, causaturus; sed in ridiculo et humilitate redactus, siluit. Desiderius vero remuneratus a rege, cum gratia est reversus.

## 28. De Ermeneghildo et Ingundae.

<a id="hist-08-la-028"></a>
[**[028]**](#hist-08-en-028) Igitur, ut saepius diximus, Ingundis a viro cum imperatoris exercitu derelicta, dum ad ipsum principem cum filio parvolo duceretur, in Africa defuncta est et sepulta. Leuvichildus vero Herminichildum filium suum, quem antedicta mulier habuit, morti tradedit. Quibus de causis commotus Gunthchramnus rex, exercitum in Hispaniis distinat, scilicet ut prius Septimaniam, quae adhuc infra Galliarum terminum habetur, eius dominatione subderint et sic in antea proficiscerentur. Dum autem hic exercitus moveretur, indecolum cum nescio quibus hominibus rusticis est repertum. Quem et Gunthchramno rege legendum miserunt, hoc modo, quasi Leuvichildus ad Fredegundem scriberet, ut quocumque ingenio exercitum illuc ire prohiberet, dicens: 'Inimicos nostros, id est Childeberthum et matrem eius, velociter interemite et cum rege Gunthchramno pacem inite, quod praemiis multis coemite. Et si vobis minus est fortassis paecunia, nos clam mittimus, tantum ut quae petimus impleatis. Cum autem de inimicis nostris ulti fuerimus, tunc Amelio episcopo ac Leubae matronae bona tribuite, per quos missis nostris ad vos accedendi aditus reseratur'. Leuba enim est socrus Bladastis ducis.

## 29. Quod Fredegundis misit, qui Childeberthum interfecerint.

<a id="hist-08-la-029"></a>
[**[029]**](#hist-08-en-029) Et licet haec ad Gunthchramnum regem perlata et nepote suo Childebertho in notitiam data fuissent, tamen Fredegundis duos cultros ferreos fieri praecepit, quos etiam caraxari profundius et venino infici iussit, ut scilicet, si mortalis adsultus vitalis non dissolverit fibras, vel ipsa venini infectio vitam possit velocius extorquere. Quos cultros duobus clericis cum haec mandata tradedit, dicens: 'Accipite hos gladius et quantocius pergite ad Childeberthum regem, adsimilantes vos esse mendicos. Cumque pedibus eius fueritis strati, quasi stipem postulantes, latera eius utraque perfodite, ut tandem Brunichildis, quae ab illo adrogantiam sumit, eo cadente conruat mihique subdatur. Quod si tanta est costodia circa puerum, ut accedere nequeatis, vel ipsam interemite inimicam. Mercis quoque operis vestri haec erit, ut, si mortui in hoc opere fueritis, parentibus vestris bona tribuam, ipsosque muneribus ditans primus in regnum meum constituam. Interim vos timorem omnem omittite, nec sit trepidatio mortis in pectore. Noveritis enim, quod cunctos homines haec causa continet. Armate viriletate animus et considerate saepius fortes viros in bello conruere, unde nunc parentes eorum nobilis effecti opibus inmensis cunctis super eminent cunctisque praecellent'. Cumque haec mulier loqueretur, clerici tremere coeperunt, difficile potantes, haec iussa posse conplere. At illa dubius cernens, medificatus potione direxit, quo ire praecepit; statimque robor animorum adcrevit, promiseruntque se omnia quae praeceperat impleturus. Nihil minus vasculum ab haec potione repletum ipsos levare iubet, dicens: 'In die illa, cum haec quae praecipio facetis, mane, priusquam opus incipiatur, hunc potum sumite. Erit vobis magna constantia ad haec peragenda'. His ita instructis, demisit eos. Quibus pergentibus adque ad urbem Sessionas accedentibus, a Rauchingo duci capti discussique omnia reserant, et sic in carcere legati sunt. Post dies vero paucos Fredegundis, certa iam, quod fuissent impleta quae fuerant imperata, misit puerum inquerere, quid aut rumor populi ferret, aut si aliquem inveniret indicantem, qui diceret Childeberthum iam interemptum fuisse. Egressus igitur puer ab ea, Sessionas urbem venit. Audiens denique hos in carcerem retenere, ad ostium adpropinquat; sed cum loqui satellitibus coepisset, et ipse captus costodiae mancipatur. Tunc omnes simul ad Childeberthum regem directi sunt, discussisque, veritatem aperiunt, indicantes se a Fredegunde missus ad eum interemendum, dicentes: 'Iussa reginae suscipimus, ut nos egenus adsimilaremus. Cumque pedibus tuis provoluti aliquid stipendii quaereremus, ab his te gladiis transfodere voluemus. Quod si adsultu signiore gladius difixisset, ipsum venenum, quod ferrum erat infectum, animam velociter penetraret'. Haec his dicentibus, diversis suppliciisy adfecti, truncatis manibus auribusque et naribus, variis sunt mortibus interempti.

## 30. Quod exercitus in Septimaniam abiit.

<a id="hist-08-la-030"></a>
[**[030]**](#hist-08-en-030) Igitur Gunthchramnus rex cummoveri exercitum in Hispaniis praecepit, dicens: 'Prius Septimaniam proventiam ditioni nostrae subdite, quae Galliis est pro pinqua, quia indignum est, ut horrendorum Gothorum terminus usque in Galliis sit extensus'. Tunc commoto omni exercitu regni sui, illuc dirigit. Gentes vero, quae ultra Ararem Rhodanumque et Sequanam commanebant, cum Burgundionibus iunctae, Arareca Rhodaniticaque litora tam de fructibus quam de pecoribus valde depopulati sunt. Multa homicidia, incendia praedasquae in regione propria facientes, sed et aeclesias denudantes, clericos ipsos cum sacerdotibus ac reliquo populo ad ipsas sacratas Deo aras interementes, usque ad urbem Nemausus processerunt. Similiter et Byturigi, Sanctonici cum Petrocoricis, Ecolesenensibus vel reliquarum urbium populum, qui tunc ad antedicti regis imperio pertenebant, usque ad Carcasonam urbem devecti, similia mala gesserunt. Sed cum ad urbem accessissent, reseratis sponte ab habitatoribus portis, nullo resistente, ingressi, nescio quo cum Carcasonensibus scandalo commote, urbem egressi sunt. Tunc Terentiolus comes quondam urbis Lemovicinae, lapide de muro proiecto percussus, occubuit. Cuius caput truncatum ad vindictam adversariorum urbi delatum est. Ex hoc omnes populus timore perterritus, ad propria regredi destinans, universa reliquid, quae vel per viam coeperat vel quae secum adduxerat. Sed et Gothi per occultas insidias multis de his spoliatis interemerunt; exim in Tholosanorum manus incedentes, quibus, dum pergerent, multa intulerant mala, spoliati ac caesi vix propria contingere potuerunt. Hi vero, qui Nemausum adgressi fuerant, devastantes uni versa regiones, succensis domibus, incensis segitibus, decisis olivitis vinitisque succisis, nihil inclusis nocere potentes, ad alias urbis progressi sunt. Erant enim valde munitae et de cybis ac reliquis necessariis adplene refertae, et horum urbana depopulantes urbisque minus inrumpere valuerunt. Tunc et Nicetius dux, cum Arvernis in haec expeditione commotus, cum reliquis urbis adsedit. Sed cum minus valerit, ad castrum quoddam pervenit; dataque fide, sponte inclusi reserantes portas, ecs credoli tamquam pacificos susciperunt. Ille vero ingressi, postposito sacramento, praesidia cuncta diripiunt, animas in captivitate subdentes. Tunc, accepto consilio, unusquisque ad propria est regressus. Tantaque per viam scelera, homicidia, praedas, direptiones per regionem propriam gesserunt, ut ea usquequaque memorare perlongum sit. Verumtamen quia segetis Provinciae igni ab hisdem succensas diximus, fame atquae inaedia pereuntes per viam relinquebantur exanimes, nonnulli in fluminibus dimersi, plerique in seditionibus interempti sunt. Ferebant enim amplius quam quinque milia in his stragibus fuisse peremptos. Sed non ecs qui remanserant coercebat aliorum interitus. Tunc et Arvernae regiones aeclesiae, quae viae publicae propinquae fuerant, a ministeriis denudatae sunt, nec fuit terminus male faciendi, nisi cum ad propria singuli pervenerunt. Quibus reversis, magna Gunthchramno regi amaritudo cordis obsedit. Duces vero supradicti exercitus ad basilicam sancti Symphoriani martyris expetierunt. Veniente itaque rege ad eius solemnitatem, repraesentati sunt sub conditione audientiae in postmodum futurae. Post dies vero quattuor, coniunctis episcopis necnon et maioribus natu laicorum, duces discutere coepit, dicens: 'Qualiter nos hoc tempore victuriam obtenere possumus, quia ea quae patres nostri secuti sunt non costodimus? Illi vero aeclesias aedificantes, in Deum spem omnem ponentes, martyres honorantes, sacerdotes venerantes, victurias obtinuerunt gentesque adversas, divino opitulante adiutorio, in ense et parma saepius subdiderunt. Nos vero non solum Deum non metuemus, verum etiam sacra eius vastamus, ministros interficimus, ipsa quoque sanctorum pignera in ridiculo discerpimus ac vastamus. Non enim potest obtenere victuria, ubi talia perpetrantur; ideo manus nostrae sunt invalidae, ensis tepiscit, nec clepius nos, ut erat solitus, defendit ac protegit. Ergo si hoc meae culpae adscribitur, iam ea Deus capite meo restituat. Gerte si vos regalia iussa contemnetis et ea quae praecipio implere differtis, iam debet securis capiti vestro submergi. Erit enim documentum omni exercitu, cum unus de prioribus fuerit interfectus. Verumtamen iam experire debemus, quid agi oporteat. Si quis iustitiam sequi destinat, iam sequatur; si quis contemnit, iam ultio publica cervice eius inmineat. Satius est enim, ut parvi contumaces pereant, quam ira Dei super omnem regionem dependat innoxiam.' Haec rege dicente, responderunt duces: 'Bonitatis tuae magnanimitas, rex optime, enarrare facile non potest: qui timor tibi in Deum sit, qui amor in aeclesiis, quae reverentia in sacerdotibus, quae pietas in pauperibus, quaeve dispensatio in egenis. Sed quia omnia, quae gloria vestra profert, recta veraquae esse censentur, quid faciemus, quod populus omnes in vitio est dilapsus omnique homine agere quae sunt iniqua delectat? Nullus regem metuit, nullus ducem, nullus comitem reveritur; et si fortassis alicui ista displicent et ea pro longaevitate vitae vestrae emendare conatur, statim seditio in populo, statim tumultus exoritur. Et in tantum unusquisque contra seniorem saeva intentione crassatur, ut vix credat evadere, si tardius silire nequiverit'. Ad haec rex ait: 'Si quis sequitur iustitiam, vivat; si quis legem mandatumque nostrum respuit, iam pereat, ne nus diutius hoc blasphemeum prosequatur'. Haec eo dicente, advenit nuntius, dicens: 'Richaredus, filius Leuvichildi, de Hispaniis egressus, Caput Arietis castrum obtenuit et ex pago Tholosano maximam partem depopulatus est hominesque captivos abduxit. Ugernum Arelatense castrum inrupit resque cunctas cum hominibus abstullit et sic se infra murus Nemausensis urbis inclusit'. Haec audiens rex, Leudeghyselum in loco Calomniosi cognomento Aegylanis ducem delegens, omnem ei provintiam Arelatensim commisit, costodisque per terminus super quattuor virorum milia collocavit. Sed et Nicetius Arvernorum dux similiter cum costodibus perrexit et finis regiones ambivit.

## 31. De interfectione Praetextati episcopi.

<a id="hist-08-la-031"></a>
[**[031]**](#hist-08-en-031) Dum haec agerentur et Fredegundis apud Rothomagensim urbem commoraretur, verba amaritudinis cum Praetextato pontifice habuit, dicens, venturum esse tempus, quando exilia, in qua detentus fuerat, reviseret. Et ille: 'Ego semper et in exilio et extra exilium episcopus fui, sum et ero; nam tu non semper regalem potentiam perfrueres. Nos ab exilio provehimur, tribuente Deo, in regnum; tu vero ab hoc regno demergeris in abyssum. Rectius enim erat tibi, ut, relecta stultitia adque malitia, iam te ad meliora converteris et ab hac iactantia, qua semper ferves, abstraheris, ut et tu vitam adipisceris aeternam et parvolum, quem genuisti, perducere ad legitimam possis aetatem'. Haec effatus, cum verba illius mulier graviter acciperit, se a conspectu eius felle fervens abstraxit. Advenientem autem dominicae resurrectionis diae, cum sacerdos ad implenda aeclesiastica officia ad aeclesiam maturius properasset, antefanas iuxta consuetudinem incipere per ordinem coepit. Cumque inter psallendum formolae decumberet, crudelis adfuit homicida, qui episcopum super formolam quiescentem, extracto baltei cultro, sub ascella percutit. Ille vero vocem emittens, ut clerici qui aderant adiuvarent, nullius ope de tantis adstantibus est adiutus. At ille plenas sanguine manus super altarium extendens, orationem fundens et Deo gratias agens, in cubiculo suo inter manus fidelium deportatus et in suo lectulo collocatus est. Statimque Fredegundis cum Beppoleno duce et Ansovaldo adfuit, dicens: 'Non oportuerat haec nobis ac reliquae plebi tuae, o sancte sacerdos, ut ista tuo cultui evenirent. Sed utinam indicaretur, qui talia ausus est perpetrare, ut digna pro hoc scelere supplicia susteneret'. Sciens autem ea sacerdos haec dolose proferre, ait: 'Et quis haec fecit nisi his, qui leges interemit, qui saepius sanguinem innocentem effudit, qui diversa in hoc regno mala commisit?' Respondit mulier: 'Sunt aput nos peritissimi medici, qui hunc vulnere medere possint. Permitte, ut accedant ad te'. Et ille: 'Iam', inquid, 'me Deus praecepit de hoc mundum vocare. Nam tu, qui his sceleribus princeps inventa es, eris maledicta in saeculo, et erit Deus ultui sanguinis mei de capite tuo'. Cumque illa discederit, pontifex, ordinata domo sua, spiritum exalavit. Ad quem sepeliendum Romacharius Constantinae urbis episcopus advenit. Magnus tunc omnes Rothomagensis cives et praesertim seniores loci illius Francos meror obsedit. Ex quibus unus senior ad Fredegundem veniens ait: 'Multa enim mala in hoc saeculo perpetrasti, sed adhuc peius non feceras, quam ut sacerdotem Dei iuberis interfici. Sit Deus ultur sanguinis innocentes velociter. Nam et omnes nos erimus inquisitores mali huius, ut tibi diutius non liceat tam crudelia exercere'. Cum autem haec dicens discederet a conspectu reginae, misit illa qui eum ad convivium provocaret. Quo renuente, rogat, ut, si convivium eius uti non vellit, saltim vel poculum auriat, ne ieiunus a regale domo discedat. Quo expectante, accepto poculo, bibit absentium cum vino et melle mixtum, ut mos barbarorum habet; sed hoc potum venenum inbutum erat. Statim autem ut bibit, sensit pectorem suum dolorem validum inminere, et quasi se incideretur intrinsecus, exclamat suis, dicens: 'Fugite, o miseri, fugite malum hoc, ne mecum pariter periamini'. Illis quoque non bibentibus, sed festinantibus abire, hic protinus excaecatus, ascensoque aequo, in tertio ab hoc loco stadio caecidit et mortuus est. Post haec Leudovaldus episcopus epistolas per omnes sacerdotes direxit et, accepto consilio, aeclesias Rothomagensis clausit, ut in his populus solemnia divina non expectaret, donec indagatione communi repperiretur huius auctur sceleris. Sed et aliquos adpraehendit, quibus supplicio subditus veritatem extorsit, qualiter per consilium Fredegundis haec acta fuerat; sed et, ea defensante, ulciscere non potuit. Ferebant etiam, ad ipsum percussores venisse, pro eo quod haec inquirere sagaciter distinaret; sed costodia vallatus suorum, nihil ei nocere potuerunt. Itaque cum haec ad Gunthchramnum regem perlata fuissent et crimen super mulierem iaceretur, misit tres episcopus ad filium, qui esse dicitur Chilperici, quem superius Chlothacharium scripsimus vocitatum, id est Artemium Synonicum, Veranum Cavellionensim et Agricium Tricassinum, ut scilicet cum his, qui parvolum nutriebant, perquirerint huius sceleris personam et in conspectu eius exhiberent. Quod cum sacerdotes locuti fuissent, responderunt seniores: 'Nobis prorsus haec facta displicent, et magis ac magis ea cupimus ulciscere. Nam non potest fieri, ut, si quis inter nos culpabilis invenitur, in conspectu regis vestri deducatur, cum nos possimus nostrorum facinora regale sanctione conpraemere'. Tunc sacerdotes dixerunt: 'Noveritis enim, quia, si persona, quae haec perpetravit, in medio posita non fuerit, rex noster cum exercitu hic veniens, omnem hanc regionem gladio incendioque vastavit, quia manifestum est, hanc interfecisse gladio episcopum, qui maleficiis Francum iussit interemi'. Et his dictis, discesserunt, nullum rationabilem responsum accipientes, obtestantes omnino, ut numquam in aeclesia illa Melantius, qui prius in loco Praetextati subrogatus fuerat, sacerdotes fungeretur officium.

## 32. De interitu Domnolae, uxoris Nectari.

<a id="hist-08-la-032"></a>
[**[032]**](#hist-08-en-032) Multa enim mala hoc tempore gesta sunt. Nam Domnola, relicta quondam Burgulini, quae fuit filia Victuri Redonensis episcopi, quam Nectarius matrimonio copulaverat, intentione de vineis cum Boboleno, referendario Fredegundis, habebat. Audiens autem ea in his vineis advenisse, misit nuntius obtestantes, ne ingredi penitus in hac possessione praesumeret. Quod illa dispiciens et res patris sui fuisse proclamans, ingressa est. Tunc ille, commota seditione, super eam cum armatis viris inruit. Qua interfecta, vineas vindecavit resque deripuit et tam viros quam mulieres qui cum ea erant interfecit gladio, nec remansit ex his, nisi qui fuga labi potuit.

## 33. De incendio urbis Parisiacae.

<a id="hist-08-la-033"></a>
[**[033]**](#hist-08-en-033) Extetit igitur in his diebus apud urbem Parisiacam mulier, quae dicerit incolis: 'Fugite, o! ab urbe et scitote eam incendio concremandam'. Quae cum a multis inrideretur, quod haec aut sortium praesagio diceret aut vana aliqua somniasset aut certe daemonii meridiani haec instinctu proferret, respondit: 'Nequaquam est ita, ut dicitis; nam in veritate loquor, quia vidi per somnium a basilica sancti Vincenti veniente virum inluminatum, tenente manu caereum et domus negutiantum ex ordine succendentem'. Denique post tertiam noctem, quod haec mulier est effata, inchoante crepusculo, quidam e civibus, accenso lumine, in prumptuario est ingressus, adsumptoque olec hac ceteris quae necessaria erant, abscessit, lumine secus cupella olei derelicto. Erat enim domus haec prima secus portam, quae ad mediam diem pandit egressum. Ex quo lumine adpraehensa domus incendio concrematur, de qua et aliae adpraehendi coeperunt. Tunc deruente igne super vinctus carceris, apparuit eis beatus Germanus, et comminuens trabem atque catenis, quibus vincti tenebantur, reserato carceris osteo, vinctos abire permisit incolomis. Ille vero egressi, se ad basilicam sancti Vincenti, in qua sepulchrum habetur beati antestitis, contulerunt. Igitur cum per totam civitatem huc adque illuc flante vento flamma ferritur totisque viribus regnaret incendium, adpropinquare ad aliam portam coepit, in qua beati Martini oraturium habebatur, qui ob hoc aliquando factum fuerat, eo quod ibi lepram maculosi hominis osculo depulisset. Vir autem, qui eum intextis virgultis in sublime construxerat, confisus in Domino nec de beati Martini virtute diffisus, se resquae suas infra eius parietis ambivit, dicens: 'Credo enim et fides mea est, quod repellat ab hoc loco incendium, qui saepius incendiis imperavit et in hoc loco leprosi hominis cutem, osculu medente, purgavit'. Adpropinquante enim illuc incendium, ferebantur validi globi flammarum, qui percutientes parietem oraturii, protinus tepiscebant. Clamabat autem populus virc ac muliere: 'Fugite, miseri, ut evadere possitis. Ecce iam igneum pondus super vos diruit, ecce favillae incendii cum carbonibus tamquam validus imber ad vos usque distenditur! Egredimini ab oraturio, ne cum eodem incendio concremimini'. At illi orationem fundentes, numquam ab his vocibus movebantur. Sed nec mulier se umquam a fenestra, per quam interdum flammae ingrediebantur, amovit, quae erat spe firmissima de virtute beati antestitis praemunita. Tantaque fuit virtus beati pontificis, ut non solum hoc oraturium cum alumni proprii domo salvaret, verum etiam nec aliis domibus, qui in circuitu erant, nocere flammis dominantibus permisisset. Ibique cecidit incendium, quod ab una parte pontes coeperat desaevire. Ab alia vero parte tam valide cuncta conflagravit, ut amnis finem inponeret. Verumtamen aeclesiae cum domibus suis non sunt adustae. Agebant enim, hanc urbem quasi consecratam fuisse antiquitus, ut non ibi incendium praevaleret, non serpens, non gliris apparuisset. Nuper autem, cum cuniculum pontis emundaretur et coenum, de quo repletum fuerat, auferretur, serpentem gliremque aereum repperierunt. Quibus ablatis, et glires ibi deinceps extra numerum et serpentes apparuerunt, et postea incendia perferre coepit.

## 34. De reclausis temptatis.

<a id="hist-08-la-034"></a>
[**[034]**](#hist-08-en-034) Et quia princeps tenebrarum mille habet artes nocendi, quid de reclausis ac Deo devotis nuper gestum fuerit, pandam. Vennocus Britto praesbiterii honore praeditus, cui in alio libro meminimus, tantae se abstinentiae dedicavit, ut indumentum de pellibus tantum uteretur, cybum de herbis agrestibus incoctis sumeret, vinum vero tantum vas ad os poneret, quod magis putaretur libare osculo quam haurire. Sed cum eidem devotorum largitas frequenter exhiberet vasa hoc plena licore, dedicit, quod peius est, extra modum haurire et in tantum dissolvi potione, ut plerumque ebrius cerneretur. Unde factum est, ut, invalescente temulentia, tempore procidente, a daemonio correptus, per inergiam vexaretur, in tantum ut, accepto cultro vel quodcumque genus teli sive lapidem aut fustem potuisset adrepere, post homines insano furore discurreret. Unde necessitas exigit, ut catenis vinctus costodiretur in cellula. In hac quoque damnatione per duorum annorum spatia debachans, spiritum exalavit. Alius quoque Anatholius Burdegalensis puer, ut ferunt, annorum duodecem, cum esset famulus cuiusdam negutiatoris, petiit sibi ad reclausionem licentia tribui. Sed, resistente diu domino, potans, eum in hoc tepiscere adque implere non posse in hac aetate, quod nitebatur adpetere, tandem, victus praecibus famoli, facultatem tribuit, ut id quod flagitabat impleret. Erat autem ibi cripta ab antiquis transvolutum eleganteque opere exposita, in cuius angulo erat cellula parva de quadratis lapidibus clausa, in qua vix unus stans homo recipi possit. In hac cellola puer ingreditur, in hac per octo aut eo amplius annos commoratus, tenui cybo potuque contentus, vigiliis orationibusquae vacabat. Post haec pavore validum perpessus, clamare coepit, intrinsecus se torqueri. Unde factum est, ut, adiuvante ei, ut credo, diabolicae partis militia, amotis quadris quibus conclusus tenebatur, eliderit parietem in terram, conlidens palmas et clamans, se a sanctis Dei peruri. Cumque diutissime in hac insania teneretur et sancti Martini crebrius confiteretur nomen ac diceret, se potius ab eo quam a sanctis aliis cruciare, Thoronus adducitui. Sed malus spiritus, credo, ob virtutem adque magnitudinem sancti conpraessus, nequaquam hominem mutelavit. Nam in loco ipso per anni curriculum degens, cum nihil male pateretur, regressus est, sed rursus quae caruerat incurrit.

## 35. De legatis Hispanorum.

<a id="hist-08-la-035"></a>
[**[035]**](#hist-08-en-035) Legati de Hispaniis ad regem Guntchramnum venerunt cum multis muneribus, pacem petentes, sed nihil certi accipiunt in responsis. Nam anno praeterito, cum exercitus Septimaneam debellasset, navis, quae de Galleis in Galliciam abierant, ex iusso Leuvieldi regis vastatae sunt, res ablatae, hominis caesi atque interfecti, nonnulli captivi abducti sunt. Ex quibus pauci quodadmodo scafis erepti, patriae quae acta fuerant nontiaverunt.

## 36. De interitu Magnovaldi.

<a id="hist-08-la-036"></a>
[**[036]**](#hist-08-en-036) Igitur aput Childebertum regem Magnovaldus causis occultis ex iussu regis interficitur hoc modo. Stante infra Mettensis urbis palatium rege et ludum spectante, qualiter animal caterva canum circumdatum fatigabatur, Magnovaldus arcessitur. Quo veniente et nesciente quae actura erant, cum reliquis dissolutus risc, prospicere pecudem coepit. At his cui iussum fuerat, cum viderit eum spectaculum intentum, librata secure caput eius inlisit. Qui cecidit et mortuus est ac, per fenestram domus proiectus, a suis sepultus est; resque eius protinus direptae, aerario publico, in quantum repertum est, sunt inlatae. Autumabant tamen quidam, eo quod post mortem fratris diversis plagis coniugem affectam interfecisset et uxorem fratris adscisset toro, extetisse causam, qua interimeretur.

## 37. Quod Childebertho filius natus est.

<a id="hist-08-la-037"></a>
[**[037]**](#hist-08-en-037) Post haec Childebertho regi filius natus est, qui a Magnerico Treverorum episcopo de sacro fonte susceptus, Theodoberthus est vocitatus. De quo tantum gaudium Gunthchramnus rex habuit, ut statim legatus dirigens, multa ei munera transmitteret, dicens: 'Per hunc enim Deus eregere Francorum regnum propria maiestates suae pietate dignabitur, si huic pater aut ipse viverit patri'.

## 38. Quod Hispani in Galliis prorupuerunt.

<a id="hist-08-la-038"></a>
[**[038]**](#hist-08-en-038) Anno denique XI. regni Childebertni regis legati iterum de Hispaniis venerunt, pacem petentes, sed nihil certi obtenentes, regressi sunt. Richaredus autem, filius Leuvichilde, usque Narbonam venit et infra terminum Galliarum praedas egit et clam regressus est.

## 39. De obito episcoporum.

<a id="hist-08-la-039"></a>
[**[039]**](#hist-08-en-039) Eo anno multi episcoporum obierunt; Badegysilus vero Cenomannorum episcopus, vir valde saevus in populo, auferens sive deripiens iniuste res diversorum. Ad cuius animum acervum adque inmitem coniux aceesserat saevior, quae illum in committendis sceleribus nequissimis consilii stimulis perurguebat. Nec praeteribat dies aut momentum ullum, in quo non aut in spoliis civium aut in diversis altercationibus crassaretur. Cotidiae autem cum iudicibus causas discutere, militias saeculares exercere, saevire in alios, alios caedibus agere non cessabat, manibus etiam propriis verberare, proterire multus ac dicere: 'Non ideo, quia clericus factus sum, et ultur iniuriarum mearum non ero?' Sed quid dicam de ceteris, cum nec ipsis quoque germanis parceret, sed ipsos magis expoliarit? Cum quo numquam iustitiam de rebus paternis maternisve adsequi potuerunt. Quinto autem anno episcopatus sui expleto, cum iam sextum ingrediens aepulum civibus cum inmensa laetitia praeparasset, a febre correptus, annum quem coeperat protinus, morte inminente, finivit. In cuius loco Berthramnus Parisiacus archidiaconus subrogatus est. Qui multis altercationibus cum relicta illius defuncti habuisse probatur, eo quod res, quae tempore Badegyseli episcopi aeclesiae datae fuerant, tamquam proprias retenebat, dicens: 'Militia haec fuit viri mei'. Et licet invita, tamen cuncta restituit. Erat enim ineffabili malitia. Nam saepius viris omnia pudenda cum ipsis ventris pellibus incidit, feminis secriciora corporis loca lamminis candentibus perussit et multa alia inique gessit, quae tacere melius potavi. Obiit et Sabaudus Arelatensis episcopus; in cuius loco Licerius regis Guntchramni refrendarius est adscitus. Gravis tunc Provinciam ipsam lues debellata est. Obiit et Euantius Viennensis episcopus; in cuius sede Virus presbiter de senatoribus, rege elegente, substituetur. Multique eo anno sacerdotum ex hoc mundo migrati sunt, quod praeterire volui?, eo quod unusquisque in urbe sua sui reliquerit monimenta.

## 40. De Pelagio Toronico.

<a id="hist-08-la-040"></a>
[**[040]**](#hist-08-en-040) Fuit autem et in urbe Thoronica Pelagius quidam, in omni malitia exercitatus, nullum iudicem metuens, pro eo quod iumentorum fiscalium costodes sub eius potestate consisterent. Ob hoc furta, superventa, pervasiones, caedes diversaque scelera tam in fluminibus quam in terris agere non cessabat. Nam plerumque arcessitum et minacibus lenibusque verbis, ut ab hac malitia desisteret, prohibere volui; sed magis odia quam aliquod fructum iustitiae ab eo recepi iuxta illud Salamoneacae Sapientiae proverbium: Argue stultum, adiciet odire te. Nam tantum in me odium miser habebat, ut saepius, spoliatis caesisque hominibus sanctae aeclesiae, exanimes reliquerit, causasque, qualiter aeclesiae vel basilicae sancti Martini damna intenderit, inquirens. Unde factum est, ut quadam vice venientibus hominibus nostris adque echinum in vasis deferentibus caederet, protereret ipsaque vasa dereperit. Quod factum cum conperissem, eum a commonione suspendi, non quasi ultur iniuriae meae, sed ut facilius eum ab hac insania redderem emendatum. At ille, electis duodecem viris, ut hoc scelus periuraret, advenit. Sed cum ego nullum vellim sacramentum suscipere, conpulsus ab eo vel a civibus nostris, amotis reliquis, ipsius tantum iuramentum suscipi, iussique eum recipi in commonione. Erat autem eo tempore mensis primus. Adveniente autem mense quinto, quo prata secare solent, pratum sanctimunialium, qui terminum prati sui adhaerebat, pervadit. In quo statim ut falcem misit, febre correptus, diae tertia spiritum exalavit. Disposuerat enim sibi sepulchrum in basilica sancti Martini vici Condatensis, quod detectum sui effractum in frustra repperierunt. Sic postea in porticum ipsius basilicae est sepultus. Vasa quoque echini, qua periuraverat, post obitum illius ab eius prumtuario sunt delata. Manifesta est autem virtus beatae Mariae, in cuius basilicam miser sacramentum protullit mendax.

## 41. De his qui Praetextatum episcopum interfecerunt.

<a id="hist-08-la-041"></a>
[**[041]**](#hist-08-en-041) Cum autem per totam terram sonus illi percurrerit, Praetextatum episcopum a Fredegunde fuisse interfectum, illa quoque, quo facilius detergeretur a crimine, adpraehensum puerum caedi iussit vehementer, dicens: 'Tu hoc blasphemium super me intulisti, ut Praetextatum urbis Rothomagensis episcopum gladio adpeteris'. Tradedit eum nepoti ipsius sacerdotis. Qui cum eum in supplicio posuisset, omnem rem evidenter aperuit dixitque: 'A regina enim Fredegunde centum solidus accepi, ut hoc facerem, a Melantio vero episcopo quinquaginta et ab archediacono civitates alios quinquaginta; insuper et promissum habui, ut ingenuus fierem, sicut et uxor mea'. In hac voce illius evaginatum homo ille gladium praedictum reum in frustra concidit. Fredegundis vero Melantium, quem prius episcopum posuerat, aeclesiae instituit.

## 42. Quod Bippolinus dux datus est.

<a id="hist-08-la-042"></a>
[**[042]**](#hist-08-en-042) Per quam cum Beppolenus dux valde fatigaretur nec iuxta personam suam ei honor debetus inpenderetur, cernens se dispici, ad Gunthchramno regem abiit. A quo accepta potestate ducatus super civitates illas, quae ad Chlotharium, Chilperici regis filium, pertinebant, cum magna potentia pergit, sed a Rhedonicis non est receptus. Andecavus vero veniens, multa mala ibidem gessit, ita ut annonas, faenum, vinum vel quicquid repperire potuisset in domibus civium, ad quas accesserat, nec expectatis clavibus, disruptis osteis, devastaret; multusque de habitatoribus loci caedibus adfecit protrivitque. Domigysilo quoque metum intulit, sed pacificatus est cum eo. Accedens autem ad urbem, dum epularetur cum diversis in tristico, subito effractum pulpitum domus, vix semivivus evasit, multis debilitatis, in eisdem tamen malis perdurans, quae prius gesserat. Multa tunc et Fredegundis in regno filii sui de rebus eius evertit. Ipse quoque ad Rhedonicus rediens et eos regi Guntchramno subdere cupiens, filium suum in hoc loco reliquit. Qui non multum intercedente tempus, inruentibus Rhedonicis, interemptus est cum multis honoratis viris. Hoc anno multa signa aparuerunt; nam mense septimo arbores visi sunt floruisse, sed et multae, quae prius poma habuerant, nova dederunt, quae usque natalis dominici tempore in ipsis arboribus habitae sunt. Fulgor per caelum in modum serpentes cueurrisse visus est.

## 43. Quod Nicetius rector Provinciae ordinatur.

<a id="hist-08-la-043"></a>
[**[043]**](#hist-08-en-043) Anno quoque duodecimo Childeberthi regis Nicetius Arvernus rector Massiliensis provinciae vel reliquarum urbium, quae in illis partibus ad regnum regis ipsius pertinebant, est ordinatus. Antestius vero in Andecavo a rege Gunthchramno dirigitur, multis ibidem damnis adfligens eos, qui in morte Domnolae, uxoris Nectarii, mixti fuerunt. Resque Boboleni, eo quod fuerit huius caput sceleris, in fisco redactis, Namnetas accessit ac lacessire Nonnichium episcopum coepit, dicens: 'Quia filius tuus in hoc facinus est admixtus, ut dignas pro cummissas suis poenas luat, meritum exigit'. Sed puer conscientia accusante territus, ad Chlotharium, filium Chilperici, aufugit. Antestius vero, acceptis fideiussoribus ab episcopo, ut in praesentia regis adesset, Santonas venit. Sonus autem his diebus exierat, quod Fredegundis occultus in Hispaniis nuntius dirigerit, eosdemque a Palladio urbis Santonicae episcopo clam susceptus et inantea transmissus fuisse. Erant autem eo tempore dies quadragensimae sanctae, et episcopus in insola maris orationis causa secesserat. Secundum consuetudinem autem, dum ad dominicae caenae festa ad aeclesiam suam, populo expectante, rediret, ab Antestio in via vallatur. Qui, nec discussa rei veritatem, dicebat: 'Non ingredieris urbem, sed exilio condemnaberis, quia suscepisti nuntius inimicae domini nostri regis'. At ille: 'Nescio', ait,'quid loquaris. Tamen quia dies sancti inminent, accedamus ad urbem, decursisque solemnitatum sanctarum festis, postea quaecumque volueris obpone, rationem a me accipies, quia quod reputas nihil est'. At ille: 'Nequaquam', inquid, 'sed non adtingis limina aeclesiae tuae, quia infidelis apparuisti domino nostro rege'. Quid plura? Tenetur in via episcopus, domus aeclesiae discribitur, resque deripiuntur. Cives cum homine obtenere non queunt, ut saltim vel celebrata solemnitate paschali discutiatur. Hisque supplicantibus et illo rennuente, tandem patefacit vulnus, qui latebat in pectore. 'Si', inquid, 'domum, quam infra territurii Biturigi termino habere dinuscitur, meae ditione, facta vinditione, subdedirit, quae flagitatis facio; alioquin non effugiet manus meas, nisi trudatur exilio'. Metuit negare episcopus; scripsit subscripsitque ac tradedit agrum, et sic, datis fideiussoribus de praesentia sua ante regem, in civitate ingredi permissus est. Transactis igitur diebus sanctis, ad regem pergit. Adfuit et Antestius, sed nihil de his quae opponebat episcopo potuit adprobare. Episcopus autem ad urbem redire iubetur et usque ad synodum futurum dilatatur, si forte aliqua de his quae opponebantur evidentius possit agnesci. Adfuit et Nonnichius episcopus, qui, datis multis muneribus, abscessit.

## 44. De eo qui regem Guntchramnum interficere voluit.

<a id="hist-08-la-044"></a>
[**[044]**](#hist-08-en-044) Fredegundis vero quasi ex nomine filii sui legatos ad Gunthchramnum r̅egem diregit. Qui, reserata petitione, accepto responso, vale dicentes abscedunt; sed, nescio quibus causis, paulolum apud metatum suum remorati sunt. Mane autem facto, procedente regem ad matutinus ac praeeunte cereo, visus est homo quasi ebrius in angulo oraturii dormitare, accinctus gladio, cuius asta pariete sustentabatur. Hoc viso, rex exclamavit, dicens, non esse simpliciter, hominem sub hoc horrore noctis tali in loco quiescere. Oppraessus autem et loris revinctus, interrogabatur, quid sibi haec vellint, quae agerit. Nec mora supplicio subditus, dicit se a legatis, qui advenerant, emissum fuisse, ut rex deberet interfici. Denique adpraehensi legati Fredegundis, nihil de his quae interrogati sunt confitentur, dicentes: 'Nos nihil ad aliud missos, nisi legationem, quam suggessimus, deferremus'. Tunc hominem illum diversis plagis adfectum et carcere mancipatum, legatus per diversa loca exilio condempnare praecepit. Manifestissime enim patuit, sub hoc dolo a Fredegunde fuisse directus, ut regem interficere deberent, quod misericordia Domini non permisit. Inter quos Baddo senior habebatur.

## 45. De interitu Desiderii ducis.

<a id="hist-08-la-045"></a>
[**[045]**](#hist-08-en-045) Cum autem legati de Hispaniis crebro ad regem Gunthchramnum venerint et nullius pacis gratiam obtenere potuissent, sed magis inimicitia pullularet, rex Gunthchramnus Albigensim urbem nepote suo Childebertho reddedit. Quod cernens Desiderius dux, qui maxime in eiusdem urbis territurio meliora facultatis suae condiderat, timens, ne ultio expetiretur ab eo propter antiquam inimicitiam, quod aliquando in eadem civitatem exercitum gloriosae memoriae Syghiberthi regis graviter adfecisset, cum Tetradia uxore sua, quam Eulalio nunc Arverno comite abstullerat, in termino Tholcsano cum rebus omnibus transiens, exercitum cummovet et contra Gothos abire disponit, divisis prius, ut ferunt, rebus inter filius et coniugem. Adsumptoque secum Austrovaldo comite, Carcasonam petit. Praeparaverant enim se, hoc audito, urbis illius cives, quasi resistere volentes; audierant autem de his prius. Denique inito bello, fugire Gothi coeperunt et Desiderius cum Austrovaldo a tergo cedere hostem. Illis quoque fugientibus, hic cum paucis ad urbem accessit. Lassati enim fuerant equites scciorum. Tunc ad portam urbis accedens, vallatus a civibus, qui infra murus erant, interfectus est cum his omnibus, qui eum fuerant prosecuti, ita ut vix pauci exinde quodadmodo evaderent, qui rem, ut gesta fuerat, nuntiarent. Austrovaldus vero audiens Desiderium mortuum, de via regressus, ad regem perrexit; qui mox in eius lccum dux statuetur.

## 46. De obito Leuvichilde regis.

<a id="hist-08-la-046"></a>
[**[046]**](#hist-08-en-046) Post haec Leuvigildus rex Hispanorum aegrotare coepit, sed, ut quidam adserunt, paenitentiam pro errore heretico agens et obtestans, ne huic heresi quisquam repperiretur consentaneus, in legem catholicam transiit, ac per septem dies in fletu perdurans pro his quae contra Deum iniquae molitus est, spiritum exalavit.
