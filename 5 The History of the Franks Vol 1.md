# BOOK FIVE

## LIBER QUINTUS

### Preface

<a id="hist-05-en-001"></a>
[**[001]**](#hist-05-la-001) I am weary of recording the diversity of civil wars which violently crush the Frankish people and kingdom. Worse still, I now see in it the beginning of that time of sorrows which the Lord foretold: “Father shall rise against son, son against father, brother against brother, neighbor against neighbor.” The examples of earlier kings ought to have terrified them, for as soon as they were divided they were killed by enemies. How often has that city of cities and head of the whole world, Rome, been destroyed by immense civil wars, and when they ceased, risen again as though from the ground! May you too, O kings, be engaged in battles such as those in which your parents labored, so that the nations, terrified by your peace, may admire your strength! Remember what Clovis, the head of your victories, did: he killed opposing kings, crushed harmful nations, and subjugated countries whose kingdom he left to you entire and unharmed. Yet when he did this, he had neither gold nor silver such as is now in your treasuries. What will you do? What do you seek? In what do you not abound? Riches overflow in your houses; wine, grain, and oil abound in your storehouses; gold and silver are heaped in your treasuries. One thing is lacking to you: because you do not have peace, you lack God’s grace. Why does one take another’s property? Why does another covet what is not his? Beware, I beg, that saying of the apostle: “If you bite and devour one another, take care lest you be consumed by one another.” Examine diligently the writings of the ancients, and you will see what civil wars bring forth. Read what Orosius writes of the Carthaginians: after saying that their city and region were overthrown after seven hundred years, he added: “What preserved it so long? Concord. What destroyed it after so long a time? Discord.” Beware discord; beware the civil wars which assail you and your people. What else is to be expected except that, when your army has fallen, you yourselves will be left without support and immediately fall, oppressed by hostile peoples? If civil war delights you, O king, carry on the war which the apostle records as taking place within man: let the spirit contend against the flesh, and let vices fall before virtues. Freed from your former bondage to the root of evils, serve your head—that is, Christ.

### 1. Of the kingdom of Childebert the younger and of his mother

<a id="hist-05-en-002"></a>
[**[002]**](#hist-05-la-002) After King Sigibert was killed at the estate of Vitry, Queen Brunhilda remained in Paris with her sons. When the deed was reported to her, she was overwhelmed by grief and mourning and did not know what to do. Duke Gundovald secretly seized her little son Childebert and carried him away, rescuing him from imminent death. He gathered the peoples over whom Childebert’s father had ruled and made the boy king when he had barely completed his fifth year. He began to reign on the day of the Lord’s Nativity. In the first year of his reign King Chilperic came to Paris. He seized Brunhilda and sent her into exile at the city of Rouen, and he took the treasures she had brought to Paris. He ordered her daughters to be kept in the city of Meaux. Roccolen then came to Tours with the men of Le Mans, plundered, and committed many crimes. I shall relate later how he was struck by blessed Martin’s power for these evils and perished.

### 2. How Merovech took Brunhilda

<a id="hist-05-en-003"></a>
[**[003]**](#hist-05-la-003) Chilperic sent his son Merovech with an army to Poitiers. Merovech abandoned his father’s instructions, came to Tours, and kept the holy days of Easter there. His army greatly devastated that region. Pretending that he wished to visit his mother, he went to Rouen. There he joined Queen Brunhilda and married her. When Chilperic heard that his son had married his uncle’s wife contrary to divine law and the canons, he was greatly embittered and hastened to the city mentioned above. Merovech and Brunhilda learned that he intended to separate them and took refuge in the basilica of Saint Martin, built of wooden planks above the city walls. The king arrived and tried by many devices to draw them out. They believed he was acting deceitfully and did not trust him. He swore to them: “If it were God’s will, he himself would not try to separate them.” They accepted this oath and left the basilica. He kissed them, received them honorably, and feasted with them. A few days later the king took Merovech with him and returned to Soissons.

### 3. The war against Chilperic, and of the malice of Rauching

<a id="hist-05-en-004"></a>
[**[004]**](#hist-05-la-004) While Chilperic and Merovech were staying there, men gathered from Champagne and attacked the city of Soissons. They put Queen Fredegund and Chilperic’s son Chlodovech to flight and wished to bring the city under their own control. When King Chilperic learned of this, he marched there with an army. He first sent messengers asking them not to injure him and thereby bring destruction upon both armies. They ignored him and prepared for battle. When battle was joined, Chilperic’s side prevailed and routed its opponents, striking down many brave and capable men. He put the remainder to flight and entered Soissons.

After these events the king began to suspect his son Merovech because of his marriage to Brunhilda, saying that the battle had arisen through Merovech’s wickedness. He stripped him of his weapons, assigned guards to him, and ordered him to be kept in free custody while he considered what to do with him.

Godinus, who had gone over from Sigibert’s side to Chilperic and had been enriched by him with many gifts, had instigated this war. Defeated in the field, he was the first to flee. The king took back the estates in the territory of Soissons that he had granted Godinus from the treasury and gave them to the basilica of blessed Medard. Godinus himself died suddenly not long afterward.

Rauching married Godinus’s widow. He was full of every kind of vanity, swollen with pride, and insolent in arrogance. He treated his subordinates as though he possessed no humanity. Raging against his own people beyond all measure of human malice and folly, he committed unspeakable evils. If a servant held a candle before him while he feasted, as was customary, Rauching had the servant’s shins bared and the candle pressed against them until its light went out. When it was relit, he did the same until the servant’s entire shins were burned. If the servant cried out or tried to move, a naked sword immediately threatened him; while the servant wept, Rauching exulted with great delight.

They say that at that time two of his servants, a young man and a girl, loved each other, as often happens. Their love had lasted two years or more when they went together to a church. When Rauching learned of it, he went to the local priest and demanded that his servants be returned to him immediately. The priest said: “You know what reverence is owed to God’s churches. You cannot take them unless you guarantee the permanence of their union and promise that they will remain free from all bodily punishment.” Rauching remained silent in doubtful thought for a long time. At last he turned to the priest, placed his hands on the altar, and swore: “I will never separate them. Rather, I will make them remain in this union. Although I am annoyed that this was done without my knowledge and consent, I gladly accept that he has not taken another man’s servant woman and she has not taken another master’s slave.” The priest trusted the crafty man’s promise without suspicion and returned the servants to him.

Rauching received them, thanked the priest, and went home. He immediately ordered a tree to be felled, its trunk split with wedges, and the wood hollowed out. He had a pit dug three or four feet deep and ordered the hollowed trunk placed in it. He laid the girl inside as though dead, had the young man thrown on top of her, replaced the cover, filled the pit with earth, and buried them alive. He said: “I have not broken my oath: they will never be separated.” When this was reported to the priest, he ran there quickly. Rebuking Rauching, he barely obtained permission to have them uncovered. He drew the young man out alive but found the girl suffocated.

Rauching was exceedingly wicked in such deeds and took pleasure only in mockery, deceit, and every perverse practice. His departure from life was therefore not undeserved; I shall describe it later. Siggo the referendary, who had held King Sigibert’s ring and had been induced by King Chilperic to retain the office he had held under Chilperic’s brother, abandoned Chilperic and went over to Sigibert’s son King Childebert. Ansovald took possession of the property Siggo had held in the territory of Soissons. Many others who had gone from Sigibert’s kingdom to Chilperic also withdrew. Siggo’s wife died not long afterward, and he married another.

### 4. How Roccolenus came to Tours

<a id="hist-05-en-005"></a>
[**[005]**](#hist-05-la-005) In those days Roccolen, sent by Chilperic, came to Tours with great boasting. He pitched camp beyond the Loire and sent messengers to us demanding that we drag Duke Guntram out of the holy basilica. Guntram was then accused of Theudebert’s death. If we refused, Roccolen said he would order the city and all its suburbs to be burned. On hearing this, we sent envoys to tell him that what he demanded had never been done in ancient times and that even now the holy basilica could not be allowed to be violated. If it were violated, neither he nor the king who had issued the command would prosper. He ought rather to fear the bishop’s holiness, whose power had straightened a paralytic’s limbs only the day before.

Roccolen feared none of this. While residing in a church house beyond the Loire, he cut apart the door that had been nailed shut. The men of Le Mans who had come with him filled bellows with the nails and carried them away. They overturned the grain stores and devastated everything. While Roccolen was doing this, God struck him. He turned yellow with jaundice, but he renewed his harsh demands: “Unless you throw Duke Guntram out of the basilica today, I will destroy all the vegetation around the city so thoroughly that the place will be fit only for the plough.”

The holy day of Epiphany arrived, and his torment grew worse and worse. Taking counsel with his men, he crossed the river and entered the city. When the psalm-singers came out of the church and hastened to the holy basilica, he followed the cross on horseback, with banners going before him. But as soon as he entered the holy basilica, the fury of his threats cooled. He returned to the church house and could take no food that day. Seriously ill, he went from there to Poitiers. It was the holy season of Lent, during which he often ate young rabbits. He made arrangements to afflict or condemn the citizens of Poitiers on the kalends of March, but on the previous day he gave up his soul. Thus his pride and swelling ceased.

### 5. Of the bishops of Langres

<a id="hist-05-en-006"></a>
[**[006]**](#hist-05-la-006) At that time Felix, bishop of Nantes, wrote me a letter full of reproaches. He even wrote that my brother had been killed because, desiring the episcopate, he had murdered a bishop. Felix wrote this because he coveted an estate belonging to the church. When I refused to give it to him, he vomited a thousand furious insults against me, as I have said. I once answered him: “Remember the prophet’s words: ‘Woe to those who join house to house and couple field to field! Shall they alone inhabit the earth?’ If only Marseille had you as its bishop! Its ships would never carry oil or other merchandise, but only paper, so that you might have more opportunity to write and defame good people. But poverty of paper puts an end to verbosity.” His greed and boasting were immense. But I will set these matters aside so as not to resemble him, and will explain how my brother departed from this life and how swiftly the Lord avenged his murderer.

When blessed Tetricus, bishop of the church of Langres, was growing old, he deposed the deacon Lampadius, to whom he was in debt. My brother wished to help the poor whom Tetricus had wrongfully despoiled and consented to Lampadius’s humiliation, thereby incurring Lampadius’s hatred. Meanwhile blessed Tetricus suffered a hemorrhage. No physicians’ remedies helped him. The clergy were distressed and felt deprived of their shepherd, so they sought Munderic. With the king’s consent he was tonsured and ordained bishop on condition that, while blessed Tetricus lived, he should govern the fortress of Tonnerre as archpriest and reside there, succeeding Tetricus when he died.

While living in that fortress Munderic incurred the king’s anger. He was accused of having supplied provisions and gifts to King Sigibert when he came against his brother Guntram. He was therefore taken from the fortress and confined in exile on the bank of the Rhône in a narrow, roofless tower. He remained there in great hardship for nearly two years. Through Bishop Saint Nicetius’s intercession he returned to Lyon and lived with Nicetius for two months. Unable to obtain the king’s permission to return to the place from which he had been expelled, he escaped by night and crossed into King Sigibert’s kingdom. He was made bishop at the village of Arisitum, with about fifteen districts under him. The Goths had formerly held these; Bishop Dalmatius of Rodez now judges them.

After Munderic’s departure the people of Langres again asked for a bishop and chose Silvester, a kinsman either of ours or of blessed Tetricus. They did this at my brother’s instigation. When blessed Tetricus died, Silvester was tonsured and ordained priest, receiving full authority over the church’s property. He prepared to travel to Lyon to receive episcopal consecration. While these arrangements were being made, he was seized by epilepsy, from which he had long suffered. He lost his senses and roared continuously for two days, breathing out his spirit on the third.

After these events Lampadius, deprived of honor and property as described above, joined Silvester’s son in hatred of the deacon Peter. He plotted with him and assured him that his father had been killed by Peter’s sorcery. Young and foolish, Silvester’s son was aroused against Peter and publicly accused him of parricide. When Peter heard this, an assembly was held before Saint Nicetius, bishop and my mother’s uncle, and Peter was taken to Lyon. There, in the presence of Bishop Siagrius, many other bishops, and secular nobles, he cleared himself by oath, declaring that he had no part in Silvester’s death.

Two years later Silvester’s son, again incited by Lampadius, overtook Deacon Peter on the road, wounded him with a lance, and killed him. Peter’s body was taken from that place to the fortress of Dijon and buried beside Saint Gregory, our great-grandfather. His killer fled to King Chilperic, leaving his property to be confiscated by King Guntram’s treasury. He wandered from place to place because of his crime and had no secure dwelling. At last, as I believe, innocent blood cried out against him to divine power. While traveling, he drew his sword and killed an innocent man. The man’s relatives and kinsmen grieved over his death, rose up, drew their swords, cut the murderer to pieces, and scattered his limbs. By God’s just judgment the wretch received such an end: he who had killed an innocent kinsman did not long survive his own guilt. This happened in the third year.

After Silvester’s death the people of Langres again asked for a bishop and received Pappolus, formerly archdeacon of Autun. They say he committed many injustices, which I pass over so as not to appear a detractor of my brothers. But I will not omit his end. In the eighth year of his episcopate, while visiting the districts and estates of the church, he saw blessed Tetricus in his sleep one night with a threatening expression. Tetricus said: “What are you doing here, Pappolus? Why do you pollute my see? Why do you invade the church? Why do you scatter the sheep entrusted to me? Leave this place, abandon the see, and depart far from this region.” As he spoke, he drove the staff he held into Pappolus’s chest with a violent blow. Pappolus awoke and wondered what it meant. A swelling formed in that place, and he suffered extreme pain. He loathed food and drink and awaited his imminent death. On the third day he vomited blood and expired. His body was carried away and buried at Langres.

Abbot Mummolus, surnamed Bonus, was appointed bishop in his place. Many praise him highly: he is chaste, sober, moderate, most prompt in every good work, a lover of justice, and devoted to charity with all his heart. After becoming bishop, he learned that Lampadius had fraudulently taken much church property and had assembled fields, vineyards, and slaves from plunder taken from the poor. He stripped Lampadius of everything and ordered him driven from his presence. Lampadius now lives in extreme poverty and earns his bread with his own hands. Let this suffice on these matters.

### 6. Of Leunastus the archdeacon of Bourges

<a id="hist-05-en-007"></a>
[**[007]**](#hist-05-la-007) In the year mentioned above—the year in which Sigibert died and his son Childebert began to reign—many miracles appeared at blessed Martin’s tomb. I recorded them in the little books I attempted to compose about these miracles. Although my language is rustic, I have not allowed what I myself saw or learned from trustworthy reports to remain hidden. Here I will describe only what befell those who negligently sought earthly remedies after receiving heavenly power. His power is revealed both in gracious healings and in the chastisement of fools.

Leunast, archdeacon of Bourges, lost his eyesight as cataracts developed. He consulted many physicians but recovered no vision at all. He came to the basilica of blessed Martin and remained there for two or three months, fasting continually and begging to recover his sight. When the feast day arrived, his eyes cleared and he began to see. He returned home and summoned a certain Jew, who applied cupping glasses to his shoulders to increase his eyesight. After blood was drawn, Leunast fell back into his former blindness. He returned to the holy church and remained there again for a long time, but he was not found worthy to recover his sight.

I believe that it was withheld because of his sin, according to the Lord’s saying: “To him who has, more will be given, and he will abound; but from him who has not, even what he has will be taken away”; and again: “Behold, you are healed; sin no more, lest something worse happen to you.” He would have remained healthy if he had not placed the Jew above divine power. The apostle warns and reproves such people: “Do not bear the yoke with unbelievers. What partnership has righteousness with iniquity? What fellowship has light with darkness? What communion has Christ with Belial? What share has a believer with an unbeliever? What agreement has the temple of God with idols? For you are the temple of the living God. Therefore come out from among them and be separate from them, says the Lord.” Let this case teach every Christian not to seek earthly remedies after being found worthy to receive heavenly medicine.

### 7. Of Senoch the recluse

<a id="hist-05-en-008"></a>
[**[008]**](#hist-05-la-008) I also wish to commemorate the men whom the Lord called from our land this year and what kind of men they were. I consider him great and acceptable to God who has placed such men from our land in his paradise. Blessed Senoch, a priest who lived at Tours, departed from the world in this way. He was a Thuringian by birth. Having become a cleric at Tours, he withdrew into a cell that he had constructed between ancient walls. He gathered monks and restored an oratory that had long lain in ruins. He performed many miracles for the sick, which we have recorded in the book of his Life.

### 8. Of the holy Germanus, bishop of Paris

<a id="hist-05-en-009"></a>
[**[009]**](#hist-05-la-009) That year blessed Germanus, bishop of Paris, also died. Among the many miracles he had performed while alive, this miracle at his funeral confirmed them. When the prisoners cried out, his body became heavy in the street; when they were released, it was lifted again without effort. Those who had been released followed his funeral freely as far as the basilica in which he was buried. Believers experience many miracles at his tomb through the Lord’s gift, so that anyone who asks justly quickly receives what he desires. Whoever wishes to investigate diligently the miracles he performed while alive will find them all by reading the book of his Life composed by the priest Fortunatus.

### 9. Of Caluppa the Recluse

<a id="hist-05-en-010"></a>
[**[010]**](#hist-05-la-010) In the same year Caluppa the recluse also died. He had been religious from infancy. He entered monastic life at the monastery of Méallet in the Auvergne territory and showed great humility toward the brothers, as we wrote in the book of his Life.

### 10. Of Patroclus the recluse

<a id="hist-05-en-011"></a>
[**[011]**](#hist-05-la-011) In the territory of Bourges there was a recluse named Patroclus who held the honor of the priesthood. He was wonderfully holy and religious and a man of great abstinence, often afflicted by various discomforts from fasting. He drank no wine, strong drink, or anything intoxicating except water lightly sweetened with honey. He ate no relish. His food was bread soaked in water and sprinkled with salt. His eyes never grew dim. He was constant in prayer; if he paused from it briefly, he read or wrote. Through prayer he often brought remedies to those suffering from chills, pustules, and other diseases. He performed many other signs that would take too long to recount in order. He always wore a hair shirt against his pure body. Departing from the world at eighty years of age, he went to Christ. We have also written a little book about his Life.

### 11. Of the Jews converted by Avitus the bishop

<a id="hist-05-en-012"></a>
[**[012]**](#hist-05-la-012) Because our God always deigns to glorify his bishops, I will relate what happened concerning the Jews at Clermont this year. Blessed Bishop Avitus frequently admonished them to lay aside the veil of the Mosaic law, understand the readings spiritually, and contemplate with pure hearts in the Holy Scriptures Christ, the Son of the living God, promised by prophetic and legal authority. But there remained over their hearts not merely the veil that covered Moses’s face, I may now say, but a wall. The bishop prayed that they would turn to the Lord and that the veil of the letter would be torn away from them.

One of them asked to be baptized at holy Easter. Born again to God through the sacrament of baptism, he proceeded in white with the others clothed in white. As the people entered the city gate, one of the Jews, instigated by the devil, poured foul-smelling oil over the convert’s head. The whole people were horrified and wanted to stone him, but the bishop would not permit it. On the blessed day when the Lord ascended gloriously to heaven after redeeming humanity, the bishop was proceeding with psalmody from the church to the basilica. The whole multitude following him rushed upon the Jews’ synagogue and tore it down from its foundations, leaving the site as level as an open field.

On another day the bishop sent envoys to the Jews, saying: “I do not compel you by force to confess the Son of God, but I preach and place the salt of knowledge in your hearts. I am a shepherd set over the Lord’s sheep. The true Shepherd who suffered for us said of you that he has other sheep not of this fold whom he must bring, so that there may be one flock and one shepherd. If you wish to believe as I do, become one flock under my guardianship; otherwise, depart from this place.” After long agitation and doubt, on the third day they reached agreement, through the bishop’s intercession as I believe, and sent him this reply: “We believe in Jesus, the Son of the living God, promised to us by the voices of the prophets. We ask to be washed in baptism so that we may no longer remain in this error.”

The bishop rejoiced at the message. After celebrating vigils on the holy night of Pentecost, he went out to the baptistery beyond the walls. The whole multitude prostrated itself before him and begged for baptism. Weeping for joy, he washed them all with water, anointed them with chrism, and gathered them into the bosom of mother church. Candles blazed and lamps shone; the whole city was white with the white-clad flock. The city’s joy was no less than that which Jerusalem was once found worthy to witness when the Holy Spirit descended upon the apostles. More than five hundred were baptized. Those who refused baptism left the city and went to Marseille.

### 12. Of Brachio the Abbot

<a id="hist-05-en-013"></a>
[**[013]**](#hist-05-la-013) After these events Brachio, abbot of the monastery of Menat, also died. He was Thuringian by birth and formerly practiced hunting in the service of Duke Sigivald, as we have written elsewhere.

### 13. How Mummolus wasted Limousin

<a id="hist-05-en-014"></a>
[**[014]**](#hist-05-la-014) To return to our subject, King Chilperic sent his son Chlodovech to Tours. Chlodovech assembled an army, passed through the territory between Tours and Angers, advanced as far as Saintes, and seized it. King Guntram’s patrician Mummolus crossed into the Limousin with a great army and fought Chilperic’s duke Desiderius. In that battle five thousand of Mummolus’s army fell and twenty-four thousand of Desiderius’s. Desiderius himself barely escaped by flight. Patrician Mummolus returned through the Auvergne, and his army devastated parts of it. He then passed into Burgundy.

### 14. How the tonsured Merovech took refuge

<a id="hist-05-en-015"></a>
[**[015]**](#hist-05-la-015) After this Merovech, while held in custody by his father, was tonsured. His clothing was changed to that worn by clerics, he was ordained priest, and he was sent to the monastery of Le Mans called Anisola to be instructed there in the priestly rule. When Guntram Boso, who was then residing in the basilica of Saint Martin as we have said, heard this, he secretly sent Subdeacon Riculf to advise Merovech to seek the basilica of Saint Martin. Merovech set out, and his servant Gailen arrived from another direction. Since his escort was small, Gailen rescued him from it on the journey. Covering his head and putting on secular clothing, Merovech sought the church of blessed Martin. While we were celebrating Mass, he found the doors of the holy basilica open and entered. After Mass he asked us to give him the blessed bread. Bishop Ragnemod of Paris, Saint Germanus’s successor, was then with us. When we refused, Merovech began to cry out that we were wrong to suspend him from communion without the consent of his brothers. With the consent of the brother bishop present and after he had formally invoked the canonical case, he received the blessed bread from us. I feared that, by suspending one man from communion, I might become the murderer of many. He had threatened to kill some of our people if he did not receive communion with us. The region of Tours suffered many disasters because of this affair.

In those days Nicetius, my niece’s husband, had a case of his own and went to King Chilperic with my deacon, who was to report Merovech’s flight to the king. When Queen Fredegund saw them, she said: “They are spies. They have come to discover what the king is doing so that they will know what to report to Merovech.” She immediately ordered them stripped and confined in exile; they were released in the seventh month.

Chilperic then sent messengers to us: “Drive that apostate out of the basilica, or I will burn that whole region with fire.” We wrote back that what had not been done in the time of the heretics could not now be done in a Christian age. He called out his army and sent it there. In the second year of King Childebert’s reign, Merovech saw that his father was intent on this course. Taking Duke Guntram with him, he considered going to Brunhilda, saying: “Far be it from me that the basilica of lord Martin should suffer violence on my account or that his region should be taken captive because of me.” He entered the basilica to keep vigil and placed the property he had with him at blessed Martin’s tomb. He prayed that the saint would help him and grant him favor so that he could obtain the kingdom.

Count Leudast had long set many traps for Guntram because of Fredegund’s love. Finally he treacherously surrounded and killed with the sword the servants Guntram had sent out into the country. He wished to kill Guntram himself if he could find a favorable opportunity. Guntram, following his own counsel and desiring revenge, seized the chief physician Marileif as he returned from the king’s presence. He had him severely beaten and took his gold, silver, and other possessions, leaving him naked. He would have killed him if Marileif had not escaped from the hands of those beating him and fled into a church. We later clothed him, spared his life, and sent him back to Poitiers.

Merovech spoke of many crimes committed by his father and stepmother. Although some of them were true, I believe that God was not pleased to have them made public by his son, as I later learned. One day I attended his banquet. While we sat together, he humbly asked me to read something for the instruction of his soul. I opened the book of Solomon and took the first verse that met my eye: “The eye that looks askance at a father, the ravens of the valleys shall pluck it out.” Merovech did not understand, but I recognized that this verse had been provided by the Lord.

Guntram then sent a servant to a woman he had known since King Charibert’s time who had a spirit of divination, asking her to foretell what would happen. He also asserted that she had previously predicted to him not only the year but the day and hour of King Charibert’s death. She sent back this answer through the servants: “King Chilperic will perish this year, and Merovech will take the whole kingdom, excluding his brothers. You will hold the dukedom of his entire kingdom for five years. In the sixth year, with the people’s favor, you will obtain the grace of the episcopate in one of the cities situated on the right bank of the Loire. You will depart from this world old and full of days.” When the returning servants reported this to their master, he was immediately carried away by vanity. As though he already sat in the episcopal chair of the church of Tours, he brought these words to me. Laughing at his folly, I said: “Such things must be asked of God; the devil’s promises should not be believed. He has been a liar from the beginning and has never stood in the truth.” Guntram departed in confusion, and I laughed heartily at a man who thought such things credible.

One night, after celebrating vigils in the holy bishop’s basilica, I lay down and fell asleep. I saw an angel flying through the air. As he passed over the holy basilica, he said in a loud voice: “Alas! Alas! God has struck Chilperic and all his sons. None of those who came from his loins will survive to rule his kingdom forever.” At that time Chilperic had four sons by different wives, besides his daughters. When these things were later fulfilled, I knew clearly that the soothsayers’ promises were false.

While Merovech and Guntram were staying at the basilica of Saint Martin, Queen Fredegund, who was secretly protecting Guntram Boso concerning Theudebert’s death, sent him a message: “If you can drive Merovech out of the basilica so that he can be killed, you will receive a great gift from me.” Thinking that assassins were ready, Guntram said to Merovech: “Why do we sit here as though sluggish and afraid, hiding around the basilica like dullards? Let our horses be brought. Let us take hawks and dogs, enjoy hunting, and delight in open spectacles.” He did this craftily to draw Merovech away from the holy basilica. Guntram was otherwise a good enough man, but he was excessively ready to commit perjury. He never gave an oath to any friend without promptly abandoning it. They left the basilica as we have described and went to the house at Jocundiacum near the city, but no one harmed Merovech.

Because Guntram was then accused of Theudebert’s death, as we have said, King Chilperic sent a written letter to Saint Martin’s tomb. It contained a request that blessed Martin write back whether Guntram could be extracted from his basilica or not. Deacon Baudegisel, who presented the letter, placed a blank sheet with it on the holy tomb. He waited three days and, receiving no written answer, returned to Chilperic. Chilperic sent other men to exact an oath from Guntram that he would not leave the basilica without the king’s knowledge. Guntram swore conditionally and gave the altar cloth as surety that he would never leave without the king’s command.

Merovech did not trust the pythoness. He placed three books on the holy tomb—the Psalter, the book of Kings, and the Gospels. Watching through the whole night, he begged the blessed confessor to show him what would happen and whether he could obtain the kingdom, so that he might know by the Lord’s indication. He then continued for three days in fasting, vigils, and prayers. Returning to the blessed tomb, he opened the book of Kings. The first verse on the page he opened was: “Because you have forsaken the Lord your God and walked after foreign gods and have not done right before his sight, the Lord your God has delivered you into the hands of your enemies.” In the Psalter he found: “Because of their deceitfulness you have brought evils upon them; you cast them down when they were lifted up. How they are brought to desolation! They have suddenly failed and perished because of their iniquities.” In the Gospels he found: “You know that after two days Passover will take place, and the Son of Man will be delivered up to be crucified.” Confused by these answers, Merovech wept for a long time at the blessed bishop’s tomb. Taking Duke Guntram with him, he departed with five hundred or more men.

After leaving the holy basilica, while traveling through the territory of Auxerre, he was captured by Erpo, King Guntram’s duke. While Erpo held him, Merovech escaped by some chance and entered the basilica of Saint Germanus. When King Guntram heard this, he was enraged. He fined Erpo seven hundred gold pieces and removed him from office, saying: “As my brother says, you detained his enemy. If you intended to do that, you ought first to have brought him to me. Otherwise you ought not to have touched the man you were pretending not to hold.”

King Chilperic’s army advanced as far as Tours, plundered, burned, and devastated that region. It did not spare Saint Martin’s property, but seized whatever it touched without any regard or fear of God. Merovech remained at the basilica just mentioned for nearly two months, then fled and reached Queen Brunhilda. The Austrasians did not receive him. His father called out an army against the people of Champagne, thinking that Merovech was hiding there, but he did no harm to them and could not find him.

### 15. Of the war between the Saxons and the Suebi

<a id="hist-05-en-016"></a>
[**[016]**](#hist-05-la-016) When Alboin entered Italy, Chlothar and Sigibert had settled the Suebi and other peoples in that region. Those who returned in Sigibert’s time—that is, those who had been with Alboin—rose against the Suebi, wishing to drive them out of the region and destroy them. The Suebi offered them a third of the land, saying: “We can live together without conflict.” The returning Saxons were angry because they had formerly held the land themselves and refused to make peace. The Suebi then offered half, and afterward two-thirds, retaining only a third for themselves. When the Saxons still refused, they offered all their cattle as well as the land if only the Saxons would refrain from war. The Saxons would not agree and sought battle.

Before the battle they discussed how they would divide the Suebi’s wives and who would take which woman after their husbands’ destruction, considering the Suebi already as good as dead. But the Lord’s mercy, which executes justice, turned their intention in another direction. When battle was joined, there were twenty-six thousand Saxons, of whom twenty thousand fell; there were six thousand Suebi, of whom only four hundred and eighty were struck down. The remainder won the victory. The surviving Saxons swore that none of them would cut his beard or hair until he had avenged himself on his adversaries. When they fought again, they fell in still greater destruction, and so the war came to an end.

### 16. Of the death of Macliavus

<a id="hist-05-en-017"></a>
[**[017]**](#hist-05-la-017) These events took place in Brittany. Macliavus and Bodicus, counts of the Bretons, had once sworn to each other that whichever survived would defend the other’s son as his own. When Bodicus died, he left a son named Theuderic. Forgetting his oath, Macliavus expelled Theuderic from his country and took his father’s kingdom. Theuderic was a fugitive and wanderer for a long time. At last God took pity on him. He gathered men from Brittany, attacked Macliavus, and killed him and his son Jacob with the sword. He restored to his own control the part of the kingdom his father had once held, while Macliavus’s son Waroch claimed the other part.

### 17. Of the doubt concerning Easter

<a id="hist-05-en-018"></a>
[**[018]**](#hist-05-la-018) King Guntram put two sons of the late Magnachar to the sword because they had uttered many detestable and execrable things against Queen Austregild and her offspring. He confiscated their property for his treasury. He himself was struck by sudden illness and lost his two sons. He grieved deeply at their deaths because he remained bereft of children.

That year there was uncertainty about Easter. In Gaul we and many cities celebrated holy Easter on the fourteenth day before the kalends of May. Others kept the solemnity with the Spaniards on the twelfth day before the kalends of April. Yet they say that the springs in Spain that are filled by God’s will were filled at the date of our Easter. At Chinon, a village in the territory of Tours, while Masses were being celebrated on the glorious day of the Lord’s Resurrection, the church shook. Terrified, the people cried out with one voice that the church was falling. They all fled, even breaking down the doors. A great plague subsequently devastated the people.

King Guntram then sent envoys to his nephew Childebert, seeking peace and asking to meet him. Childebert came to him with his nobles. They met at the bridge called the Stone Bridge, greeted each other, and exchanged kisses. King Guntram said: “Because of my sins I have been left without children. I therefore ask that this nephew of mine be my son.” Placing Childebert on his own chair, he handed over his whole kingdom to him, saying: “Let one shield protect us and one spear defend us. If I have sons, I will nevertheless count you as one of them, so that the affection I promise you today with God as witness may endure among you and them.” Childebert’s nobles made the same promise on his behalf. They ate and drank together, honored each other with fitting gifts, and departed in peace.

They sent an embassy to King Chilperic demanding that he restore what he had taken from their kingdoms; if he delayed, he should prepare a battlefield. Chilperic treated this demand with contempt. He ordered circuses to be built at Soissons and Paris and offered spectacles in them to the people.

### 18. Of the bishop Praetextatus and the death of Merovech

<a id="hist-05-en-019"></a>
[**[019]**](#hist-05-la-019) After these events, Chilperic heard that Praetextatus, bishop of Rouen, was giving gifts to the people contrary to the king’s interests, and ordered him to be summoned. When the bishop had been examined, Chilperic discovered that Queen Brunhilda’s property had been entrusted to him. He seized it and ordered Praetextatus to be kept in exile until an episcopal hearing. A council was then assembled and the bishop was brought before it. The bishops who had gathered were in the basilica of Saint Peter the Apostle at Paris. The king said to him, “What were you thinking, bishop, when you united my enemy Merovech, who ought to have been my son, with his aunt—that is, with his uncle’s wife? Were you unaware of what the decrees of the canons have ordained in such a case? You are proved to have transgressed not only in this, but also to have plotted with him and given gifts so that I might be killed. You made a son his father’s enemy; you seduced the common people with money, so that none would keep the faith they owed me; and you wished to deliver my kingdom into another’s hands.” As he spoke, the multitude of Franks began to roar and wanted to break down the doors of the basilica, intending to drag the bishop out and stone him, but the king forbade it.

When Bishop Praetextatus denied doing what the king had alleged, false witnesses came forward displaying various valuables and saying, “You gave us these and those things so that we would pledge our loyalty to Merovech.” He answered, “You are telling the truth when you say that I have often given you gifts, but my purpose was not to expel the king from his kingdom. Since you used to present me with mules, excellent horses, and other things, what else could I do but reward you in like manner?”

When the king had returned to his quarters, we gathered and sat in the sacristy of the basilica of blessed Peter. While we were talking, Aetius, archdeacon of the church of Paris, suddenly entered, greeted us, and said, “Listen to me, priests of the Lord assembled here. Either you will exalt your name at this time and shine with the glory of a good reputation, or no one henceforth will regard you as priests of God, if you do not vigorously defend your own cause or permit your brother to be destroyed.” None of the priests answered him, for they feared the fury of the queen, at whose instigation these things were being done. As they sat silently with their fingers pressed to their lips, I said, “I beg you, most holy priests of God, to attend to my words, especially you who appear to be on familiar terms with the king. Give him holy and priestly counsel, lest by raging against a servant of God he perish under God’s wrath and lose both kingdom and glory.” At these words all remained silent. I continued, “Remember, my lord priests, the prophetic saying: ‘If the watchman sees a man’s iniquity and does not speak, he will be answerable for the soul that perishes.’ Therefore do not be silent, but preach and set the king’s sins before his eyes, lest some evil befall him and you be held accountable for his soul. Or do you not know what happened recently? How Chlodomer seized Sigismund and cast him into prison, and Avitus, God’s priest, told him, ‘Do not lay a hand on him, and when you march into Burgundy you will gain the victory.’ But Chlodomer rejected the priest’s words, went away and killed Sigismund with his wife and children, and then invaded Burgundy, where he was overwhelmed by the army and killed. And what of the Emperor Maximus? When he compelled blessed Martin to share communion with a certain murderous bishop, and Martin yielded to the impious king in order more easily to free men condemned to death, the judgment of the eternal King followed: Maximus was driven from the empire and condemned to a most wretched death.” No one answered me; they all sat in dumb amazement. Two flatterers among them—lamentable though it is to say this of bishops—reported to the king that he had no greater enemy to his cause than me.

Immediately one of the courtiers was sent at a run to bring me before him. When I arrived, the king was standing beside a shelter made of branches, with Bishop Berthram on his right and Ragnemod on his left. Before them was a stool covered with bread and various dishes. When the king saw me, he said, “Bishop, you ought to dispense justice to everyone, yet I receive no justice from you. As I see it, you consent to iniquity, and the proverb is fulfilled in you: one crow does not peck out another crow’s eye.” I replied, “If one of us wishes to stray from the path of justice, O king, you can correct him; but if you transgress, who will rebuke you? We speak to you, and if you are willing, you listen; but if you refuse, who will condemn you except him who declared himself to be justice?” Incensed against me by his flatterers, he answered, “I have found justice with everyone, but with you I cannot find it. Yet I know what to do to disgrace you before the people and make everyone perceive that you are unjust. I will summon the people of Tours and tell them, ‘Cry out against Gregory that he is unjust and renders justice to no one.’ When they raise this cry, I will answer, ‘If I, who am king, cannot obtain justice from him, how will you, who are lesser men, obtain it?’” I replied, “You do not know whether I am unjust. He to whom the secrets of the heart are laid bare knows my conscience. As for the people’s false clamor at your instigation, it means nothing, because everyone knows that you prompted it. In that acclamation it is not I but you who will be disgraced. But why say more? You have the law and the canons. You ought to examine them diligently, and if you fail to observe their commands, know that God’s judgment hangs over you.”

Then, as though soothing me—and acting deceitfully, he thought I did not understand—he turned to the broth set before him and said, “I prepared this broth for you. It contains nothing but poultry and a few chickpeas.” Recognizing his flattery, I said, “Our food ought to be doing God’s will, not delighting in such delicacies, so that we never neglect his commands. You who accuse others of injustice, promise first that you will not disregard the law and the canons; then we will believe that you are pursuing justice.” He stretched out his right hand and swore by almighty God that he would in no way neglect what the law and the canons taught. Then I took bread, drank some wine, and departed.

That night, after the nocturnal hymns had been sung, I heard heavy blows being struck on the door of my lodging. I sent a servant and learned that messengers from Queen Fredegund were standing there. When they were admitted, I received the queen’s greeting. The servants then begged me not to oppose her cause and promised two hundred pounds of silver if Praetextatus were crushed despite my resistance. “We already have the promise of all the bishops,” they said. “Only do not proceed against us.” I answered, “Even if you gave me a thousand pounds of gold and silver, what else could I do except what the Lord commands? I promise only this: I will follow whatever the others agree upon in accordance with the decrees of the canons.” Not understanding what I meant, they thanked me and left. In the morning some bishops came to me with a similar message, and I gave them a similar answer.

When we assembled in the basilica of Saint Peter, the king arrived in the morning and said, “The authority of the canons has ordained that a bishop caught in theft be removed from the episcopal office.” We asked who the bishop was against whom the charge of theft was brought. The king replied, “You have seen the valuables that he stole from us.” Three days earlier he had shown us two bundles filled with valuables and various ornaments, valued at more than three thousand solidi, as well as a small purse containing about two thousand gold pieces. He claimed that the bishop had stolen these things from him.

Praetextatus answered, “I believe you remember that when Queen Brunhilda left the city of Rouen, I came to you and told you that I held five packages of her property in trust. Her servants kept coming to me to recover them, but I was unwilling to return them without your advice. You, O king, said to me, ‘Get these things out of your hands and let the woman’s property return to her, lest enmity arise between me and my nephew Childebert because of them.’ When I returned to the city, I handed one bundle to the servants, for they could not carry more. They came back and demanded the others. Again I sought your magnificence’s advice, and you ordered me, saying, ‘Get rid of them, bishop, get rid of them, lest this matter cause scandal.’ I handed two more over to them, but the remaining two stayed with me. Why do you now slander me and accuse me of theft, when this matter should be regarded as custody, not theft?”

The king replied, “If this deposit was in your hands for safekeeping, why did you open one of the bundles, cut into pieces an embroidered border woven with gold thread, and give them to men who were to drive me from the kingdom?” Bishop Praetextatus answered, “I have already told you that I had accepted gifts from them. Since I had nothing at hand to give in return, I took these things and gave them reciprocal gifts. What belonged to Merovech, my son, whom I received from the font of regeneration, I regarded as my own.”

Seeing that he could not overcome Praetextatus with these calumnies, King Chilperic, greatly astonished and troubled in conscience, left us, summoned some of his flatterers, and said, “I confess that I have been defeated by the bishop’s words, and I know that what he says is true. What am I to do now so that the queen’s will concerning him may be fulfilled?” He then told them, “Go to him and speak as though offering advice on your own initiative: ‘You know that King Chilperic is pious and tenderhearted and quickly moved to mercy. Humble yourself before him and say that you committed the acts of which he accuses you. Then we will all prostrate ourselves at his feet and obtain pardon for you.’” Deceived by these words, Bishop Praetextatus promised to do so.

In the morning we assembled at the customary place. The king arrived and said to the bishop, “If you gave these men gifts in return for gifts, why did you demand oaths that they would keep faith with Merovech?” The bishop answered, “I confess that I sought their friendship for him. I would have summoned not only a man but, if it had been lawful, an angel from heaven to be his helper, for, as I have often said, he was my spiritual son through baptism.” As the altercation grew more intense, Bishop Praetextatus prostrated himself on the ground and said, “I have sinned against heaven and before you, most merciful king. I am a vile murderer; I wished to kill you and set your son upon your throne.” At these words the king prostrated himself at the feet of the bishops, saying, “Hear, most pious priests, this guilty man confessing an abominable crime.” Weeping, we raised the king from the ground. He ordered Praetextatus to leave the basilica and withdrew to his own quarters.

He then sent a book of canons containing a newly attached quaternion with what purported to be apostolic canons, including this: “A bishop convicted of murder, adultery, or perjury shall be deposed from the priesthood.” When these had been read and Praetextatus stood dumbfounded, Bishop Berthram said, “Hear, brother and fellow bishop: since you do not possess the king’s favor, you cannot enjoy our affection either until you merit the king’s indulgence.” After this the king demanded that either Praetextatus’s tunic be torn, or the hundred and eighth psalm, which contains the curses against Judas Iscariot, be recited over his head, or at least that judgment be written against him so that he might never again receive communion. I resisted these demands in accordance with the king’s promise that nothing would be done outside the canons.

Praetextatus was then taken from our sight and placed under guard. When he tried to escape from custody at night, he was severely beaten and driven into exile on an island in the sea near the city of Coutances.

After this a report spread that Merovech was again attempting to seek sanctuary in Saint Martin’s basilica. Chilperic ordered the basilica to be guarded and all its entrances closed. The guards left one door open, through which a few clergy could enter for the services, and kept the other doors shut, which caused much distress to the people.

While we were staying at Paris, signs appeared in the sky: twenty rays in the north, rising in the east and hastening toward the west. One was longer and another stood out above the rest; when it had risen high, it soon faded, and then the others that had followed disappeared. I believe they foretold Merovech’s death.

While Merovech was hiding in the Champagne region of Rheims and did not trust himself openly to the Austrasians, he was surrounded by the men of Tarabanna. They said that if he came to them, they would abandon his father Chilperic and submit to him. He quickly took his strongest men and went to them. Revealing the snares they had prepared, they confined him in a certain villa, surrounded him with armed men, and sent messengers to his father. On hearing this, Chilperic resolved to hasten there. Merovech, held in a small lodging and fearing that he would suffer many torments in vengeance for his enemies, summoned his retainer Gailen and said, “Until now we have shared one soul and one purpose. I beg you not to allow me to be delivered into my enemies’ hands. Take a sword and strike me down.” Gailen did not hesitate, but stabbed him with a knife. When the king arrived, Merovech was found dead. Some then asserted that the words of Merovech reported above had been invented by the queen, and that Merovech had in fact been secretly killed by her order.

Gailen was seized, and after his hands and feet had been cut off, his ears and the tips of his nostrils severed, and many other tortures inflicted, they put him to a miserable death. They also raised Grindio high, bound to a wheel; and they beheaded and killed Ciucilo, who had once been count of King Sigibert’s palace. Many others who had come with Merovech were cruelly put to death by various torments. People were also saying at the time that Bishop Egidius and Guntram Boso had played the leading part in this entrapment: Guntram because Queen Fredegund had secretly befriended him for the murder of Theudebert, and Egidius because he had long enjoyed her favor.

### 19. Of the almsgivings of Tiberius

<a id="hist-05-en-020"></a>
[**[020]**](#hist-05-la-020) When the Emperor Justin lost his reason and became insane, and the empire was governed solely through the Augusta Sophia, the people, as I have already said in the preceding book, chose Tiberius as Caesar. He was energetic, vigorous, and wise, generous in almsgiving and an excellent defender of the poor. He distributed to the poor much of the treasure that Justin had accumulated. The Augusta frequently rebuked him for reducing the state to poverty, saying, “What I gathered over many years you are scattering and squandering in a short time.” But he answered, “Our treasury will not be exhausted, provided that the poor receive alms and captives are ransomed. For this is the great treasure, since the Lord says, ‘Lay up for yourselves treasures in heaven, where neither rust nor moth destroys, and where thieves do not break in and steal.’ Therefore let us gather in heaven, through the poor, from what God has given us, so that the Lord may deign to increase our wealth in this world.” Because he was, as I have said, a great and true Christian, the more cheerfully he assisted the poor by his distributions, the more abundantly the Lord provided for him.

While walking through the palace, he saw in the pavement of a room a marble slab on which the Lord’s cross was carved. He said, “Lord, we protect our foreheads and breasts with your cross, and behold, we trample a cross beneath our feet!” He immediately ordered it to be removed. When the slab had been dug out and raised, they found another beneath it bearing the same sign. This was reported to him, and he ordered that slab removed as well. When it was taken away, they found a third, which was likewise removed at his command. Beneath it they discovered a great treasure containing more than a thousand hundredweight of gold. The gold was carried away, and he distributed it to the poor still more generously, as had been his custom; and because of his good will the Lord never allowed him to lack anything.

I will not omit what the Lord sent him afterward. Narses, the commander in Italy, had a large house in a certain city. When he left Italy with many treasures, he came to that city and secretly dug a large cistern in his house, in which he deposited many thousands of hundredweight of gold and silver. He killed those who knew of it and entrusted the hidden treasure to only one old man, binding him by an oath. After Narses died, these riches lay hidden underground. When the old man observed Tiberius’s continual almsgiving, he went to him and said, “Caesar, if it would benefit me in some way, I will disclose something of great importance to you.” Tiberius replied, “Tell me whatever you wish. It will benefit you if you report something that will be of use to me.” The old man said, “I know where Narses’s treasure is hidden, and now that I am at the end of my life I cannot keep it secret.” Caesar Tiberius rejoiced and sent his servants to the place. The old man led the way, and they followed in astonishment. They came to the cistern, uncovered it, and entered. There they found so much gold and silver that those carrying it away could scarcely empty it over many days. From this treasure Tiberius distributed still more to the needy with cheerful generosity.

### 20. Of Salonius and Sagittarius the bishops

<a id="hist-05-en-021"></a>
[**[021]**](#hist-05-la-021) A disturbance now arose concerning the bishops Salonius and Sagittarius. They had been brought up by Saint Nicetius, bishop of Lyons, and had received the office of deacon. In his time Salonius was appointed bishop of the church of Embrun and Sagittarius of that of Gap. But once they had obtained their episcopates and were left to their own discretion, they began to revel with insane fury in violent seizures, assaults, murders, adulteries, and various crimes. On one occasion, while Victor, bishop of Troyes, was celebrating the anniversary of his birth, they sent out a band of men and rushed upon him with swords and arrows. The attackers tore his garments, beat his servants, and carried off the vessels and all the provisions for the banquet, leaving the bishop in deep humiliation.

When King Guntram learned of this, he ordered a synod to assemble in the city of Lyons. The bishops met with the blessed patriarch Nicetius, examined the case, and found Salonius and Sagittarius clearly guilty of the crimes of which they were accused. They decreed that men who had committed such acts should be deprived of the honor of the episcopate. But the two, knowing that the king was still favorably disposed toward them, approached him, protesting that they had been unjustly removed and asking permission to go to the pope of the city of Rome. The king granted their petition, gave them letters, and allowed them to depart. Appearing before Pope John, they declared that they had been removed without any legitimate grounds. The pope sent letters to the king directing that they be restored to their sees. The king obeyed without delay, though not before rebuking them at length. But, worse still, no amendment followed.

They nevertheless sought peace with Bishop Victor and surrendered the men whom they had sent to make the attack. Victor remembered the Lord’s command not to return evil for evil to one’s enemies, did them no harm, and allowed them to go free. For this he was afterward suspended from communion, because, after accusing them publicly, he had privately spared his enemies without consulting the brethren before whom he had made the accusation. Through the king’s favor, however, he was later restored to communion.

Salonius and Sagittarius became involved daily in greater crimes. In the battles which, as I mentioned above, Mummolus fought against the Lombards, they armed themselves like laymen and killed many with their own hands. Among their own citizens, they would rage until blood flowed, beating some with clubs when moved to anger. The people’s outcry therefore reached the king once more, and he ordered the two bishops to be summoned. When they arrived, he refused to admit them to his presence, intending that a hearing should first be held and that only if they were found worthy should they be allowed to appear before the king.

Sagittarius, moved to wrath and taking this procedure badly, being fickle, vain, and prone to streams of irrational talk, began to declaim at length against the king. He declared that the king’s sons could not inherit the kingdom because their mother had been taken from the household of Magnachar and brought to the king’s bed. He did not understand that, whatever the rank of the women, the children born of kings are called kings’ sons. When the king heard this, he was greatly angered. He took away their horses, servants, and everything else they possessed and ordered them to be confined in monasteries far from him, where they were to do penance, leaving each only a single cleric. He sternly warned the local judges to guard them with armed men and to allow no one access to visit them.

At that time the king’s sons were still alive, and the elder fell ill. The king’s intimates approached him and said, “If the king would graciously deign to hear his servants’ words, we would speak in your ears.” He answered, “Say whatever you wish.” They said, “Perhaps these bishops were innocent and have been condemned to exile, and the king’s sin is thereby increased in some respect; perhaps that is why our lord’s son is dying.” He replied, “Go at once and release them, asking them to pray for our little children.” They went, and the bishops were released.

On leaving the monasteries, Salonius and Sagittarius met, embraced and kissed one another because they had not seen each other for so long, and returned to their cities. They appeared so contrite that they seemed never to cease chanting psalms, observed fasts, gave alms, and spent their days and nights completing the book of David’s songs, meditating on hymns and readings. But this holiness did not long remain unimpaired, and they turned backward once again. They often spent the nights feasting and drinking so that, while their clergy were celebrating matins in church, they would call for cups and pour out wine. There was no mention of God at all, and no course of recollection was observed. When dawn broke, they would rise from dinner, wrap themselves in soft coverings, and, buried in sleep and wine, sleep until the third hour of the day. Nor were women lacking with whom they could defile themselves. On rising, they bathed and reclined at table; when they rose from that meal in the evening, they longed for dinner until the hour of daylight mentioned above. They behaved in this way every day until God’s wrath crashed down upon them, as I shall relate later.

### 21. Of Winnoc the Briton

<a id="hist-05-en-022"></a>
[**[022]**](#hist-05-la-022) At that time Winnoch the Briton came from Brittany to Tours, practicing extreme abstinence and wishing to go to Jerusalem. He wore no garment except one made from sheepskins stripped of their wool. Because he seemed to me a very religious man, and in order to retain him more easily, I honored him with the grace of the presbyterate.

The religious woman Ingitrude was accustomed to collect water from Saint Martin’s tomb. When this water ran short, she asked that a vessel of wine be brought to the blessed tomb. After a night had passed, she ordered some of it to be taken from there in the presence of this priest. When it was brought to her, she said to him, “Remove the wine, and pour in only one drop of the little holy water that remains.” When he had done so, wonderful to relate, at the descent of that single drop the vessel, which had been half empty, was filled. It was emptied two or three times and each time refilled by only one drop. There is no doubt that blessed Martin’s power was at work in this as well.

### 22. Of the death of Samson, Chilperic's son

<a id="hist-05-en-023"></a>
[**[023]**](#hist-05-la-023) After these events, Samson, King Chilperic’s younger son, was seized by dysentery and fever and departed this life. He had been born while King Chilperic was being besieged at Tournai by his brother. His mother, fearing death, cast him away and wanted to destroy him. Unable to do so, and rebuked by the king, she ordered him to be baptized. He was baptized and received from the font by the bishop himself, but died before completing a single five-year period. His mother Fredegund also became gravely ill during those days, but recovered.

### 23. Of the prodigies shown

<a id="hist-05-en-024"></a>
[**[024]**](#hist-05-la-024) Afterward, on the night of the third day before the Ides of November, while I was celebrating the vigils of blessed Martin, a great portent appeared to me. A shining star seemed to gleam in the middle of the moon, and other stars appeared close above and below it. The halo that usually signifies rain also appeared around it. What these things signified I do not know. This year I also saw the moon frequently turn black, and there were severe thunderstorms before the Lord’s Nativity. Those bright lights around the sun, which country people call “suns,” also appeared, as I mentioned they had before the disaster in Auvergne. It is said that the sea advanced beyond its usual limits, and many other signs appeared.

### 24. How Chilperic seized Poitiers

<a id="hist-05-en-025"></a>
[**[025]**](#hist-05-la-025) Guntram Boso came to Tours with a few armed men, forcibly carried off his daughters, whom he had left in the holy basilica, and took them to the city of Poitiers, which belonged to King Childebert. King Chilperic also invaded Poitiers, and his nephew’s men were driven out by Chilperic’s men. They brought Ennodius from his office as count into the king’s presence. He was condemned to exile, and his property was surrendered to the treasury. A year later, however, he was restored to both his native land and his possessions. Guntram Boso left his daughters in the basilica of blessed Hilary and went over to King Childebert.

### 25. Of the death of Dacco and Dracolenus

<a id="hist-05-en-026"></a>
[**[026]**](#hist-05-la-026) In the third year of King Childebert’s reign, which was the seventeenth year of Chilperic and Guntram, Dacco, the son of the late Dagaric, abandoned King Chilperic and wandered from place to place. He was treacherously seized by Duke Dracolenus, who was also called Industrius. Dracolenus took him in chains to King Chilperic at Brannacum, having given Dacco an oath that he would obtain his life from the king. But he forgot his oath, made wicked allegations against Dacco before the king, and arranged for his death. While Dacco was being held in chains and saw that there was no possibility of escape, he sought penance from a priest without the king’s knowledge. After receiving it, he was killed.

When Dracolenus was swiftly returning to his own country, Guntram Boso was at that time attempting to take his daughters away from Poitiers. Hearing of this, Dracolenus threw himself in Guntram’s path, but Guntram and his companions, being prepared, resisted and tried to defend themselves. Guntram sent one of his friends to Dracolenus with this message: “Go and tell him, ‘You know that we have entered into an alliance with one another. I beg you to abandon your ambush against me. I do not forbid you to take whatever property you wish; only let me, even stripped bare, go wherever I wish with my daughters.’”

But Dracolenus, vain and fickle as he was, replied, “Here is the rope with which other guilty men have been led bound to the king at my direction. Today this man too will be tied with it and taken there in chains.” Having said this, he spurred his horse with his heels and charged at Guntram at full speed. He struck a useless blow, his spear shaft broke, and his sword fell to the ground. Seeing death hanging over him, Guntram called upon the Lord’s name and the great power of blessed Martin, raised his spear, and gripped Dracolenus by the throat. While he held him suspended above the horse, one of Guntram’s friends struck Dracolenus in the side with a lance and finished him. His companions fled, and after stripping the body Guntram departed freely with his daughters.

Afterward Severus, Guntram’s father-in-law, was gravely accused before the king by his sons. On hearing this, Severus went to the king with rich gifts. He was seized on the road, stripped of his property, taken into exile, and ended his life by a most wretched death. His two sons, Burgolenus and Dodo, were condemned to death on a charge of treason. One was forcibly killed by the army; the other was captured in flight and died after his hands and feet had been cut off. All their property and their father’s was surrendered to the treasury, for they possessed great treasures.

### 26. How the army went into Brittany

<a id="hist-05-en-027"></a>
[**[027]**](#hist-05-la-027) Then, by King Chilperic’s command, the men of Tours, Poitiers, Bayeux, Le Mans, and Angers, together with many others, went into Brittany and took up positions against Waroch, son of the late Macliav, on the river Vicinonia. Waroch made a treacherous night attack on the Saxons of Bayeux and killed a great many of them. Three days later he made peace with King Chilperic’s commanders, gave his son as a hostage, and bound himself by an oath to remain faithful to King Chilperic. He also surrendered the city of Vannes on condition that he be allowed to govern it by the king’s command and pay the tribute and all other dues arising from it every year without being reminded. When this had been done, the army withdrew from the region.

Afterward King Chilperic ordered fines to be exacted from the poor and the junior members of churches and basilicas because they had not marched with the army, although it had not been customary for them to perform any public service. Waroch later forgot his promise. Wishing to break what he had done, he sent Eunius, bishop of the city of Vannes, to King Chilperic. The king, moved to anger, rebuked Eunius and ordered him to be condemned to exile.

### 27. Of the deposition of Salonius and Sagittarius

<a id="hist-05-en-028"></a>
[**[028]**](#hist-05-la-028) In the fourth year of Childebert’s reign, the eighteenth year of Kings Guntram and Chilperic, a synod was held in the city of Chalon by command of King Guntram. Various cases were considered, and the old calamity concerning the bishops Salonius and Sagittarius was renewed. Charges were brought against them: they were accused not only of adultery but also of murder. The bishops judged that these offenses could be expiated by penance, but the further charge was added that they were guilty of treason and betrayal of their country. For this reason they were deprived of the episcopate and thrust under guard into the basilica of blessed Marcellus. They escaped from it by flight and departed, wandering in different directions, until other bishops were appointed in their cities.

### 28. Of the tax-registers of Chilperic

<a id="hist-05-en-029"></a>
[**[029]**](#hist-05-la-029) King Chilperic ordered new and oppressive tax assessments to be made throughout his kingdom. Many people therefore abandoned their cities or their own property and sought other kingdoms, considering it better to live as strangers elsewhere than to be subjected to such danger. It had been decreed that a landowner should render one amphora of wine for every arpent of his own land. Many other levies were also imposed on the remaining lands and on slaves, and the people could not meet them.

When the people of Limoges saw themselves burdened by such an exaction, they assembled on the Kalends of March and wanted to kill Mark the referendary, who had been ordered to carry out these measures. They would certainly have done so had Bishop Ferreolus not rescued him from imminent danger. The assembled multitude also seized the tax registers and burned them. The king was greatly incensed and sent agents from his own entourage, who afflicted the people with enormous losses, terrified them with punishments, and punished them with death. It is also reported that abbots and priests were stretched upon stakes and subjected to various torments, because the royal envoys falsely alleged that they had assisted the insurgents in burning the registers. Still harsher tributes were imposed afterward.

### 29. Of the devastation of the Bretons

<a id="hist-05-en-030"></a>
[**[030]**](#hist-05-la-030) The Bretons also devastated the region of Rennes severely with fire, plunder, and captivity. They advanced as far as the village of Cornutium, fighting as they went. Bishop Eunius was brought back from exile but was assigned to Angers for his maintenance and was not permitted to return to his own city of Vannes. Duke Bippolenus was sent against the Bretons and ravaged several parts of Brittany with sword and fire, which provoked still greater fury.

### 30. Of the empire of Tiberius

<a id="hist-05-en-031"></a>
[**[031]**](#hist-05-la-031) While these things were happening in Gaul, Justin completed the eighteenth year of his reign and ended both his madness and his life. After his burial, Caesar Tiberius assumed the empire to which he had long since been admitted. When the people, according to local custom, expected him to proceed to the spectacle in the circus, and were planning an ambush against him on behalf of Justinian, who was then regarded as Justin’s nephew, Tiberius instead went in procession through the holy places. When the prayer was completed, he summoned the pope of the city and entered the palace with the consuls and prefects. He was then clothed in purple, crowned with the diadem, and seated upon the imperial throne, and his rule was confirmed amid immense acclamations. When the circus factions learned what had happened, they covered their heads in confusion and withdrew without achieving anything, unable to harm a man who had placed his hope in God.

A few days later Justinian came and threw himself at the emperor’s feet, bringing him fifteen hundredweight of gold as a token of gratitude. With his customary patience, Tiberius raised him up and ordered him to remain in the palace. But the Augusta Sophia, forgetting the promise she had once made to Tiberius, tried to lay snares for him. When Tiberius went to his estate to enjoy the vintage for thirty days according to imperial custom, Sophia secretly summoned Justinian and wished to elevate him to the empire. On discovering this, Tiberius returned swiftly to Constantinople, seized the Augusta, and stripped her of all her treasures, leaving her only her daily sustenance. He removed her servants and replaced them with others chosen from his own faithful men, expressly commanding that none of her former attendants should have access to her. Justinian he merely rebuked, and afterward loved him so greatly that he promised his own daughter to Justinian’s son and in turn sought Justinian’s daughter for his own son; but this arrangement was never carried out. Tiberius’s army defeated the Persians and returned victorious with such a mass of booty that it seemed sufficient to satisfy human greed. Twenty captured elephants were brought to the emperor.

### 31. Of the Attacks of the Bretons

<a id="hist-05-en-032"></a>
[**[032]**](#hist-05-la-032) That year the Bretons were extremely aggressive around the cities of Nantes and Rennes. They carried off immense plunder, overran the fields, stripped the vineyards of their fruit, and led away captives. Bishop Felix sent an embassy to them, and although the Bretons promised to make amends, they refused to fulfill any of their promises.

### 32. Of the basilica of Saint Denis outraged by a woman

<a id="hist-05-en-033"></a>
[**[033]**](#hist-05-la-033) At Paris a certain woman fell under accusation, many people alleging that she had left her husband and was having intercourse with another man. Her husband’s relatives approached her father and said, “Either prove your daughter’s innocence, or let her die, so that this shameful conduct does not bring disgrace upon our family.” The father answered, “I know my daughter to be entirely innocent; what these wicked people are saying is not true. Nevertheless, so that the accusation may go no further, I will establish her innocence by an oath.” They said, “If she is innocent, affirm it by an oath over the tomb of the blessed martyr Denis.” The father agreed. When the appointed day came, they assembled at the holy martyr’s basilica. The father raised his hands over the altar and swore that his daughter was not guilty. The husband’s party, on the other hand, declared that he had perjured himself.

As they argued, they drew their swords, rushed at one another, and slaughtered each other before the altar itself. They were men of high rank and among the foremost at King Chilperic’s court. Many were wounded by swords; the holy basilica was spattered with human blood; its doors were pierced by javelins and swords; and the impious missiles raged as far as the tomb itself. When the violence was with difficulty calmed, services ceased at the place until all these events were brought to the king’s attention. The men hastened into the king’s presence but were not received into favor. They were sent back to the local bishop with instructions that, if they were not found guilty of this crime, they should be admitted to communion. They then made amends for their misconduct before Bishop Ragnemod, who presided over the church of Paris, and were received back into ecclesiastical communion. Not many days later, when the woman was summoned to judgment, she ended her life with a noose.

### 33. Of the prodigies

<a id="hist-05-en-034"></a>
[**[034]**](#hist-05-la-034) In the fifth year of King Childebert’s reign, great floods afflicted the region of Auvergne. Rain did not cease for twelve days, and the Limagne was so inundated that many people were prevented from sowing their seed. The rivers Loire and Allier, the latter also called the Elacer, as well as the other torrents flowing into it, swelled so greatly that they passed boundaries they had never crossed before. They caused great destruction of livestock, loss of crops, and the wreck of buildings. Similarly, the Rhône and Saône joined together, burst their banks, brought severe losses upon the people, and overthrew part of the wall of the city of Lyons. When the rains ceased, the trees blossomed a second time, for it was the month of September.

In the Touraine that year, in the morning before daylight, a flash was seen to run across the sky and fall toward the east. A sound like trees crashing down was heard throughout that land. It cannot be supposed to have come from trees, because it was heard for fifty miles or more. That same year the city of Bordeaux was violently shaken by an earthquake, and the city walls were in danger of being overthrown. The whole population was so terrified of death that they thought they would be swallowed up with the city if they did not flee. Many therefore moved to other cities. The tremor extended to neighboring cities and reached as far as Spain, though less violently. Immense rocks were dislodged from the Pyrenees and struck down cattle and men.

A fire of divine origin also burned a suburb of Bordeaux. Houses and threshing floors with their grain were suddenly caught by the flames and consumed, with no external source of ignition, perhaps by divine command. The city of Orléans likewise suffered a severe conflagration, so that nothing at all remained to the wealthier inhabitants; and if anyone rescued anything from the fire, it was snatched away by thieves who pressed in. In the territory of Chartres, blood flowed from a loaf that had been broken. The city of Bourges was also severely battered by hail at that time.

### 34. Of the disease of the dysentery and the death of Chilperic's sons

<a id="hist-05-en-035"></a>
[**[035]**](#hist-05-la-035) These portents were followed by a most severe pestilence. While the kings were quarreling and again preparing civil war, dysentery spread through nearly all Gaul. Those who suffered had a violent fever with vomiting, severe pain in the loins, and heaviness of the head or neck. What they brought up from their mouths was saffron-colored or green. Many asserted that it was a hidden poison, while the country people called the pustules “coral pustules.” This is not incredible, because cupping vessels applied to the shoulders or legs brought relief to many: blisters swelled and burst, the corrupt matter drained away, and they were freed. Herbs that counteract poisons, when taken as drinks, also brought relief to many.

This illness began in the month of August and first seized small children and youths, laying them on their sickbeds. I lost sweet and beloved little children whom I had cherished in my lap, carried in my arms, or fed with my own hand, nourishing them with the most attentive care by serving them food. But, wiping away my tears, I say with blessed Job, “The Lord gave, the Lord has taken away; as it pleased the Lord, so has it been done. Blessed be his name forever.”

At this time King Chilperic became gravely ill. When he recovered, his younger son, who had not yet been reborn by water and the Holy Spirit, fell sick. Seeing that he was at the point of death, they washed him in baptism. When he improved slightly, his elder brother Chlodobert was seized by the same disease. His mother Fredegund, seeing him in danger of death, repented too late and said to the king, “Divine goodness has long borne with us in our evil conduct. It has often afflicted us with fevers and other troubles, but no amendment followed. Behold, we are now losing our sons. The tears of the poor, the laments of widows, and the sighs of orphans are destroying them, and no hope remains for us to accumulate wealth for anyone. We store up treasure without knowing for whom we are gathering it. Behold, our treasures remain without an owner, filled with plunder and curses. Were our storehouses not overflowing with wine? Were our granaries not filled with grain? Were our treasuries not crammed with gold, silver, precious stones, necklaces, and the other ornaments of empire? Behold, we are losing the fairest thing we possessed. Come now, if it pleases you: let us burn all the unjust tax registers, and let our treasury be content with what sufficed for your father, King Chlothar.”

Having spoken, the queen beat her breast with her fists and ordered the registers that had come from her cities through Mark to be brought and thrown into the fire. Turning again to the king, she said, “Why do you delay? Do as you have seen me do, so that even if we lose our sweet children, we may at least escape perpetual punishment.” Then the king, stricken at heart, consigned all the tax registers to the flames. Once they had been burned, he sent men to prohibit future assessments.

Afterward the younger child wasted away under the violence of the disease and died. With the deepest grief they carried him from the villa of Brinnacum to Paris and ordered him to be buried in the basilica of Saint Denis. Chlodobert was placed on a bier and taken to Soissons, to the basilica of Saint Medard. They laid him beside the holy tomb and made vows for him, but in the middle of the night, exhausted and faint, he breathed his last. They buried him in the basilica of the holy martyrs Crispin and Crispinian. There was great lamentation among all the people: mourning men and women dressed in dark garments followed this funeral as is customary at the funerals of husbands and wives. King Chilperic afterward distributed many gifts to churches, basilicas, and the poor.

### 35. Of Queen Austregild

<a id="hist-05-en-036"></a>
[**[036]**](#hist-05-la-036) In these days Austrigildis, the queen of King Guntram, was carried off by this disease. Before she breathed out her wretched spirit, seeing that she could not escape, she drew deep sighs and wished to have companions in her death, arranging that others’ funerals should be mourned at her own obsequies. It is said that, in the manner of Herod, she made this request of the king: “I would still have had hope of living had I not perished at the hands of wicked physicians, for the potions I received from them have violently taken away my life and caused me to lose this light swiftly. Therefore, so that my death may not pass unavenged, I beg and adjure you with an oath that, as soon as I depart this light, those men be slaughtered by the sword. Just as I can live no longer, let them not glory after my death; let there be one grief for our friends and theirs alike.” Having spoken, she yielded up her unhappy soul. When the customary funeral rites had been completed, the king, constrained by his wicked wife’s oath, fulfilled her iniquitous command. He ordered the two physicians who had attended her to be struck down by the sword. The judgment of many wise people was that this deed was not free from sin.

### 36. Of the bishop Heraclius and the count Nanthinus

<a id="hist-05-en-037"></a>
[**[037]**](#hist-05-la-037) Count Nanthinus of Angoulême was also wasted by this disease and died. But his actions against priests and the churches of God require a fuller account. His uncle Maracharius had long held the office of count in that city. When his term was completed, he joined the Church, became a cleric, and was ordained bishop. He built and repaired churches and ecclesiastical houses with great vigilance. In the seventh year of his episcopate, enemies put poison in his fish; he accepted it unsuspectingly and was cruelly killed. Divine mercy did not long leave his death unavenged. Frontonius, on whose advice the crime had been committed, immediately took the episcopate, but God’s judgment overtook him and he died after occupying it for one year.

After Frontonius’s death, Heraclius, a priest of Bordeaux who had once served as an envoy of Childebert the Elder, was ordained bishop. Nanthinus sought the office of count in that city in order to avenge his uncle’s death. Once he had obtained it, he inflicted many injuries on the bishop. He said, “You keep with you the murderers who killed my uncle, and you admit to your table priests involved in this crime.” As the enmity grew, he gradually began to seize by force the church estates that Maracharius had left by written testament, asserting that the Church ought not to acquire the property of a testator who had been killed by its own clergy. Later, having killed some laymen, he went further: he seized a priest, bound him, and pierced him with a spear. While the priest still lived, Nanthinus had his hands twisted behind his back and fastened him to a stake, seeking to force from him an admission that he had been involved in the affair. The priest denied it and, as blood poured from the wound, gave up his spirit. The bishop, aroused by this, ordered Nanthinus to be barred from the doors of the church.

When the bishops assembled at the city of Saintes, Nanthinus begged to obtain the bishop’s peace, promising to restore all the church property that he had wrongfully taken and to show himself humble before the bishop. Heraclius, desiring to obey his brethren’s instructions, granted everything requested; nevertheless, he commended the priest’s cause to almighty God and received the count back in charity. Nanthinus returned to the city and stripped, shattered, and demolished the houses that he had wrongfully seized, saying, “Even if the Church recovers them, let it find them desolate.” The bishop was moved once more and suspended him from communion. While these things were happening, the blessed bishop completed the course of his life and departed to the Lord.

A few months later Nanthinus was seized by the disease mentioned above, after some bishops had admitted him to communion in return for gifts and flattery. Consumed by an intense fever, he cried out, “Alas, alas! I am being burned by Bishop Heraclius; I am tormented by him; I am summoned by him to judgment. I acknowledge my crime; I remember that I unjustly inflicted injuries on the bishop. I beg for death, so that I may no longer be tormented by this torture.” As he cried these words in the height of his fever, his bodily strength failed and he poured out his unhappy soul, leaving unmistakable evidence that this had happened to him in vengeance for the blessed bishop. His lifeless body turned so black that one would have thought it had been burned over hot coals. Let everyone be astonished at these things, marvel at them, and fear to inflict injuries on priests, for the Lord is the avenger of his servants who hope in him.

### 37. Of Martin of Galicia the bishop

<a id="hist-05-en-038"></a>
[**[038]**](#hist-05-la-038) At this time the blessed Martin, bishop of Galicia, also died, causing great mourning among his people. He was born in Pannonia and, setting out from there to visit the holy places of the East, became so learned that no one of his time was considered his superior. He then came to Galicia, where he was ordained bishop while the relics of blessed Martin were being carried there. Having spent about thirty years in that episcopate, he departed to the Lord full of virtues. He himself composed the verses over the southern door of the basilica of Saint Martin.

### 38. Of the persecution of the Christians in Spain

<a id="hist-05-en-039"></a>
[**[039]**](#hist-05-la-039) There was a great persecution of Christians in Spain that year. Many were sent into exile, deprived of their property, wasted by hunger, imprisoned, scourged, and slaughtered by various torments. The chief instigator of this crime was Goisuintha, whom King Leovigild had married after her union with King Athanagild. Divine vengeance followed, and she who had branded God’s servants with the mark of humiliation was herself made notorious before all peoples: a white cloud covered one of her eyes and drove from her eyelids the light that her mind did not possess.

King Leovigild had two sons by another wife. The elder had been betrothed to the daughter of Sigibert, King Chilperic’s younger brother. Ingund, King Sigibert’s daughter, was sent to Spain with a great retinue and was received with much rejoicing by her grandmother Goisuintha. Goisuintha would not allow her to remain long in the Catholic religion, but began to entice her with flattering words to be rebaptized into the Arian heresy. Ingund resisted courageously and said, “It is enough that I was once washed from original sin in saving baptism and confessed the Holy Trinity in one equality. I declare that I believe this with my whole heart, and I will never turn back from this faith.” On hearing this, Goisuintha burned with furious anger, seized the girl by the hair, and dashed her to the ground. After kicking her for a long time until she was covered with blood, she ordered her to be stripped and plunged into a baptismal pool. But, as many assert, Ingund never turned her mind away from our faith.

Leovigild gave the couple a city in which to reside and rule. When they had gone there, Ingund began to urge her husband to abandon the deceit of heresy and acknowledge the truth of the Catholic law. He resisted for a long time, but at length, moved by her preaching, he was converted to the Catholic faith and received the name John when he was anointed with chrism. When Leovigild heard this, he began seeking an opportunity to destroy him. Hermenegild understood this and joined the emperor’s side, forming an alliance with the imperial general who was then attacking Spain. Leovigild sent messengers to him, saying, “Come to me, for there are matters that we should discuss together.” Hermenegild replied, “I will not go, because you are hostile to me now that I am a Catholic.” Leovigild then gave the emperor’s general thirty thousand solidi to withdraw his support and marched against his son with an army.

Hermenegild summoned the Greeks and went out against his father, leaving his wife in the city. When Leovigild approached from the opposite direction and Hermenegild found himself abandoned by his allies and saw that he could accomplish nothing, he sought refuge in a nearby church, saying, “Let my father not come upon me. It is unlawful for either a father to be killed by a son or a son by a father.” On hearing this, Leovigild sent Hermenegild’s brother to him. The brother gave an oath that Hermenegild would not be humiliated and said, “Come yourself and prostrate yourself at our father’s feet, and he will forgive you everything.” Hermenegild asked that his father be summoned. When Leovigild entered, Hermenegild prostrated himself at his feet. His father seized and kissed him, soothed him with flattering words, and led him to the camp. Then, forgetting his oath, he signaled to his men, who seized Hermenegild, stripped him of his garments, and dressed him in vile clothing. Leovigild returned to the city of Toledo, removed Hermenegild’s attendants, and sent him into exile with only one young servant.

### 39. Of the death of Chlodovech

<a id="hist-05-en-040"></a>
[**[040]**](#hist-05-la-040) After the death of his sons, King Chilperic, full of grief, withdrew with his wife to the forest of Cothia in the month of October. At the queen’s instigation he sent his son Clovis to Brinnacum, evidently so that Clovis too might perish in the same destruction. The disease that had killed his brothers was raging severely there at the time, but Clovis suffered no harm. The king himself then came to the villa of Cala in the city of Paris. A few days later he ordered Clovis to come to him. I will not be reluctant to describe the manner of Clovis’s death.

While he was living with his father at the villa mentioned above, Clovis began to boast and speak immaturely: “Behold, my brothers are dead, and the whole kingdom remains to me. All Gaul will be subject to me, and fate has granted me the entire empire. Behold, my enemies are in my hands, and I will inflict on them whatever I please.” He also made unseemly disparaging remarks about his stepmother, Queen Fredegund. When she heard this, she was seized with overwhelming fear. Some days later a certain man came and told the queen, “It is Clovis’s treachery that has caused you to sit bereaved of your sons. Desiring the daughter of one of your servant women, he killed your sons through sorcery practiced by her mother. I warn you therefore not to expect anything better for yourself, now that the hope through which you were to reign has been taken away.”

Terrified, inflamed with fury, and stricken by this fresh bereavement, the queen seized the girl whom Clovis had desired, had her severely beaten, ordered her hair to be cut off, and commanded that she be fixed upon a split log in front of Clovis’s lodging. She also had the girl’s mother driven away and tortured for a long time until she extorted from her a confession affirming that these allegations were true. Fredegund then reported these and similar things to the king and demanded vengeance upon Clovis.

The king went hunting and ordered Clovis to be summoned secretly. When he arrived, Dukes Desiderius and Bobo seized him by the king’s command and placed him in handcuffs. He was stripped of his weapons and clothing, covered with a vile garment, and brought in chains to the queen. She ordered him to be kept in custody, wishing to force from him whether matters were as she had heard, whose advice he had followed, at whose instigation he had acted, and with whom he had formed close friendships. He denied the other charges but disclosed friendships with many people.

After three days the queen ordered him to be taken in chains across the river Marne and guarded at a villa called Nocitum. While in custody there he was struck with a knife and died, and he was buried in that very place. Meanwhile messengers came to the king saying that Clovis had pierced himself with his own blow, and they affirmed that the knife with which he had struck himself was still fixed in the wound. King Chilperic was deceived by these words and did not weep for the son whom he himself, so to speak, had delivered to death at the queen’s instigation.

Clovis’s servants were scattered in different directions. His mother was cruelly killed; his sister was sent to a monastery after being deceived by the queen’s servants, and she remains there now, having taken the habit. All their wealth was transferred to the queen. The woman who had made the accusation against Clovis was condemned to be burned alive. As she was being led away, the wretched woman began to cry out that she had spoken lies, but her words accomplished nothing. She was tied to a stake and burned alive in the flames.

Clovis’s treasurer was brought back from Bourges by Chuppa, count of the stable, and sent in chains to the queen to be subjected to various tortures. But the queen ordered him to be released from both punishment and chains, and at my intercession with the king she allowed him to go free.

### 40. Of the bishops Elafius and Eunius

<a id="hist-05-en-041"></a>
[**[041]**](#hist-05-la-041) Afterward Elafius, bishop of Châlons, was sent as an envoy to Spain on business for Queen Brunhilda. Seized by a severe fever, he breathed his last; his body was carried back and buried in his own city. Bishop Eunius, whose binding by the Bretons I mentioned above, was not permitted to return to his own city, and the king ordered that he be maintained at public expense in Angers. When he came to Paris and was celebrating the sacred mysteries on the Lord’s day, he uttered a cry like a whinny and fell to the ground. Blood poured from his mouth and nostrils, and he was carried away in men’s hands, but he recovered. He was excessively devoted to wine and was often so disgracefully drunk that he could not walk.

### 41. Of the legates of Galicia and the prodigies

<a id="hist-05-en-042"></a>
[**[042]**](#hist-05-la-042) Mir, king of Galicia, sent envoys to King Guntram. As they were passing through the territory of Poitiers, which King Chilperic then held, their presence was reported to him. He ordered them to be brought before him under guard and kept in custody at Paris. At that time a wolf came from the woods into the city of Poitiers and entered through a gate. The gates were closed, and it was trapped within the city walls and killed. Some people also asserted that they had seen the sky on fire. The river Loire rose higher than in the previous year after the torrent of the Cher joined it. An exceedingly violent south wind blew, flattening forests, tearing up houses, carrying away fences, and rolling people along until they were destroyed. Its path was about seven acres wide, but its length could not be estimated. Cocks also often crowed loudly at the beginning of the night. The moon was eclipsed, and a comet appeared. A severe pestilence among the people followed. After a year the Suebi envoys were released and returned home.

### 42. Of Maurilius bishop of Cahors

<a id="hist-05-en-043"></a>
[**[043]**](#hist-05-la-043) Maurilius, bishop of the city of Cahors, was gravely ill with gout, but in addition to the pains caused by the disease itself he inflicted great torments upon himself: he often pressed red-hot iron against his shins and feet in order to increase his suffering. Since many were seeking his episcopate, he chose Ursicinus, who had once been referendary to Queen Ultrogotha. Maurilius asked that Ursicinus be consecrated while he himself still lived, and then departed from this world.

Maurilius was very generous in almsgiving and deeply instructed in the ecclesiastical Scriptures. He could usually recite from memory the succession of the various generations described in the books of the Old Testament, which many find difficult to retain. He was also just in judgment and defended the poor of his church against the hands of wicked judges, according to the words of Job: “I delivered the poor from the hand of the powerful, and helped the needy who had no helper. The mouth of the widow blessed me, for I was an eye to the blind, a foot to the lame, and a father to the helpless.”

### 43. Of the altercation with the heretic

<a id="hist-05-en-044"></a>
[**[044]**](#hist-05-la-044) King Leovigild sent Agila as an envoy to Chilperic, a man endowed with no intelligence or capacity for ordered reasoning, but perverse in his will concerning the Catholic faith. When his journey brought him to Tours, he began to provoke me about the faith and attack the doctrines of the Church. “The sentence pronounced by the ancient bishops was unjust,” he said, “when it asserted that the Son is equal to the Father. How can he be equal to the Father in power when he says, ‘The Father is greater than I’? It is not right that he should be considered like one to whom he declares himself inferior, for whom he groans with the anguish of death, and to whom finally, as he dies, he commends his spirit as though possessing no power. It is therefore evident that he is inferior to the Father in both age and power.”

I asked whether he believed that Jesus Christ is the Son of God, and whether he acknowledged him to be God’s wisdom, light, truth, life, and righteousness. He replied, “I believe that the Son of God is all these things.” I said, “Then tell me when the Father was without wisdom, without light, without life, without truth, or without righteousness. Just as the Father could not exist without these, neither could he exist without the Son. These attributes belong supremely to the mystery of the Lord’s name. Nor would the Father be Father if he had no Son. As for your saying that he declared, ‘The Father is greater than I,’ know that he said this from the humility of the flesh he assumed, so that you may understand that redemption was accomplished through humility, not through power. You who say, ‘The Father is greater than I,’ ought to remember that elsewhere he says, ‘I and the Father are one.’ Both the fear of death and the commendation of the spirit must be referred to the weakness of the body, so that he may be believed to be true man just as he is true God.”

He answered, “Whoever fulfills another’s will is also inferior to him. A son is always inferior to his father, because he does his father’s will, while the father is not shown to do the son’s will.” I replied, “Understand that the Father in the Son and the Son in the Father always subsist in one Godhead. So that you may know that the Father does the Son’s will, if evangelical faith remains in you, hear what our God Jesus himself said when he came to raise Lazarus: ‘Father, I thank you because you have heard me. I knew that you always hear me, but I said this for the sake of the crowd standing around, so that they may believe that you sent me.’ And when he came to his Passion, he said, ‘Father, glorify me with the glory that I had with you before the world existed.’ The Father answered from heaven, ‘I have both glorified it and will glorify it again.’ The Son is therefore equal in the Godhead, not inferior and possessing nothing less. For if you confess God, you must acknowledge him complete and lacking nothing; if you deny that he is truly complete, you do not believe him to be God.”

He said, “Did God’s Son begin to be called such from the man he assumed? For there was a time when he did not exist.” I answered, “Hear David speaking in the person of the Father: ‘From the womb, before the morning star, I begot you.’ And John the Evangelist says, ‘In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God.’ This Word was therefore made flesh and dwelt among us, and through him all things were made. Blinded by the poison of persuasion, you people think nothing worthy of God.”

He asked, “Do you also call the Holy Spirit God and declare him equal to the Father and the Son?” I replied, “In the three there is one will, one power, and one operation: one God in Trinity and threefold in unity; three persons, but one kingdom, one majesty, one power and omnipotence.” He said, “The Holy Spirit, whom you proclaim equal to the Father and the Son, is understood to be inferior to both, because he is promised by the Son and described as sent by the Father. No one promises anything unless it is subject to his own dominion, and no one sends anyone except an inferior, as the Son himself says in the Gospel: ‘Unless I go away, the Paraclete will not come; but if I go, I will send him to you.’”

I answered, “The Son spoke rightly before his Passion, because unless he returned victorious to the Father and prepared in man, by redeeming the world with his own blood, a dwelling worthy of God, the Holy Spirit, himself God, could not descend into a heart defiled by fanaticism and infected with the stain of original sin. For Solomon says, ‘The Holy Spirit will flee deceit.’ If you have any hope of resurrection, do not speak against the Holy Spirit, because according to the Lord’s sentence, ‘Whoever blasphemes against the Holy Spirit will not be forgiven, either in this world or in the world to come.’” He replied, “He who sends is God; he who is sent is not God.”

I asked whether he believed the teaching of the apostles Peter and Paul. When he answered, “I believe it,” I added, “When the apostle Peter rebuked Ananias for fraud concerning a field, consider what he said: ‘Why did you think to lie to the Holy Spirit? You have not lied to men, but to God.’ And Paul, distinguishing the degrees of spiritual gifts, says, ‘One and the same Spirit works all these things, distributing to each one as he wills.’ One who does what he wills is reduced to no one’s power. As I said above, you people think nothing rightly about the Holy Trinity, and the death of your own founder, Arius, demonstrated how iniquitous is the perversity of this sect.”

He answered, “Do not blaspheme a law you do not observe. We do not believe what you believe, yet we do not blaspheme it, because it is not considered a crime if both are worshipped. In common speech we say that it is not harmful if someone passing between pagan altars and God’s church venerates both.” Seeing his folly, I said, “As I see, you show yourself both a defender of pagans and a champion of heretics, since you defile ecclesiastical doctrine and preach that the filth of the pagans should be adored. You would do better if that faith armed you which Abraham saw at the oak, Isaac in the ram, Jacob in the stone, and Moses in the bush; which Aaron bore on his breastplate, David exulted in with the timbrel, and Solomon preached in understanding; which all the patriarchs, the prophets, and the law itself either sang in oracles or prefigured in sacrifices; and which our Martin, still present with us, now supports by his intercession, possesses in his heart, and displays in his works. Then you too would be converted and believe in the inseparable Trinity; you would receive a blessing from me, your heart would be purged of the poison of false belief, and your iniquities would be blotted out.”

Moved to fury and gnashing his teeth like a madman, he said, “May my soul escape from the bonds of this body before I receive a blessing from any priest of your religion.” I answered, “May the Lord never allow my religion or faith to grow so lukewarm that I would distribute his holy things to dogs and offer the sacred pearls of great price to filthy swine.” At this he abandoned the argument, rose, and departed. Afterward, when he had returned to Spain, he was weakened by illness and, compelled by necessity, was converted to our religion.

### 44. Of what Chilperic wrote

<a id="hist-05-en-045"></a>
[**[045]**](#hist-05-la-045) At the same time King Chilperic wrote a short treatise maintaining that the Holy Trinity should not be named with any distinction of persons, but only as God. He asserted that it was unworthy to speak of God as a person in the way one speaks of a man of flesh, and affirmed that he who is the Father is also the Son, and that he who is Father and Son is likewise the Holy Spirit. “Thus,” he said, “he appeared to the prophets and patriarchs, and thus the law itself proclaimed him.” When he had ordered these things to be read to me, he said, “This is what I wish you and the other teachers of the churches to believe.”

I replied, “Abandon this belief, pious king. You ought to follow what the other teachers of the Church after the apostles have left me, what Hilary and Eusebius taught, and what you yourself confessed at baptism.” The king angrily replied, “It is plain to me that in this matter I have Hilary and Eusebius as powerful enemies.” I answered, “You ought to take care not to offend either God or his saints. Know that the Father is one person, the Son another, and the Holy Spirit another. Neither the Father nor the Holy Spirit assumed flesh, but the Son, so that he who was the Son of God might also be called the son of a virgin for the redemption of man. Neither the Father nor the Holy Spirit suffered, but the Son, so that he who had assumed flesh in the world might himself be offered for the world. What you say about persons must be understood not corporeally but spiritually. In these three persons there is one glory, one eternity, and one power.”

Moved to anger, he said, “I will expound these things to men wiser than you, and they will agree with me.” I answered, “Anyone willing to follow what you propose will never be wise, but foolish.” Gnashing his teeth, he fell silent. Not many days later, when Salvius, bishop of Albi, arrived, Chilperic ordered these things to be recited to him and begged him to agree. Salvius rejected them so completely that, if he could have laid hands on the sheet in which they were written, he would have torn it to pieces. The king accordingly abandoned this undertaking.

The same king wrote other books in verse, as though following Sedulius, but his verses did not conform in any respect to the rules of meter. He also added letters to my alphabet—namely ō, as the Greeks have, æ, th, and uui—whose characters are these: Δ ϸ. He sent letters to all the cities of his kingdom directing that boys be taught in this way and that books written in former times be smoothed with pumice and rewritten.

### 45. Of the death of Agroecula the bishop

<a id="hist-05-en-046"></a>
[**[046]**](#hist-05-la-046) At this time Agroecula, bishop of Chalon, died. He was a very distinguished and prudent man of senatorial family. He erected many buildings in that city, restored houses, and built a church, which he supported with columns, adorned with various marbles, and decorated with mosaic. He practiced great abstinence: he never took luncheon, only dinner, and he sat at it at such a time that he rose while the sun was still above the horizon. He was not particularly gracious, but very eloquent. He died in the forty-eighth year of his episcopate and the eighty-third year of his life. Flavus, King Guntram’s referendary, succeeded him.

### 46. Of the death of Dalmatius the bishop

<a id="hist-05-en-047"></a>
[**[047]**](#hist-05-la-047) At the same time Dalmatius, bishop of the city of Rodez, departed from this world. He was a man eminent in every holiness, abstinent in food and from the desires of the flesh, very generous in almsgiving and kind to all, and steadfast in prayer and vigils. He built a church, but because he repeatedly demolished it in order to improve it, he left it unfinished. After his death many sought the episcopate, as usually happens. The priest Transobadus, who had formerly been his archdeacon, pursued it most actively, confident because he had entrusted his son to Gogo, who was then the king’s foster-father.

The bishop had drawn up a testament in which he specified what gift the king should receive after his death, and adjured them under terrible oaths that no outsider, no greedy man, and no man bound by the marriage tie should be ordained in that church. Instead, a man free from all these things, occupied solely in the praises of the Lord, was to be appointed. The priest Transobadus prepared a banquet for the clergy in that city. While they were reclining at table, one of the priests began to revile the deceased bishop with shameful language and went so far as to call him deranged and foolish. As he was speaking, a cupbearer arrived to offer him a drink. He took the cup, but as he brought it toward his mouth he began to tremble. The cup slipped from his hand, and leaning his head on another priest beside him, he gave up his spirit. He was carried from the banquet to the grave and covered with earth. Afterward the bishop’s testament was set aside, and Theodosius, who then held the archdeaconate of that city, was ordained bishop in the presence of King Childebert and his nobles.

### 47. Of the countship of Eunomius

<a id="hist-05-en-048"></a>
[**[048]**](#hist-05-la-048) When Chilperic heard of all the evils that Leudast was inflicting on the churches of Tours and the entire population, he sent Ansovald there. Ansovald arrived for Saint Martin’s festival and gave me and the people a choice, and Eunomius was raised to the office of count. Leudast, seeing himself removed, went to Chilperic and said, “Until now, most pious king, I have guarded the city of Tours. Now that I have been removed from office, see how it will be guarded. Know that Bishop Gregory intends to deliver it to Sigibert’s son.” On hearing this, the king replied, “By no means. You are adding these accusations because you have been removed.” Leudast answered, “The bishop says still worse things about you. He says that your queen is committing adultery with Bishop Berthram.” The king was enraged and ordered Leudast to be beaten with fists and heels, loaded with irons, and shut up in prison.

### 48. Of the malice of Leudast

<a id="hist-05-en-049"></a>
[**[049]**](#hist-05-la-049) Since this book demands an ending, I wish to relate something of Leudast’s actions, but first it seems appropriate to describe his family, native country, and character. There is an island near Poitiers called Gracina, where he was born to a slave named Leuchadius who belonged to a royal vine-grower. Summoned from there to service, he was assigned to the royal kitchen. Because his eyes were sore in his youth and could not endure the harshness of the smoke, he was removed from the pestle and promoted to the bread-basket. But while pretending to delight in working the kneaded masses of dough, he ran away and abandoned his service. Brought back from flight two or three times and unable to be restrained from running away again, he was punished by having one ear slit. Later, unable by any means to conceal the mark inflicted on his body, he fled to Queen Marcoveifa, whom King Charibert loved excessively and had taken to his bed in place of her sister. She received him gladly, promoted him, and appointed him keeper of her finest horses.

Now possessed by vanity and given over to pride, he sought the office of count of the stables. Once he had obtained it, he despised and neglected everyone, was inflated with vanity, dissolute in luxury, inflamed with greed, and was carried here and there as his patroness’s own fosterling in her affairs. After her death, enriched with estates, he offered gifts to King Charibert and retained the same position. Afterward, when the sins of the people brought their consequences, he was appointed count of Tours. There he boasted still more with the arrogant pride of glorious honor, showing himself rapacious in plunder, swollen with quarrels, and filthy with adulteries. By sowing discord and bringing false accusations, he amassed considerable treasures.

After Charibert’s death, when that city had fallen to Sigibert’s share, Leudast went over to Chilperic, and all that he had unjustly accumulated was plundered by the adherents of the king I have named. When King Chilperic invaded the city of Tours through his son Theudebert, I had already come to Tours, and Theudebert strongly commended Leudast to me so that he might obtain the office of count he had previously held. Leudast made himself exceedingly humble and submissive toward me, repeatedly swearing over the holy bishop’s tomb that he would never act contrary to the order of reason and that he would be faithful to me in all things, both in my own affairs and in the needs of the Church. For he feared what later happened, namely that King Sigibert might again bring that city under his own dominion. After Sigibert died and Chilperic again succeeded to the kingdom, Leudast entered upon the office of count. When Merovech came to Tours, however, he plundered all Leudast’s property wherever it could be found. Leudast himself had hidden in Brittany during the two years when Sigibert held Tours.

Once he had obtained the countship, as I have said, he was carried away by such fickleness that he would enter the church house wearing a breastplate and coat of mail, girt with a quiver, carrying a spear in his hand, and with a helmet on his head. He felt safe from no one because he was hostile to everyone. If he sat in judgment with elders, whether laymen or clergy, and saw anyone pursuing justice, he would immediately fly into a rage and belch out insults against the citizens. He ordered priests to be dragged out in handcuffs and soldiers to be beaten with clubs, exercising a cruelty that can scarcely be described. When Merovech, who had plundered his property, departed, Leudast became my accuser, falsely asserting that Merovech had followed my advice in taking his possessions. But after inflicting these losses he again swore oaths and gave the pall from blessed Martin’s tomb as surety that he would never oppose me.

### 49. Of the snares which he made for us

<a id="hist-05-en-050"></a>
[**[050]**](#hist-05-la-050) It would take too long to recount in order all Leudast’s perjuries and other evil deeds. Let me come instead to the way he tried to supplant me with unjust and abominable calumnies, and the way divine vengeance descended upon him, fulfilling the words, “Everyone who supplants will be supplanted,” and again, “He who digs a pit will fall into it.”

After the many evils he inflicted on me and mine, and after many plunderings of ecclesiastical property, he joined to himself the priest Riculf, a man perverted by similar malice, and went so far as to say that I had alleged a crime against Queen Fredegund. He asserted that if my archdeacon Plato or my friend Galienus were subjected to punishment, they would certainly prove that I had spoken these words. The king was enraged and, as I said above, ordered Leudast to be beaten with fists and heels, loaded with chains, and shut up in prison. Leudast claimed that he had the cleric Riculf through whom I was supposed to have spoken.

This Riculf, a subdeacon equally prone to fickleness, had taken counsel with Leudast about the affair a year earlier and sought grounds for taking offense, so that he might take offense at me and go over to Leudast. Having finally found them, he went to Leudast, prepared every deceit and snare for four months, and then returned to me with Leudast himself, begging me to receive him back with his pardon. I confess that I did so, and publicly received a secret enemy into my house. When Leudast departed, Riculf prostrated himself at my feet and said, “Unless you help me quickly, I am going to perish. Behold, at Leudast’s instigation I have said what I ought not to have said. Send me away to another kingdom; if you do not, I will be seized by the royal officers and suffer mortal punishment.” I answered, “If you have uttered anything contrary to reason, your word will fall upon your own head. I will not send you to another kingdom, lest I become suspect in the king’s eyes.”

Leudast then became Riculf’s accuser, saying that he had heard the aforementioned statements from the subdeacon Riculf. Riculf was again placed in chains and committed to custody, while Leudast was released. Riculf said that Galienus and the archdeacon Plato had been present on the day when the bishop uttered these words. Meanwhile the priest Riculf, who had already received Leudast’s promise of the episcopate, was so elated that he equaled the pride of Simon Magus. Although he had sworn an oath to me three times or more over Saint Martin’s tomb, on the Friday of Easter week he assailed me with such insults and spitting that he could scarcely keep his hands off me, confident in the snare he had prepared.

The next day, Holy Saturday itself, Leudast came to the city of Tours. Pretending to be engaged in other business, he seized the archdeacon Plato and Galienus, bound them in chains, and ordered them to be led to the queen chained and stripped of their clothing. When I heard this while sitting in the church house, sad and troubled, I entered the oratory and took up the book of David’s songs, hoping that when opened it would offer some verse of consolation. There I found: “He led them forth in hope, and they were not afraid; and the sea covered their enemies.” Meanwhile the prisoners entered the river on the bridge, which was supported by two boats. The boat carrying Leudast sank, and had he not saved himself by swimming, he might have perished with his companions. The other boat, which was lashed to it and carried the prisoners, was held up above the water by God’s help.

The prisoners were brought before the king and vehemently accused, so that they might be sentenced to death. But the king reconsidered, released them from their chains, and kept them unharmed under free custody. Duke Berulf and Count Eunomius came to Tours and invented a story that King Guntram wished to capture the city. They said that the city must therefore be secured by guards to prevent any negligence. They deceitfully posted guards at the gates who, while pretending to protect the city, were actually guarding me. They also sent men to advise me to take the church’s finest possessions and flee secretly to Auvergne, but I did not consent.

The king summoned the bishops of his kingdom and ordered the case to be diligently examined. While the cleric Riculf was repeatedly questioned in secret and spread many falsehoods against me and mine, a certain carpenter named Modestus said to him, “You wretched man, why do you stubbornly devise these things against your bishop? It would be better for you to be silent, seek the bishop’s pardon, and obtain his favor.” Riculf began to shout aloud, “Here is the man who orders me to be silent so that I may not pursue the truth! Here is the queen’s enemy, who will not allow the charge against her to be investigated!” This was immediately reported to the queen. Modestus was seized, tortured, scourged, bound in chains, and placed in custody. Held in chains and stocks between two guards, at midnight, while the guards slept, he poured out a prayer to the Lord, begging him to deign in his power to visit the wretched man, and asking that he who had been bound though innocent might be released through the visitation of bishops Martin and Medard. Soon his bonds burst apart, the stocks broke, the door opened, and while I was keeping vigil at night he entered the basilica of Saint Medard.

The bishops assembled at the villa of Brinnacum and were ordered to sit together in one house. The king then arrived, greeted everyone, received a blessing, and sat down. Berthram, bishop of the city of Bordeaux, who had been implicated in this charge with the queen, presented the case and challenged me, saying that I had alleged the crime against him and the queen. I truthfully denied having said it, though I acknowledged that I had heard others say it, not that I had invented it. Outside the house there was a great outcry among the people: “Why are these charges brought against God’s priest? Why does the king pursue such things? Could the bishop have said such things even about a slave? Alas, alas, Lord God, give help to your servant!”

The king said, “A charge against my wife is regarded as a reproach to me. If you judge that witnesses should be produced against the bishop, behold, they are here. But if you decide that this should not be done and that the matter should be committed to the bishop’s word, say so; I will willingly hear your instructions.” Everyone marveled at the king’s prudence and patience. When all declared, “An inferior person cannot be believed against a priest,” the resolution was that Masses should be celebrated at three altars and that I should clear myself of these words by an oath. Although this was contrary to the canons, it was nevertheless carried out for the king’s sake. I will not conceal that Queen Rigunthis, sharing my distress, fasted with her entire household until a servant brought word that I had fulfilled everything exactly as prescribed.

The bishops then returned to the king and said, “The bishop has fulfilled everything that was commanded. What now remains for you, O king, except to deprive Berthram, his brother’s accuser, of communion?” Berthram answered, “I have only reported what I heard.” When they asked who had told him, he replied that he had heard it from Leudast. Leudast, true to the weakness of his counsel and purpose, had already fled. All the bishops then resolved that the sower of scandal, defamer of the queen, and accuser of the bishop should be barred from every church because he had withdrawn himself from the hearing. They sent a subscribed letter to the other bishops who had not been present, and each returned to his own place.

When Leudast heard this, he sought refuge in the basilica of Saint Peter at Paris. But when he heard the king’s edict that no one in his kingdom should shelter him, and especially when he learned that his son whom he had left at home had died, he came secretly to Tours and transferred his best possessions to Berry. The king’s servants pursued him, but he escaped by flight. His wife was captured and confined in exile in the district of Tournai. The cleric Riculf was condemned to death. I with difficulty obtained his life, but could not secure exemption from torture. No object and no metal could have endured such blows as this most wretched man. From the third hour of the day he hung suspended from a tree with his hands tied behind his back. At the ninth hour he was taken down, stretched on the rack, and beaten with clubs, rods, and doubled leather straps, not by one or two men, but by as many torturers as could crowd around his wretched limbs. When he was at the point of death, he revealed the truth and publicly laid bare the secrets of the plot. He said that the charge had been brought against the queen so that she might be expelled from the kingdom; then, after her brothers and father had been killed, Clovis would obtain the kingdom, Leudast the duchy, and the priest Riculf—who had been Clovis’s friend since the time of blessed Bishop Eufronius—would seek the episcopate of Tours, having promised the archdeaconate to the cleric Riculf.

When I returned to Tours by God’s grace, I found the church thrown into confusion by the priest Riculf. Under Bishop Eufronius he had been advanced from among the poor and ordained archdeacon; subsequently promoted to the presbyterate, he had withdrawn to his own property. He was always elated, inflated, and presumptuous. While I was still staying with the king, he impudently entered the church house as though he were already bishop, inventoried the church’s silver, and brought its remaining property under his own authority. He enriched the senior clergy with gifts, bestowed vineyards, and distributed meadows, but afflicted the junior clergy with many blows from clubs and even with his own hand, saying, “Acknowledge your lord, who has obtained victory over his enemies and whose genius has cleansed the city of Tours of the people of Auvergne.” The wretch did not know that, apart from five bishops, all the others who have held the episcopate of Tours were related to the stock of my ancestors. He often used to tell his intimates that a prudent man could be deceived only by perjuries.

Even after my return he continued to despise me. He did not come to greet me as the other citizens had done, but rather threatened to kill me. On the advice of the provincials I ordered him to be removed to a monastery. When he was being strictly confined there, messengers from Bishop Felix, who had supported the affair mentioned above, intervened. The abbot was circumvented by perjuries, and Riculf escaped and went to Bishop Felix, who eagerly received a man whom he ought to have abhorred.

Leudast meanwhile went to Berry, taking with him all the treasures he had stripped from the poor. Not long afterward the men of Berry, together with the local judge, rushed upon him and took all the gold, silver, and other things he had brought, leaving him nothing but what he wore. They would have taken his life as well had he not escaped by flight. Recovering his strength, he returned with some men of Tours and attacked his robbers, killed one, recovered part of the property, and came back to the Touraine. When Duke Berulf heard this, he sent his servants with arms to seize him. Seeing that capture was imminent, Leudast abandoned his possessions and sought refuge in the basilica of Saint Hilary at Poitiers. Duke Berulf sent the captured property to the king. Leudast would leave the basilica, burst into various people’s houses, and openly carry off plunder. He was also often caught in adultery in the holy portico itself. The queen, indignant that a place consecrated to God should be polluted in this way, ordered him to be expelled from the holy basilica. Once expelled, he went back to his hosts in Berry and begged them to hide him.

### 50. What the blessed Salvius foretold of Chilperic

<a id="hist-05-en-051"></a>
[**[051]**](#hist-05-la-051) Although I ought to have mentioned above my conversation with the blessed bishop Salvius, it escaped my mind, and I do not consider it sacrilegious to write it down afterward. After the synod mentioned above, when I had already taken leave of the king and wished to return home, I resolved not to depart until I had kissed this man and taken leave of him. I looked for him and found him in the atrium of the house at Brinnacum. I told him that I was now about to return home. We withdrew a short distance, and while we were conversing he said to me, “Do you see above this roof what I behold?” I answered, “I see the new roofing that the king recently ordered to be put on.” He asked, “Do you see nothing else?” I replied, “I see nothing else.” I suspected that he was speaking jokingly, and added, “If you see something more, describe it.” Drawing a deep sigh, he said, “I see the drawn sword of divine wrath hanging over this house.” The priest’s words did not prove false, for twenty days later the king’s two sons, whose deaths I described above, died.

## Praefatio

<a id="hist-05-la-001"></a>
[**[001]**](#hist-05-en-001) Taedit me bellorum civilium diversitatis, que Francorum gentem et regnum valde proterunt, memorare; in quo, quod peius est, tempore illud quod Dominus de dolorum praedixit initium iam videmus: Consurgit pater in filium, filius in patrem, frater in fratrem, proximus in propinquum. Debebant enim eos exempla anteriorum regum terrere, qui, ut divisi, statim ab inimicis sunt interempti. Quotiens et ipsa urbs urbium et totius mundi capud ingens bella civilia diruit; quae cessante, rursum quasi ab humo surrexit. Utinam et vos, o regis, in his proelia, in quibus parentes vestri desudaverunt, exercimini, ut gentes, vestra pace conterritae, vestris viribus praemirentur! Recordamini, quid capud victuriarum vestrarum Chlodovechus fecerit, qui adversos reges interficet, noxias gentes elisit, patrias subiugavit, quarum regnum vobis integrum inlesumque reliquit! Et cum hoc facerit, neque aurum neque argentum, sicut nunc est in thesauris vestris, habebat. Quid agetis? Quid quaeritis? Quid non habundatis? In domibus dilitiae supercrescunt, in prumtuariis vinum, triticum oleumque redundat, in thesauris aurum atque argentum coacervatur. Unum vobis deest, quod, pacem non habentes, Dei gratiam indegetis. Cur unus tollit alteri suum? Cur alter concupiscit alienum? Cavete illo, quaeso, apostoli: Si ab invicem mordetis et comeditis, vidite, ne ab invicem consummamini. Scrutamini diligenter veterum scripta, et videbitis, quid civilia bella parturiant. Requirite, quod de Carthaginensibus scribat Orhosius, qui, cum post septingentos annos subversam dicat civitatem et regionem eorum, addidit: Quae res eam tamdiu servavit? Concordia. Quae res eam post tanta distruxit tempora? Discordia. Cavete discordiam, cavete bella civilia, quae vos populumque vestrum expugnant. Quid aliud sperandum erit, nisi cum exercitus vester caeciderit, vos sine solatio relicti atque a gentibus adversis oppressi protinus conruatis? Si tibi, o rex, bellum civili delectat, illut quod apostolus in hominem agi meminit exerce, ut spiritus concupiscat adversus carnem et vitia virtutibus caedant; et tu liber capite tuo, id est Christo, servias, qui quondam radicem malorum servieras conpeditus.

## 1. De Childeberthi iunioris regno et matre eius.

<a id="hist-05-la-002"></a>
[**[002]**](#hist-05-en-002) Igitur interempto Sigybertho rege apud Victuriacum villam, Brunichildis regina cum filiis Parisius resedebat. Quod factum cum ad eam perlatum fuisset et, conturbata dolore aclucto, quid ageret ignoraret, Gundovaldusduxadpraehensum Childeberthum, filium eius parvolum, furtim abstulit ereptumque ab immenente morte, collectisque gentibus super quas pater eius regnum tenuerat, regem instituit, vix lustro aetatis uno iam peracto. Qui die dominici natalis regnare coepit. Anno igitur primo regni eius Chilpericus rex Parisius venit adpraehensamque Brunichildem apud Rodomaginsem civitatem in exilio trusit thesaurisque eius, quos Parisius detulerat, abstulit; filias vero eius Meledus urbe tenire praecipit. Tunc Roccolenus cum Cinomannicis Toronus venit et praedas egit et multa scelera fecit, quod in sequenti, qualiter a virtute beati Martini pro tantis malis quae gessit percussus interiit, memoramus.

## 2. Quod Merovechus Brunechilde accepit.

<a id="hist-05-la-003"></a>
[**[003]**](#hist-05-en-003) Chilpericus vero filium suum Merovechum cum exercitu Pectavus dirigit. At ille, relictam ordinationem patris, Toronus venit ibique et dies sanctos paschae tenuit. Multum enim regionem illam exercitus eius vastavit. Ipsi vero simolans ad matrem suam ire velle, Rodomago petiit; et ibi Brunichilde reginae coniungitur, ea quoque sibi in matrimonio sociavit. Haec audiens Chilpericus, quod scilicet contra fas legemque canonicam uxorem patrui accepisset, valde amarus dicto citius ad supra memoratum oppidum dirigit. At ille, cum haec cognovissent, quod eosdem separare decernerit, ad basilicam sancti Martini, quae super murus civitatis ligneis tabulis fabrecata est, confugium faciunt. Rex vero adveniens, cum in multis ingeniis eos exinde auferre niteretur et illi, dolosae eum potantes facere, non crederent, iuravit eis, dicens: 'Si', inquid, 'voluntas Dei foret, ipse hos separare non conaretur'. Haec illi sacramenta accipientes, de basilica egressi sunt; exosculatisque et dignanter acceptis, epulavit cum eis. Post dies vero paucus adsumpto secum rex Merovecho Sessionas rediit.

## 3. Bellum contra Chilpericum, et de malitia Rauchingi.

<a id="hist-05-la-004"></a>
[**[004]**](#hist-05-en-004) Cum autem ibidem commorarentur, collecti aliqui de Campania Sessionas urbe adgrediuntur, fugatamque ex ea Fredegundem regina atque Chlodovechum, filium Chilperici, volebant sibi subdere civitatem. Quod ut Chilpericus rex conperit, cum exercitu illuc dirigit, mittens nuntius, ne sibi iniuriam facerent et excidium de utroque eveniret exercitu. Illi autem haec neglegentes, praeparantur ad bellum; commissoque proelio, invaluit pars Chilperici atque fugavit partem sibi adversam, multus ex ea strenuos atque utilis viros prosternens; fugatusque reliquos, Sessionas ingreditur. Quae postquam acta sunt, rex propter coniugatione Brunichildis suspectum habere coepit Merovechum, filium suum, dicens, hoc proelium eius nequitia surrexisse; spoliatumque ab armis, datis custodibus, libere custodire praecipit, tractans, quid de eo in posterum ordinaret. Godinus autem, qui a sorte Sigyberthi se ad Chilpericum transtulerat et multis ab eo muneribus locopletatus est, caput belli istius fuit; sed in campo victus, primus fuga dilabitur. Villas vero, quas ei rex a fisco in territurio Sessionico indulserat, abstulit et basilicae contulit beati Medardi. Ipse quoque Godinus non post multum tempus repentina morte praeventus, interiit. Cuius coniugem Rauchingus accepit, vir omni vanitate repletus, superbia tumidus, elatione protervus, qui se ita cum subiectis agebat, ut non cognusceret in se aliquid humanitatis habere, sed ultra modum humanae malitiae atque stultitiae in suos deseviens, nefanda mala gerebat. Nam si ante eum, ut adsolet, convivio utenti puer cereum tenuisset, nudari eius tibias faciebat atque tamdiu in his cereum comprimi, donec lumine privaretur; iterum cum inluminatus fuisset, similiter faciebat, usque dum totae tibiae famuli tenentes exurirentur. Quod si vocem emittere aut se de loco illo alia in parte movere conatus fuisset, nudus ilico gladius imminibat, fiebatque, ut, hoc flente, iste magna laetitia exultaret. Aiebant enim quidam, quod eo tempore duo de famulis eius, ut sepe contingit, mutuo se amore dilixisse, virum scilicet et puella. Cumque haec dilectio per duorum annorum aut eo amplius spatio traheretur, coniuncti pariter aeclesiam petierunt. Quod cum Rauchingus conperisset, accedit ad sacerdotem loci; rogat, sibi protinus reddi suos famulos excusatus. Tunc sacerdos ait ad eum: 'Nosti enim, quae veneratio debeat inpendi aeclesiis Dei; non enim poteris eos accepere, nisi ut fidem facias de permanente eorum coniunctione; similiter et ut de omni poena corporali liberi maneant repromittas'. At ille, cum diu ambiguus cogitatione siluisset, tandem conversus ad sacerdotem, posuit manus suas super altarium cum iuramento, dicens, quia: 'Numquam erunt a me separandi, sed potius ego faciam, ut in hacconiunctionepermaneant, quia, quamquam mihi molestum fuerit, quod absque mei consilii coniventia ista sint gesta, illud tamen libens amplectur, quod nec hic ancillam alterius neque haec extranei servum acceperit'. Crededit sacerdus illi simpliciter promissionem hominis callidi reddeditque hominis excusatos. Quibus ille acceptis et gratias agens, abscessit ad domum suam. Et statim iussit elidere arborem truncatamque colomnam eius per capita cuneo scissam praecipit excavare; effossamque in altitudine trium aut quattuor pedum humum, deponi vas iubet in foveam. Ibique puellam ut mortuam conponens, puerum desuper iactare praecipit, positoque operturium, fossam humo replevit sepelivitque eos viventes, dicens, quia: 'Non frustravi iuramentum meum, ut non separarentur hi in sempiternum'. Qui cum sacerdote nuntiata fuissents, cucurrit velociter; et increpans hominem, vix obtenuit, ut detegerentur. Verumtamen puerum vivum extraxit, puellam vero repperit suffocatam. In talibus enim operibus valde nequissimus erat, nullam habens aliam potius utilitatem, nisi in caccinnis ac dolis omnibusque perversis rebus. Unde non inmerito taliter excessit a vita, qui talia gessit, cum frueretur hanc vitam; quod in posterum degesturi sumus. Siggo quoque referendarius, qui anolum regis Sigyberthi tenuerat et ab Chilperico rege ita provocatus erat, ut servitium, quod tempore fratris sui habuerat, obteneret, ad Childeberthum regem, Sigyberthi filium, relicto Chilperico, transivit resque eius, quas in Sessionico habuerat, Ansovaldus obtinuit. Multi autem et alii de his, qui se de regno Sigyberthi ad Chilpericum tradiderant, recesserunt. Uxor quoque Siggonis non post multum temporis spatio obiit; sed ille aliam rursus accepit.

## 4. Quod Roccolenus Toronus venit.

<a id="hist-05-la-005"></a>
[**[005]**](#hist-05-en-005) His diebus Roccolenus, ab Chilperico missus, Toronus advenit cum magna iactantia, et ultra Legerem castra ponens, nuntios ad nos dirixit, ut scilicit Gunthchramnum, qui tunc de morte Theudoberthi inpetebatur, a basilica sancta deberemus extrahere. Quod si non facerimus, et civitatem et omnia suburbana eius iuberet incendio concremare. Quo auditu, mittimus ad eum legationem, dicentes, haec ab antiquo facta non fuisse, quae hic fieri deposcebat, sed nec modo permitti posse, ut basilica sancta violaretur; quod si fierit, nec sibi fore prosperum nec rege, qui haec iussa mandasset; metueretque magis sanctitatem antestetis, cuius virtus hesterna die paralitica membra dirixisset. Nihil his ille furmidans, cum in domo aeclesiae ultra Legerem resederet, domum ipsam, quae clavis adfixerat, dissecet. Ipsos quoque clavos Caenomannici, qui tunc cum eodem advenerant, inpletis follibus portant, annonas evertunt et cuncta devastant. Sed dum haec Roccolenus agit, a Deo percutitur, morboque regio croceus effectus, mandata aspera remittit, dicens: 'Nisi hodie proieceritis Gunthchramnum ducem de basilica, ita cuncta virentia quae sunt circa urbem atteram, ut dignus fiat aratro locus ille'. Interim advenit dies sanctus epifaniae; et hic magis ac magis torquere coepit. Tunc accepto a suis consilio, amne transacto, ad civitatem accedit. Denique cum psallentes de ęclesiam egressi, ad sanctam basilicam properarent, hic post crucem, praecidentibus signis, aequo superpositus ferebatur. Verum ubi basilicam sanctam introit, mox furor minantis intepuit; regressusque ad eclesiam, nihil cibi ea die accipere potuit. Exinde cum valde anillus esset, Pectavo abiit. Erant enim dies sanctae quadraginsimae, in qua fetus cunicolorum saepe comedit. Dispositis vero actionibus, quibus in Kalendas Martias cives Pectavus vel adfligeret vel damnaret, pridie animam reddedit; et sic superbia tumorque quievit.

## 5. De episcopis Lingonicis.

<a id="hist-05-la-006"></a>
[**[006]**](#hist-05-en-006) Eo tempore Felix Namneticae urbis episcopus litteras mihi scripsit plenas opprobriis, scribens etiam fratrem meum ob hoc interfectum, eo quod ipse cupidus episcopati episcopum interfecisset. Sed ut haec scriberet, villam ecclesiae concupivit. Quam cum dare nollem, evomit in me, ut dixi, plenus furore opprobria mille. Cui aliquando ego respondi: 'Memento dicti prophetici: Vae his qui iungunt domum ad domum et agrum ad agrum copulant! Numquid soli inhabitabunt terram? O si te habuisset Massilia sacerdotem! Numquam naves oleum aut reliquas species detulissent, nisi caitam tantum, quo maiorem oportunitatem scribendi ad bonos infamandos haberes. Sed paupertas cartae finem inponit verbositati'. Inmensae enim erat cupiditatis atque iactantiae. Sed ego ista postponens, ne illi similis appaream, illud explicabo, qualiter germanus meus ab hac luce migraverit, et quam velocem in percussorem eius Dominus praestiterit ultionem. Consenescente beato Tetrico ecclesiae Lingonicae sacerdote, cum Lampadio diacono, quem creditorem habuit, deicisset et frater meus, dum pauperibus, quos ille male spoliaverat, opitulare cupiens, consensisset in eius humiliatione, odium ex hoc incurrit. Interea beatus Tetricus a sanguine sauciatur. Cui cum nulla medicorum fomenta valerent, conturbati clerici et a pastore utpote destituti, Mundericum expetunt. Qui a rege indultus ac tonsoratus, episcopus ordinatur, sub ea specie, ut, dum beatus Tetricus viveret, hic Ternoderinsim castrum ut archipresbiter regerit atque in eo commoraretur, migrante vero decessore, iste succederet. In quo castro dum habitaret, iram regis incurrit. Adserebatur enim contra eum, quod ipse Sigibertho regi adversus fratrem suum Guntchramnum venienti alimenta et munera praebuisset. Igitur extractus a castro, in exilio super ripam Rhodani in turre quadam arta atque detecta retruditur; in qua per duos fere annos cum grandi exitu commoratus, obtinente beato Nicetio episcopo, Lugduno regreditur habitavitque cum eo per duos menses. Sed cum obtinere non posset a rege, ut in loco, unde eiectus fuerat, restitueretur, nocte per fugam lapsus, ad Sigiberti regnum pertransiit et apud Arisitensim vicum episcopus instituetur, habens sub se plus minus dioceses XV, quas primum quidem Gothi tenuerant, nunc vero Dalmatius Rutenensis episcopus iudicat. Quo abeunte, iterum Lingonici Silvestrum, propinquum vel nostrum vel beati Tetrici, episcopum expetunt. Sed ut eum peterent, fratris mei hoc instinctu fecerunt. Interea transeunte beato Tetrico, hic, tonso capite, presbiter ordinatur, accepta omni potestate de rebus ecclesiae. Qui vero, ut benedictionem episcopalem Lugduno accipiat,' iter parat. Quae dum aguntur, ipse, quia iam diu epilenticus erat, ab hoc morbo correptus, asperius ex sensu factus et per dies duos assiduae dans mugitum, tertia die spiritum exalavit. Quae peracta, Lampadius, ut superius dictum est, ab honore et facultate privatus, in odio Petri diaconi cum filio Silvestri coniungitur, machinans atque confirmans, patrem suum ab ipso fuisse maleficiis interfectum. At ille aetate iuvenis, sensu levis contra eum commovetur, ipsum inpetens publice parricidam. Porro ille haec audiens, facto placito in praesentia sancti Niceti episcopi, avunculi matris meae, Lugduno dirigitur; et ibi, Siagrio episcopo coram adstante vel aliis sacerdotibus multis cum saecularium principibus, se sacramento exuit, numquam se in morte Silvestri mixtum fuisse. Post duos vero annos instigatus iterum a Lampadio filius Silvestri, adsecutus in via Petrum diaconum, lancea sauciatum interfecit. Quod cum factum fuisset, de eo loco elevatus et ad Divionensim delatus castrum, secus sanctum Gregorium, proavum nostrum, sepelitur. Iste vero fugam iniens, ad Chilpericum regem transiit, facultatibus suis fisco regis Guntchramni dimissis. Cumque per diversa vagaretur pro commisso scelere nec ei essit locus firmus ad commorandum, tandem, ut credo, contra eum sanguine insonte ad divinam potentiam proclamante, in quodam loco, dum iter ageret, innocentem hominem evaginato gladio interemit. Cuius parentes condolentes propinqui exitum, commota seditione, extractis gladiis, eum in frustra concidunt membratimque dispergunt. Tale iusto iudicio Dei exitum miser accepit, ut, qui propinquum innocentem interimerat, ipse nocens diutius non maneret. Nam tertio ei haec evenerunt anno. Denique Silvestri post transitum Lingonici iterum episcopum flagitantes, Pappolum, qui quondam archidiaconus Agustidunensis fuerat, accipiunt. Qui multa, ut asserunt, egit iniqua, quae a nobis praetermittuntur, ne detractores fratrum esse videamur. Tamen, quale habuerit exitum, non omittam. Anno octavo episcopatus sui, dum dioceses ac villas ecclesiae circuiret, quadam nocte dormienti apparuit beatus Tetricus vultu minaci. Cui ita: 'Quid tu', inquit, 'hic Pappole? Ut quid sedem meam polluis? Ut quid ecclesiam pervadis? Ut quid oves mihi creditas sic dispergis? Cede loco, relinque sedem, abscede longius a regione'. Et haec dicens, virgam quam habebat in manu pectori eius cum ictu valido inpulit. In quo ille evigilans, dum cogitat, quid hoc esset, ficta in loco illo defigitur ac dolore maximo cruciatur. Abhorret cibum potumque et mortem iam sibi proximam praestolatur. Quid plura? Tertia die, cum sanguinem ore proicerit, exspiravit; exinde elatus, Lingonas est sepultus. In cuius loco Mummolus abba, quem Bonum cognomento vocant, episcopus substituitur. Quem multi magnis laudibus prosecuntur: esse eum castum, sobrium, moderatum ac in omni bonitate promptissimum, amantem iustitiam, caritatem omni intentione diligentem. Qui, accepto episcopatu, cognoscens, quod Lampadius multum de rebus fraudasset ecclesiae ac de spoliis pauperum agros vineasque vel mancipia congregasset, eum ab omni re nudatum a praesentia sua iussit abigi. Qui nunc in maxima paupertate degens, manibus propriis victum quaerit. Haec de his sufficiant.

## 6. De Leunaste archidiacono Bitorigo.

<a id="hist-05-la-007"></a>
[**[007]**](#hist-05-en-007) Anno vero quo supra, id est quo, mortuo Sigybertho, Childeberthus, filius eius, regnare coepit, multae virtutes ad sepulchrum beati Martini apparuerunt, quas in illis libellis scripsi, quos de ipsis miraculis conponere temptavi. Et licet sermone rustico, tamen celare passus non sum, quae aut ipse vidi aut a fidelibus relata cognovi. Hic tantum, quid neglegentibus evenerit, qui post virtutem caelestem terrena medicamenta quaesierunt, exsolvam, quia, sicut per gratiam sanitatum, ita et in castigationem stultorum virtus eius ostenditur. Leonastis Biturigus archidiaconus, decedentibus cataractis, lumen caruit oculorum. Qui cum, per multos medicos ambulans, nihil omnino visionis recipere possit, accessit ad basilicam beati Martini; ubi per duos aut tres menses consistens et ieiunans assiduae, lumen ut reciperet flagitabat. Adveniente autem festivitate, clarificatis oculis cernere coepit; regressus quoque domum, vocato quodam Iudaeo, ventosas, quorum beneficio oculis lumen augeret, humeris superponit. Decedente quoque sanguine, rursus in redeviva caecitate redigitur. Quod cum factum fuisset, rursum ad sanctum templum regressus est. Ibique iterum longo spatio commoratus, lumen recipere non meruit. Quod ei ob peccatum non praestitum reor iuxta illud dominicum oraculum: Qui habet, dabitur ei et habundavit; et qui non habet, ipsum quod habet aufertur ab eo; et illud: Ecce sanus factus es, iam noli peccare, ne quid tibi deterius eveniat. Nam perstiterat hic in sanitate, si Iudaeum non induxisset super divinam virtutem. Tales enim et monet et arguit apostulus, dicens: Nolite enim iugum ducere cum infidelibus. Quae enim participatio iustitiae cum iniquitate? Aut quae societas luci cumtenebris? Quae autem communicatio Christi cum Belial? Aut quae pars fideli cum infidele? Qui autem consensus templo Dei cum idolis? Vos estis enim templum Dei vivi. Propterea exite de medio eorum et separamini ab his, ait Dominus. Ideo doceat unumquemque christianum haec causa, ut, quando caelestem accipere meruerit medicinam, terrena non requirat studia.

## 7. De Senoch reclauso.

<a id="hist-05-la-008"></a>
[**[008]**](#hist-05-en-008) Sed et illud commemorare libet, qui vel quales viri hoc anno a Domino sunt vocati. Unde magnum eum et Deo acceptabilem ego censeo, qui tales de nostra terra suo paradiso collocavit. Nam benedictus Senoch presbiter, qui apud Turonus morabatur, sic migravit a saeculo. Fuit enim genere Theifalus, et in Turonico clericus factus, in cellulam, quam ipse inter parietes antiquos conposuerat, se removit, collectisque monachis, oratorium, quod multo tempore dirutum fuerat, reparavit. Idem super infirmos multas virtutes fecit, quas in libro vitae eius scripsimus.

## 8. De sancto Germano Parisiorum episcopo.

<a id="hist-05-la-009"></a>
[**[009]**](#hist-05-en-009) Eo anno et beatus Germanus Parisiorum episcopus transiit. In cuius exsequiis multis virtutibus, quas in corpore gesserat, hoc miraculum confirmationem fecit. Nam carcerarius adclamantibus, corpus in platea adgravatum est, solutisque eisdem, rursum sine labore levatur. Ipsi quoque, qui soluti fuerant, in obsequium funeris usque basilicam, in qua sepultus est, liberi pervenerunt. Ad sepulchrum autem eius multas virtutes, Domino tribuente, credentes experiuntur, ita ut quisque iusta petierit velociter exoptata reportet. Quis tamen strenuus virtutes illius, quas in corpore fecit, sollicite vult inquirere, librum vitae illius, qui a Fortunato presbitero conpositus est, legens, cuncta repperiet.

## 9. De Caluppane reclauso.

<a id="hist-05-la-010"></a>
[**[010]**](#hist-05-en-010) Eodem quoque anno et Caluppa reclausus obiit. Hic autem ab infantia sua semper religiosus fuit, et apud monasterium Melitensim termini Arverni conversus, in magna se humilitate fratribus praebuit, sicut in libro vitae eius scripsimus.

## 10. De Patroclo reclauso.

<a id="hist-05-la-011"></a>
[**[011]**](#hist-05-en-011) Fuit autem in Biturigo termino reclausus nomine Patroclus, presbiterii honore praeditus, mirae sanctitatis ac religionis, vir magnae abstinentiae, qui plerumque ab inaedia diversis incommodis vexabatur. Vinum, sicera vel omne quod inebriare potest non bibebat praeter aquam parumper melle linitam; sed nec pulmentum aliquod utebatur. Cuius victus erat panis in aqua infusus atque sale respersus. Huius oculi numquam caligaverunt. Erat enim in oratione assiduus, quam si parumper praetermisisset, aut legebat aut scribebat. Frigoriticis, pustulis laborantibus vel reliquis morbis saepe per orationem remedia conferebat. Sed et alia signa multa fecit, quae per ordinem longum est enarrare. Cilicium semper puro adhibens corpori. Qui octogenaria aetate abscedens a saeculo, migravit ad Christum. Scripsimus et de huius vita libellum.

## 11. De Iudaeis conversis per Avitum episcopum.

<a id="hist-05-la-012"></a>
[**[012]**](#hist-05-en-012) Et quia semper Deus noster sacerdotes suos glorificare dignatur, quid Arverno de Iudaeis hoc anno contigerit pandam. Cum eosdem plerumque beatus Avitus episcopus commoneret, ut, relicto velamine legis Moysaicae, spiritaliter lecta intellegerent et Christum, filium viventis Dei, prophetica et legali auctoritate promissum, corde purissimo in sacris litteris contemplarent, manebat in pectoribus eorum, iam non dicam, velamen illud, quod facies Moysi obumbrabatur, sed paries. Sacerdos quoque orans, ut, conversi ad Dominum, velamen ab eis litterae rumperetur, quidam ex his ad sancta pascha ut baptizaretur expetiit, renatusque Deo per baptismi sacramentum, cum albatis reliquis in albis et ipse procedit. Ingredientibus autempopulis portam civitatis, unus Iudaeorum super capud conversi Iudaei oleum foetidum, diabulo instigante, diffudit. Quod cum cunctus aborrens populus voluissent eum urguere lapidibus, pontifex ut fieret non permisit. Die autem beato, quo Dominus ad caelos post redemptum hominem gloriosus ascendit, cum sacerdos de aeclesiam ad basilicam psallendo procederet, inruit super sinagogae Iudaeorum multitudo tota sequentium, distructamque a fundamentis, campi planitiae locus adsimilatur. Alia autem die sacerdos eis legatos mittit, dicens: 'Vi ego vos confiteri Dei Filium non inpello, sed tamen praedico et salem scientiae vestris pectoribus trado. Pastor sum enim dominicis ovibus superpositus; et de vobis ille verus Pastor, qui pro nobis passus est, dixit, habere se alias oves quae non sunt ex ovili suo, quas eum oporteat adducere, ut fiat unus grex et unus pastor. Ideoque si vultis credere ut ego, estote unus grex, costodi me posito; sin vero aliud, abscedite a loco. Illi autem diu aestuantes atque dubitantes, tertia die, ut credo, obtentum pontificis coniuncti in unum, ad eum mandata remittunt, dicentes: 'Credimus Iesum, filium Dei vivi, nobis prophetarum vocibus repromissum; et ideo petimus, ut abluamur baptismum, ne in hoc delicto permaneamus. Gavisus autem nuntio pontifex, noctem sanctam pentecosten vigilias caelebratas, ad baptistirio forasmoraneum egressus est; ibique omnis multitudo coram eo prostrata, baptismum flagitavit. At ille prae gaudio lacrimans, cunctos aqua abluens, crismate liniens, in sinu matris eclesiae congregavit. Flagrabant caerei, lampades refulgebant, albecabat tota civitas de grege candido nec minor fuit urbi gaudium, quam quondam, Spiritu sancto discendente super apostulos, Hierusalem videre promeruit. Fuerunt autem qui baptizati sunt amplius quingenti. Hii vero qui baptismum noluerunt discedentes ab illa urbe, Massiliae redditi sunt.

## 12. De Brachione abbate.

<a id="hist-05-la-013"></a>
[**[013]**](#hist-05-en-013) Transiit post haec et Brachio abba cellulae Manatensis. Fuit autem genere Thoringus, in servitio Sigivaldi quondam ducis venationem exercens, sicut alibi scripsimus.

## 13. Quod Mummolus Lemovicas vastavit.

<a id="hist-05-la-014"></a>
[**[014]**](#hist-05-en-014) Ergo ut ad propositum revertamur, Chilpericus rex Chlodovechum filium suum Toronus transmisit. Qui, congregato exercitu, inter terminum Toronicum et Andecavum usque Sanctonas transiit eamque pervasit. Mummolus vero patricius Gunthchramni regis cum magno exercitu usque Lemovicinum transiit et contra Desiderium ducem Chilperici regis bellum gessit. In quo proelio ceciderunt de exercitu eius quinque milia, de Desiderii vero viginti quattuor milia. Ipse quoque Desiderius fugiens vix evasit. Mummolus vero patricius per Arvernum rediit eamque per loca exercitus eius devastavit. Et sic in Burgundiam peraccessit.

## 14. Quod Merovechus tunsoratus confugit.

<a id="hist-05-la-015"></a>
[**[015]**](#hist-05-en-015) Post haec Merovechus, cum in custodia a patre reteneretur, tunsoratus est, mutataque veste, qua clericis uti mos est, presbiter ordenatur et ad monasterium Cinnomannicum qui vocatur Aninsola dirigitur, ut ibi sacerdotali eruderetur regula. Haec audiens Gunthchramnus Boso, qui tunc in basilica sancti Martini, ut diximus, resedebat, misit Riculfum subdiaconum, ut ei consilium occulte praeberet expetendi ad basilicam sancti Martini. Qui cum abisset, ab alia parte Gailenus puer eius advenit. Cumque parvum solatium qui eum ducebant haberent, ab ipso Gaileno in itenere excussus est, opertoque capite indutusque veste saeculari, beati Martini templum expetiit. Nobis autem missa caelebrantibus, in sanctam basilicam, aperta repperiens ostia, ingressus est. Post missa autem petiit, ut ei eulogias dare deberemus. Erat autem tunc nobiscum Ragnemodus Parisiacae sedis episcopus, qui sancto Germano successerat. Quod cum refutaremus, ipse clamare coepit et dicere, quod non recte eum a communione sine fratrum conibentia suspenderemus. Illo autem haec dicente, cum consensu fratris qui praesens erat, contestatam causam canonicam, eulogias a nobis accepit. Veritus autem sum, ne, dum unum a communione suspendebam, in multos existerem homicida. Minibatur enim aliquos de populo nostro interficere, si communionem nostram non meruisset. Multas tamen pro hac causa Toronica regio sustenuit clades. His diebus Necetius, vir neptis meae, propriam habens causam, ad Chilpericum regem abiit cum diacono nostro, qui regi fugam Merovechi narraret. Quibus visis, Fredegundis regina ait: 'Exploratores sunt et ad sciscitandum quid agat rex advenerunt, ut sciant, quid Merovecho renuntient'. Et statim expoliatis in exilio retrudi praecepit; de quo mense septimo relaxati sunt. Igitur Chilpericus nuntius ad nos direxit, dicens: 'Eiecite apostatam illam de basilicam; sin autem aliud, totam regionem illam igne succendam'. Cumque nos rescripsissemus, inpossibile esse, quod temporibus hereticorum non fuerat, christianis nunc temporibus fieri, ipsi exercitum commovit et illuc dirigit. Anno autem secundo Childeberthi regis, cum videret Merovechus patrem in hac deliberatione intentum, adsumpto secum Gunthchramnum ducem, ad Brunichildem pergere cogitat, dicens: 'Absit, ut propter meam personam basilica domni Martini violentiam perferat aut regio eius per me captivitate subdatur'. Et ingressus basilicam, dum vigilias ageret, res quas secum habebat ad sepulchrum beati Martini exhibuit, orans, ut sibi sanctus succurrerit atque ei concederit gratiam suam, ut regnum accepere possit. Leudastis tunc comis, cum multas ei in amore Fredegundis insidias tenderit, ad extremum pueros eius, qui in pago egressi fuerant, circumventus dolis gladio trucidavit, ipsumque interimere cupiens, si repperire loco oportuno potuisset. Sed illi consilio usus Gunthchramni et se ulcisci desiderans, redeunte Marileifo archietro de praesentia regis, conpraehendi praecepit; caesumque gravissime, ablato auro argentoque eius vel reliquis rebus, quas secum exhibebat, nudum reliquit; et interfecisset utique, si non inter manu caedentium elapsus eclesiam expetisset. Quem nos postea indutum vestimentis, obtenta vita, Pectavo remisimus. Merovechus vero de patre atque novercam multa crimina loquebatur; quae cum ex parte vera essent, credo, acceptum non fuisse Deo, ut haec per filium vulgarentur, sicut in sequentibus cognovi. Quaedam enim die ad convivium eius accedi. Dum pariter sederemus, suppliciter petiit aliqua ad instructionem animae legi. Ego vero, reserato Salomonis libro, versiculum qui primus occurrit arripui, qui haec contenebat: Oculum, qui aversus aspexerit patrem, effodiant eum corvi de convallibus. Illo quoque non intellegente, consideravi hunc versiculum ad Dominum praeparatum. Tunc direxit Gunthchramnus puerum ad mulierem quandam, sibi iam cognitam a tempori Chariberthi regis, habentem spiritum phitonis, ut ei quae erant eventura narraret. Asserebat praeterea, ipsam sibi ante hoc tempus non solum annum, sed et diem et horam, in qua rex Chariberthus obiret, denuntiasse. Quae haec ei per puerus mandata remisit: 'Futurum est enim, ut rex Chilpericus hoc anno deficiat et Merovechus, exclusis fratribus, omni capiat regnum. Tu vero ducatum totius regni eius annis quinque tenebis. Sexto vero anno in una civitatum, quae super Legeris alveum sita est, in dextera eius parte, favente populo, episcopatus gratiam adipiscis ac senes et plenus dierum ab hoc mundo migrabis'. Cumque haec pueri redeuntes domino nuntiassent, statim ille vanitate elatus, tamquam se iam in cathedram Toronice eclesiae resederet, ad me haec detulit verba. Cuius ego inridens stultitiam, dixi: 'A Deo haec poscenda sunt; nam credi non debent quae diabolus repromittit. Ille autem ab initio mendax est et in veritate numquam stetit'. Illo quoque cum confusione discedente, valde inridebam hominem, qui talia credi putabat. Denique quadam nocte vigilias in basilica sancti antestitis caelebratas, dum lectulo decubans obdormissem, vidi angelum per aera volantem. Cumque super sanctam basilicam praeteriret, voce magna ait: 'Heu heu! Percussit Deus Chilpericum et omnes filios eius, nec superavit de his qui processerunt ex lumbis eius qui regat regnum illius in aeternum'. Erant ei eodem tempore de diversis uxoribus filii quattuor, excepto filiabus. Cum autem haec in posterum inpleta fuissent, tunc a liquidum cognovi, falsa esse quae promiserant arioli. Igitur commorantibus his apud basilicam sancti Martini, misit ad Guntchramnum Bosonem Fredegundis regina, quaequae ei iam pro morte Theodoberthi patrocinabatur occulte, dicens: 'Si Merovechum eiecere potueris de basilica, ut interficiatur, magnum de me munus accipies'. At ille praesto putans esse interfectores, ait ad Merovechum: 'Ut quid hic quasi signes et timidi resedemus et ut hebetis circa basilicam occulimur? Veniant enim equi nostri, et acceptis accipitribus, cum canibus exerceamur venationem spectaculisque patulis iocundemur'. Hoc enim agebat callide, ut eum a sancta basilica separaret. Gunthchramnus vero alias sane bonus – nam in periuriis nimium praeparatus erat –, verumtamen nulli amicorum sacramentum dedit, quod non protinus omisisset. Egressi itaque, ut diximus, de basilica, ad Iocundiacensim domum civitate proximam progressi sunt; sed a nemine Merovechus nocetus est. Et quia inpetebatur tunc Gunthchramnus de interitu, ut diximus, Theodobertlii, misit Chilpericus rex epistulam scriptam ad sepulchrum sancti Martini, quae habebat insertum, ut ei beatus Martinus rescriberet, utrum liceret extrahi Gunthchramnum de basilica eius an non. Sed Baudegyselus diaconus, qui hanc epistulam exhibuit, cartam puram cum eadem quam detulerat ad sanctum tumolum misit. Cumque per triduum expectasset et nihil rescripti reciperet, redivit ad Chilpericum. Ille vero misit alios, qui Gunthchramno sacramenta exigerent, ut sine eius scientiam basilicam non relinqueret. Qui ambienter iurans, pallam altaris fideiussorem dedit, numquam se exinde sine iussione rege egressurum. Merovechus vero non credens phitonissae, tres libros super sancti sepulchrum posuit, id est psalterii, Regum, euangeliorum, et vigilans tota nocte, petiit, ut sibi beatus confessor quid eveniret ostenderet, et utrum possit regnum accepere an non, ut, Domino indicante, cognuscerit. Post haec continuato triduo in ieiuniis, vigiliis atque orationibus, ad beatum tumolum iterum accedens, revolvit librum, qui erat Regum. Versus autem primus paginae, quem reseravit, hic erat: Pro eo quod dereliquistis dominum Deum vestrum et ambolastis post deos alienos nec fecistis rectum ante conspectum eius, ideo tradedit vos dominus Deus vester in manibus inimicorum vestrorum. Psalterii autem versus hic est inventus: Verum propter dolositatem posuisti eis mala; deiecisti eos, dum allevarentur. Quomodo facti sunt in desolatione? Subito defecerunt, et perierunt propter iniquitates suas. In euangeliis autem hoc repertum: Scitis, quia post biduum pascha fiet, et Filius hominis traditur, ut crueifigatur. In his responsibus ille confusus, flens diutissime ad sepulchrum beati antestetis, adsumpto secum Gunthchramno duce, cum quingentus aut eo amplius viris discessit. Egressus autem basilicam sanctam, cum iter ageret per Audisiodorensim territurium, ab Erpone duce Gunthchramni regis conpraehensus est. Cumque ab eo deteneretur, casu nescio quo dilapsus, basilica sancti Germani ingressus est. Audiens haec Gunthchramnus rex, in ira commotus, Erponem septingentis aureis damnat et ab honorem removet, dicens: 'Retenuisti, ut ait frater meus, inimicum suum. Quodsi hoc facere cogitabas, ad me eum debuisti prius adducere; sin autem aliud, nec tangere debueras, quem tenere dissimulabas'. Exercitus autem Chilperici regis usque Toronus accedens, regionem illam in praedas mittit, succendit atque devastat nec rebus sancti Martini pepercit, sed quod manum tetigit, sine ullo Dei intuetu aut timorem deripuit. Merovechus prope duos menses ad antedictam basilicam resedens, fugam iniit et ad Brunichildem reginam usque pervenit; sed ab Austrasiis non est collectus. Pater vero eius exercitum contra Campanensis commovit, putans, eum ibidem occultare. Sed nihil nocuit nec eum potuit repperire.

## 15. Bellum inter Saxonis et Suavos.

<a id="hist-05-la-016"></a>
[**[016]**](#hist-05-en-016) Et quia tempore illo, quo Alboenus in Italia ingressus est, Chlothacharius et Sigyberthus Suavos et alias gentes in loco illo posuerunt, hi qui tempore Sigyberthi regressi sunt, id est qui cum Alboeno fuerant, contra hos consurgunt, volentes eos a regione illa extrudere ac delere. At illi obtulerunt eis tertiam partem terrae, dicentes: 'Simul vivere sine conlisione possumus'. Sed ille contra eos irati, eo quod ipse hoc antea tenuissent, nullatenus pacificare voluerunt. Dehinc obtulerunt eis iterum iste medietatem, post haec duas partes, sibi tertiam relinquentes. Nolentibus autem illis, obtulerunt cum terra omnia pecora, tantumto ut a bello cessarent. Sed nec hoc illi adquiescentis, certamen expetiunt. Et inter se ante certamen, qualiter uxores Suavorum deviderent et qui quam post eorum exitu acciperet, tractant, potantes, eos iam quasi interfectus habere. Sed Domini miseratio, quae iustitiam facit, in aliam partem voluntatem eorum retorsit. Nam confligentibus illis, erant enim XXVI milia Saxonum, ex quibus XX milia caeciderunt; Suavorum quoque VI milia, ex quibus quadringenti octuagenta tantum prostiati sunt, reliqui vero victuriam obtenuerunt. Illi quoque qui ex Saxonibus remanserant detestati sunt, nullus se eorum barbam neque capillos incisurum, nisi prius se de adversariis ulciscerent. Quibus iterum decertantibus, in maiore excidio conruerunt; et sic a bello cessatum est.

## 16. De interitum Macliavi.

<a id="hist-05-la-017"></a>
[**[017]**](#hist-05-en-017) In Brittanis haec aeta sunt. Macliavus quondam et Bodicus Brittanorum comites sacramentum inter se dederant, ut, quis ex eis superviveret, filius patris alterius tamquam proprius defensaret. Mortuus autem Bodicus reliquit filium Theudericum nomen. Quem Macliavus, oblitus sacramenti, expulsum a patria, regnum patris eius accipit. Hic vero multo tempore profugus vaguusque fuit. Cui tandem misertus Deus, collectis secum a Brittania viris, se super Macliavum obiecit eumque cum filio eius Iacob gladio interemet partemque regni, quam quondam pater eius tenuerat, in sua potestate restituit; partem vero aliam Warochus, Macliavi filius, vindicavit.

## 17. De dubietate paschae.

<a id="hist-05-la-018"></a>
[**[018]**](#hist-05-en-018) Gunthchramnus vero rex duos Magnacharii quondam filios gladio interemit, pro eo quod in Austregildem reginam eiusque subolis multa detestabilia atque exsecranda proferrent, facultatesque eorum fisco suo redegit. Ipse quoque duos filios suos subito morbo oppressus perdedit; de quorum funere valde contristatus est, eo quod orbatus absque liberis remansisset. Eo anno dubietas paschae fuit. In Galliis vero nosp cum multis civitatibus quarto decimo Kalendas Maias sanctum paschae celebravimus. Alii vero cum Spanis duodecimo Kalendas Aprilis solemnitatem hanc tenuerunt; tamen, ut ferunt, fontes illi, qui in Spaniis nutu Dei conplentur, in nostrum pascha repleti sunt. Cainone vero Toronicum vicum, dum ipso glorioso resurrectionis dominicae die missae caelebrarentur, eclesia contremuit, populusque conterritus a pavore, unam vocem dedit, dicens, quod eclesia caderet, cunctique ab ea, etiam effractis ostiis, per fugam lapsi sunt. Magna post haec lues populum devastavit. Post haec Gunthcnramnus rex ad Childeberthum, nepotem suum, legatos mittit, pacem petens ac depraecans eum videre. Tunc ille cum proceribus suis ad eum venit; qui ad Pontem quem Petreum vocitant coniuncti sunt, cumsalutantes atque invicem osculantes se. Gunthchramnus rex ait: 'Evenit inpulso peccatorum meorum, ut absque liberis remanerem, et ideo peto, ut hic nepus meus mihi sit filius'. Et inponens eum super cathedram suam, cunctum ei regnum tradedit, dicens: 'Una nos parma protegat unaque asta defendat. Quod si filius habuero, te nihilhominus tamquam unum ex his reputabo, ut illa cum eis tecumque permaneat caretas, quam tibi hodie ego pollicior, teste Deo'. Proceris vero Childeberthi similiter pro eodem polliciti sunt. Et manducantes simul atque bibentes dignisque se muneribus honorantes, pacifici discesserunt, ad Chilpericum regem legationem mittentis, ut redderet, quod de eorum regno minuerat; quod si differret, campum praepararet ad bellum. Quod ille dispiciens, apud Sessionas atque Parisius circus aedificare praecepit, eosque populis spectaculum praebens.

## 18. De Praetextato episcopo et interitu Merovechi.

<a id="hist-05-la-019"></a>
[**[019]**](#hist-05-en-019) His ita gestis, audiens Chilpericus, quod Praetextatus Rothomagensis episcopus contra utilitatem suam populis munera daret, eum ad se arcessire praecepit. Quem discussum, repperit cum eodem res Brunichildis reginae conmendatas; ipsasque ablatas, eum in exilium usque sacerdotalem audientiam retenere praecepit. Coniuncto autem concilio, exhibitus est. Erant autem episcopi qui advenerant apud Parisius in basilica sancti Petri apostoli. Cui rex ait: 'Quid tibi visum est, o episcope, ut inimicum meum Merovechum, qui filius esse debuerat, cum amita sua, id est patrui sui uxore, coniungeres? An ignarus eras, quae pro hac causa canonum statuta sancsexissent? Etiam non hic solum excessisse probaris, sed etiam cum illo egisti, datis muneribus, ut ego interficerer. Hostem autem filium patri fecisti, seduxisti paecuniam plebem, ut nullus mecum fidem habitam custodiret, voluistique regnum meum in manu alterius tradere'. Haec eo dicente, infremuit multitudo Francorum voluitque ostea basilicae rumpere, quasi ut extractum sacerdotem lapidibus urgueret; sed rex prohibuit fieri. Cumque Praetextatus episcopus ea quae rex dixerat facta negaret, advenerunt falsi testis, qui ostendebant speciess aliquas, dicentes: 'Haec et haec nobis dedisti, ut Merovechum fidem promittere deberimus'. Ad haec ille dicebat: 'Verum enim dicitis vos a me saepius muneratus; sed non haec causa extetit, ut rex eieceretur a regno. Nam et cum vos mini et equos optimos et res alias praeberetis, numquid poteram aliud facere, nisi et ego vos simile sorte remunerarem?' Recedente vero regem ad metatum suum, nos, collecti in unum, sedebamus in secretarium basilicae beati Petri. Confabulantibusque nobis, subito venit Aetius archaediaconus Parisiacae eclesiae, salutatisque nobis, ait: 'Audite me, o sacerdotes Domini, qui in unum collecti estis; aut enim hoc tempore exaltabitis nomen vestrum et bonae famae gratia refulgebitis, aut certe nullus vos amodo pro Dei sacerdotibus est habiturus, si personas vestras sagaciter non eregitis aut fratrem perire permittetis'. Haec eo dicente, nullus sacerdotum ei quicquam respondit. Timebant enim regine fururem, cuius instinctu haec agebantur. Quibus intentis et ora digitis conpraementibus, ego aio::'Adtenti estote, quaeso, sermonibus meis, o sanctissimi sacerdotes Dei, et praesertim vos, qui familiariores esse regi vidimini; adhibite ei consilium sanctum atque sacerdotalem, ne exardiscens in ministrum Dei pereat ab ira eius et regnum perdat et gloriam. Haec me dicente, silebant omnes. Illis vero silentibus, adieci: 'Mementote, domini mi sacerdotes, verbi prophetici, quo ait: Si viderit speculatur iniquitatem hominis et non dixerit, reus erit animae pereuntes. Ergo nolite silere, sed praedicate et ponite ante oculos regis peccata eius, ne forte ei aliquid mali contingat et vos rei sitis pro anima eius. An ignoratis, quid novum gestum fuerit tempore? Quomodo adpraehensum Sigymundum Chlodomeris retrusit in carcerem, dixitque ei Avitus Dei sacerdus: „Ne inicias manum in eo, et cum Burgundiam petieris, victuriam obtenebis“. Ille vero abnuens quae ei a sacerdote dicta fuerant, abiit ipsumque cum uxore et filiis interemit petiitque Burgundiam, ibique obpraessus ab exercitu, interemptus est. Quid Maximus imperatur? Cum beatum Martinum conpulisset communicare cuidam homicide episcopo, et ille, quo facilius addictus morte liberaret, regi impio consensisset, prosequente Regis aeterni iudicio, ab imperio depulsus Maximus morte pessima condemnatus est'. Haec me dicente, non respondit ullus quicquam, sed erant omnes intenti stupentes. Duo tamen adolatores ex ipsis – quod de episcopis dici dolendum est – nuntiaverunt regi, dicentes, quia nullum maiorem inimicum suis causis quam me haberet. Ilico unus ex aulicis cursu rapitu ad me repraesentandum dirigitur. Cumque venissem, stabat rex iuxta tabernaculum ex ramis factum, et ad dexteram eius Berthchramnus episcopus, ad levam vero Ragnemodus stabat; et erat ante eos scamnum pane desuper plenum cum diversis fercolis. Visoque me, rex ait: 'O episcope, iustitiam cunctis largire debes: et ecce! ego iustitiam a te non accipio; sed, ut video, consentis iniquitati, et impletur in te proverbium illud, quod corvus oculum corvi non eruet. Ad haec ego: 'Si quis de nobis, o rex, iustitiae tramitem transcendere voluerit, a te corrigi potest; si vero tu excesseris, quis te corripiet? Loquimur enim tibi; sed si volueris, audis; si autem nolueris, quis te condemnavit, nisi is qui se pronuntiavit esse iustitiam?' Ad haec ille, ut erat ab adolatoribus contra me accensus, ait: 'Cum omnibus enim inveni iustitiam et tecum invenire non possum. Sed scio, quid faciam, ut noteris in populis, et iniustum te esse in omnibus perpatiscat. Convocabo enim populum Toronicum et dicam eis: „Voceferamini contra Gregorium, quod sit iniustus et nulli hominum iustitiam praestit“. Illis quoque haec clamantibus respondebo: „Ego qui rex sum iustitiam cum eodem invenire non possum, et vos qui minores estis invenietis?“' Ad haec ego: 'Quod sim iniustus, tu nescis. Scit enim ille conscientia mea, cui occulta cordis sunt manifesta. Quod vero falso clamore populus, te insultante, vociferat, nihil est, quia sciunt omnes a te haec emissa. Ideoque non ego, sed potius tu in adclamatione notaberis. Sed quid plura? Habes legem et canones; haec te diligenter rimari oportet, et tunc quae praeciperint si non observaberis, noveris, tibiDei iudicium imminere'. At illi quasi me demulcens, quod dolose faciens potabat me non intellegere, conversus ad iuscellum, quod coram eo erat positus, ait: 'Propter te haec iuscella paravi, in qua nihil aliud praeter volatilia et parumper ciceris continetur'. Ad haec ego, cognuscens adulationis eius, dixi: 'Noster cibus esse debet facere voluntatem Dei et non in his diliciis dilectare, ut ea quae praecipit nullo casu praetermittamus. Tu vero, qui alios de iustitia culpas, pollicire prius, quod legem et canones non omittas; et tunc credimus, quod iustitiam prosequaris'. Ille vero, porrectam dexteram, iuravit per omnipotenti Deo, quod ea quae lex et canones edocebant nullu praetermitteret pactu. Post haec, accepto pane, hausto etiam vino, discessi. Ea vero nocte, decantatis nocturnalibus hymnis, osteum mansionis nostrae gravibus audio cogi verberibus; missoque puero, nuntius Fredegundis reginae adstare cognusco. Quibus introductis, salutationem reginae suscipio. Deinde praecantur pueri, ut in eius causis contrarius non existam, simulque ducentas argenti promittunt libras, si Praetextatus, me inpugnante, obpraemeretur. Dicebant enim: 'Iam omnium episcoporum promissionem habemus; tantum tu adversus non incedas'. Quibus ego: 'Si mihi mille libras auri argentique donetis, num quid aliud facere posso, nisi quae P Dominus agirei praecipit? Unum tantum pollicior, quod ea quae ceteri secundum canonum statuta consenserint sequar'. At illi, non intellegentes quae dicebam, gratias agentes, discesserunt. Mane autem facto, aliqui de episcopis ad me venerunt, simile mandato ferentes; quibus ego similia respondis. Convenientibus autem nobis in basilica sancti Petri, mane rex adfuit dixitque: 'Episcopus enim in furtis depraehensus ab episcopali officio ut avellatur, canonum auctoritas sancxit'. Nobis quoque respondentibus, quis ille sacerdus esset, cuius furti crimen inrogaretur, respondit rex: 'Vidistis enim species, quas nobis furtu abstulit'. Ostenderat enim nobis ante die tertia rex duo volucra, species et diversis ornamentis referta, quae praeciebantur amplius quam tria milia solidorum; sed et saccolum cum nummismati auri pondere, tenentem quasi duo milia. Haec enim dicebat rex sibi ab episcopo fuisse furata. Qui respondit: 'Recolere vos credo, discendente a Rothomaginse urbe Brunichilde regina, quod venerim ad vos, dixique vobis, quia res eius, id est quinque sarcinas, conmendatas haberem, et frequentius advenire puerus eius ad me, ut ea redderem, et nolui sine consilio vestro. Tu autem dixisti mihi, o rex: „Eiece haec a te, et revertantur ad mulierem res suae, ne inimicitia inter me et Childeberthum, nepotem meum, pro his rebus debeat pullulare“. Reversus ego ad urbem, unum volucrum tradidi puerus; non enim valebant amplius ferre. Reversi iterum requirebant alios. Iterum consiliatus sum magnificentiam vestram. Tu autem praecipisti, dicens: „Eiece, eiecehaec a te, o sacerdos, ne faciat scandalumhaeccausam“. Iterum tradidi eis duo ex his; duo autem alii remanserunt mecum. Tu autem quid nunc calumniaris et me furti argues, cum haec causa non ad furtum, sed ad custodiam debeat deputare?' Ad haec rex: 'Si hoc depositum penes te habebatur ad custodiendum, cur solvisti unum ex his et lymbum aureis contextum filis in partibus desecasti et dedisti per virus, qui me a regno deiecerent?' Praetextatus episcopus respondit: 'Iam dixi tibi superius, quia munera eorum acciperam, ideoque haec, cum non haberem de praesenti quid darem, hinc praesumpsi et eis vicissitudinem munerum tribui. Proprium mihi esse videbatur, quod filio meo Merovecho erat, quem de lavacro regenerationis excipi'. Videns autem rex Chilpericus, quod eum his calumniis superare nequiret, adtonitus valde ac conscientia confusus, discessit a nobis vocavitque quosdam de adolatoribus suis et ait: 'Victum me verbis episcopi fateor et vera esse quod dicit scio; quid nunc faciam, ut reginae de eo voluntas adimpleatur?' Et ait: 'Ite, et accedentes ad eum, dicite, quasi consilium ex vobismet ipsis dantes: „Nosti, quod sit rex Chilpericus pius atque conpunctus et cito flectatur ad misericordiam; humiliare sub eo et dicito, ab eo obiecta a te perpetrata fuisse. Tunc nos prostrati omnes coram pedibus eius, dare tibi veniam inpetramus“'. His seductus Praetextatus episcopus, pollicitus est se ita facturum. Mane autem facto, convenimus ad consuetum locum; adveniensque et rex, ait ad episcopum: Si munera pro muneribus his hominibus es largitus, quur sacramenta postulasti, ut fidem Merovecho servarent?' Respondit episcopus: 'Petii, fateor, amicitias eorum habere cum eo, et non solum hominem, sed, si fas fuisset, angelum de caelo evocaveram, qui esset adiutor eius; filius enim mihi erat, ut saepe dixi, spiritalis ex lavacro'. Cumque haec altercatio altius tolleretur, Praetextatus episcopus, prostratus solo, ait:'Peccavi in caelo et coram te, o rex misericordissime; ego sum homicida nefandus; ego te interficere volui et filio tuo in solio tuo eregere'. Haec eo dicente, prosternitur rex coram pedibus sacerdotum, dicens: Audite, o piissimi sacerdotes, reum crimen exsecrabile confitentem'. Cumque nos flentes regem elevassemus a solo, iussit eum basilicam egredi. Ipse vero ad metatum discessit, transmittens librum canonum, in quo erat quaternio novus adnixus, habens canones quasi apostolicus, continentes haec: Episcopus in homicidio, actulterio et periurio depraehensus, a sacerdotio divillatur. His ita lectis, cum Praetextatus staret stupens, Berthechramnus episcopus ait: 'Audi, o frater et coepiscope, quia regis gratiam non habes, ideoque nec nostram caritatem uti poteris, priusquam regis indulgentia merearis'. His ita gestis, petiit rex, ut aut tonicam eius scinderetur aut centisimus octavus psalmus, qui maledictionibus Scarioticas continet, super caput eius recitaretur aut certe iudicium contra eum scriberetur, ne in perpetuc communicaret. Quibus condicionibus ego restiti, iuxtag promissum regis, ut nihil extra canones gereretur. Tunc Praetextatus a nostris raptus oculis, incustodia positus est. De qua fugire temptans nocte, gravissime caesus, in insola maris, quod adiacet civitati Constantinae, in exilio est detrusus. Post haec sonuit, quod Merovechus iterum basilica sancti Martini conaretur expetere. Chilpericus vero custodire basilicam iubet et omnes claudi aditus. Custodis autem unum osteum, per quod pauci clerici ad officium ingrederentur, relinquentes, reliqua ostea clausa tenebant; quod non sine taedio populis fuit. Cum autem apud Parisius moraremur, signa in caelo apparuerunt, id est viginti radii a parte aquilonis, qui ab oriente surgentes, ad occidentem properabant; ex quibus unus prolixior et alius supereminens, ut est in sublime elevatus, mox defecit, et sic reliqui qui secuti fuerant evanuerunt.'Credo, interitum Merovechi pronuntiassent. Merovechus vero, dum in Remensem campaniam latitaret nec palam se Austrasiis crederit, a Tarabannensibus circumventus est, dicentibus, quod, relicto patre eius Chilperico, ei se subiugarent, si ad eos accederit. Qui velociter, adsumptis secum viris fortissimis, ad eos venit. Hi praeparatus detegentes dolos, in villam eum quandam concludunt et, circumseptum cum armatis, nuntius patri dirigunt. Quod ille audiens, illuc properare distinat. Sed hic cum in hospitiolo quodam reteneretur, timens, ne ad vindictam inimicorum multas lueret poenas, vocatum ad se Gailenum familiarem suum, ait: 'Una nobis usque nunc et anima et consilium fuit; rogo, ne patiaris me in manibus inimicorum tradi, sed, accepto gladio, inruas in me'. Quod ille nec dubitans, eum cultro confodit. Advenientem autem regem, mortuos est repertus. Extetirunt tunc qui adsererent, verba Merovechi, quae superius diximus, a regina fuisse conficta, Merovechum vero eius fuisse iussu clam interemptum. Gailenum vero adpraehensum, abscisis manibus et pedibus, auribus vel narium summitatibus et aliis multis cruciatibus adfectum, infiliciter negaverunt. Grindionem quoque intextum rotae in sublime sustulerunt; Ciucilonem, qui quondam comes palatii Sygiberthi regis fuerat, absciso capite, interficerunt. Sed et alios multos, qui cum eodem venerant, crudile nece diversis mortibus adficerunt. Loquebantur etiam tunc homines, in hac circumventionem Egidium episcopum et Gunthchramnum Bosonem fuisse maximum caput, eo quod Gunthchramnus Fredegundis reginae occultas amicitias potiretur pro interfectione Theodoberthi; Egidius vero, quod ei iam longo tempore esset carus.

## 19. De aelimosinis Tiberii.

<a id="hist-05-la-020"></a>
[**[020]**](#hist-05-en-020) Cum autem Iustinus imperator, amisso sensu, amens effectus esset et per solam Sophiam augustam eius imperium regiretur, populi, ut in superiore libro iam diximus, Tiberium caesarem elegerunt utilem, strinuum atque sapientem, aelymosinarium inopumque optimum defensorem. Qui cum multa de thesauris, quos Iustinus adgregavit, pauperibus erogaret et augusta illa eum frequentius increparet, quod rem publicam redegisset in paupertate, diceritque: 'Quod ego multis annis congregavi, tu infra pauco tempus prodegi dispergis', agebat ille: 'Non deerit fisco nostro; tantum pauperis aelymosinam accipiant, aut captivi redimantur. Hoc est enim magnum thesaurum, dicente Domino: Thesaurizate vobis thesaurus in caelo, ubi neque erugo neque tinea corrumpit, et ubi fures non effodiunt nec furantur. Ergo de quod Deus dedit, congregemus per pauperes in caelo, ut Dominus nobis augere dignetur in saeculo'. Et quia, ut diximus, magnus et verus christianus erat, dum hilare distributione pauperibus opem praestat, magis ac magis ei Dominus subministrat. Nam deambolans per palatium, vidit in pavimento domus tabolam marmoream, in qua crux dominica erat sculpta, et ait: 'Crucem tuam, Domine, frontem nostram munimus et pectora, et ecce crucem sub pedibus conculcamus!' Et dicto citius iussit eam auferre; defossamque tabulam atque erectam, inveniunt subter et aliam hoc signum habentem. Nuntiantesque, iussit et illam auferri. Qua amota, repperiunt et tertiam; iussumque eius et haec aufertur. Qua ablata, inveniunt magnum thesaurum, habentem supray mille auri centinaria. Sublatumque aurum pauperibus adhuc habundantius, ut consueverat, subministrat; nec ei Dominus aliquid defecere faciet pro bona voluntate sua. Quid ei in posterum Dominus transmiserit, non omittam. Narsis ille dux Italiae, cum in quadam civitate domum magnam haberet, Italiam cum multis thesauris egressus, ad supra memoratam urbem advenit ibique in domo sua occultae cisterna magna fodit, in qua multa milia centenariorum auri argentique reposuit idque, interfectis consciis, uno tantummodo seni per iuramentum condita conmendavit. Defuncto quoque Narsites, haec sub terra latebant. Cumque supradictus senex huius aelymosinas assidue cerneret, pergit ad eum, dicens: 'Si', inquid, 'mihi aliquid prodest, magnam rem tibi caesar, edicam'. Cui ille: 'Dic', ait, 'quod volueris. Proderit enim tibi, si quiddam nobis profuturum esse narraveris'. 'Thesaurum', inquid, 'Narsitis reconditum habeo, quod in extremum vitae positus caelare non possum'. Tunc caesar Tiberius gavisus, mittit usque ad locum pueros suos; praecedente vero sene, hi secuntur attoniti. Pervenientesque ad cesternam deopertamque ingrediuntur, in qua tantum aurum argentumque repperiunt, ut per multos dies vix evacuaretur a deportantibus. Et ex hoc ille amplius hilari erogatione dispensavit egenis.

## 20. De Salunio et sagittario episcopis.

<a id="hist-05-la-021"></a>
[**[021]**](#hist-05-en-021) Igitur contra Salonium Sagittariumque episcopos tumultus exoritur. Hi enim a sancto Nicetio Lugdunensi episcopo educati, diaconatus officio sunt sortiti; huiusque tempore Salonius Ebredunensis urbis, Sagittarius autem Vappinsis ecclesiae sacerdotes statuuntur. Sed, adsumpto episcopatu in proprio relati arbitrio, coeperunt in pervasionibus, caedibus, homicidiis, adulteriis diversisque in sceleribus insano furore crassari, ita ut quodam tempore, celebrante Victore Tricassinorum episcopo sollemnitatem natalicii sui, emissa cohorte, cum gladiis et sagittis inruerent super eum. Venientesque sciderunt vestimenta eius, ministros ceciderunt, vasa vel omne apparatum prandii auferentes, relinquentes episcopum in grandi contumelia. Quod cum rex Guntchramnus comperisset, congregari synodum apud urbem Lugdunensim iussit. Coniunctique episcopi cum patriarcha Nicetio beato, discussis causis, invenerunt eos de his sceleribus quibus accusabantur valde convictos; praeceperuntque, ut qui talia commiserant episcopatus honore privarentur. At illi, cum adhuc propitium sibi regem esse nossent, ad eum accedunt, inplorantes se iniuste remotos, sibique tribui licentiam, ut ad papam urbis Romae accedere debeant. Rex vero annuens petitionibus eorum, datis epistolis, eos abire permisit. Qui accedentes coram papa Iohanne exponunt se nullius rationis existentibus causis dimotos. Ille vero ad regem epistolas dirigit, in quibus locis suis eosdem restitui iubet. Quod rex sine mora, castigatos prius verbis multis, implevit. Sed nulla, quod peius est, fuit emendatio subsecuta. Tamen Victoris episcopi pacem petierunt, traditis hominibus quos in seditione direxerant. Sed ille recordatus praecepti dominici, non debere reddi inimicis mala pro malis, nihil his mali faciens, liberos abire permisit. Unde in posterum a communione suspensus est, pro eo quod, publice accusans, clam inimicis pepercisset absque consilio quibus accusaverat fratrum. Sed per favorem regis iterum in communione revocatus est. Hi vero in maioribus sceleribus cotidie miscebantur; et in proeliis illis, sicut iam supra meminimus, quae Mummolus cum Langobardis gessit, tamquam unus ex laicis, accincti arma, plurimos propriis manibus interfecerunt. In civibus vero suis, nonnullos commoti felle verberantes fustibus, usque ad effusionem sanguinis saeviebant. Unde factum est, ut clamor populi ad regem denuo procederet, eosdemque rex arcessiri praecepit. Quibus advenientibus, noluit suis obtutibus praesentari, scilicet ut, prius habita audientia, si idonei inveniebantur, sic regis praesentiam mererentur. Sed Sagittarius felle commotus, hanc rationem dure suscipiens, ut erat levis ac vanus et in sermonibus inrationabilibus profluus, declamare plurima de rege coepit ac dicere, quod filii eius regnum capere non possint, eo quod mater eorum ex familia Magnacharii quondam adscita regis torum adisset, ignorans, quod, praetermissis nunc generibus feminarum, regis vocitantur liberi, qui de regibus fuerant procreati. His auditis, rex commotus valde, tam equos quam pueros vel quaecumque habere poterant abstulit; ipsosque in monasteriis a se longiori accensu dimotos, in quibus paenitentiam agerent, includi praecepit, non amplius quam singulos eis clericos relinquens; iudices locorum terribiliter commonens, ut ipsos cum armatis custodire debeant, ne cui ad eos visitandos ullus pateat aditus. Superstes enim erant his diebus filii regis, ex quibus senior aegrotare coepit. Accedentes autem ad regem familiares eius, dixerunt: 'Si propitius audire dignaretur rex verba servorum suorum, loquerentur in auribus tuis. Qui ait: 'Loquimini quae libet'. Dixeruntque: 'Ne forte innocentes hi episcopi exilio condemnati fuissent, et peccatum regis augeatur in aliquo, et ideo filius domini nostri pereat'. Qui ait: 'Ite quantocius et laxate eos, deprecantes, ut orent pro parvulis nostris'. Quibus abeuntibus, dimissi sunt. Egressi igitur de monasteriis, coniuncti sunt pariter, et se osculantes, eo quod olim a se visi non fuerant, ad civitates suas regressi sunt, et in tantum conpuncti sunt, ut viderentur numquam a psallentio cessare, celebrare ieiunia, aelemosinas exercere, librum Davitici carminis explere per diem noctesque in hymnis ac lectionibus meditando deducere. Sed non diu haec sanctitas inlibata permansit, conversique sunt iterum retrorsum; et ita plerumque noctes epulando atque bibendo ducebant, ut, clericis matutinas in eclesia celebrantibus, hi pocula poscerent et vina libarent. Nulla prorsus de Deo erat mentio, nullus omnino cursus memoriae habebatur. Renitente aurora, surgentes a cena, mollibus se indumentis operientes, somno vinoque sepulti, usque ad horam diei tertiam dormiebant. Sed nec mulieres deerant, cum quibus polluerentur. Exsurgentes igitur, abluti balneis, ad convivium discumbebant; de quo vespere surgentes, caenae inhiabant usque ad illud lucis tempus, quo superius diximus. Sic faciebant singulis diebus, donec im Dei diruit super eos; quod in posterum memoraturi sumus.

## 21. De Winnoco Brittone.

<a id="hist-05-la-022"></a>
[**[022]**](#hist-05-en-022) Tunc Winnocus Britto in summa abstinentia a Brittaniis venit Toronus, Hierusolimis accedere cupiens, nullum alium vestimentum nisi de pellibus ovium lana privatis habens; quem nos, quo facilius teneremus, quia nobis relegiosus valde videbatur, presbiterii gratia honoravimus. Inghitrudis autem relegiosa consuetudinem habebat, aquam de sepulchrum sancti Martini collegere. Qua aqua deficiente, rogat, vas cum vino ad beati tumulum deportari. Transacta autem nocte, eum exinde hoc presbitero praesenti adsumi mandavit; et ad se delatum, ait presbitero: 'Aufer hinc vino et unam tantum guttam de aqua benedicta, unde parum superest, effunde'. Quod cum fecisset, mirum dictu, vasculum, quod semeplenum erat, ad unius guttae discensum impletum est. Idem bis aut tertio vacuatum, per unam tantum guttam est impletum; quod non ambigetur, et in hoc beati Martini fuisse virtutem.

## 22. De obitu Samsonis, filii Chilperici.

<a id="hist-05-la-023"></a>
[**[023]**](#hist-05-en-023) His ita gestis, Samson, filius Chilperici regis iunior, a desenteria et febre conpraehensus, a rebus humanis excessit. Hic vero, cum Chilpericus rex Tornacum a fratre obsederetur, natus est; quem mater ob metum mortis a se abiecit et perdere voluit. Sed cum non potuisset, obiurgata a rege, eum baptizare praecepit. Qui baptizatus et ab ipso episcopo susceptus, lustro uno nec perfuncto, defunctus est. Nam et mater eius Fredegundis in his diebus graviter egrotavit, sed convaluit.

## 23. De prodigiis ostensis.

<a id="hist-05-la-024"></a>
[**[024]**](#hist-05-en-024) Post haec in nocte, quod erat tertio Idus Novembris, apparuit nobis beati Martini vigilias celebrantibus magnum prodigium; nam in medio lunae stilla fulgens visa est elucere, et super ac subter lunam aliae stillae propinquae apparuerunt. Sed et circolus ille, qui pluviam plerumque significat, circa eam apparuit. Sed quae haec figuraverint, ignoramus. Nam et luna hoc anno sepe in nigridinem versam videmus, et ante natalem Domini gravia fuere tonitrua. Sed et splendores illi circa solem, sicut iam ante cladem Arvernam fuisse commemoravimus, quod rustici soles vocant, apparuerunt; et mare ultra modum egressum adserunt, et multa alia signa apparuerunt.

## 24. Chilpericus Pectavus invasit.

<a id="hist-05-la-025"></a>
[**[025]**](#hist-05-en-025) Guntchramnus Boso Toronus cum paucis armatis veniens, filias suas, quas in basilica sancta reliquerat, vim abstulit et eas Pectavus civitatem, qui erat Childeberthi regis, perduxit. Chilpericus quoque rex Pectavum pervasit, atque nepotis sui hominis ab eius sunt hominibus effugati Ennodium ex comitatu ad regis praesentiam perduxerunt. Quem exilio damnatum, facultates eius fisco subdiderunt. Sed post annum et patriae et facultatibus redditus est. Gunthchramnus Boso, relictis filiabus suis in basilica beati Hilari, ad Childebertum regem transiit.

## 25. De interitu Dacconis et Dracoleni.

<a id="hist-05-la-026"></a>
[**[026]**](#hist-05-en-026) Anno quoque tertio Childeberthi regis, qui erat Chilperici et Gunthchramni septimus decimus annus, cum Dacco, Dagarici quondam filius, relicto regi Chilperico, huc illucque vagaretur, a Dracoleno duci, qui dicebatur Industrius, fraudolenter adpraehensus est. Quem vinctum ad Chilpericum regem Brannacum deduxit, data ei sacramenta, quod vitam illius cum rege obteniret. Sed oblitus sacramenti, egit cum principe, nefarias res adserens, ut moreretur. Ille quoque cum vinctus deteneretur et cernerit se paenitus non evasurum, a presbitero, rege nesciente, paenitentiam petiit. Qua accepta, interfectus est. Cum autem idem Dracolenus velociter reverteretur in patriam, his diebus Guntchramnus Boso filias suas Pectavo auferre conabatur. Quod audiens Dracolenus, se super eum obiecit; sed illi, sicut erant parati, resistentes, se defensare nitebantur. Gunthchramnus vero misit unum de amicis suis ad eum, dicens: 'Vade et die ei: „Scis enim, quod foedus inter nos initum habemus; rogo, ut te de meis removeas insidiis. Quantumvis de rebus tollere non prohibeo; tantum mihi, etsi nudo, liceat cum filiabus meis accedere, quo voluero"'. At ille, ut erat vanus ac levis: 'Ecce', inquid, 'funicolum, in quo alii culpabiles legati ad regem me ducente directi sunt, in quo et hic hodie legandus illuc educitur vinctus'. Et haec cum dixisset, calcaneorum ictibus urguens equum, ad illum veloci cursu dirigit; et casso eum verberans ictu, astili divisum, ensis ad terram ruit. Guntchramnus vero cum super se mortem cernerit inminere, invocato nomen Domini et virtutem magnam beati Martini elevatoque contu, Dracolenum artat in faucibus, suspensumque de equo sursum unus de amicis suis eum lancia latere verberatum finivit. Fugatisque sociis ipsumque spoliatum, Gunthchramnus cum filiabus liber abscessit. Post haec Severus, socer eius, a filiis apud regem graviter accusatur. Haec ille audiens, cum magnis muneribus ad regem petiit. Qui in via adpraehensus et spoliatus atque in exilium deductus, morte pessima vitam finivit. Sed et duo filii eius Burgolenus et Dodo, ob crimen maiestatis lesi iudicium mortis susceptum, unus ab exercitu vi obpressus est, alius in fugam adpraehensus, truncatis manibus et pedibus, interiit; resque omnes tam eorum quam patris fisco conlatae sunt. Erant enim eis magni thesauri.

## 26. Quod exercitus in Brittaniis abiit.

<a id="hist-05-la-027"></a>
[**[027]**](#hist-05-en-027) Dehinc Toronici, Pictavi, Baiocassini, Caenomannici et Andecavis cum aliis multis in Brittania ex iussu Chilperici regis abierunt et contra Varocum, filium quondam Macliavi, ad Vicinoniam fluvium resedent. Sed ille dolose per nocte super Saxones Baiocassinos ruens, maximam exinde partem interfecit. Post die autem tertia cum ducibus regis Chilperici pacem faciens et filium suum in obsedatum donans, sacramentum se constrinxit, quod fidelis regi Chilperico esse deberet. Venitus quoque civitatem refudit sub ea condicione, ut, se mereretur eam per iussionem regis regere, tributa vel omnia quae exinde debebantur annis singulis, nullo admonente, dissolverit. Quod cum factum fuisset, exercitus ab ea loca remotus est. Post haec Chilpericus rex de pauperibus et iunioribus eclesiae vel basilicae bannos iussit exigi, pro eo quod in exercitu non ambulassent. Non enim erat consuetudo, ut his ullam exsolverent publicam functionem. Post haec Varochus obliviscens promissionis suae, volens inrumpere quod fecerat, Eunium episcopum Veneticae urbis ad Chilpericum regem dirigit. At ille ira commotus, obiurgatum eum exilio damnare praecipit.

## 27. De eiectione Saloni et Sagittari.

<a id="hist-05-la-028"></a>
[**[028]**](#hist-05-en-028) Anno quoque quarto Childeberthi, qui fuit 18. Guntchramni et Chilperici regum, apud Cavellonum civitatem sinodus acta est ex iussu principis Guntchramni; discussisque diversis causis, contra Salonium et Sagittarium episcopos iteratur illa antiqua calamitas. Obiciuntur eis crimina, et non solum de adulterium, verum etiam de homicidiis accusantur. Sed haec per paenitentiam purgari censentis episcopi, illud est additum, quod essent rei maiestatis et patriae proditores. Qua de causa ab episcopato discincti, in basilica beati Marcelli sub custodia detruduntur. Ex qua per fugam lapsi, discesserunt per diversa vagantes, donec in civitatibus eorum alii subrogati sunt.

## 28. De discriptionibus Chilperici.

<a id="hist-05-la-029"></a>
[**[029]**](#hist-05-en-029) Chilpericus vero rex discriptiones novas et gravis in omne regno suo fieri iussit. Qua de causa multi relinquentes civitates illas vel possessiones proprias, alia regnas petierunt, satius ducentes alibi peregrinare quam tali pericolo subiacere. Statutum enim fuerat, ut possessor de propria terra unam anforam vini per aripennem redderit. Sed et alii functionis infligebantur multi tam de reliquis terris quam de mancipiis; quod implere non poterat. Lemovicinus quoque populus, cum se cernerit tali fasci gravari, congregatus in Kalendas Martias Marcumque refrendarium qui haec agere iussus fuerat, interficere voluit; et fecisset utique, nisi eum episcopus Ferreolus ab inminente discrimine liberasset. Areptis quoque libris discriptionum, incendio multitudo coniuncta cremavit. Unde multum molestus rex, dirigens de latere suo personas, inmensis damnis populum adflixit suppliciisque conteruit, morte multavit. Ferunt etiam, tunc abbatis atque presbiteros ad stipitis extensus diversis subiacuisse tormentis, calumniantibus regalibus missis, quod in siditione populi ad incendendus libros satellitis adfuissent, acerbiora quoque deinceps infligentes tributa.

## 29. De vastatione Brittanorum.

<a id="hist-05-la-030"></a>
[**[030]**](#hist-05-en-030) Brittani quoque graviter regionem Redonicam vastaverunt incendio, praeda, captivitate. Qui usque Cornutium vicum debellando progressi sunt. Eunius vero episcopus de exilio reductus, Andecavo ad pascendum delegatur nec ad civitatem suam Veneticam redire permittitur. Byppolenus vero dux contra Brittanus dirigitur et loca aliqua Brittaniae ferro incendioque obpraemit; quae res maiorem insaniam excitavit.

## 30. De imperio Tiberii.

<a id="hist-05-la-031"></a>
[**[031]**](#hist-05-en-031) Dum haec geruntur in Galliis, Iustinus, impleto imperii octavo decimo anno, amentiam quam incurrerat cum vita finivit. Quo sepulto, Tiberius caesar arripuit iam olim adgressum imperium. Sed cum eum secundum consuetudinem loci ad spectaculum circi prestularet populus processurum, parare ei cogitans pro parte Iustiniani insidias, qui tunc nepus Iustini habebatur, ille per loca sancta processit. Conpletaque oratione, vocatum ad se urbis papam, cum consolibus ac praefectis palatium est ingressus. Dehinc indutus purpora, diademate coronatus, throno imperiale inpositus, cum inmensis laudibus imperium confirmavit. Factionarii quoque operientes ad circum, cum cognovissent quae acta fuerant, pudore confusi, sine effectu regressi sunt, nihil homini, qui in Deo spem posuerat, adversare valentes. Transactis igitur paucis diebus, adveniens autem Iustinianus, pedibus se proicit imperatoris, quindecem ei centenaria auri deferens ob meritum gratiae. Quem ille secundum patientiae suae ritum collegens, in palatium iussit adsistere. Sophia vero augusta inmemor promissionis, quam quondam in Tiberium habuerat, insidias ei temptavit intendere. Procedentem autem eum ad villam, ut iuxta ritum imperiale triginta diebus ad vindimiam iocundaretur, vocato clam Iustiniano, Sophia voluit eum erigere in imperio. Quod cumpertum, Tiberius cursu veloci ad Constantinopolitanam civitatem regreditur adpraehensamque augustam ab omnibus thesaurus spoliavit, solum ei victus cotidiani alimentum relinquens. Segregatos pueros eius ab ea, alios posuit de fidelibus suis, mandans prorsus, ut nullus de anterioribus ad eam haberet accessum. Iustinianum vero obiurgatum tanto in posterum amore dilexit, ut filio eius filiam suam promitteret rursumque filio suo filiam suam expeteret; sed non est res sortita effectum. Exercitus eius Persas debellavit, victorque regressus, tantam mole praedae detulit, ut crederetur cupiditate humanae posse sufficire. Viginti elifanti capti ad imperatorem deducti sunt.

## 31. De insidiis Brittanorum.

<a id="hist-05-la-032"></a>
[**[032]**](#hist-05-en-032) Brittani eo anno valde infesti circa urbem fuere Namneticam atque Redonicam. Qui inmensam auferentes praedam, agros pervadunt, vineas a fructibus vacuant et captivus adducunt. Ad quos cum Filex episcopus legationem misisset, emendare promittentes, nihil de promissis implire voluerunt.

## 32. De basilica sancti Dionisii iniuriata per mulierem.

<a id="hist-05-la-033"></a>
[**[033]**](#hist-05-en-033) Apud Parisius autem mulier quaedam ruit in crimine, adserentibus multis, quasi quod, relicto viro, cum alio misceretur. Igitur parentes illius accesserunt ad patrem, dicentes: 'Aut idoneam redde filiam tuam, aut certe moriatur, ne stuprum hoc generi nostro notam infligat'. 'Novi', inquit pater, 'ego filiam meam bene idoneam; nec est verum verbum hoc, quod mali homines proloquuntur. Tamen ne crimen consurgat ulterius, innocentem eam faciam sacramento'. Et illi: 'Si', inquiunt, 'est innoxia, super tumulum hoc beati Dionisi martyris sacramentis adfirma'. 'Faciam', inquit pater. Tunc inito placito ad basilicam martyris sancti conveniunt; elevatisque pater manibus super altarium, iuravitque, filiam non esse culpabilem. E contrario vero periurasse eum, alii a parte viri pronuntiant. His ergo altercantibus, evaginatis gladiis in se invicem proruunt atque ante ipsum altarium se trucidantur. Erant enim maiores natu et primi apud Chilpericum regem. Saucianturque multi gladiis, respergitur sancta humano cruore basilica, ostia iaculis fodiuntur et ensibus, atque usque ad ipsum sepuicrum tela iniqua desaeviunt. Quod dum vix mitigatur, locus officium perdidit, donec ista omnia ad regis notitiam pervenirent. Hi vero properantes ad praesentiam principis, non recipiuntur in gratia; sed et ad episcopum loci illius remissi, iussum est, ut, si de hoc facinus culpabiles non inveniebantur, sociarentur communioni. Tunc ab episcopo Ragnimodo, qui Parisiacae ecclesiae praeerat, componentes quae male gesserant, in communione ecclesiastica sunt recepti. Mulier vero non post multis diebus, cum ad iudicium vocaretur, laqueo vitam finivit.

## 33. De prodigiis.

<a id="hist-05-la-034"></a>
[**[034]**](#hist-05-en-034) Anno quinto Childeberthi regis Arvernorum regionem diluvia magna praessirunt, ita ut per dies 12 non cessaret a pluvia, tantaque inundatione Limane est infusum, ut multos, ne simentem iacerent, prohiberet. Flumina quoque Leger Flavarisque, quem Elacrem vocitant, vel reliqui torrentes decurrentes in eum ita intumuerunt, ut terminus, quos numquam excesserant, praeterirent. Quae grande de pecoribus excidium, de culturis detrimentum, de aedificiis fecere naufragium. Pari modo Rhodanus cum Arare coniunctus, ripas excidens, grave damnum populis intulit, murus Lugdunensis civitatis aliqua ex parte subvertit. Quiescentibus vero pluviis; arbores denuo floruerunt; erat enim mensis Septembris. In Toronico vero eo anno mane, priusquam dies inlucescerit, fulgor per caelum cucurrisse visus est et ad orientis plagam caecidisse. Sed et sonitus tamquam diruentes arbores per totam terram illam auditus est; quod ideo non est de arbore aestimandum, quia in quinquaginta aut amplius milia est auditum. Ipso anno graviter urbis Burdegalensis a terrae motu concussa est, moeniaque civitatis in discrimine eversionis extetirunt; atque ita omnes populus metu mortis exterritus est, ut, si non fugiret, potaret se cum urbe dehiscere. Unde et multi ad civitatis alias transierunt. Qui tremor ad vicinas civitatis porrectus est et usque Spaniam attigit, sed non tam valide. Tamen de Pirineis montibus inmense lapides sunt commoti, qui pecora hominisque prostraverunt. Nam et vicus Burdegalensis incendium divinitus ortum exussit, ita ut subito conpraehensi igni tam domus quam areae cum annoniss incendio cremarentur, nullum paenitus incitamentum Habens ignis alieni, forsitan iussione divina. Nam et Aurilianensis civitas grave incendio conflagravit, in tantum ut ditioribus nihil paenitus remaneret; et si aliquis ab igne quicquam eripuit, ab insistentibus furibus est dereptum. Apud terminum Carnotenum verus de effracto pane sangues effluxit. Graviter tunc et Beturica civitas a grandine verberata est.

## 34. De desenteriae morbo et filiis Chilperici mortuis.

<a id="hist-05-la-035"></a>
[**[035]**](#hist-05-en-035) Sed haec prodigia gravissima lues est subsecuta. Nam et discordantibus reges et iterum bellum civile parantibus, desentericus morbus paene Gallias totas praeoccupavit. Erat enim his qui patiebantur valida cum vomitu febris renumque nimius dolor; caput grave vel cervix. Ea vero quae ex ore proiciebantur colore croceo aut certe viridia erant. A multis autem adserebatur veninum occultum esse, – rusticiores vero coralis hoc pusulas nominabant – quod non est incredibile, quia missae in scapulis sive cruribus ventosae, procedentibus erumpentibusque visicis, decursa saniae, multi liberabantur. Sed et herbae, quae venenis medentur, potui sumptae, plerisque praesidia contulerunt. Et quidem primum haec infirmetas a mense Augusto initiata, parvulus aduliscentes arripuit lectoque subegit. Perdedemus dulcis et caros nobis infantulos, quos aut gremiis fovimus aut ulnis baiolavimus aut propria manu, ministratis cibis, ipsos studio sagatiore nutrivimus, Sed, abstersis lacrimis, cum beato Iob dicimus: Dominus dedit, Dominus abstulit; quomodo Domino placuit, ita factum est. Sit nomen eius benedictum in saecula. Igitur in his diebus Chilpericus rex graviter egrotavit. Quo convaliscente, filius eius iunior, necdum aqua et Spiritu sancto renatus, aegrotare coepit. Quem in extremis videntis, baptismo abluerunt. Quo parumper melius agente, frater eius senior nomen Chlodoberthus ab hoc morbo correpitur; ipsumque in discrimine mortis Fredegundis mater cernens, sero penetens, ait ad regem: 'Diu nos male agentes pietas divina sustentat; nam sepe nos febribus et aliis malis corripuit, et emendatio non successit. Ecce! iam perdimus filios. Ecce! iam eos lacrimae pauperum, lamenta viduarum, suspiria orfanorum interimunt, nec spes remanet cui aliquid congregemus. Thesaurizamus, nescientes, cui congregemus ea. Ecce thesauri remanent a possessore vacui, rapinis ac maledictionibus pleni! Numquid non exundabant prumptuaria vino? Numquid non horrea replebantur frumento? Numquid non erant thesauri referti auro, argento, lapidibus praeciosis, monilibus vel reliquis imperialibus ornamentis? Ecce quod pulchrius habebamus perdimus! Nunc, si placet, venite; incendamus omnes discriptionis iniquas, sufficiatque fisco nostro, quod sufficit patri regique Chlothario'. Haec effata regina, pugnis verberans pectus, iussit libros exhibere, qui de civitatibus suis per Marcum venerant, proiectosque in igne, iterum ad rege conversa: 'Quid tu', inquid, 'moraris? Fac quod vidis a me fieri, ut, etsi dulces natos perdimus, vel poenam perpetuam evadamus'. Tunc rex, conpunctus corde, tradedit omnes libros discriptionum igne; conflagratusque, misit qui futuras prohiberent discriptionis. Post haec infantulus iunior, dum nimio labore tabescit, extinguetur. Quem cum maximo merore deducentes a villa Brinnaco Parisius, ad basilicam sancti Dionisie sepelire mandaverunt. Chlodoberthum vero conponentes in feretro, Sessionas ad basilicam sancti Medardi duxerunt, proicientesque eo ad sanctum sepulchrum, voverunt vota pro eo; sed media nocte anilus iam et tenuis spiritum exalavit. Quem in basilica sanctorum Crispini atque Crispiniani martirum sepelierunt. Magnus quoque hic planctus omni populo fuit; nam viri lugentes mulieresque lucubribus vestimentis induti, ut solet in coniugum exsequiis fieri, ita hoc funus sunt prosecuti. Malta postea Chilpericus rex eclesiis sive basilicis vel pauperibus est largitus.

## 35. De Austrigilde regina.

<a id="hist-05-la-036"></a>
[**[036]**](#hist-05-en-036) His diebus Austrigildis Guntchramni principis regina ab hoc morbo consumpta est; sed priusquam nequam spiritum exalaret, cernens, quod evadere non posset, alta trahens suspiria, voluit leti sui habire participes, agens, ut in exsequiis eius aliorum funera plangerentur. Fertur enim Herodiano more regem petisse, dicens: 'Adhuc spes vivendi fuerat, si non inter iniquorum medicorum manus interissem; nam potionis ab illis acceptae mihi vi abstulerunt vitam et fecerunt me hanc lucem velociter perdere. Et ideo, ne inulta mors mea praetereat, quaeso et cum sacramenti interpositione coniuro, ut, cum ab hac luce discessero, statim ipse gladio trucidentur; ut, sicut ego amplius vivere non queo, ita nec ille post meum obitum glorientur, sed sit unus dolus nostris pariter ac eorum amicis'. Haec effata, infilicem animam tradidit. Rex vero, peracto ex more iusticio, oppressus iniquae coniugis iuramento, implevit piaeceptum iniquitatis. Nam duos medicos, qui ei studium adhibuerant, gladio ferire piaecepits; quod non sine peccato facto fuisse, multorum censit prudentia.

## 36. De Eraclio episcopo et Nanthino comite.

<a id="hist-05-la-037"></a>
[**[037]**](#hist-05-en-037) Hac itaque aegritudine et Nanthinus Equolisinensis comes exinanitus interiit. Sed quae contra sacerdotes vel ecclesias Dei egerit, altius repetenda sunt. Denique Maracharius, avunculus eius, diu in ipsa urbe usus est comitatum. Quo officio completo, ecclesiae sociatur, clericusque factus, ordinatur episcopus. Qui multum vigilanter vel ecclesias vel ecclesiae domos et erigens et componens, septimo sacerdotii anno, iniecto ab inimicis in capite piscis veneno, simpliciter accipiens, crudeliter enecatur. Sed non diu inultam eius mortem pertulit divina clementia; nam Frontonius, cuius consilios hoc scelus est perpetratum, adsumto confestim episcopatu, uno in eo degens anno, praecurrente iudicio Dei, interiit. Cuius post obitum Heraclius Burdigalensis presbiter, qui quondam legatus Childeberti senioris fuerat, episcopus ordinatur. Nanthinus vero ob requirendam avunculi sui mortem comitatum in ipsa urbe expetiit. Quo accepto, multas episcopo iniurias inrogavit. Aiebat enim: 'Homicidas illos, qui avunculum meum interfecerunt, tecum retines; sed et presbiteros huic noxae admixtos ad convivium recipes. Deinde inimicitia increscente, paulatim coepit villas ecclesiae, quas Maracharius testamento scripto reliquerat, violenter invadere, adserens, non debere ecclesiam eius facultatem adipisci, a cuius clericis testator fuerat interfectus. Post ista vero, iam aliquibus ex laicis interfectis, addidit, ut adpraehensum presbiterum alligaret ac contu perfoderet. Cui adhuc viventi, retortis post tergum manibus, adpenso ad stipitem, elicere quaerebat, si in hac causa fuisset admixtus. Sed cum ille negaret, profluente cruore de vulnere, reddidit spiritum. Qua de causa commotus episcopus, iussit, eum ab ecclesiae foribus prohiberi. Convenientibus autem apud civitatem Sanctonas sacerdotibus, deprecabatur Nanthinus, ut pacem episcopi mereretur, promittens se omnes ecclesiae res, quas sine ratione abstulerat, redditurum atque humilem exhibere se sacerdoti. At ille fratrum iussionibus obaudire procurans, cuncta quae petebantur indulsit; causam tamen presbiteri omnipotenti Deo commendans, comitem in caritate recepit. Qui post ista regressus urbem, domos illas quas male pervaserat spoliat, elidit ac dissicit, dicens: 'Etsi hoc ab ecclesia recipitur, vel desertum inveniatur'. Qua de causa iterum motus episcopus, eum a communione suspendit. Quae dum aguntur, impletum beatus pontifex vitae cursum migravit ad Dominum. Nanthinus quoque ab aliquibus episcopis, intercedentibus praemiis atque adolationibus, communicatur. Post paucos vero menses a supradicto morbo corripitur; qui nimia exustus febre, clamabat, dicens: 'Heu, heu! Ab Eraclio antistiti exuror, ab illo crucior, ab illo ad iudicium vocor. Cognosco facinus; reminiscor, me iniuste iniurias intulisse pontifici; mortem deprecor, ne diutius crucier hoc tormento'. Haec cum maxima in febre clamaret, deficiente robore corporis, infelicem animam fudit, indubia relinquens vestigia, hoc ei ad ultionem beati antistitis evenisse. Nam exanime corpus ita nigredinem duxit, ut putares eum prunis superpositum fuisse combustum. Ergo omnes haec obstupescant, admirentur et metuant, ne inferant iniurias sacerdotibus! quia ultor est Dominus servorum suorum sperantibus in se.

## 37. De Martino Calliciense episcopo.

<a id="hist-05-la-038"></a>
[**[038]**](#hist-05-en-038) Hoc tempore et beatus Martinus Galliciensis episcopus obiit, magnum populo illi faciens plane tum. Nam hic Pannoniae ortus fuit, et exinde ad visitanda loca sancta in Oriente properans, in tantum se litteris inbuit, ut nulli secundus suis temporibus haberetur. Exinde Gallitiam venit, ubi, cum beati Martini reliquiae portarentur, episcopus ordinatur. In quo sacerdotio impletis plus minus triginta annis, plenus virtutibus migravit ad Dominum. Versiculos, qui super ostium sunt a parte meridiana in basilica sancti Martini, ipse composuit.

## 38. De persecutione christianorum in Spaniis.

<a id="hist-05-la-039"></a>
[**[039]**](#hist-05-en-039) Magna eo anno in Hispaniis christianis persecutio fuit, multique exiliis dati, facultatibus privati, fame decocti, carcere mancipati, verberibus adfecti ac diversis suppliciis trucidati sunt. Caput quoque huius sceleris Goisuintha fuit, quam post Athanachilde regis conubium rex Leuvichildus acceperat; sed quae Dei servis notam humilitatis inflixefat, prosequente ultione divina, ipsa queque est omnibus populis facta notabilis. Nam unum oculum nubs alba contegens, lumen, quod mens non habebat, pepulit a palphebris. Erant autem Leuvichildo regi ex alia uxore duo filii, quorum senior Sigyberthi iunior Chilperici regis filiam disponsaverat. Sed Ingundis, Sigyberthi regis filiam, cum magno apparato in Hispaniis directa, ab avia Goisuintha cum gaudio magno suscepitur. Quam nec passa est in relegione catholica diu commorare; sed ut rebaptizaretur in Arriana herese, blandis coepit sermonibus inlecere. Sed illa viriliter reluctans, coepit dicere: 'Sufficit satis me ab originale peccato baptismo salutare semel abluta fuisse et sanctam Trinitatem in una aequalitate esse confessam. Haec me credere ex corde toto confiteor nec umquam ab hac fide ibo retrorsum'. Haec illa audiens, iracundiae furore succensa, adpraehensam per comam capitis puellam in terram conlidit, et diu calcibus verberatam ac sanguine cruentatam iussit spoliari et piscinae inmergi; sed, ut adserunt multi, numquam animum suum a fide nostra reflexit. Leuvichildus autem dedit eis unam de civitatibus, in qua resedentes regnarent. Ad quam cum abissent, coepit Ingundis praedicare viro suo, ut, relicta heresis fallacia, catholicae legis veritatem agnuscerit. Quod ille diu refutans, tandem commotus ad eius praedicationem, conversus est ad legem catholicam ac, dum crismaretur, Iohannis est vocitatus. Quod cum Leuvichildus audisset, coepit causas querere, qualiter eum perderet. Ille vero haec intellegens, ad partem se imperatoris iungit, legans cum praefectum eius amicitias, qui tunc Hispaniam inpugnabat. Leuvichildus autem dirixit ad eum nuntius, dicens: 'Veni ad me, quia extant causae, quas conferamus simul'. Et ille: 'Non ibo, quia infensus es mihi, pro eo quod sim catholicus'. At ille, datis praefecto imperatoris triginta milibus solidorum, ut se ab eius solacio revocaret, commotu exercitu, contra eum venit. Herminigildus vero, vocatis Grecis, contra patrem egreditur, relicta in urbe coniuge sua. Cumque Leuvichildus ex adverso veniret, relictus a solacio, cum viderit nihil se praevalere posse, eclesiam, qui erat propinquam, expetiit, dicens: 'Non veniat super me pater meus; nefas est enim, aut patrem a filio aut filium a patre interfici'. Haec audiens Leuvichildus, misit ad eum fratrem eius; qui, data sacramenta ne humiliaretur, ait: 'Tu ipse accede et prosterneree pedibus patris nostri, et omnia indulget tibi'. At ille poposcit vocare patrem suum; quo ingrediente, prostravit se ad pedes illius. Ille vero adpraehensum osculavit eum et blandis sermonibus delinitum duxit ad castra, oblitusque sacramenti, innuit suis et adpraehensum spoliavit eum ab indumentis suis induitque illum veste vile; regressusque ad urbem Tolidum, ab latispueris eius, misit eum in exilio cum uno tantum puerolo.

## 39. De interitu Chlodovechi.

<a id="hist-05-la-040"></a>
[**[040]**](#hist-05-en-040) Igitur post mortem filiorum Chilpericus rex mense Octobrio in Cothiam silvam plenus luctu cum coniuge resedebat. Tunc Chlodovechum, filium suum, Brinnacum, faciente regina, transmisit, ut scilicet et ipse ab hoc interitu deperiret. Graviter ibi his diebus morbus ille, qui fratres interficerat, seviebat; sed nihil ibidem incommodi pertulit. Ipsi enim rex Calam Parisiacae civitatis villam advenit. Post paucus vero dies Chlodovechum ad se venire praecepit; cui qualis interitus fuerit, dicere non pigebit. Igitur cum in supradicta villam apud patrem habitaret, coepit inmaturae iactare vel dicere: Ecce, mortuos fratres meus, ad me restitit omne regnum; mihi universae Galliae subicientur, imperiumque universum mihi fata largita sunt! Ecce inimicis in manu positis inferam quaecumque placuerit!' Sed et de noverca sua Fredegunde regina non condecibilia detractabat. Quae illa audiens, pavore nimio terrebatur. Post dies vero aliquot adveniens quidam ait reginae: 'Ut urbata de filiis sedeas, dolum id Chlodovechi est operatum. Nam ipsi concupiscens unius ancillarum tuarum filia, maleficiis tuos per matrem eius filios interficit, ideoque moneo, ne speres de te melius, cum tibi spes per quam regnare debueras sit ablata'. Tunc regina timore perterrita, furore succensa, nova orbitate conpuncta, adpraenensam puellam, in qua oculus iniecerat Chlodovechus, graviter verberatam, incidi comam capitis eius iussit ac scisso sode inpositam defigi ante metatum praecipit Chlodovechi. Matrem quoque puellae relegatam et turmentis diu cruciatam, elicuit ab ea professione, quae hos sermones veros esse firmaret. Regi exim haec et alia huiuscemodi insinuans, vindictam de Chlodovechum poposcit. Tunc rex in venatione directus, eum praecepit arcessire secretius. Quo adveniente, ex iussu regis adpraehensus in manicis a Desiderio atque Bobone ducibus, nudatur armis et vestibus, ac vili indumento contectus, reginae vinctus adducitur. Ad illa in custodia eum retinere praecepit, elecere ab eo cupiens, si haec ita ut audierat se haberent, vel cuius consilium usus fuerit, aut cuius haec instinctu fecisset, vel cum quibus maximae amicitias conlegasset. At ille reliqua denegans, amicitias multorum detexit. Denique post triduum regina vinctum iussit eum transire Matronam fluvium et in villa cui Nocito nomen est custodire. In qua custodia cultro percussus interiit ipsoque in loco sepultus est. Interea advenerunt nuntii ad regem, qui dicerent, quod ipse se ictu proprio perfodisset, et adhuc ipsum cultrum, de quo se perculit, in loco stare vulneris adfirmabant. Quibus verbis rex Chilpericus inlusus, nec flevit, quem ipse, ut ita dicam, morte tradiderat instigante regina. Servientes quoque illius per diversa dispersi sunt. Mater autem eius crudele morte negata; soror ipsius in monasterio delusa a pueris reginae transmittitur, in quo nune, veste mutata, consistit; opesque eorum omnes reginae dilatae sunt. Mulier, quae super Chlodovechum locuta fuerat, diiudicatur incendio concremare. Quae cum duceretur, reclamare coepit misera, se mendacia protulisse; sed nihil proficientibus verbis, legatam ad stipitem, vivens exuritur flammis. Thesaurarius Chlodovechi a Chuppani stabuli comite de Biturigo retractus, vinctus reginae transmissus est, diversis cruciatibus exponendus; sed eum regina et a suppliciis et a vinculis iussit absolvi liberumque, nobis obtenentibus [ad regi], abire permisit.

## 40. De Elafio et Eunio episcopis.

<a id="hist-05-la-041"></a>
[**[041]**](#hist-05-en-041) Post haec Elafius Catalaunensis episcopus propter causas Brunichildis reginae in Hispaniis in legatione directus, correptus a febre nimia, spiritum exalavit, et exinde delatus mortuus, ad civitatem suam sepultus est. Eonius quoque episcopus, quem ligatum Brittanorum supra meminimus, ad civitatem suam regredi non permissus, ut Andecavus pasceretur de publico, a rege praeceptum est. Qui Parisius adveniens, dum die dominica sacrosancta sollemnia celebraret, emissa cum hinnitu vocem, terrae conmit. Erumpente vero ab ore eius et naribus sanguinem, inter manus deportatus est, sed convaluit. Nimio enim vino deditus erat et plerumque ita deformiter inebriabatur, ut gressum facere non valeret.

## 41. De legatis Calliciensibus ac prodigiis.

<a id="hist-05-la-042"></a>
[**[042]**](#hist-05-en-042) Mirus rex Galliciensis legatos ad Guntchramnum regem dirixit. Cumque per Pectavum terminume praeterirent, quod tunc Chilpericus rex tenebat, nuntiata sunt ei. At ille sub custodia sibi eos exhibere praecepit et Parisius custodire. Eo tempore apud Pectavensem civitatem lopus ex silvis veniens, per portam ingressus est; clausisque portis, infra murus ipsius urbis obpressus, occisus est. Adserebant enim quidam et caelum ardentem se vidisse. Leger fluvius maior ab anno superiore fuit, postquam ei Cares torrens adiunxit. Ventus auster nimium violente cucurrit, ita ut silvas prosterneret, domus erueret, saepes efferret ipsoeque hominis ad internitionem usque volutaret. Erat enim spatium eius in latitudine quasi iugera septem, longitudo autem non potuit aestimare. Nam et galli plauso cantu in initio noctis saepe dederunt. Luna cumtenebricata est, et comitis stilla apparuit. Gravis autem lues in populo subsecuta est. Legati autem Sueborum post annum demissi, ad propria redierunt.

## 42. De Maurilione Cadurcorum episcopo.

<a id="hist-05-la-043"></a>
[**[043]**](#hist-05-en-043) Maurilio Cadurcensis urbis episcopus graviter aegrotabat ab humore podagrico; sed super hos dolores, quos ipse humor commovit, magnos sibi cruciatus addebat; nam saepe candentem ferrum tibiis ac pedibus defigebat, qao facilius cru ciatum sibi amplius adderit. Sed cum episcopatum eius multi expeterent, ipse Ursicinum, qui quondam referendarius Ultrogotho reginae fuerat, elegit; quem, dum adhuc viveret, benedici deprecans, migravit a saeculo. Fuit autem valde elemosinarius, in scripturis ecclesiasticis valde instructus, ita ut seriem diversarum generationum, quae in libris Veteris Testament! describitur, quod a multis difficile retinetur, hic plerumque memoriter recensiret. Fuit etiam et in iudiciis iustus ac defendens pauperes ecclesiae suae de manu malorum iudicum iuxta illud Iob: Conservavi egenum de manu potentis, et inopi, cui non erat adiutor, auxiliatus sum. Os viduae benedixit me, cum essem oculus caecorum, pes clodorum et invalidorum pater.

## 43. De altercatione cum heretico.

<a id="hist-05-la-044"></a>
[**[044]**](#hist-05-en-044) Leuvichildus vero rex Agilanem legatum ad Chilpericum mittit, virum nulli ingenii aut dispositiones rationes conperitum, sed tantum voluntatem in catholica lege perversum. Quem cum via Toronus detulisset, lacessire nos de fide et inpugnare eclesiastica dogmata coepit. 'Iniqua', inquid, 'fuit antiquorum episcoporum lata sententia, quae aequalem adseruit Filium Patri; vel qualiter', inquid, 'poterit esse Patri aequalis in potestate, qui ait: Pater maior me est? Non est ergo aequum, ut ei similis estimetur, cui se minorem dicit, cui tristitia mortis ingemit, cui postremo moriens spiritum, quasi nulla praeditus potestate, commendat. Unde patet, eum et aetate et potestate paterna minorem'. Ad haec ego interrogo, si crederit, Iesum Christum filium Dei esse, si eundemque esse Dei sapientiam, si lumen, si veritatem, si vitam, si iustitiam fateretur. Qui ait: 'Credo, haec omnia esse filium Dei'. Et ego: 'Die ergo mihi, quando Pater sine sapientia, quando sine lumine, quando sine vita, quando sine veritatem, quando sine iustitia fuerit. Sicut enim Pater sine istis esse non potuit, ita et sine Filio esse non potuit. Quae maximae ad dominice nominis mistirium coaptantur. Sed nec Pater esset utique, si filium non haberet. Quod autem eum dixisse ais: Pater maior me est, scias, eum hoc ex adsumptae carnis humilitate dixisse', ut cognuscase, non potestate, sed humilitate fuisse redemptum. Nam tu qui dicis: Pater maior me est, oportit te meminere, quod alibi ait: Ego et Pater unum sumus. Nam et mortis timor et commendatio spiritus ad infirmitatem corporis est referenda, ut, sicut verus Deus, ita et verus homo credatur'. Et ille: 'Cuius quis implet voluntatem, eius et iunior est; semper filius minor est patri, quia ille facit voluntatem patris, nec pater illius voluntatem facere non probatur'. Ad haec ego: 'Intellegi, quia Pater in Filio et Filius in Patre in una semper Deitate subsistit. Nam ut cognuscas, Patrem Fili facere voluntatem, si in te fides euangelica manet, audi, quid ipse Iesus deus noster, cum ad resuscitandum venit Lazarum, ait: Pater, gratias ago tibi, quoniam audisti me. Et ego sciebam, quia semper me audis; sed propter turbam, qui circumstat, dixi, ut credant, quia tu me misisti. Sed etcum ad passionem venit, ait: Pater, clarifica me claritatem quam habui apud temet ipsum, priusquam mundus fierit. Cui Pater de caelo respondit: Et clarificavi et iterum clarificabo. Aequalis est ergo Filius in Deitati non minor; sed neque aliquid minus habens. Nam si Deum confiteris, necesse est, integrum fatiares et nihil egentem; si verum integrum esse negas, Deum esse non credis'. Et ille: 'Ex adsumptum hominem coepit? Dei filius vocitari; nam erat quando non erat'. Et ego: 'Audi David dicentem ex persona Patris: Ex utero ante lucifero genui te. Et Iohannis euangelista ait: In principio erat Verbum, et Verbum erat apud Deum, et Deus erat Verbum. Hoc ergo Verbum caro factum est et habitabit in nobis, per quem facta sunt omnia. Nam vos caecati veneno persuasionis, nihil dignum de Deo sentitis'. Et ille: 'Numquid et Spiritum sanctum Deum dicitis aut aequalem Patri Filioque decernitis?' Cui ego: 'Una in tribus est voluntas, potestas, operatio; unus Deus in trinitate et trinus in unitate. Tres personae, sed unum regnum, una maiestas, una potentia omnipotentiaque'. Et ille: 'Spiritum sanctum, quem aequalem Patri profertis ac Filio, utrisque minor accipitur, quia et a Filio promissus et a Patre legitur missus. Nemo enim promittit, nisi quod suae dominationis subsistit, et nemo mittit nisi inferiorem sibi, sicut ipse ait in euangelio: Nisi ego abiero, Paraclitus ille non veniet; si autem abiero, mittam illum ad vos'. Ad haec ego respondi: 'Bene Filius ante passionem ait, quia, nisi ille ad Patrem victor remeaverit ac proprio sanguine redempto mundo dignum Deo ex homine praeparet habitaculum, non potest Spiritus sanctus, idemDeus, in pectore fanatico et originalis criminis labi infectum discendere. Spiritus enim sanctus, ait Salomon, fugiet fictum. Tu autem, si spem aliquam resurrectionis habis, noli loqui adversus Spiritum sanctum, quia iuxta sententia Domini: In Spiritu sancto blasphemante non remittitur neque in hoc saeculo neque in futuro'. Et ille: 'Deus est qui mittit, non est Deus qui mittitur'. Ad haec ego interrogo, si crediderit doctrinam Petri Paulique apostolorum. Respondentem autem eo: 'Credo', adiaci: 'Cum arguerit Petrus apostolus Annaniam pro fraude fundi, vide, quid dicat: Quid tibi visum est mentire Spiritui sancto? Non es enim mentitus hominibus, sed Deo. Et Paulus, cum gratiarum spiritalium distinguerit gradus: Haec omnia, inquid, operatur unus adque idem Spiritus, dividens unicuique prout vult. Qui enim quod voluerit facit, in nullius redegitur potestate. Nam vos, ut superius dixi, nihil recte de Trinitate sancta sentitis, et quam iniqua sit huius sectae perversitas, ipsius auctoris vestri, id est Arrii, expraessit interitus'. Ad haec ille respondit: 'Legem, quam non colis, blasphemare noli; nos vero quae creditis etsi non credimus, non tamen blasphemamus, quia non deputatur crimine, si et illa et illa colantur. Sic enim vulgato sermone dicimus, non esse noxium, si inter gentilium aras et Dei eclesiam quis transiens utraque venereture'. Cuius ego stultitiam cernens, aio: 'Ut video, et gentilium defensorem et hereticorum adsertorem te esse manifestas, cum et eclesiastica dogmata maculas et paganorum spurcitias praedicas adorari. Satius', inquio, 'faciebas, si ea te armaret fides, quam Abraham ad ilicem, Isac in ariete, Iacob in lapide, Moyses vidit in sente; quam Aaron portavit in logio, David exultavit in timphano, Salomon praedicavit in intellectu; quam omnes patriarchae, prophetae sive lex ipsa vel oraculis caecinit vel sacrificiis figuravit; quam et nunc praesens suffragatur Martinus noster vel possidit in pectore vel ostendit in opere, ut et tu conversus crederis inseparabilem Trinitatem, et acceptam a nobis benedictionem purgatoque a malae credulitatis veneno pectore, delerentur iniquitatis tuae'. At ille furore commotus et nescio quid quasi insanus frendens, ait: 'Ante anima ab huius corporis vinculis emicet, quam ab ullo relegionis vestrae sacerdote benedictione accipiam'. Et ego: 'Nec nostram Dominus relegionem sive fidem ita tepiscere faciat, ut distribuamus sanctum eius canibus ac praetiosarum margaritarum sacra porcis squalentibus exponamus'. Ad haec ille, relicta altercatione, surrexit et abiit. Sed post haec, cum in Hispaniis reversus fuisset, in infirmitate debilitatus, ad nostram relegionem, necessitate cogente, conversus est.

## 44. De his quae Chilpericus scripsit.

<a id="hist-05-la-045"></a>
[**[045]**](#hist-05-en-045) Per idem tempus Chilpericus rex scripsit indicolum, ut sancta Trinitas non in personarum distinctione, sed tantum Deus nominaretur, adserens indignum esse, ut Deus persona sicut homo carneus nominetur; adfirmans etiam, ipsum esse Patrem, qui est Filius, idemque ipsum esse Spiritum sanctum, qui Pater et Filius. 'Sic', inquid, 'prophetis ac patriarchis apparuit, sic eum ipsa lex nuntiavit'. Cumque haec mihi recitare iussisset, ait: 'Sic', inquid, Volo, ut tu vel reliqui doctores eclesiarum credatis'. Cui ego respondi: 'Hac credulitate relicta, pie rex, hoc te oportit sequi, quod nobis post apostolus alii doctores eclesiae reliquerunt, quod Elarius Eusebiusque docuerunt, quod et in baptismo es confessus'. Tunc iratus rex ait: 'Manifestum est mihi in hac causa Elarium Eusebiumque validos inimicos habere'. Cui ego respondi: 'Observare te convenit, neque Deum neque sanctos eius habere offensos. Nam scias, quia in persona aliter Pater, aliter Filius, aliter Spiritus sanctus. Non Pater adsumpsit carnem neque Spiritus sanctus, sed Filius, ut, qui erat Dei filius, ipse ad redemptionem hominis filius haberetur et virginis. Non Pater passus neque Spiritus sanctus, sed Filius, ut, qui carnem adsumpserat in mundo, ipse offerritur pro mundo. De personis vero quod ais non corporaliter, sed spiritaliter sentiendum est. In his ergo tribus personis una gloria, una aeternitas, una potestas'. At ille commotus ait: 'Sapientioribus a te haec pandam, qui mihi consentiant'. Et ego: 'Numquam erit sapiens, sed stultus, qui haec quae proponis sequi voluerit'. Ad haec ille frendens siluit. Non post multos vero dies adveniente Salvio Albigense episcopo, haec ei praecepit recensire, depraecans, ut sibi consentaneus fieret. Quod ille audiens, ita respuit, ut, si cartam, in qua haec scripta tenebantur, potuisset attingere, in frustra discerperit. Et sic rex ab hac intentione quievit. Scripsit alios libros idem rex versibus, quasi Sedulium secutus; sed versiculi illi nulla paenitus metricae conveniunt ratione. Addit autem et litteras litteris nostris, id est co, sicut Graeci habent, ae, the, uui, quarum caracteres hi sunt: Et misit epistulas in universis civitatibus regni sui, ut sic pueri docerentur ac libri antiquitus scripti, planati pomice, rescriberentur.

## 45. De obitu Agroeculae episcopi.

<a id="hist-05-la-046"></a>
[**[046]**](#hist-05-en-046) Agroecula enim Cabillonnensis episcopus hoc obiit tempore; fuitque homo valde elegans ac prudens, genere senaturio. Malta in civitate illa aedificia fecit, domus composuit, ecclesiam fabricavit, quam colomnis fulcivit, variavit marmore, mosevo depixit. Magnae autem abstinentiae fuit; nam numquam prandium usus est, nisi tantum cenam, ad quam sic temporive resedebat, ut sole stante consurgeret. Humanitatis exiguae, facundiae vero magnae erat. Obiit autem episcopatus anno quadragisimo octavo, evi autem octogisimo tertio. Cui Flavus, referendarius Gunthramni regis, successit.

## 46. De obitu Dalmatii episcopi.

<a id="hist-05-la-047"></a>
[**[047]**](#hist-05-en-047) Eo tempore et Dalmatius Rutenae civitatis episcopus migravit a saeculo, vir in omni sanctitate praecelsus, abstinens vel a cibis vel a concupiscentiis carnis, valde elymosinarius et cunctis humanus, in oratione et vigiliis satis stabilis. Ecclesiam construxit, sed dum eam ad emendationem saepius distruit, inconpositam dereliquit. Post cuius obitum multi, ut fit, episcopatum petibant. Transobadus vero presbiter, qui quondam archidiaconus eius fuerat, maxime in hoc intendibat, fidus, quod filium suum cum Gogone, qui tunc regis erat nutricius, commendaverat. Condidirat autem episcopus testamentum, in quo regis exenium quid post eius obitum accepiret indecabat, adiurans terribilibus sacramentis, ut in ecclesia illa non ordinaritur extraneus, non cupidus, non coniugali vinculo nexus, sed ab his omnibus expeditus, qui in solis tantum dominicis laudibus degebat, substitueritur. Transobadus autem presbiter epulum in ipsa urbe clericis praeparat. Resedentibus autem illis, unus praesbiterorum coepit antestitem memoratum inpudicis blasphemare sermonibus et usque ad hoc erupit, ut eum delerum et fatuum nominaret. Haec eo dicente, pincerna poculum oblaturus advenit. At ille acceptum dum ori proximat, tremire coepit, laxatumque de manu calicem, super alium, qui sibi erat proximus, caput reclinans, reddidit spiritum, ablatusque ab epulo ad sepulchrum, humo contectus est. Post haec relicto testamento antestitis in praesentia Childeberthi regis ac procerum eius, Theodosius, qui tunc archediaconatum urbis illius potiebatur, episcopus ordinatus est.

## 47. De comitatu Eunomii.

<a id="hist-05-la-048"></a>
[**[048]**](#hist-05-en-048) Audiens autem Chilpericus omnia mala, quae faciebat Leudastis ecclesiis Toronicis vel omni populo, Ansovaldum illuc dirigit. Qui veniens ad festivitatem sancti Martini, data nobis populo optionem, Eunomius in comitatum erigitur. Denique Leudastis cernens se remotum, ad Chilpericum dirigit, dicens: 'Usque nunc, o piissime rex, custodivi civitatem Turonicam; nunc autem, me ab actione remoto, vide, qualiter custodiatur. Nam noveris, quia Gregorius episcopus eam ad filium Sygiberthi tradere distinat'. Quod audiens rex, ait: 'Nequaquam, sed quia remotus es, ideo haec adponis'. Et ille: 'Maiora', inquit, 'de te ait episcopus; dicit enim, reginam tuam in adulterio cum episcopo Berthramno misceri'. Tunc iratus rex, caesum pugnis et calcibus, oneratum ferro recludi praecepit in carcere.

## 48. De malitia Leudastis.

<a id="hist-05-la-049"></a>
[**[049]**](#hist-05-en-049) Sed quia liber finem postulat, narrare aliquid de eius actionibus libet; sed prius videtur genus ac patriam moresque ordiri. Gracina Pictavensis insula vocitatur, in qua a fiscalis vinitoris servo Leuchadio nomine nascitur. Exinde ad servitium arcessitus, culinae regiae deputatur. Sed quia lippis erat in adolescentia oculis, quibus fumi acerbitas non congruebat, amotus a pistillo, promovitur ad cophinum. Sed dum inter firmentatas massas se delectari consimulat, servitium fugam iniens dereliquit. Cumque bis aut tertio reductus a fugat lapsu teneri non possit, auris unius incisione multatur. Dehinc cum notam inflictam corpori occulere nulla auctoritate valeret, ad Marcoveifam reginam, quam Chariberthus rex nimium diligens in loco sororis toro adsciverat, fugit. Quae libenter eum colligens, provocat equorumque meliorum deputat esse custodem. Hinc iam obsessus vanitate ac superbiae deditus, comitatum ambit stabulorum; quo accepto, cunctos despicit ac postponit, inflatur vanitate, luxuria dissolvitur, cupiditate succenditur et in causis patronae alumnus proprius huc illucque defertur. Cuius post obitum refertus praedis, locum ipsum cum rege Charibertho, oblatis muneribus, tenere coepit. Post haec, peccatis populi ingruentibus, comes Turonus destinatur ibique se amplius honoris gloriosi supercilio iactitat, ibique se exhibete rapacem praedis, turgidum rixis, adulteriis lutulentum. Ubi seminando discordias et inferendo calumnias non modicos thesauros adgregavit. Post obitum vero Chariberthi, cum in Sigiberthi sorte civitas illa venisset, transeunte eo ad Chilpericum, omnia quae inique adgregaverat a fidelibus nominati regis direpta sunt. Pervadente igitur Chilperico rege per Theodobertum, filium suum, urbem Turonicam, cum iam ego Turonus advenissem, mihi a Theodobertho strenue commendatur, ut scilicet comitatum, quem prius habuerat, potiretur. Multum se nobis humilem subditumque reddebat, iurans saepius super sepulchrum sancti antistitis, numquam se contra rationis ordinem esse venturum seque mihi tam in causis propriis quam in ecclesiae necessitatibus in omnibus esse fidelem. Timebat enim, quod postea evenit, ne urbem illam iterum rex Sigiberthus in suo dominio revocaret. Quo defuncto, succedente iterum Chilperico in regno, iste in comitatum accedit. Adveniente autem Turonus Merovecho, omnes res eius usquequaque diripuit. Sed hic, dum Sigiberthus duos annos Turonus tenuit, hic in Brittaniis latuit. Qui, adsumpto, ut diximus, comitatu, in tali levitate elatus est, ut in domo ecclesiae cum toracibus atque loricis, praecinctus pharetra et contum manu gerens, capite galeato ingrederetur, de nullo securus, quia omnibus erat adversus. Iam si in iudicio cum senioribus vel laicis vel clericis resedisset et vidisset hominem iustitiam prosequentem, protinus agebatur in furias, ructabat convicia in civibus; presbiteros manicis iubebat extrahi, milites fustibus verberari, tantamque utebatur crudelitatem, ut vix referri possit. Discedente autem Merovecho, qui res eius diripuerat, nobis calumniator existit, adserens fallaciter Merovechum nostro usum consilio, ut res eius auferret. Sed post inlata damna iterat iterum sacramenta pallamque sepulchri beati Martini fideiussorem donat, se nobis numquam adversaturum.

## 49. De insidiis, quas nobis fecit.

<a id="hist-05-la-050"></a>
[**[050]**](#hist-05-en-050) Sed quia longum est per ordinem prosequi periurias vel reliqua mala eius, veniamus ad illud, qualiter me voluit iniquis ac nefariis calamniis supplantare, vel qualiter in eum ultio divina descendit, ut illud adimpleretur: Omnis subplantans subplantabitur; et iterum: Qui fodit foveam, incidit in ea. Igitur post multa mala quae in me meisque intulit, post multas direptiones rerum ecclesiasticarum, adiuncto sibi Riculfo presbitero simili malitia perverso, ad hoc erupit, ut diceret, me crimen in Fredegundem reginam dixisse; adserens, si archidiaconus meus Plato aut Galienus amicus noster subdirentur poenae, convincerent me utique haec locutum. Tunc rex iratus, ut supra diximus, iussit eum pugnis calcibusque caesum oneratumque catenis recludi in carcerem. Nam Riculfum clericum se habere dicebat, per quem haec locutus fuisset. Hic vero Riculfus subdiaconus, simili levitate perfacilis, ante hoc anno consilio cum Leudaste de hac causa habito, causas offensionis requirit, quibus scilicet me offenso ad Leudastem transiret; nanctisque tandem, ipsum adivit, ac per menses quattuor dolos omnes ac muscipulas praeparatas, ad me cum ipso Leudaste revertitur, deprecans, ut eum debeam recipere excusatum. Feci, fateor, et occultum hostem publice in domo suscepi. Discedente vero Leudaste, ipse se pedibus meis sternit, dicens: 'Nisi succurras velociter, periturus sum. Ecce, instigante Leudaste, locutus sum quod loqui non debui! Nunc vero aliis me regnis emitte; quod nisi feceris, a regalibus conprehensus, mortales poenas sum lueturus'. Cui ego aio: 'Si quid incongruum rationi effatus es, sermo tuus in caput tuum erit; nam ego altero te regno non mittam, ne suspectus habear coram rege'. Post ista Leudastis extitit accusator eius, dicens, se sermones iam dictos a Riculfo audisse subdiacono. At ille iterum vinctus, relaxato Leudaste, custodiae deputatur, dicens, Galienum eadem die et Platonem archidiaconem fuisse praesentes, cum haec est episcopus elocutus. Sed Riculfus presbiter, qui iam promissionem de episcopatu a Leudaste habebat, in tantum elatus fuerat, ut magi Simonis superbia aequaretur. Qui tertio aut eo amplius mihi sacramentum super sepulchrum sancti Martini dederat, in die sexta paschae in tantum me conviciis et sputis egit, ut vix manibus temperaret, fidus scilicet doli quem praeparaverat. In crastina autem die, id est sabbati in ipsa pascha, venit Leudastis in urbe Turonica, adsimilansque aliud negotii agere, adprehensis Platonem archidiaconem et Galienum in vincla conectit, catenatosque ac exutos veste iubet eos ad reginam deduci. Haec ego audiens, dum in domo ecclesiae residerem, mestus turbatusque ingressus oratorium, Davitici carminis sumo librum, ut scilicet apertus aliquem consolationis versiculum daret. In quo ita repertum est: Eduxit eos in spe, et non timuerunt; et inimicos eorum operuit mare. Interea ingressi in fluvium super pontem, qui duabus lintribus tenebatur, navis illa, quae Leudastem vehebat, dimergitur, et nisi natandi fuisset adminiculo liberatus, cum sociis forsitan interisset. Navis vero alia quae huic innexa erat, quae et vinctos vehebat, super aquas Dei auxilio elevatur. Igitur deducti ad regem qui vincti fuerant, incusantur instanter, ut capitali sententia finirentur. Sed rex recogitans, absolutos a vinculo libera custodia reservat inlaesos. Ad civitatem vero Turonuse Berulfus dux cum Eunomio comite fabulam fingit, quod Gunthramnus rex capere vellet Turonicam civitatem, et idcirco, ne aliqua neglegentia accederet, oportere, ait, urbem custodia consignari. Ponunt portis dolose custodes, qui civitatem tueri adsimilantes, me utique custodirent. Mittunt etiam qui mihi consilium ministrarent, ut ad occultum, adsumptis melioribus rebus ecclesiae, Arverno fuga secederem; sed non acquievi. Igitur rex, arcessitis regni sui episcopis, causam diligenter iussit exquiri. Cumque Biculfus clericus saepius discuteretur occulte et contra me vel meos multas fallacias promulgaret, Modestus quidam faber lignarius ait ad eum: 'O infelix, qui contra episcopum tuum tam contumaciter ista meditaris! Satius tibi erat silere, et, petita venia episcopi, gratiam inpetraris'. Ad haec ille clamare coepit voce magna ac dicere: 'En ipse, qui mihi silentium indicit, ne prosequar veritatem! En reginae inimicum, qui causam criminis eius non sinet inquiri!' Nuntiantur protinus haec reginae. Adprehenditur Modestus, torquetur, flagellatur, et in vincla compactus, custodiae deputatur. Cumque inter duos custodes catenis et cippo teneretur vinctus, media nocte, dormientibus custodibus, orationem fudit ad Dominum, ut dignaretur eius potentia miserum visitare, et qui innocens conligatus fuerat, visitatione Martini praesulis ac Medardi absolveretur. Mox, disruptis vinculis, confracto cippo, reserato ostio, sancti Medardi basilicam, nocte nobis vigilantibus, introiit. Congregati igitur apud Brinnacum villam episcopi, in unam domum resedere iussi sunt. Dehinc adveniente rege, data omnibus salutatione ac benedictione accepta, resedit. Tunc Berthramnus Burdegalensis civitatis episcopus, cui hoc cum regina crimen inpactum fuerat, causam proponit meque interpellat, dicens, a me sibi ac reginae crimen obiectum. Negavi ego in veritate haec locutum, et audisse quidem haec alios, nam non excogitasse. Nam foris domum rumor in populo magnus erat, dicentium: 'Cur haec super sacerdotem Dei obiciuntur? Cur talia rex prosequitur? Numquid potuit episcopus talia dicere vel de servo? Heu, heu, domine Deus, largire auxilium servo tuo!' Rex autem dicebat: 'Crimen uxoris meae meum habetur obprobrium. Si ergo censitis, ut super episcopum testes adhibeantur, ecce adsunt! Certe, si videtur, ut haec non fiant et in fidem episcopi committantur, dicite; libenter audiam quae iubetis'. Mirati sunt omnes regis vel prudentiam vel patientiam simul. Tunc cunctis dicentibus: 'Non potest persona inferior super sacerdotem credi', restitit ad hoc causa, ut, dictis missis in tribus altaribus, me de his verbis exuerem sacramento. Et licet canonibus essent contraria, pro causa tamen regis impleta sunt. Sed nec hoc sileo, quod Rigunthis regina, condolens doloribus meis, ieiunium cum omni domo sua celebravit, quousque puer nuntiaret, me omnia sic implesse, ut fuerant instituta. Igitur regressi sacerdotes ad regem aiunt: 'Impleta sunt omnia ab episcopo quae imperata sunt. O rex, quid nunc ad te, nisi ut cum Berthramno accusatore fratris communione priveris?' Et ille: 'Non', inquid, 'ego nisi audita narravi'. Quaerentibus illis, quis hoc dixerit, respondit, se haec a Leudaste audisse. Ille autem secundum infirmitatem vel consilii vel propositionis suae iam fugam inierat. Tunc placuit omnibus sacerdotibus, ut sator scandali, infitiator reginae, accusator episcopi, ab omnibus arceretur ecclesiis, eo quod se ab audientia subtraxisset. Unde et epistolam subscriptam aliis episcopis qui non adfuerant transmiserunt. Et sic unusquisque in loco suo regressus est. Leudastis vero haec audiens, basilicam sancti Petri Parisius expetiit. Sed cum audisset edictum regis, ut in suo regno a nullo colligeretur, et praesertim quod filius eius, quem domi reliquerat, obisset, Turonus occulte veniens, quae melius habuit in Biturigo transposuit. Prosequentibus vero regalibus pueris, ipse per fugam labitur. Capta quoque uxore eius, in pago Tornacensi exilio recluditur. At Riculfus clericus ad interficiendum deputatur. Pro cuius vita vix obtinui, tamen de tormentis excusare non potui. Nam nulla res, nullum metallum tanta verbera potuit sustinere, sicut hic miserrimus. Nam ab hora tertia diei, revinctis postergum manibus, suspensus ad arborem dependebat; ad horam vero nonam depositus, extensus ad trocleas, caedebatur fustibus, virgis ac loris duplicibus, et non ab uno vel duobus, sed quanti accedere circa miseros potuerunt artus, toti caesores erant. Cum autem iam in discrimine esset, tunc aperuit veritatem et archana doli publice patefecit. Dicebat enim ob hoc reginae crimen obiectum, ut, eiecta de regno, interfectis fratribus et patre, Chlodovechus regnum acciperet, Leudastis ducatum, Riculfus vero presbiter, qui iam a tempore beati Eufronii episcopi amicus erat Chlodovechi, episcopatum Turonicum ambiret, huic Riculfo clerico archidiaconatu promisso. Nos vero cum Dei gratia Turonus reversi, invenimus ecclesiam conturbatam pei Riculfum presbiterum. Nam hic sub Eufronio episcopo de pauperibus provocatus, archidiaconus ordinatus est. Exinde ad presbiterium admotus, recessit ad propria. Semper elatus, inflatus, praesumptiosus; nam, me adhuc commorante cum rege, hic, quasi iam esset episcopus, in domo ecclesiae ingreditur impudenter, argentum describit ecclesiae reliquasque res sub sua redegit potestate. Maiores clericos muneribus ditat, largitur vineas, prata distribuit; minoribus vero fustibus plagisque multis etiam manu propria adfecit, dicens: 'Recognoscite dominum vestrum, qui victoriam de inimicis obtinuit, cuius ingenium Turonicam urbem ab Arvernis populis emundavit'; ignorans miser, quod praeter quinque episcopos reliqui omnes, qui sacerdotium Turonicum susceperunt, parentum nostrorum prosapiae sunt coniuncti. Illud saepe suis familiaribus dicere erat solitus, hominem prudentem non aliter nisi in periuriis quis decipere possit. Sed cum me reverso adhuc despiceret nec ad salutationem meam, sicut reliqui cives fecerant, adveniret, sed magis me interficere minitaret, cum consilio provincialium eum in monasterio removeri praecipio. Cumque ibidem artius distringeretur, intercedentibus Felicis episcopi missis, qui memoratae causae fautor extiterat, circumventum periuriis abbatem, fuga labitur et usque ad Felicem accedit episcopum, eumque ille ambienter collegit, quem execrare debuerat. Leudastis vero in Biturigo pergens, omnes thesauros quos de spoliis pauperum detraxerat secum tulit. Nec multo post, inruentibus Biturigis cum iudice loci super eum, omne aurum argentumque vel quod secum detulerat abstulerunt, nihilque ei nisi quod super se habuit relinquentes; ipsamque abstulissent vitam, nisi fuga fuisset elapsus. Resumptis dehinc viribus, cum aliquibus Turonicis iterum inruit super praedones suos, interfectoque uno, aliqua de rebus ipsis recepit et in Turonico revertitur. Audiens haec Berulfus dux, misit pueros suos cum armorum apparatu, ut apprehenderent eum. Ille vero cernens, se iam iamque capi, relictis rebus, basilicam sancti Hilarii Pictavensis expetiit. Berulfus vero dux res captas regi transmisit. Leudastis enim egrediebatur de basilica, et inruens in domibus diversorum, praedas publice exercebat. Sed et in adulteriis saepe in ipsam sanctam porticum deprehensus est. Commota autem regina, quod scilicet locus Deo sacratus taliter pollueretur, iussit eum a basilica sancta eici. Qui eiectus, ad hospites suos iterum in Biturigo expetiit, deprecans, se occuli ab eis.

## 50. Quae beatus Salvius de Chilperico praedixit.

<a id="hist-05-la-051"></a>
[**[051]**](#hist-05-en-051) Et licet de beati Salvii episcopi conlocutione superius memorare debueram, sed quia mentem excessit, esse sacrilegum non arbitror, si in posterum scribatur. Igitur cum, vale post sinodum memoratam regi iam dicta, ad propria redire vellimus, non ante discidere placuit, nisi hunc virum, libatis osculis, linquerimus. Quem quesitum in atrio Brinnacinsis domus repperi. Cui dixi, quia iam eram ad propriam rediturus. Tunc remoti paululum, dum hinc inde sermocinaremur, ait mihi: 'Videsne super hoc tectum quae ego suspicio?' Cui ego: 'Video enim supertegulum, quod nuper rex poni praecepit'. Et illi: 'Aliud, inquid, 'non aspicis?' Cui ego: 'Nihil aliud video'. Suspicabar enim, quod aliquid ioculariter loqueretur. Et adiaci: 'Si tu aliquid magis cernis, enarra'. At ille, alta trahens suspiria, ait: 'Video ego evaginatum irae divinae gladio super domum hanc dependentem'. Verumtamen non fefellit dictio sacerdotem; nam post dies viginti duo filii regis, quos superius mortuos scripsimus, obierunt. EXPLICIT LIBER QUINTUS, FINITUS IN ANNO QUINTO CHILDEBERTI REGIS.
