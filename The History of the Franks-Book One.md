# BOOK ONE

## The Scene

Gregory of Tours was born about 538 into a Gallo-Roman senatorial family with close episcopal connections. Raised principally in the Auvergne, he became a deacon and was consecrated bishop of Tours in 573. He wrote from personal acquaintance with many of the rulers, bishops, and other figures in his narrative, while also drawing on Scripture, chronicles, earlier histories, written records, oral testimony, and his own observation. He died in 594.

The ten Books of the Histories move from creation to 591. Book One is a chronological and theological prologue extending from Adam to the death of Saint Martin in 397; the later books carry the account through the Frankish kingdoms and into Gregory’s own episcopate. Gregory’s chronological totals and synchronisms are retained as transmitted and flagged where needed, not silently corrected. His reports of miracles, visions, and divine judgments remain part of the history he wrote: they express his understanding of sanctity, justice, and God’s action in human affairs.

## ENGLISH TRANSLATION

### Gregory’s First Preface

<a id="hist-01-en-001"></a>
[**[001]**](#hist-01-la-001) As the cultivation of liberal letters was declining—or rather perishing—in the cities of Gaul, while many deeds were being done, both righteous and wicked, the savagery of the peoples was raging, the fury of kings was growing sharper, churches were being attacked by heretics and defended by catholics, faith in Christ was burning in many and growing lukewarm in some, and churches themselves were being enriched by the devout or stripped by the faithless, no grammarian skilled in the art of dialectic could be found to depict these things either in prose or in metrical verse. Many therefore kept lamenting: “Woe to our days, for the study of letters has perished among us, and no orator is found among the peoples who can publish present deeds upon the page.” As I continually heard these and similar complaints, I could not conceal, for the remembrance of the past and the knowledge of those to come, either the struggles of the wicked or the life of those who lived rightly, even in uncultivated speech. I was especially impelled by the fact that I have often heard my own people say with wonder: “Few understand the philosophizing orator; many understand the rustic speaker.” I also wished the beginning of the first book to be devoted to a calculation of the years from the very beginning of the world, and I have set down its chapters below.

### The Author’s Profession of Faith and Purpose

<a id="hist-01-en-002"></a>
[**[002]**](#hist-01-la-002) As I am about to write of the wars of kings with hostile peoples, of martyrs with pagans, and of churches with heretics, I wish first to set forth my faith, so that whoever reads me may not doubt that I am catholic. I have also thought fit, for the sake of those who despair as the end of the world approaches, to gather the sum of earlier years from the chronicles and histories and explain openly how many years there have been since the world’s beginning. But first I beg the reader’s pardon if in letters or syllables I transgress the art of grammar, with which I am not fully imbued. My one concern is to retain without pretence or hesitation of heart what is preached as worthy of belief in the church, for I know that one who is subject to sin can obtain pardon before God through pure faith.

I believe in God the Father almighty. I believe in Jesus Christ, His only Son, our Lord, born of the Father, not made, not after time, but always with the Father before all time. For the Father could not be called Father unless He had a Son, nor would the Son be a Son if He did not have a Father. Those who say, “There was a time when He was not,” I abominate and declare separated from the church. I believe that this Christ is the Word of the Father, through whom all things were made. I believe that this Word was made flesh, that the world was redeemed by His passion, and that His humanity, not His deity, underwent the passion. I believe that He rose on the third day, freed lost humanity, ascended into heaven, sits at the right hand of the Father, and will come to judge the living and the dead.

I believe that the Holy Spirit proceeded from the Father and the Son, not inferior as though He had not existed before, but equal, always God coeternal with the Father and the Son, of one substance in nature, equal in omnipotence, coeternal in essence; He was never without the Father or the Son, and is not inferior to the Father or the Son. I believe that this holy Trinity subsists in the distinction of persons: one person of the Father, another of the Son, another of the Holy Spirit. In this Trinity I confess one deity, one power, one essence. I believe that blessed Mary was a virgin before the birth and remained a virgin after the birth. I believe that the soul is immortal, yet has no share in the divine essence. I faithfully believe all things instituted by the 318 bishops at Nicaea.

Concerning the end of the world, I hold what I learned from earlier writers: first the Antichrist will come. The Antichrist will first introduce circumcision, declaring himself Christ; then he will place his statue in the temple at Jerusalem to be adored, as we read that the Lord said: “You will see the abomination of desolation standing in the holy place.” But the Lord Himself makes that day known to the eyes of all, saying: “Of that day and hour no one knows, neither the angels of heaven nor the Son, but the Father alone.” Here we may also answer the heretics who attack us by asserting that the Son is inferior to the Father because He does not know that day. Let them understand that it is the Christian people, called the son, of whom God foretells: “I will be their father, and they shall be my sons.” For if He had foretold this of the only-begotten Son, He would never have placed the angels before Him. Thus He says: “Neither the angels of heaven nor the sons,” showing that He said this not of the only-begotten but of the adopted people. Our end is Christ Himself, who in generous kindness will grant us eternal life if we turn to Him.

The chronicles of Eusebius, bishop of Caesarea, and of the priest Jerome speak plainly concerning the calculation of this world’s years and unfold the reckoning of the whole sequence. Orosius too, investigating most diligently, gathered the entire number of years from the world’s beginning to his own time. Victorius likewise did this when he investigated the order of the paschal solemnities. Following the copies of these writers, I wish, if the Lord deigns to lend His aid, to compute the whole accumulation of years from the formation of the first man to my own time. I shall accomplish this more readily if I begin with Adam himself.

### 1. Adam and Eve

<a id="hist-01-en-003"></a>
[**[003]**](#hist-01-la-003) In the beginning the Lord formed heaven and earth in His Christ, who is the beginning of all things—that is, in His Son. After creating the elements of the whole world, He took a clod of fragile clay and formed man in His image and likeness; He breathed into his face the breath of life, and he became a living soul. While he slept, a rib was taken from him and the woman Eve was created. There is no doubt that this first man Adam bore the type of the Redeemer before he sinned. For the Redeemer, sleeping in the sleep of His passion, produced water and blood from His side and presented to Himself the virgin and immaculate church, redeemed by blood and cleansed by water, having neither spot nor wrinkle: washed by water because of the spot, stretched upon the cross because of the wrinkle. These first human beings lived blessed amid the delights of paradise, but, beguiled by the serpent’s cunning, they transgressed the divine commandments; cast out from the angelic dwelling, they were assigned to the labors of the world.

### 2. Cain and Abel

<a id="hist-01-en-004"></a>
[**[004]**](#hist-01-la-004) The woman knew the attendant and conceived and bore two sons. But while God graciously accepted the sacrifice of one, the other swelled with kindled envy and, rising as a new parricide to shed his brother’s blood, overpowered, defeated, and killed his brother.

### 3. Enoch the Righteous

<a id="hist-01-en-005"></a>
[**[005]**](#hist-01-la-005) Thereafter the whole race rushed into abominable crime except righteous Enoch, who walked in the ways of God and was taken up by the Lord Himself because of his righteousness, thus being removed from amid a sinful people. For we read: “Enoch walked with God, and he did not appear, because God took him.”

### 4. The Flood

<a id="hist-01-en-006"></a>
[**[006]**](#hist-01-la-006) The Lord, moved against the iniquities of a people not walking in His paths, sent the Flood, and with its inundation destroyed every living soul from the face of the earth. Only Noah, most faithful and especially His own, bearing in himself the type of the Redeemer, did He preserve in the ark, together with his wife and the wives of his three sons, for the restoration of posterity. Here heretics object to us: why did Holy Scripture say that the Lord was angry? Let them understand that our God is not angry as a man is. He is moved to terrify, drives away to recall, and is angry to correct. Nor do I doubt that the form of the ark bore the type of mother church. Passing among the waves and rocks of this age, she carries us in her maternal bosom, defending us from threatening evils with loving embrace and protection. From Adam to Noah there are ten generations: Adam, Seth, Enos, Cainan, Mahalalel, Jared, Enoch, Methuselah, Lamech, Noah. In these ten generations 2,242 years are found. **[FLAG — Gregory’s received chronology.]** The book of Joshua plainly explains that Adam was buried in the land of Enacim, formerly called Hebron.

### 5. Cush, the Inventor of the Idol

<a id="hist-01-en-007"></a>
[**[007]**](#hist-01-la-007) After the Flood Noah had three sons: Shem, Ham, and Japheth. Nations issued from Japheth, and likewise from Ham and Shem; as the ancient history says, the human race was dispersed by them beneath the whole heaven. Ham’s firstborn was Cush. He possessed the whole magical art through the devil’s instruction and was the first inventor of idolatry. At the devil’s instigation he was the first to set up an idol for adoration; he also showed men stars and fire falling from heaven by false power. He crossed into Persia. The Persians called him Zoroaster, that is, “living star.” From him they also learned to adore fire; they worship as a god the man who was himself consumed by divine fire.

### 6. Babylon

<a id="hist-01-en-008"></a>
[**[008]**](#hist-01-la-008) When multiplied humanity was dispersing through every land, they came from the east and found a grassy plain at Shinar. There they built a city and strove to construct a tower that would reach the heavens. God, confounding their vain thought and their language at once, scattered them over the whole wide-extending earth. The city was called Babel, that is, confusion, because there God confounded their languages. This is Babylon, built by the giant Nimrod, son of Cush. As Orosius’s history relates, it was laid out as a square on the wondrous level of the plain. Its wall, made of baked brick set in bitumen, was fifty cubits broad and two hundred cubits high, with a circuit of 470 stades. One stade contains five arpents. Fifty gates stood on each side, making one hundred. **[FLAG — the transmitted figures say “quinae,” fifty on each side, but give one hundred as the total.]** The doors of these gates were formed of cast bronze in wondrous size. The same historian relates many other things about this city, adding: “And although the building possessed such magnificence, nevertheless it was conquered and overthrown.”

### 7. Abraham and Ninus

<a id="hist-01-en-009"></a>
[**[009]**](#hist-01-la-009) Noah’s first son was Shem; in the tenth generation from him Abraham was born: Noah, Shem, Arphaxad, Shelah, Eber, Peleg, Reu, Serug, and Terah, who begot Abraham. In these ten generations—from Noah to Abraham—942 years are found. **[FLAG — Gregory’s received chronology.]** At that time Ninus reigned; he built the city Ninus, which they call Nineveh, whose extent Jonah the prophet defined as a three days’ journey. Abraham was born in the forty-third year of his reign. This Abraham is the beginning of our faith. He received the promise. Christ our Lord revealed to him that He would be born and suffer for us in exchange for the victim, saying in the Gospels: “Abraham rejoiced that he might see my day; and he saw it and was glad.” Severus relates in his chronicle that this burnt offering was offered on Mount Calvary, where the Lord was crucified, just as is famously reported in Jerusalem to this day. On this mountain stood the holy cross to which the Redeemer was fixed, and from which His blessed blood flowed. Abraham received the sign of circumcision, showing that what he bore in the body we should bear in the heart, as the prophet says: “Circumcise yourselves for your God, and circumcise the foreskin of your heart; and do not follow strange gods”; and again: “No one uncircumcised in heart shall enter my holy places.” After adding a syllable to his name, God called this Abraham the father of many nations.

### 8. Isaac, Esau, Job, and Jacob

<a id="hist-01-en-010"></a>
[**[010]**](#hist-01-la-010) When Abraham was one hundred years old, he begot Isaac. In the sixtieth year of Isaac’s life twin sons were born to Rebecca. The first was Esau, that is Edom, meaning earthly, who sold his birthright because of gluttony. He is the father of the Idumeans. In the fourth generation from him Jobab was born: Esau, Reuel, Zerah, Jobab, who is also Job. He lived 249 years; in the eighty-ninth year he was delivered from his affliction. After the affliction he lived 170 years, with all his possessions restored twofold, and rejoiced in as many sons as he had lost. **[FLAG — Gregory’s transmitted chronology and figures.]**

### 9. Joseph in Egypt

<a id="hist-01-en-011"></a>
[**[011]**](#hist-01-la-011) The second son was Jacob. Jacob was beloved of God, as He says through the prophet: “Jacob I loved, but Esau I hated.” After wrestling with the angel he was called Israel, from whom come the Israelites. He begot the twelve patriarchs, whose names are Reuben, Simeon, Levi, Judah, Issachar, Zebulun, Dan, Naphtali, Gad, and Asher. After these he begot Joseph by Rachel in the ninety-second year of his life. He loved him more than his other sons. He also had Benjamin by her, the last of all. When Joseph was in the sixteenth year of his life, bearing the type of the Redeemer, he saw dreams which he related to his brothers: as though he were binding sheaves and his brothers’ sheaves adored his; and again, as though the sun, moon, and eleven stars came before him. This bred great hatred toward him among his brothers. Inflamed with envy, they sold him for thirty pieces of silver to Ishmaelites passing into Egypt. When famine threatened and his brothers went down into Egypt, they were recognized by Joseph, but they did not recognize Joseph. After subjecting them to many trials and bringing Benjamin there, he revealed himself to them; Benjamin too had been born of his mother Rachel. Afterward all the Israelites went down into Egypt and through Joseph enjoyed Pharaoh’s favor. Jacob blessed his sons and died in Egypt; Joseph buried him in his father’s tomb in the land of Canaan. After Joseph and Pharaoh died, the whole race was subjected to servitude. Through Moses it was freed after the ten plagues of Egypt, when Pharaoh was drowned in the Red Sea.

### 10. The Crossing of the Red Sea

<a id="hist-01-en-012"></a>
[**[012]**](#hist-01-la-012) Since many have said much about this crossing of the sea, I have thought fit to insert something here about the situation of the place and the crossing itself. The Nile runs through Egypt, as you well know, and irrigates it by its flood; hence the Egyptians are called Nilicolae, dwellers by the Nile. Many who have explored the region say that its banks are now filled with sacred monasteries. On its bank stands not the Babylon mentioned above, but the city of Babylon in Egypt, where Joseph built granaries of squared stone and cement with wondrous workmanship. They were broader at the bottom and narrowed toward the top, so that grain could be poured in through a small opening; these granaries are still seen today. The king set out from this city with armies of chariots and a great force of infantry to pursue the Hebrews. The river just mentioned comes from the east, turns toward the west, and goes to the Red Sea. From the west a lake or arm of the Red Sea advances eastward, about fifty miles long and eighteen broad. At the head of this lake the city Clysma was built, not for the fertility of the place—nothing is more barren—but for its harbor, because ships coming from India rest there for the harbor’s convenience; goods brought there are carried through all Egypt. The Hebrews came through the desert toward this lake as far as the sea itself, found fresh water, and pitched camp. They were hemmed into this narrow place by both desert and sea, as it is written: “Pharaoh, hearing that the sea and desert had shut them in and that there was no way by which they could proceed, set out to pursue them.” When the people cried to Moses as the enemy approached, the rod was cast over the sea at the Divine command and the sea was divided. They proceeded through the dry bed, walled on every side by water, as Scripture says; unharmed, they reached the shore opposite Mount Sinai, and crossed under Moses’ leadership while the Egyptians were drowned.

Many things are told about this crossing, as I have said, but I have taken care to include in this page what I learned from wise men, and certainly from those men who had approached that very place. They also say that the ruts made by the chariot wheels remain to this day and can be seen in the depths as far as the eye’s sight reaches. If a slight disturbance of the sea covers them, when it grows still they are divinely renewed as they had been. Others say that, making a small circuit by sea, they returned to the same shore from which they had entered. Others assert that there was one entrance for all; some, that a separate way lay open to each tribe, using the testimony of the psalm: “Who divided the Red Sea into divisions.” I must understand these divisions spiritually, not literally. For in this world too, figuratively called a sea, there are many divisions; not all can pass to life equally or by one road. Some cross at the first hour: those reborn through baptism who can remain immaculate from every carnal defilement until the end of their present life. Others at the third hour: those converted at a greater age. Others at the sixth: those restrained by the ardor of wantonness. As the evangelist records, they are hired for work in the Lord’s vineyard at each of these hours according to their own faith. These are the divisions by which this sea is crossed.

As for those who came from the sea and returned along the shore of the lake, this is what the Lord says to Moses: “Let them return and pitch camp opposite Pi-hahiroth, which is between Migdol and the sea opposite Baal-zephon.” There is no doubt that the crossing of the sea and the pillar of cloud bore the type of my baptism, as blessed Paul the apostle says: “I would not have you ignorant, brothers, that our fathers were all under the cloud, and all were baptized into Moses in the cloud and in the sea.” The pillar of fire bore the type of the Holy Spirit. From the birth of Abraham to the departure of the children of Israel from Egypt and the crossing of the Red Sea—which was Moses’ eightieth year—462 years are computed. **[FLAG — Gregory’s received chronology.]**

### 11. The People in the Desert, and Joshua

<a id="hist-01-en-013"></a>
[**[013]**](#hist-01-la-013) Then for forty years the Israelites dwelt in the desert, were instructed in the laws, were tested, and were fed with angelic food. After receiving the Law and crossing the Jordan with Joshua, they obtained the land of promise.

### 12. The Captivity of the Israelite People, and the Generations down to David

<a id="hist-01-en-014"></a>
[**[014]**](#hist-01-la-014) After this crossing, because they neglected the divine commandments, they were often subjected to the service of foreigners. But when they turned back and groaned, the Lord delivered them by the arm of strong men. Afterward they asked the Lord through Samuel for a king, as other nations had; they received first Saul and then David. From Abraham to David there are fourteen generations: Abraham, Isaac, Jacob, Judah, Perez, Hezron, Ram, Amminadab, Nahshon, Salmon, Boaz, Obed, Jesse, David. David begot Solomon by Bathsheba. Solomon was raised to the kingdom through Nathan the prophet, his brother, and his mother.

### 13. Solomon and the Building of the Temple

<a id="hist-01-en-015"></a>
[**[015]**](#hist-01-la-015) When David died and his son began to reign, the Lord appeared to him and promised to grant what he asked. Setting earthly riches aside, he sought wisdom instead. This pleased the Lord, so that he heard from Him: “Because you did not seek the kingdoms of the world or their riches, but asked for wisdom, therefore receive it. Before you no one was so wise, and after you there will be none.” He later proved this by the judgment he gave between two women disputing over one infant. This Solomon built the temple in the Lord’s name with wondrous workmanship, bringing into it much gold, silver, bronze, and iron, so that some said no building like it had ever been made in the world. From the departure of the children of Israel from Egypt to the building of the temple—which was in the seventh year of Solomon’s reign—480 years are found, as the history of Kings testifies. **[FLAG — Gregory’s transmitted chronology.]**

### 14. The Division of the Israelite Kingdom

<a id="hist-01-en-016"></a>
[**[016]**](#hist-01-la-016) After Solomon’s death the kingdom was divided into two parts through Rehoboam’s harshness. Two tribes remained with Rehoboam and were called Judah; ten tribes went to Jeroboam and were called Israel. Thereafter they turned aside to idolatry, and neither the prophecies of the prophets, nor the prophets’ deaths, nor the ruin of their country, nor even the destruction of their own kings subdued them.

### 15. The Captivity in Babylon

<a id="hist-01-en-017"></a>
[**[017]**](#hist-01-la-017) At last the Lord, angered against them, raised up Nebuchadnezzar, who carried them captive into Babylon with all the ornaments of the temple. In this captivity the distinguished prophet Daniel went unharmed among hungry lions, and the three boys went dewy into the midst of the fire as captives. Ezekiel also prophesied in this captivity, and Ezra the prophet was born. From David to the desolation of the temple and the removal into Babylon there are fourteen generations: David, Solomon, Rehoboam, Abijah, Asa, Jehoshaphat, Joram, Uzziah, Jotham, Ahaz, Hezekiah, Manasseh, Amon, Josiah. In these fourteen generations 390 years are found. **[FLAG — Gregory’s received chronology and genealogy.]** They were freed from this captivity through Zerubbabel, who afterward restored both temple and city.

But I think this captivity bears the type of that captivity into which the sinful soul is led away and from which, unless Zerubbabel—that is, Christ—frees it, it will suffer a dreadful exile. The Lord Himself says in the Gospel: “If the Son frees you, you will be free indeed.” May He establish in us a temple in which He deigns to dwell, in which faith shines like gold, the eloquence of preaching gleams like silver, and all the ornaments of that visible temple become bright in the honor of our senses. May He also grant a saving effect to our good will, because “unless He builds the house, those who build it labor in vain.” This captivity is said to have lasted seventy years.

### 16. The Birth of Christ

<a id="hist-01-en-018"></a>
[**[018]**](#hist-01-la-018) After they returned through Zerubbabel, as I have said, they were now murmuring against God, now rushing after idols or imitating the abominations practiced by the nations; despising God’s prophets, they were delivered to the nations, subjugated, and slaughtered. At last the Lord Himself, promised by the voices of patriarchs and prophets, entered the womb of the Virgin Mary through the Holy Spirit and deigned to be born for the redemption both of that nation and of all nations. From the removal into captivity to the birth of Christ there are fourteen generations: Jechoniah, Shealtiel, Zerubbabel, Abiud, Eliakim, Azor, Zadok, Achim, Eliud, Eleazar, Matthan, Jacob, and Joseph, the husband of Mary, of whom our Lord Jesus Christ was born; Joseph is counted as the fourteenth.

### 17. The Various Kingdoms of the Nations

<a id="hist-01-en-019"></a>
[**[019]**](#hist-01-la-019) Lest I appear to know only the history of the Hebrew people, I record the other kingdoms and the times at which they existed in relation to the Israelites. In Abraham’s time Ninus reigned over the Assyrians, Europs over the Sicyonians, and the sixteenth ruling power—which in their language they called a dynasty—was over the Egyptians. In Moses’ time the seventh Tropas reigned among the Argives; Cecrops was the first in Attica; the twelfth Cenchris was among the Egyptians and was drowned in the Red Sea; the sixteenth Agatadis was among the Assyrians; and Maratis was among the Sicyonians. In Solomon’s time, when he reigned over Israel, the fifth Silvius reigned among the Latins, Fistus among the Lacedaemonians, the second Oxion among the Corinthians; it was the 126th year of the Egyptian Thebes; Eutropes ruled the Assyrians and the second Agasastus the Athenians. When Amon reigned over Judea and the people went into captivity in Babylon, Argeus ruled the Macedonians, Cyces the Laedi, Vaphres the Egyptians, Nebuchadnezzar—who carried them away captive—the Babylonians, and the sixth Servius the Romans. **[FLAG — Gregory reproduces a received synchronistic scheme; its names, ordinals, and chronology are not silently modernized.]**

### 18. When Lyon Was Founded

<a id="hist-01-en-020"></a>
[**[020]**](#hist-01-la-020) After these rulers, Julius Caesar was the first emperor and obtained sole rule of the empire. The second was Octavian, Julius Caesar’s descendant, whom they call Augustus and after whom the month August is named. I find it most clearly recorded that Lyon in Gaul was founded in the nineteenth year of his rule. Later, illustrious with the blood of martyrs, it was called most noble.

### 19. The Gifts of the Magi and the Killing of the Infants

<a id="hist-01-en-021"></a>
[**[021]**](#hist-01-la-021) In the forty-third year of Augustus’s rule our Lord Jesus Christ, as I have said, was born according to the flesh of the Virgin Mary at Bethlehem, the town of David. The Magi from the east saw His immense star and came with gifts; offering their humble presents, they adored the child. Herod, out of zeal for his kingdom, killed the little infants while striving to persecute Christ the God. He himself was afterward struck by divine judgment. **[FLAG — Gregory’s regnal chronology.]**

### 20. The Miracles and Passion of Christ

<a id="hist-01-en-022"></a>
[**[022]**](#hist-01-la-022) While our Lord God Jesus Christ preached repentance, granted the grace of baptism, promised the heavenly kingdom to all nations, and worked prodigies and signs among the peoples—bringing wine from water, extinguishing fevers, giving light to the blind, restoring life to the buried, freeing those possessed by unclean spirits, and restoring the wretched lepers’ disfigured skin—by these and many other signs He most plainly declared to the peoples that He was God. Among the Jews anger was kindled, envy stirred, and a mind fed on the blood of the prophets plotted unjustly to kill the just one. So that the oracles of the ancient prophets might be fulfilled, He was betrayed by a disciple, condemned by the chief priests, mocked by the Jews, crucified with the wicked, and, after giving up His spirit, guarded by soldiers. When these things had been done, darkness came over the whole world, and many, turning and groaning, confessed Jesus to be the Son of God.

### 21. Joseph, Who Buried Him

<a id="hist-01-en-023"></a>
[**[023]**](#hist-01-la-023) Joseph, who laid Him in his own tomb after treating Him with spices, was also arrested, shut in a little cell, and guarded by the chief priests themselves. As the Acts of Pilate sent to the emperor Tiberius relate, they showed greater cruelty toward him than toward the Lord Himself: the Lord was guarded by soldiers, Joseph by the priests. But when the Lord rose, the guards were terrified by an angelic vision and He was not found in the tomb. At night the walls of the cell in which Joseph was held were lifted high; an angel released him from custody, and the walls were restored to their place. When the chief priests reproached the guards and urgently demanded the holy body from them, the soldiers said: “You return Joseph, and we will return Christ; but, as we recognize the truth, neither can you now return God’s benefactor nor we God’s Son.” The priests were confounded, and the soldiers were released under this excuse.

### 22. James the Apostle

<a id="hist-01-en-024"></a>
[**[024]**](#hist-01-la-024) It is related that when the apostle James saw that the Lord had died upon the cross, he abjured and swore that he would never eat bread unless he saw the Lord risen. On the third day the Lord returned in triumph after despoiling hell and, appearing to James, said: “Arise, James, and eat, for I have now risen from the dead.” This is James the Just, whom they call the brother of the Lord because he was a son born to Joseph by another wife.

### 23. The Day of the Lord’s Resurrection

<a id="hist-01-en-025"></a>
[**[025]**](#hist-01-la-025) I believe that the Lord’s resurrection occurred on the first day, not the seventh, as many think. This is the day of the resurrection of our Lord Jesus Christ, which I properly call the Lord’s Day because of His holy resurrection. It first saw light at the beginning, and it first merited to behold the Lord rising from the tomb. From the captivity of Jerusalem and the desolation of the temple to the passion of our Lord Jesus Christ—that is, to the seventeenth year of Tiberius—668 years are computed. **[FLAG — Gregory’s received chronology.]**

### 24. The Ascension of the Lord, and the Deaths of Pilate and Herod

<a id="hist-01-en-026"></a>
[**[026]**](#hist-01-la-026) After rising, the Lord disputed with His disciples about the kingdom of God for forty days; then, as they watched, He was taken up in a cloud, carried into heaven, and sat gloriously at the Father’s right hand. Pilate sent an account of these events to Tiberius Caesar and informed him of Christ’s powers as well as His passion and resurrection. These Acts are still preserved among us in writing today. Tiberius read the account to the Senate, which angrily rejected it because the report had not first come to them. From this the first seeds of hatred against Christians sprouted. Pilate did not remain unpunished: because of the crime of his malice—that is, the killing which he carried out against our Lord Jesus Christ—he killed himself with his own hands. Many think he was a Manichee because of what is read in the Gospel: “Some Galileans came, reporting to Him those whose blood Pilate had mixed with their sacrifices.” King Herod likewise, while raging against the Lord’s apostles, was divinely struck for such great crimes; swelling and swarming with worms, he took a knife to purge the evil and released himself by a blow of his own hand.

### 25. The Passion of the Apostles, and Nero

<a id="hist-01-en-027"></a>
[**[027]**](#hist-01-la-027) The blessed apostle Peter came to Rome under the emperor Claudius, fourth from Augustus, and by preaching there with many miracles proved most plainly that Christ is the Son of God. From those days Christians began to be found in the city of Rome. As the name of Christ spread more and more among the peoples, the ancient serpent’s envy arose against it, and savage malice entered the emperor’s whole heart. For the dissolute, vain, and proud Nero—submissive to men and in turn desiring men, the foulest violator of his mother, sisters, and nearest women relatives—was the first, to complete the mass of his wickedness, to stir persecution against those believing in the worship of Christ. He kept with him Simon Magus, a man of utter malice and master of every device of the magical art. When Simon had been cast down by the Lord’s apostles Peter and Paul, Nero was moved against them because they preached Christ, the Son of God, and refused to adore idols. He ordered Peter to be killed by the cross and Paul by the sword. Nero himself, attempting to escape the sedition stirred against him, killed himself with his own hand at the fourth milestone from the city.

### 26. James, Mark, and John the Evangelist

<a id="hist-01-en-028"></a>
[**[028]**](#hist-01-la-028) Then James, the brother of the Lord, and Mark the evangelist were crowned with glorious martyrdom for Christ’s name. Stephen the deacon and martyr had been the first of all to enter this path. A great calamity for the Jews followed the killing of the apostle James. At Vespasian’s coming the temple was burned, and 600,000 Jews perished by sword and famine in that war. **[FLAG — Gregory’s transmitted figure.]** Domitian, the second after Nero to rage against Christians, sent the apostle John into exile on the island of Patmos and inflicted various cruelties upon the people. After Domitian’s death the blessed apostle and evangelist John returned from exile. Old, full of days, and living a life perfected in God, he laid himself in the tomb. It is said that he will not taste death until the Lord comes again to judge, as He Himself says in the Gospel: “Thus I wish him to remain until I come.”

### 27. The Persecution under Trajan

<a id="hist-01-en-029"></a>
[**[029]**](#hist-01-la-029) Trajan, third after Nero, stirred a persecution against Christians. Under him blessed Clement—who was the third bishop of the Roman church—suffered; Saint Simeon, bishop of Jerusalem and son of Cleophas, is said to have been crucified for Christ’s name; and Ignatius, bishop of Antioch, was led to Rome and assigned to the beasts. These things occurred in Trajan’s time.

### 28. Hadrian, the Heresies, Justin, and Polycarp

<a id="hist-01-en-030"></a>
[**[030]**](#hist-01-la-030) After him Aelius Hadrian, Domitian’s successor, was made emperor. Jerusalem is called Aelia after Aelius Hadrian because he rebuilt it. After these passions of the saints, it was not enough for the adversary to have stirred unbelieving nations against the worshippers of Christ without also stirring schisms among Christians themselves. He provoked heresies, and divided catholic faith was expounded in conflicting ways. Under Antoninus’s rule the insane Marcionite and Valentinian heresies arose. The philosopher Justin, after writing books for the catholic church, was crowned with martyrdom for Christ’s name. In Asia, when persecution arose, the most blessed Polycarp, disciple of the apostle and evangelist John, was consecrated to the Lord by fire like a most pure burnt offering in the eightieth year of his life. Many in Gaul too were crowned with heavenly gems through martyrdom for Christ’s name; faithful histories of their passions are preserved among us to this day.

### 29. Saint Photinus, Irenaeus, and the Other Martyrs of Lyon

<a id="hist-01-en-031"></a>
[**[031]**](#hist-01-la-031) Among these was Photinus, the first bishop of the church of Lyon, who, full of days and afflicted with various torments, suffered for Christ’s name. The most blessed Irenaeus, successor to this martyr, had been sent to this city by blessed Polycarp and shone with admirable virtue; in a short time he especially restored the city wholly to Christianity by his preaching. But when persecution came, the devil waged such war there through the tyrant, and so great a multitude of Christians was slaughtered for confessing the Lord’s name, that rivers of Christian blood ran through the streets. I could not gather either their number or their names, for the Lord inscribed them in the book of life. His executioner afflicted blessed Irenaeus with various torments in his own presence and consecrated him to Christ the Lord through martyrdom. After him forty-eight martyrs suffered, of whom I read that the first was Vettius Epagatus.

### 30. The Seven Men Sent to Preach in Gaul

<a id="hist-01-en-032"></a>
[**[032]**](#hist-01-la-032) Under the emperor Decius many wars arose against the Christian name, and there was such slaughter of believers that they could not be numbered. Babylas, bishop of Antioch, with three little boys—Urban, Prilidan, and Epolon—and Sixtus, bishop of the Roman church, the archdeacon Lawrence, and Hippolytus were consummated through martyrdom for confessing the Lord’s name. Valentinian and Novatian, the chief heretics of that time, raged against our faith at the enemy’s prompting.

At this time seven men were ordained bishops and sent to preach in the Gauls, as the history of the passion of the holy martyr Saturninus relates. It says: “In the consulship of Decius and Gratus, as is retained in faithful memory, the city of Toulouse first had the holy Saturninus as its foremost and highest priest.” These were sent: Catianus as bishop to the people of Tours, Trophimus to those of Arles, Paul to Narbonne, Saturninus to Toulouse, Dionysius to Paris, Stremonius to Clermont, and Martial was appointed bishop to Limoges. Of these, blessed Dionysius, bishop of Paris, afflicted with various torments for Christ’s name, ended his present life with the sword threatening. Saturninus, already assured of martyrdom, said to his two priests: “Behold, I am now offered, and the time of my release is at hand. I ask that you not abandon me altogether until I complete the debt of my end.” When he was seized and led to the Capitol, abandoned by them, he was dragged alone. Seeing himself deserted by them, he is said to have prayed: “Lord Jesus Christ, hear me from Your holy heaven, that this church may never deserve to have a bishop from among these citizens forever.” I know that it has happened in that city to this very day. He was bound to the feet of a raging bull, hurled from the Capitol, and ended his life. Catianus, Trophimus, Stremonius, Paul, and Martial, living in the highest holiness, acquired peoples for the church and extended Christ’s faith among all; they departed in blessed confession. Thus those who left the earth through martyrdom and these through confession were joined together equally in heaven.

### 31. The Church at Bourges

<a id="hist-01-en-033"></a>
[**[033]**](#hist-01-la-033) One of the disciples of these men came to the city of Bourges and announced Christ the Lord, the salvation of all, to the people. A few of them believed; clerics were ordained, received the rite of psalmody, and were instructed how to build a church and celebrate solemn rites to almighty God. Since they still had little means for building, they asked a certain citizen for a house from which they might make a church. The senators and other leading men of the place were then bound to pagan cults; those who had believed were among the poor, according to the Lord’s reproach to the Jews: “Prostitutes and tax collectors go before you into the kingdom of God.”

Unable to obtain the house from the man they asked, they found a certain Leocadius, a leading senator of Gaul, who was of the stock of Vettius Epagatus, whom I mentioned above as having suffered for Christ’s name at Lyon. When they made their petition and faith known to him together, he answered: “If my house, which I have in the city of Bourges, were worthy of this work, I would not refuse to provide it.” Hearing this, they prostrated themselves at his feet, offered three hundred gold pieces with a silver dish, and said that it was fitting for this ministry. He accepted three of the gold pieces as a blessing and graciously waived the rest. Though still entangled in the error of idolatry, he became a Christian and made his house a church. This is now the first church in the city of Bourges, constructed with wondrous workmanship and illustrious with the relics of the first martyr Stephen.

### 32. Chrocus and the Temple of the Auvergne

<a id="hist-01-en-034"></a>
[**[034]**](#hist-01-la-034) In the twenty-seventh place Valerian and Gallienus obtained the Roman empire and stirred a grievous persecution against Christians in their time. Then Cornelius at Rome and Cyprian at Carthage made the churches illustrious with their blessed blood. In their time Chrocus, king of the Alamans, set his army in motion and ranged through the Gauls. This Chrocus is said to have been exceedingly arrogant. Having committed many iniquities, he gathered the nation of the Alamans, as I have said, by the counsel of his wicked mother, ranged through all the Gauls, and overthrew from their foundations all buildings constructed in antiquity. Coming into the Auvergne, he burned, demolished, and overthrew the temple which in the Gallic tongue they call Vasso Galate. It had been made and fortified with wondrous workmanship. Its wall was double: inside it was constructed of small stone, outside of carved squared stones. The wall was thirty feet thick. Within it was adorned with marble and mosaic; the pavement of the building was laid with marble, and above it was covered with lead.

### 33. The Martyrs Who Suffered near the Auvergne

<a id="hist-01-en-035"></a>
[**[035]**](#hist-01-la-035) Near this city the martyrs Liminius and Antolianus rest. There Cassius and Victorinus, joined in the love of Christ with brotherly affection, obtained the kingdoms of heaven together through the shedding of their own blood. Antiquity reports that Victorinus was a slave of the priest of the temple just named. While he often went to the village called the Christians’ village to persecute Christians, he found Cassius, a Christian. Moved by his preaching and miracles, he believed in Christ; leaving the filth of pagan rites and consecrated in baptism, he became great in the working of miracles. Not long afterward, joined on earth through martyrdom, as I have said, they came together equally to the heavenly kingdoms.

### 34. Saint Privatus the Martyr

<a id="hist-01-en-036"></a>
[**[036]**](#hist-01-la-036) When the Alamans burst into the Gauls, Saint Privatus, bishop of the city of Javols, was found in a cave of Mount Memmate, where he devoted himself to fastings and prayers, while the people of the fortress of Grèdo had been shut in by his warning. But while the good shepherd refused to hand his sheep over to wolves, he was compelled to sacrifice to demons. Abhorring and rejecting that filth alike, he was beaten with clubs until he was thought lifeless. After a few days he breathed out his spirit from that very battering. Chrocus himself was captured at Arles, a city of Gaul, afflicted with various torments, and died by the sword, not undeservedly paying the penalties he had inflicted on the saints of God.

### 35. Quirinus, Bishop and Martyr

<a id="hist-01-en-037"></a>
[**[037]**](#hist-01-la-037) Under Diocletian, who ruled the Roman empire in the thirty-third place, a grievous persecution against Christians raged for four years, so that on one occasion, on the most sacred day of Easter itself, a great multitude of Christian people was killed for worshipping the true God. At that time Quirinus, priest of the church of Siscia, bore a glorious martyrdom for Christ’s name. The pagans’ savagery tied a millstone to his neck and drove him into the whirlpool of a river. When he fell into the depths, he was borne upon the waters for a long time by divine power; the waters did not swallow him whom no weight of sin pressed down. The surrounding multitude, marveling at what had happened, set aside their gentile fury and hastened to free the priest. Seeing this, he would not allow himself to be withdrawn from martyrdom. Raising his eyes to heaven, he said: “Lord Jesus, who sit gloriously at the Father’s right hand, do not allow me to be removed from this course, but receive my soul and deign to join me to Your martyrs in eternal rest.” With these words he gave up his spirit. His body was taken up by the Christians and committed to burial with veneration.

### 36. The Birth of Saint Martin and the Finding of the Cross

<a id="hist-01-en-038"></a>
[**[038]**](#hist-01-la-038) Constantine obtained the Roman empire in the thirty-fourth place and reigned prosperously for thirty years. In the eleventh year of his rule, when peace had been restored to the churches after Diocletian’s departure, the most blessed bishop Martin was born at Sabaria, a city of Pannonia, to parents who were pagan but not of the lowest rank. In the twentieth year of his own rule this Constantine killed his son Crispus by poison and his wife Fausta in a heated bath, because they were said to have wished to betray his kingdom. In his time the venerable wood of the Lord’s cross was found through the diligence of his mother Helena, the Hebrew Judas pointing it out; after baptism Judas was called Quiriacus. The historian Eusebius wrote his chronicle down to this time. From the twenty-first year of Constantine’s rule the priest Jerome continued it, recording that the priest Juvencus composed the Gospels in verse at the aforesaid emperor’s request.

### 37. James, Bishop of Nisibis

<a id="hist-01-en-039"></a>
[**[039]**](#hist-01-la-039) Under the rule of Constans lived James of Nisibis. The ears of divine clemency, inclined toward his prayers, repelled many dangers from his city. Maximinus, bishop of Trier, is also found to have been powerful in all holiness.

### 38. The Death of Anthony the Monk

<a id="hist-01-en-040"></a>
[**[040]**](#hist-01-la-040) In the nineteenth year of Constantine the Younger, Anthony the monk died in the 105th year of his life. The most blessed Hilary, bishop of Poitiers, was sentenced to exile at the persuasion of heretics. Writing books there for the catholic faith, he sent them to Constantius, who in the fourth year of his exile ordered him released and permitted him to return home.

### 39. The Coming of Saint Martin

<a id="hist-01-en-041"></a>
[**[041]**](#hist-01-la-041) Then my light arose, and Gaul was illuminated by new rays of lamps: at that time the most blessed Martin began to preach in the Gauls. Declaring Christ, the Son of God, to be true God among the peoples through many miracles, he turned away the incredulity of the gentiles. He destroyed temples, suppressed heresy, and built churches; while he shone with many other virtues, he restored three dead persons to life to complete the record of his praise. In the fourth year of Valentinian and Valens, Saint Hilary died at Poitiers and went to heaven, full of holiness and faith and endowed with many virtues; it is also read that he raised the dead.

### 40. The Matron Melania

<a id="hist-01-en-042"></a>
[**[042]**](#hist-01-la-042) The noble matron Melania, an inhabitant of the city of Rome, went to Jerusalem out of devotion, leaving her son Urban in Rome. She showed herself to everyone in such complete goodness and holiness that the inhabitants called her Thecla.

### 41. The Death of the Emperor Valens

<a id="hist-01-en-043"></a>
[**[043]**](#hist-01-la-043) After Valentinian’s death, Valens succeeded to the whole empire. He ordered monks to be compelled into military service and commanded those who refused to be beaten with clubs. Afterward the Romans waged a most savage war in Thrace, in which the slaughter was so great that the Romans, deprived of the protection of their horses, fled on foot. While they were being cut down by the Goths in a very great massacre and Valens, wounded by an arrow, was fleeing, he entered a small hut. The enemy approached and set the little house on fire above him, and he was deprived even of the burial he desired. Thus divine vengeance for the shed blood of the saints, long delayed, at last came forth. Thus far Jerome; from this time forward the priest Orosius wrote further.

### 42. The Rule of Theodosius

<a id="hist-01-en-044"></a>
[**[044]**](#hist-01-la-044) When the emperor Gratian saw that the republic was destitute, he made Theodosius his colleague in the empire. This Theodosius placed all his hope and trust in God’s mercy. He subdued many nations not so much by the sword as by vigils and prayers, strengthened the republic, and entered the city of Constantinople as a victor.

### 43. The Death of Maximus the Tyrant

<a id="hist-01-en-045"></a>
[**[045]**](#hist-01-la-045) Maximus, having gained a victory through tyranny by subduing the Britons, was made emperor by the soldiers. Establishing his seat in the city of Trier, he killed the emperor Gratian after ensnaring him by deceit. To this Maximus the blessed Martin came when he was already bishop. Theodosius, who had placed all his hope in God, took over the whole empire in Gratian’s place. Thereafter, relying on divine utterances, he deprived Maximus of the empire and killed him.

### 44. Urbicus, Bishop of the Auvergne

<a id="hist-01-en-046"></a>
[**[046]**](#hist-01-la-046) Among the people of the Auvergne, the first bishop after the bishop and preacher Stremonius was Urbicus, a convert from the senators. He had a wife who, according to ecclesiastical custom, lived religiously apart from the priest’s company. Both devoted themselves to prayer, almsgiving, and good works. While they were doing these things, the enemy’s malice, always jealous of holiness, was stirred in the woman; inflaming her with desire for her husband, it made her a new Eve. Burning with lust and covered by the darkness of sin, the woman went through the darkness of night to the church house. Finding everything barred, she began to beat upon the doors of the ecclesiastical house and cried out: “How long, priest, will you sleep? How long will you not unbar the closed door? Why do you reject your attendant? Why do you not hear Paul’s precepts with hardened ears? For he wrote: ‘Return to one another, lest Satan tempt you.’ Behold, I return to you; I do not run to a stranger, but return to my own vessel.”

As she long proclaimed these and similar words, the priest’s religious resolve at last grew lukewarm. He ordered her admitted to his chamber, used intercourse with her, and then ordered her to leave. Coming to himself too late and grieving over the crime committed, he sought a monastery in his diocese to perform penance. There, washing away with groans and tears what he had committed, he returned to his own city. When the course of his life was complete, he departed from the world. A daughter was born from this union and remained in religion. The priest himself was buried with his wife and daughter in the crypt of Chantoin beside the public embankment. Legonus was appointed bishop in his place.

### 45. Saint Illidius the Bishop

<a id="hist-01-en-047"></a>
[**[047]**](#hist-01-la-047) When Legonus died, Saint Illidius succeeded, a man of exceptional holiness and illustrious virtue. He was so eminent in holiness that his fame reached even foreign territories. Thus it happened that he was sought out and healed the daughter of the emperor of Trier who was afflicted by an unclean spirit, as I recorded in the book I wrote about his life. As fame reports, he was very old, full of days and full of good works. Completing the course of this life with a blessed end, he went to Christ and was buried in a crypt outside that city. He had an archdeacon named Justus, who was just in merit as well as name. He too completed the course of his life in good works and was placed beside his master’s tomb. After the passing of the blessed confessor Illidius, such great virtues appeared at his glorious tomb that they can neither be written in full nor retained in memory. Saint Nepotianus succeeded him.

### 46. Nepotianus and Artemius, Bishops

<a id="hist-01-en-048"></a>
[**[048]**](#hist-01-la-048) Saint Nepotianus was regarded as the fourth bishop among the people of the Auvergne. Envoys were sent from Trier into Spain, among them a certain Artemius, admirable in wisdom and beauty and flourishing in early life, who was seized by the force of fevers. While the others went ahead, he was left sick in the Auvergne; at that time he was bound by a betrothal at Trier. Visited by Saint Nepotianus and anointed with holy oil, he was restored to health by the Lord’s gift. When he had received the word of preaching from the saint, he forgot both his earthly betrothed and his own possessions and was joined to the holy church. Made a cleric, he excelled in such holiness that at blessed Nepotianus’s death he became his successor in ruling the folds of the Lord’s flock.

### 47. The Chastity of the Two Lovers

<a id="hist-01-en-049"></a>
[**[049]**](#hist-01-la-049) At the same time a certain Injuriosus, one of the senators of the Auvergne and a man of great wealth, sought in marriage a girl like himself; the betrothal gift was given, and the wedding day appointed. Each was their father’s only child. When the day came and the wedding solemnity had been celebrated, they were placed in one bed according to custom. But the girl, deeply distressed, turned toward the wall and wept most bitterly. He said: “Why are you troubled? Tell me, I ask.” She remained silent, and he added: “I implore you by Jesus Christ, the Son of God, that you wisely explain to me what grieves you.” Then she turned to him and said:

“If I lament all the days of my life, will there ever be tears enough to wash away so immense a sorrow in my breast? I had resolved to keep my little body immaculate for Christ, untouched by a man. But woe to me, who have been so abandoned by Him that I could not accomplish what I desired, and on this last day, which I ought never to have seen, I have lost what I had preserved from the beginning of my life. Behold, abandoned by the immortal Christ, who promised me paradise as a dowry, I have obtained the companionship of a mortal man. Instead of unfading roses, the spoil of withering roses does not adorn but disfigures me. When I ought to have put on the robe of purity above the fourfold river of the Lamb, this garment presents a burden, not an honor. But why do I prolong my words further? Unhappy I, who ought to have merited heaven by lot, am plunged today into the abyss. Oh, if these things were to happen to me, why was not the day that was the beginning of my life also its end? Oh, if only I had entered death’s gate before receiving the nourishment of milk! Oh, if only the kisses of my sweet nurses had been expended at my funeral! Earthly forms horrify me, because in place of worldly life I look upon the Redeemer’s pierced hands. I do not see diadems shining resplendent with gems, when I contemplate in my mind that crown of thorns. I reject the spaces of your earth spread far and wide, because I desire the loveliness of paradise. Your halls horrify me when I look upon the Lord seated above the stars.”

The young man, moved with compassion as she uttered these things with great weeping, said: “My most noble parents had us as their only children in the Auvergne and wished to join us to propagate their line, lest an outside heir succeed them when they departed the world.” She replied: “The world is nothing, riches are nothing, the pomp of this age is nothing, and the very life we enjoy is nothing. Rather, that life must be sought which is not closed by the termination of death, is not dissolved by any stain, and is not ended by any setting; where man remains in eternal beatitude, lives with a light that does not set and, what is greater than all these, enjoys the Lord’s own presence in perpetual contemplation and, translated into an angelic state, rejoices in indissoluble gladness.”

He said: “Your sweetest words have shone eternal life upon me like a great radiance. Therefore, if you wish to abstain from carnal desire, I will become a sharer in your purpose.” She answered: “It is difficult for the male sex to grant this to women. Nevertheless, if you act so that we remain immaculate in the world, I will give you a share in the dowry promised to me by my spouse and Lord Jesus Christ, to whom I have devoted myself as both handmaid and bride.” Then, armed with the standard of the cross, he said: “I will do what you urge.” They joined right hands and rested. For many years afterward they lay in one bed, but with praiseworthy chastity, as was later revealed at their deaths.

When the girl’s contest was complete and she was departing to Christ, the man performed the funeral office. As he placed the girl in the tomb, he said: “I thank You, Lord Jesus Christ, eternal Lord and my God, because I return this treasure immaculate to Your mercy, just as I received it committed by You.” At this she smiled and said: “Why do you speak without being asked?” He himself followed her not long after she was buried. Although their tombs had been placed on different walls, a new miracle appeared to manifest their chastity. When morning came and the people approached the place, they found together the tombs which they had left far apart: those whom heaven holds as companions, the monument does not separate here in their buried bodies. To this day the inhabitants of the place have wished to call them the Two Lovers. I have mentioned them in the Book of Miracles.

### 48. The Death of Saint Martin

<a id="hist-01-en-050"></a>
[**[050]**](#hist-01-la-050) In the second year of the rule of Arcadius and Honorius, Saint Martin, bishop of Tours, full of virtues and holiness, bestowing many benefits upon the sick, departed from the world at the village of Candes in his own diocese, in the eighty-first year of his life and the twenty-sixth of his episcopate, and passed blessedly to Christ. He died in the middle of the night, which was a Sunday, in the consulship of Atticus and Caesarius. Many heard singers in heaven at his passing, as I explained more fully in the first book of his Virtues.

When the holy man of God first began to fall sick at the village of Candes, as I have said, the people of Poitiers assembled for his death just as the people of Tours did. When he died, a great dispute arose between the two peoples. The people of Poitiers said: “He is our monk; he was our abbot; we shall reclaim him as one committed to us. Let it suffice you that, while he was a bishop in the world, you enjoyed his conversation, shared his table, were strengthened by his blessings, and moreover delighted in his miracles. Let all these things suffice you, and permit us to carry away at least his lifeless corpse.”

The people of Tours answered: “If you say that the miracles done for you suffice, know that while he was with you he worked more than here. To pass over most of them, he raised two dead persons for you and one for us; and, as he himself often said, his power was greater before his episcopate than after it. Therefore what he did not complete while living with us, let him fulfill when dead. He was taken from you, but given to us by God. If the custom instituted of old is preserved, a man shall have his tomb, by God’s command, in the city where he was ordained. Certainly, if you wish to vindicate him by the privilege of the monastery, know that his first monastery was among the people of Milan.”

While they disputed thus, night closed as the sun sank. The body was placed in the middle, the doors were barred, and it was guarded by both peoples; when morning came, the people of Poitiers intended to carry it away by force. But almighty God would not let the city of Tours be deprived of its own patron. At midnight the whole company of Poitiers was overcome by sleep, and none of that multitude remained to watch. When the people of Tours saw them asleep, some lifted the most sacred body and passed it through the window while others received it from outside. They placed it in a boat and went down the river Vienne with all their people; entering the bed of the Loire, they made their way to the city of Tours with abundant psalmody and great praises. The people of Poitiers, awakened by their voices and no longer possessing the treasure they had guarded, returned home in great confusion.

If anyone asks why only one bishop, Litorius, held office between the passing of Bishop Catianus and Saint Martin, let him know that the city of Tours was long without priestly blessing because pagans prevented it. Those who appeared to be Christians at that time celebrated the divine office secretly and in hiding. If any Christians were discovered by pagans, they were afflicted with blows or cut down by the sword. From the Lord’s passion to the passing of Saint Martin, 412 years are computed. **[FLAG — Gregory’s received chronology.]**

Here ends the first book, containing 5,596 years computed from the beginning to the passing of the blessed bishop Martin. **[FLAG — Gregory’s total for the book.]**

<a id="hist-01-la-001"></a>
[**[001]**](#hist-01-en-001) Decedente atque immo potius pereunte ab urbibus Gallicanis liberalium cultura litterarum, cum nonnullae res gererentur vel rectae vel inprobae, ac feretas gentium desaeviret, regum furor acueretur, eclesiae inpugnarentur ab hereticis, a catholicis tegerentur, ferveret Christi fides in plurimis, tepisceret in nonnullis, ipsae quoque eclesiae vel ditarentur a devotis vel nudarentur a perfides, nec repperire possit quisquam peritus dialectica in arte grammaticus, qui haec aut stilo prosaico aut metrico depingeret versu: ingemescebant saepius plerique, dicentes: 'Vae diebus nostris, quia periit studium litterarum a nobis, nec reperitur rethor in populis, qui gesta praesentia promulgare possit in paginis'. Ista etenim atque et his similia iugiter intuens dici, pro commemoratione praeteritorum, ut notitiam adtingerint venientum, etsi incultu effatu, nequivi tamen obtegere vel certamena flagitiosorum vel vitam recte viventium; et praesertim his inlicitus stimulis, quod a nostris fari plerumque miratus sum, quia: 'Philosophantem rethorem intellegunt pauci, loquentem rusticum mult'. Libuit etiam animo, ut pro suppotatione annorum ab ipso mundi principio libri primi poniretur initium, cuius capitula deursum subieci.

### Liber primus

<a id="hist-01-la-002"></a>
[**[002]**](#hist-01-en-002) Scripturus bella regum cum gentibus adversis, martyrum cum paganis, eclesiarum cum hereticis, prius fidem meam proferre cupio, ut qui ligirit me non dubitet esse catholicum. Illud etiam placuit propter eos, qui adpropinquantem finem mundi disperant, ut, collectam per chronicas vel historias anteriorum annorum summam, explanitur aperte, quanti ab exordio mundi sint anni. Sed prius veniam legentibus praecor, si aut in litteris aut in sillabis grammaticam artem excessero, de qua adplene non sum inbutus; illud tantum studens, ut quod in eclesia credi praedicatur sine aliquo fuco aut cordis hesitatione reteneam, quia scio, peccatis obnoxium per credulitatem puram obtenire posse veniam apud Deum. Credo ergo in Deum patrem omnipotentem. Credo in Iesum Christum, filium eius unicum, dominum nostrum, natum a patre, non factum, non post tempora, sed ante cunctum tempus semper fuisse cum patre. Nec enim pater dici potuerat, nisi haberit filium; neque filius esset, si patrem utique non haberet. Illos vero, qui dicunt: 'Erat quando non erat', execrabiliter rennuo et ab eclesia segregare contestor. Credo, Christum hunc verbum esse patris, per quem facta sunt omnia. Hunc verbum carne factum credo, cuius passionem mundus redemptus est, et humanitatem, non deitatem subiacuisse passione, credo. Credo, eum tertia die resurrexisse, hominem perditum liberasse, ascendisse caelos, sedere a dexteram Patris, venturum ac iudicaturum vivos et mortuos. Credo, sanctum Spiritum a Patre et Filio processisse, non minorem et quasi ante non esset, sed aequalem et semper cum Patre et Filio coaeternum deum, cumsubstantialem natura, aequalem omnipotentia, consempiternum esse essentia et nunquam sine Patre fuisse vel Filio, neque minorem Patri vel Filio. Credo, banc Trinitatem sanctam in distinctione subsistere personarum, et aliam quidem personam Patris, aliam Fili, aliam Spiritus sancti. In qua Trinitate unam Deitatem, unam potentiam, unam essentiam esse, confiteor. Credo beatam Mariam, ut virginem ante partum, ita virginem et post partum. Credo animam inmortalem, nec tamen partem habere Deitatis, Et omnia quae a 318 episcopis Nicaene instituta sunt credo fideliter. De fine vero mundi ea sentio quae a prioribus didici, Antechristum prius esse venturo. Antechristus vero primum circumcisionem inducit, se asserens Christum, deinde in templo Hierusolimis statuam suam collocat adorandam, sicut Dominum dixisse legimus: Videbitis abhuminationem desolationes stantem in loco sancto. Sed diem illam omnibus hominibus oculi ipse Dominus manifestat, dicens: De die autem illa et ora nemo scit, neque angeli caelorum neque filius, nisi Pater solos. Sed et hic respondibimus hereticis, qui nos inpugnant, asserentis, minorem esse Filium Patri, qui hanc diem ignoret. Cognoscant ergo, hunc filium christianum populum nuncopatum, de quo a Deo praedicetur: Ego ero illis in patre, et ipsi erunt mihi in filios. Si enim haec de unigenito Filio praedixisset, nunquam ei angelos praeposuisset. Sic enim ait: Neque angeli caelorum neque filios; ostendens non de unigenito, sed de adoptivo populo haec dixisse. Noster vero finis ipse Christus est, qui nobis vitam aeternam, si ad eum conversi fuerimus, larga benignitate praestabit. De subpotatione vero huius mundi evidenter chronicae Eusebii Caesariensis episcopi ac Hieronimi presbiteri prolocuntur et rationem de omni annorum serie pandunt. Nam et Horosius diligentissime haec inquaerens, omnem numerum aniiorum ab initio mundi usque ad suum tempus in unum colligit. Hoc etiam et Victurius cum ordine paschalis solemnitates inquirere fecit. Ergo et nos scriptorum supra memoratorum exsemplaria sequentes, cupimus a primi homines conditione, si Dominus dignabitur suum commodare auxilium, usque ad nostrum tempos cunctam annorum congeriem conpotare. Quod facilius adinplemus, si ab ipso Adam sumamus exordium.

## 1. De Adam et Ewa.

<a id="hist-01-la-003"></a>
[**[003]**](#hist-01-en-003) Principio Dominus caelum terramque in christo suo, qui est omnium principium, id est in Filio suo, furmavit, qui post creata mundi totius elementa, glebam adsumens fragilis limi, hominem ad suam imaginem similitudinemque plasmavit et insufflavit in faciem eius spiraculum vitae, et factus est in animam viventem. Cuius dormienti ablata costa, mulier Ewa creata est. Nec dubium enim est, quod hic primus homo Adam, antequam peccaret, tipum Redemptoris domini praetulisset. Ipsi enim in passionis sopore obdormiens, de latere suo dum aquam cruoremque producit, virginem inmaculatamque eclesiam sibi exhibuit, redemptam sanguine, latice emundatam, non habentem maculam aut rugam, id est limphis ablutam propter maculam, extensam in crucem propter rugam. Hi ergo primi hominis inter amoena paradisibeativiventis, anguis astu inlecti, divina praecepta transiliunt, eiectique ab angelica sede, mundi laboribus depotantur.

## 2. De Cain et Abel.

<a id="hist-01-la-004"></a>
[**[004]**](#hist-01-en-004) Cognitum autem satellitem, mulier concipit peperitque duos filios. Sed dum Deus unius sacrificium dignanter suscipit, alius invidia inflammante tumiscit, et in fraterni sanguinis effusionem novus parecida consurgens, fratrem opprimit, vincit, interimit.

## 3. De Enoch iusto.

<a id="hist-01-la-005"></a>
[**[005]**](#hist-01-en-005) Exhinc cunctum genus in facinus exsecrabile ruit praeter Enoch iustum, qui ambolans in viis Dei, ab ipso Domino propter iustitiam adsumptus, de medio peccantes populi liberatur. Sic enim legimus: Ambolavit Enoch cum Deo, et non conparuit, quia Deus tulit eum.

## 4. De diluvio.

<a id="hist-01-la-006"></a>
[**[006]**](#hist-01-en-006) Dominus ergo commotus contra iniquitates populi, non in suis semitis gradientes, diluvium mittit cunctamque animam viventem de superficiem terrae diluvium inundante delivit; tantum Noefidelissimum ac peculiarem sibi suiquetipus speciem praeferentem cum sua vel trium natorum coniugibus posteritates reparandae gratia in arca reservavit. Increpant nobis hic heretici, cur Scriptura sancta Dominum dixissit iratum. Cognoscant ergo, quia Deus noster non ut homo irascitur: commovetur enim ut terreat, pellet ut revocet, irascitur ut emendit. Sed nec hoc ambigo, quod species illa arcae tipum matris gessisset aeclesiae. Ipsa enim inter fluctus et scupulos huius saeculi transiens, nos ab inminentibus malis materno gestamini fovens, pio amplexu ac protectione defendit. Ab Adam ergo usque ad Noe generationes 10, id est Adam, Seth, Enos, Cainan, Malalehel, Iareth, Enoch, Mattusalam, Lamech, Noe. In his ergo decim generationebus inveniuntur anni 2242. Adam vero in terra Enacim sepultum, quae prius Ebron vocabatur, liber Iesu Nave evidenter explanat.

## 5. De Chus adinventorem staticuli.

<a id="hist-01-la-007"></a>
[**[007]**](#hist-01-en-007) Habebat ergo Noe post diluvium tres filius, Sem, Cham et Iafeth. De Iafeth egressae sunt gentes, similiter et de Cham sive de Sem. Et, sicut ait vetus historia, ab his dissiminatum est genus humanum sub universo caelo. Primogenitus vero Cham Chus. Hic fuit totius artis magicae, inbuente diabolo, et primus idolatriae adlnventor. Hic primus staticulum adorandum diabuli instigatione constituit; qui et stellas et ignem de caelo cadere falsa vertute hominibus ostendebat. Hic ad Persas transiit. Huno Persi vocitavere Zoroastren, id est viventem stelam. Ab hoc etiam ignem adorare consuiti, ipsum divinitus ignem consumptum ut deum colunt.

## 6. De Babillonia.

<a id="hist-01-la-008"></a>
[**[008]**](#hist-01-en-008) Cumque multiplicati hominis dispergerentur per universas terras, egressi ab Oriente, Senachar gramineumrepperiunt campum. In quo aedificantes civitatem, turrem qui caelos adtengeret nituntur struere. Quorum vana cogitatione simul et lingua ipsiusque confutans Deus, mundum late patentem in universa terra dispersit, vocatumque est nomen civitatis Babel, hoc est confusio, eo quod ibi confudisset Deus linguas eorum. Haec est Babilonia a Nebroth gygante aedificata, filio Chus. Et, sicut Horosi narrat historia, mira campi planitiae in quadrum disposita est. Murus eius ex coctili latere infusu bitumine in latum habet cubitus quinquaginta, altitudinis cubitus 200, in circuitu stadia 470. Unus stadius habet aripennes quinque. Vicinae quinae portae per unumquemque latus sitae sunt, quae faciunt 100. Harum portarum ustia mirae magnitudinis ex aere fusile sunt formata. Multa et alia de hac civitate isdem historiographus narrat, addens: Et cum tanta fuissit honestas aedificii, attamen victa atque subversa est.

## 7. De Abraham et Nino.

<a id="hist-01-la-009"></a>
[**[009]**](#hist-01-en-009) Primus autem filius Noe Sem; de quo generatione decima natus est Abraham: id est Noe, Sem, Arphaxath, Sale, Eber, Falech, Rheu, Saruch, Thare, qui genuit Abraham. In his ergo decim generationibus, hoc est a Noe usque Abraham, inveniuntur anni 942. Eo tempore regnabat Ninus, qui aedificavit Ninum civitatem, quam Nineven vocant; cuius in tribus mansionibus spatium amplitudines Ionas propheta determinat. Huius quadragisimo tertio regni anno natus est Abraham. Hic est Abraham initium fidei nostrae. Hic accepit repromissionis. Huic se Christus dominus noster nasciturum ac pro nobis passurum in victimae conmutationem monstravit, ipso in euangeliis sic dicente: Abraham exsultavit, ut viderit diem meum; et vidit, et gavisus est. Hoc vero holocaustum in monte Calvariae, quo Dominus crucifixus est, oblatum fuisse, Severus narrat in chronica, sicut et hodiequae in ipsa Hierusolimorum urbe celebre fertur. In hoc monte crux sancta, in qua Redemptur adfixus est, stetit, de qua et beatus illi cruor efluxit. Hic ergo Abraham accepit signum circumcisiones, ostendens, ut quod ille gessit in corpore nos portemus in corde, dicente propheta: Circumcidite vos Deo vestro, et circumcidite praeputium cordis vestri; et nolite sequi deos alienos; et iterum: Omnes incircumcisus corde non intrint in sancta mea. Hunc Abraham Deus post adiectam nomini sillabam patrem multarum gentium nuncupavit.

## 8. De Isaac, Isau, et Iob et Iacob.

<a id="hist-01-la-010"></a>
[**[010]**](#hist-01-en-010) His cum centum esset annorum, genuit Isaac. Porro Isaac sexagisimo aetatis anno nati sunt fili gemini de Rebecca. Primus Esau, qui Edom, id est terrenus; qui propter gulam vindedit primogenita sua. Ipsi est pater Idomeorum, de cuius generatione quarta natus est Iobab, hoc est Isau, Raguel, Zara, Iobab, qui et Iob. Is vixit annos 249, octugisimo nono anno ab infirmitate liberatus est. Post infirmitatem autem 170 annis, restituta in duplum omni facultate, cuni tantis sicut perdederat filiis iocundatus est.

## 9. De Ioseph in Aegipto.

<a id="hist-01-la-011"></a>
[**[011]**](#hist-01-en-011) Secundus Iacob. Iacob dilectus Dei, sicut ait per prophetam: Iacob dilixi, Esau autem odio habui. Hic post angelicam luctam vocatus est Israhel, a quo Israhelitae. Hic genuit duodecim patriarcas, quorum haec sunt nomina: Rubin, Semeon, Levi, ludas, Isachar, Zabulon, Dan, Neptalem, Gad et Aser. Post hos genuit Ioseph de Rachel, cum esset nonagisimo secundo aetatis suae anno. Hunc coeteris filiis plus dilexit. Habuit ex ea et Beniamin ultimum omnium. Ioseph autem sextum decimum aetatis suae annum habens, tipumpraeferens Redemptoris, vidit somnia quae fratribus retulit: quasi manipolus legens, suum fratrum manipoliadorarent; et iterum, quasi sol et luna cum undecim stelis procederent ante eum. Quae res magnum ei cum fratribus odium generavit. Unde et inflammati invidia, triginta eum argenteis Ismahelitis in Aegypto transeuntibus vindedirunt. Inminente autem fame, cum discendissent hi in Aegypto, cogniti sunt a Ioseph, nec tamen ipsi Ioseph cognoverunt. Ipse tamen se his post multas eorum fatigationes et adducto Beneamen declaravit; de Rachel enim matre sua natus et hic fuerat. Post haec discendunt cuncti Israhelitae in Aegyptum et per Ioseph Faraonis gratiam abutuntur. Iacob autem post benedictus filios suos in Aegypto moritur et in sepulchro patris sui a Ioseph sepelitur in terra Chanaan. Mortuo autem Ioseph atqueFaraone, subicitur cuncta generatio servituti. Quae per Moysen post decim plagas Aegypti liberatur, dimerso Pharaone in mari Rubrum.

## 10. De transitu rubri maris.

<a id="hist-01-la-012"></a>
[**[012]**](#hist-01-en-012) Et quoniam de hoc maris transitu plures multa dixerunt, visum est, ut de situ loci illius vel ipsius transitus aliqua huic inseram lectione. Nilus per Aegyptum, sicut optimae nostis, decurrit et ipse eam inpetu suo inrigat, unde et Aegypti Niliculae appellantur. Cuius nunc litora multi locorum perlustratores referta sacris monasthiriis dicunt esse. Super ripam vero eius non Babilonia, de qua supra miminimus, sed Babilonia civitas collocatur, in qua Ioseph horrea miro opere de lapidibus quadris et cimentum aedificavit, ita ut ad fundum capatiora, ad summa vero constricta sint, ut per parvolum foramen ibidem tritecum iacteretur; quae horrea usque hodie cernuntur. De hac civitate rex ad persequendum Hebraeos cum exercitibus curruum ac multa pedestri manu dirictus est. Antedictus vero fluvius ab oriente veniens, ad occidentalem plagam versus Rubrum mare vadit; ab occidente vero stagnum sive brachium de mare Rubrum progreditur et vadit contra orientem, habens in longo milia circiter quinquaginta, in lato autem decim et octo. In huius stagni capite Clysma civitas aedificata est, non propter fertilitatem loci, cum nihil sit plus sterele, sed propter portum, quia navis ab Indis venientes ibidem ob portus opportunitatem quiescunt; ubi conparate merces per totam Aegyptum deportantur. Ad hunc stagnum per desertum Hebraei tendentis, usque ipsum mare venerunt, inventisque dulcibus aquis, castra metati sunt. In hoc ergo artum locum tam ab herimo quam ab ipso mari considerunt, sicut scriptum est: Audiens Pharao, quod conclusissit eos mare atque desertum, nec esset eis via, qua possint pergere, ad persequendum eos dirixit. Cumque inminentibus his Moysi populus adclamasset, secundum iussum Divinitatis proiectam virgam super mare, divisum est, illisque per sicca gradientibus et, ut Scriptura ait, murum aquarum undique vallatis in litus illud quod est contra montem Sina inlaesi prursus, demersis Aegyptiis, Moyse duce transgrediuntur. De quo transitu multa, ut dixi, narrantur; sed nos quod a sapientibus et certe illis hominibus, qui in eodem locum accesserant, virum cognovimus, ea inserere studuemus paginae. Aiunt etiam, sulcos, quos rotae curruum fecerant, usque hodie permanere et, quantum acies oculorum videre potest, in profundo cerni. Quos si modicum commotio maris obtexerit, illo quiescente, rursum divinitus renovantur, ut fuerant. Dicunt alii, quod ad ipsam ripam, factum modico per mare circuitum, unde ingresse fuerant, sint reversi. Alii vero asserent, unum cunctis ingressum, nonnulli, unicuique tribui suam patuisse viam, illud testimonium psaltirii abutentes: Qui divisit mare Rubrum in divisiones. Quas nos divisiones spiritaliter et non secundum littera intellegere oportit. Sunt enim et in hoc saeculo, quod figuraliter mare dicitur, multae divisiones; non enim aequaliter possunt aut per unam viam ad vitam cunctis transire. Alii autem transeunt in horam primam, id est qui renati per baptismum, inmaculati ab omni inquinamentum carnis perdurare usque ad vitae praesentes exitum possunt; alii ad oram tertiam, videlicet qui maiore aetate convertuntur; alii ad sextam, qui luxoriae fervore coerceunt. Et per has quasque horas, sicut euangelista conmemorat, ad operam dominicae viniae secundum fidem propriam conducuntur. Haec sunt divisiones, quibus per hoc mare transitur. Illud vero quod a mare usque venientes, litus stagni tenentes revertuntur, illud est quod Dominus ad Moysen dicit: Reversi castra metentur e regione Phiahiroth, quae est inter Magdalum et mare contra Belsephon. Nec enim dubium est, quod transitus ille maris vel columna nubis tipum gesserit nostri baptismatis, dicente beato Paulo apostolo: Nolo vos ignorare, fratres, quia patris nostri omnes sub nube fuerunt, et omnes in Moysen baptizati sunt in nube et in mare. Colomna vero ignis typum sancti Spiritus praetulit. A nativitate ergo Abrahae usque ad egressum filiorum Israhel ex Aegyptu vel transitum maris Rubri, quod fuit Moysi octuaginsimus annus, subpotantur anni numero 462.

## 11. De populo in deserto et Iosue.

<a id="hist-01-la-013"></a>
[**[013]**](#hist-01-en-013) Exin per 40 annos Israhelitae herimum utuntur, inbuuntur legibus, probantur victibusque pascuntur angelicis. Deinceps enim post acceptam legem transgressuque cum Iesu Nave Iordanne terra repromissioness accipiunt.

## 12. De captivitate populi Israhelitici et generationebus usque David.

<a id="hist-01-la-014"></a>
[**[014]**](#hist-01-en-014) Post cuius transitum, dum praecepta divina postponunt, saepe in alienigenarum servitio subiugantur. Sed cum conversi ingemiscunt, tribuente Domino, per virorum fortium brachium liberantur. Post haec per Samuelem regem, sicut reliquae gentes habent, a Domino postolant; accipiunt primum Saul, deinde David. Ab Abraham ergo usque ad David generationes 14: id est Abraham, Isaac, Iacob, Iudas, Phares, Esdrom, Aram, Aminadab, Naason, Salma, [Booz], Obeth, Iesse, David. David autem genuit Salamonem de Beisabee. His per Nathan prophetam, fratrem suum, et matrem in regno evectus est.

## 13. De Salamone et aedificatione templi.

<a id="hist-01-la-015"></a>
[**[015]**](#hist-01-en-015) Defuncte autem David, cum [filius eius] regnare coepisset, apparuit ei Dominus et, quod peteret ut indulgeat, pollicitui. Adille terrenas divitias posponens, sapientia magis expetiit. Quod ratum Domino fuit, ita ut ab ecdem audiret: Quia non quaesisti regna mundi nec divitias eius, sed postolasti sapientiam, ideo accepias eam. Ante te non fuit sapiens sic, et post te non erit. Quod postea iudicium illud, quod inter duas mulier es de uno infante litegantes intulit, conprobavit. Hic Salamon aedificavit templum nomini Domini miro opere, multum ibi auri argentique, aeres ac ferri ingerens, ita ut diceretur a quibusdam, numquam simile mundo fuisse aedificium fabricatum. Ab egressu ergo filiorum Israhel ex Aegypto usque ad aedificationem templi, quod fuit annos septimus regni Salamonis, inveniuntur anni 480, sicut Regum testatur historia.

## 14. De divisione regni Israhelitici.

<a id="hist-01-la-016"></a>
[**[016]**](#hist-01-en-016) Post mortem autem Salamonis divisum per duritiamRoboae regnum in duas partes, restiterunt duae tribus ad Roboam, quod luda appellabatur; ad Hieroboam autem decim tribus, quod Israhel vocabatur. Post haec igitur ad idolatria declinantes nec prophetarum vaticinia nec eorum interitus nec cladis patriae nec ipsorum etiam regumeos excidia domuerunt.

## 15. De captivitate in Babillonia.

<a id="hist-01-la-017"></a>
[**[017]**](#hist-01-en-017) Donec iratus contra eos Dominus excitavit Nabuchodonosor, qui eos in Babiloniam cum omnia templi urnamenta captivos abduxit. In qua captivitatem et Danihel propheta eximius, inter esurientes leonis inlesus, et tres pueri in medium igneum rorulenti abiere captivi. In hac captivitate et Hiezechihel prophetavit, et Hesdras propheta natus est. A David autem usque ad desolationem templi et transmigrationem in Babilonia generationes 14, id est David, Salamon, Roboam, Abia, Asa, Iosaphath, Ioram, Ozia, Ioatha, Achaz, Ezechihel, Mannases, Amon, Iosias. In his ergo 14 generationibus anni inveniuntur numero 390. De hac vero captivitate per Zorobabilliberantur; qui postea et templum et civitatem restituit. Sed haec captivitas typum illius captivitatis, ut poto, gerit, in qua anima peccatrix abducitur, quam nisi Zorobabil, id est Christus, liberaverit, horribiliter exsulavit. Ipse enim Dominus in euangelio dicit: Si vos Filius liberaverit, vere liberi eritis. Ipse enim sibi in nobis templum, in quo dignitur habitare, constituat, in quo fides ut aurum luceat, in quo eloquium praedicationes ut argentum splendeat, in quo omnia visibilis templi illius ornamenta in nostrorum sensuum honestate clariscant. Bonae etiam voluntate nostrae ipse salubrae effectu indulgeat, quia: nisi ipse aedificaverit domum, in vanum laborant qui aedificant ea. Haec vero captivitas annis70 fuisse dioitur.

## 16. De nativitate Christi.

<a id="hist-01-la-018"></a>
[**[018]**](#hist-01-en-018) Reversi autem per Zorobabil, sicut dixemus, nunc contra Deum murmorantes, nunc post idola conruentes B vel abuminationes, quae gentes exerceunt, imitantes, dum Dei prophetas contempnunt, gentibus traduntur, subiugantur, intercedunt; donec ipse Dominus patriarcharum prophetarumque vocebus repromissus, virginis Mariae utero per Spiritum sanctum inlapsus, ad redimptione nasci tam illius gentes quam omnium gentium dignaretur. A transmigratione ergo usque nativitatem Christi generationes 14, id est Iechonias, Salathiel, Zorobabil, Abiud, Eliachim, Azor, Sadoch, Achim, Eliuth, Eleazar, Mathan, Iacob, Ioseph, vir Marie, de qua dominus noster Iesus Christus nascitur; qui Ioseph quartus decimus conputatur.

## 17. De diversis gentium regnis.

<a id="hist-01-la-019"></a>
[**[019]**](#hist-01-en-019) Ergo ne videamur unius tantum Hebreae gentes habere notitiam, reliqua regna, quae vel quali Isranelitarum fuerint tempore, memoramus. Tempore Abrahae Ninus regnabat super Assirios; Sicionis Eorops; apud Aegyptios autem sexta decima erat potestas, quam sua lingua dinastiam vocabant. Tempore Moysi apud Argivus regnabat septimus Tropas; in Attica Caecros primus; apud Aegyptius Cencris duodecimus, qui et in mare obrutus est Rubro; apud Assirios sextus decimus Agatadis; apud SicioniusMaratis. Tempore vero Salamones, quando regnabat super Israhel, apud Latinus quintus regnabat Silvius; Lacidaemoniis Fistus; Corinthiis secundus Oxion Aegyptiorum Thebei centissimo vicissimo sexto anno; super Assirios Eutropes; Athiniensibus secundus Agasastus. Tempore quo Amon regnabat super Iudeam, quando captivitas in Babilonia abiit, Macedoniis praeerat Argeus; Laedorum Cyces; Aegyptiorum Vafres; apud Babiloniam Nabuchodonosor, qui eos captivos abduxit; Romanorum sextus Servius.

## 18. Quo tempore Lugdunos sit condita.

<a id="hist-01-la-020"></a>
[**[020]**](#hist-01-en-020) Post hos imperator primus lulius Caesar fuit, qui tutius imperii obtenuit monarchiam; secundus Octavianus, lulii Caesaris nepus, quem Augustum vocant, a quo et mensis Agustus est vocitatus. Cuius nono decimo imperii anno Lugdunum Galliarum conditam manefestissime repperimus; quae postea, inlustrata martyrum sanguine, nobilissima nuncupatur.

## 19. De muneribus magorum et necem infantum.

<a id="hist-01-la-021"></a>
[**[021]**](#hist-01-en-021) Anno XLIII imperii Agusti dominus noster Iesus Christus, ut diximus, ex virgine Maria in Bethleem David oppidum secundum carnem natus est. Cuius inmensum sidus magi ab oriente cernentes, cum muneribus veniunt et puerum subplicis oblatis donis adorant. Herodes vero ob zelo regni sui, dum Deum Christum persequi nititur, parvolus infantes interimit. Ipse quoque postmodum iudicio divino percussus est.

## 20. De mirabilibus et passione Christi.

<a id="hist-01-la-022"></a>
[**[022]**](#hist-01-en-022) Domino autem Deo nostro Iesu Christo paenitentiam praedicante, baptismi gratiam tribuente vel caelestem regnum cunctis gentibus promittente atque prodigia et signa per populos operante, hoc est dum de aquas vina profert, dum febris extinguit, dum caecis lumen tribuit, dum sepultis vitam restituit, dum obsessus ab inmundis spiritibus liberat, dum leprosus miserabili turpentes cute reformat, hac dum alia multa signa faciens manefestissime se Deum populis esse declarat, in Iudaeis ira succenditur, invidia exagitatur, ac mens de sanguine profetarum pasta, ut iustum interimat, iniuste molitur. Ergo, ut veterum vatum conplerentur oracula, a discipolo traditur, a pontificibus condemnatur, a Iudaeis inluditur, cum iniquis crucifigitur, a militibus, amisso spiritu, custoditur. His igitur actis, tenebrae super universum mundum factae sunt, et multi conversi ingemiscentes, Iesum filium Dei confessi sunt.

## 21. De Ioseph, qui eum sepelivit.

<a id="hist-01-la-023"></a>
[**[023]**](#hist-01-en-023) Adpraehensum autem et Ioseph, qui eum aromatibus conditum in suo monumentu recondedit, in cellolam includitur et ab ipsis sacerdotum principibus custoditur, maiorem in eum habentes sevitiam, ut Gesta Pilati ad Tiberium imperatorem missa referunt, quam in ipsum Dominum, ut cum ille a militibus, hic ab ipsis sacerdotibus custodiretur. Sed resurgente Domino, custodibus visione angelica territis, cum non inveniretur in tumulo, nocte parietis de cellola, in qua Ioseph tenebatur, suspenduntuir in sublimi, ipse vero de custodia, absolvente angelo, liberatur, parietibus restitutes in locum suum. Cumque pontifecis custodibus exprobrarent et sanctum corpus ab eisdem instanter inquirerent, dicunteis militis: 'Reddite vos Ioseph, et nos reddimus Christum; sed ut virum agnuscimus, neque vos benefactorem Dei neque nos Dei filium reddere nunc valemus'. Tunc illis confusis, milites sub hac excusatione liberantur.

## 22. De Iacobo apostulo.

<a id="hist-01-la-024"></a>
[**[024]**](#hist-01-en-024) Fertur Iacobus apostolus, cum Domino iam mortuum vidisset in cruce, detestasse atque iurasse, numquam se comisurum panem, nisi Dominum cerneret resurgentem. Tertia demum die rediens Dominus, spoliato tartaro cum triumphum, Iacobo se ostendens ait: 'Surge, Iacobi, comedi, quia iam a mortuis resurrexi'. Hic est Iacobus iustus, quem fratrem Domini nuncupant, pro eo quod Ioseph fuerit filius ex alia uxore progenitus.

## 23. De die resurrectiones dominicae.

<a id="hist-01-la-025"></a>
[**[025]**](#hist-01-en-025) Dominicam vero resurrectionem die prima facta credimus, non septimam, sicut multi putant. Hic est dies resurrectiones domini nostri Iesu Christi, quem nos propriae dominicum pro sancta eius resurrectione vocamus. Hic primus lucem vidit in principio, et hic primus Dominum resurgentem contemplare meruit de sepulchro. A captivitate vero Hierusolimae et desolationem templi usque ad passionem domini nostri Iesu Christi, id est usque Tiberii septimo decimo anno, subpotantur anni 668.

## 24. De ascensione Domini.

<a id="hist-01-la-026"></a>
[**[026]**](#hist-01-en-026) Resurgens autem Dominus, per quadraginta dies cum discipolis de regno Dei disputans, videntibus illis in nube susceptus evectusque in caelis, ad Patris dexteram resedet gloriosus. Pylatus autem gesta ad Tiberium caesarem mittit et ei tam de virtutibus Christi quam de passione vel resurrectione eius insinuat. Quae gesta apud nos hodie etenentur scripta. Tiberiusautem hoc adsenatu recensivit, quod senatus cumira respuit pro eo, quod non ad eum primitus advenissent. Hinc etenim contra christianos prima odiorum germina pullularunt. Pylatus autem non permanens inpunitus, ob suae malitiae scelere, hoc est pro nece quam in dominum nostrum Iesum Christum exercuit, propriis se manebus interfecit. Quem Manicheum fuisse multi putant secundum illud quod in euangelio legitur: Venirunt quidam de Galileis, nuntiantes ei, quorum sanguine miscuit Pylatus cum sacrificiis eorum. Sic et Herodes rex, dum in apostulos Domini saevit, percussus divinitus ob tanta scelera, intumiscens ac scatens vermibus, accepto cultro, ut malum purgaret, propriae se manus ictu liberavit.

## 25. De passione apostolorum atque Nerone.

<a id="hist-01-la-027"></a>
[**[027]**](#hist-01-en-027) Beatus Petros apostolus sub imperatore Claudio, quarto ab Augusto, Romam adgreditur, ibique praedicans, in multis virtutibus manefestissime Christum esse Dei filium conprobavit. Ab illis enim diebus christiani apud civitatem Romanam esse coeperunt. Cum autem nomen Christi per populos magis ac magis dilataretur, oritur contra haec antiqueserpentes invidia, et totisse imperatorispraecordiis inmittit saeva malignitas. Nam Nero ille luxoriosus, vanus atque superbus virorum succuba et rursum virorum appetitor, matris, sororum ac proximarum quaeque mulierum spurcissimus violator, ad complendam malitiae suae molem primus contra Christi cultum persecutione m excitat in credentes. Habebat enim secum Simonem magum, virum totius malitiae et omnes magicae artis argumento magistrum. Hunc elisum per apostolus Domini Petrum atque Paulum, commotus contra eos, cur Christum, filium Dei, praedicarent et idola adorare contempnerent, Petrum crucem, Paulum gladio iubet interfice. Ipse quoque excitatam super se seditionem fugire temptans, quarto ab Urbe lapide propria se manum interfecit.

## 26. De Iacobo, Marco et Iohanne euangelista.

<a id="hist-01-la-028"></a>
[**[028]**](#hist-01-en-028) Tunc Iacobus, frater Domini, et Marcus euangelista pro Christi nomine glorioso martyrio coronati sunt. Primus tamen omnium hanc viam levita Stefanus [et] martyr intravit. Magna post Iacobi apostoli necem Iudaeos calamitas adsecuta est. Nam adveniente Vispasiano et templum incensum est, et sexcenta milia Iudaeorum eo bello gladio et fame adfectae sunt. Domicianus autem secundus post Neronem in christianis saevit, Iohannem apostolum in insolam Pathmos relegat in exilium et diversas crudilitates in populus agitat. Post cuius mortem beatus Iohannis apostolus et euangelista de exilio rediit; qui senex et plenus dierum perfectaeque in Deum vitae vivens se deposuit in sepulchro. Hic fertur non gustare morte, donec iteratis Dominus iudicaturus adveniat, ipso in euangeliis ita dicente: Sic eum volo manere, donec veniam.

## 27. De persecutione sub Traiano.

<a id="hist-01-la-029"></a>
[**[029]**](#hist-01-en-029) Tertius post Neronem persecutionem in christianos Traianus movet. Sub quo beatus Clemens – tertius Romanae eclesiae fuit episcopus – passus et sanctus Simion Hierusolimitanus episcopus, Cleuphe filius, pro Christi nomine crucefixus adseritur, et Ignatius Anthyocinsis episcopus Romae ductus, bisteis deputatur. Haec sub Traiani temporibus acta sunt.

## 28. De Adriano.

<a id="hist-01-la-030"></a>
[**[030]**](#hist-01-en-030) Post hunc Helius Adrianus imperator creatus est. Unde et Hierusolima Helia ab Helio Adriano vocatur, successore Domiciani, eo quod eam reparaverit. Post has vero passiones sanctorum non fuit satis parti adversae gentes incredulas contra christicolas excitasse, nisi commoveret et in ipsis christianis scismas. Concitat hereses, et divisa fides catholica aliter aliterque disseritur. Nam sub Antonini imperio Marcionitana et Valentiniana hereses insana surrexit; et Iustinus philosophus post scriptos catholicae ecclesiae libros martyrio pro Christi nomine coronatur. In Asia autem, orta persecutione, beatissimus Policarpus, Iohannis apostoli et euangelistae discipulus, octoginsimo aetatis suae anno velut holocaustum purissimum per ignem Domino consecratur. Sed et in Galleis multi pro Christi nomine sunt per martyrium gemmis caelestibus coronati; quorum passionumhistoriae apud nos fideliter usque hodie retinentur.

## 29. De sancto Photino, Hirineo vel reliquis martyribus Lugdunensibus.

<a id="hist-01-la-031"></a>
[**[031]**](#hist-01-en-031) Ex quibus et ille primus Lugdunensis ecclesiae Photinus episcopus fuit, qui plenus dierum, diversis adfectus suppliciis, pro Christi nomine passus est. Beatissimus vero Hireneus, huius successor martyris, qui a beato Policarpo ad hanc urbem directus est, admirabili virtute enituit; qui in modici temporis spatio praedicatione sua maxime in integrum civitatem reddidit christianam. Sed veniente persecutione, talia ibidem diabulus bella per tyrannum exercuit, et tanta ibi multitudo christianorum ob confessione dominici nominis est iugulata, ut per plateas flumina currerent de sanguine christiano; quorum nec numerum nec nomina collegere potuimus, Dominus enim eos in libro vitae conscripsit. Beatum Hirenaeum diversis in sua carnifex praesentia poenis adfectum Christo domino per martyrium dedicavit. Post hunc et 48 martyres passi sunt, ex quibus primum fuisse legimus Vectium Epagatum.

## 30. De septem viris in Galleis ad praedicandum missis.

<a id="hist-01-la-032"></a>
[**[032]**](#hist-01-en-032) Sub Decio vero imperatore multa bella adversum nomen christianum exoriuntur, et tanta stragis de credentibus fuit, ut nec numerari quaeant. Babillas episcopus Anthiocinus cum tribus parvolis, id est Urban, Prilidan et Epolon, et Xystus Romanae eclesiae episcopus et Laurentius archidiaconus et Hyppolitus ob dominici nominis confessionem per martyrium consummati sunt. Valentinianus et Novatianus maxime tunc heretiquorum principes contrafidem nostram,inimico inpellente, crassantur. Huius tempore septem viri episcopi ordenati ad praedicandum in Galliis missi sunt, sicut historia passiones sancti martyres Saturnini denarrat. Ait enim: Sub Decio et Grato consolibus, sicut fideli recordationem retenitur, primum ac summum Tholosana civitas sanctum Saturninum habere coeperat sacerdotem. Hic ergo missi sunt: Turonicis Catianus episcopus, Arelatensibus Trophimus episcopus, Narbonae Paulos episcopus, Tolosae Saturninus episcopus, ParisiacisDionisius episcopus, Arvernis Stremonius episcopus, Lemovicinis Martialis est distinatus episcopus. De his vero beatus Dionisius Parisiorum episcopus, diversis pro Christi nomine adfectus poenis, praesentem vitam gladio inminente finivit. Saturninus vero, iam securos de martyrio, dicit duobus presbiteris suis: 'Ecce ego iam immolor et tempus meae resolutiones instat. Rogo, ut, usque dum debetum finem impleam, a vobis paenitus non relinquar'. Cumque conpraehensus ad Capitulium duceretur, relictus ab his, solus adtrahitur. Igitur cum se ab illis cerneret derelictum, orasse fertur: 'Domine Iesu Christe, exaudi me de caelo sancto tuo, ut numquam haec eclesia de his civibus mereatur habere ponteficem in sempiternum'. Quod usque nunc in ipsa civitate ita evenisse cognovimus. Hic vero tauri furentes vestigiis allegatus ac de Capitulio praecipitatus, vitam finivit. Catianus vero, Trophimus Stremoneusque et Paulos atque Marcialis, in summa sanctitate viventes, adquisitus eclesiae populos ac fidem Christi per omnibus dilatatam, felice confessione migrarunt. Et sic tam isti per martyrium quam hii per confessionem relinquentes terras, in caelestibus pariter sunt coniuncti.

## 31. De aecclesia Bituriga.

<a id="hist-01-la-033"></a>
[**[033]**](#hist-01-en-033) De horum vero discipulis quidam Bituricas civitatem adgressus, salutare omnium, Christum dominum populis nuntiavit. Ex his ergo pauci quodadmodo credentes, clerici ordinati, ritum psallendi suscipiunt, et qualiter ecclesiam construant vel omnipotenti Deo sollemnia caelebrare debeant, inbuuntur. Sed illis parvam adhuc aedificandi facultatem habentibus, cives cuiusdam domum, de qua ecclesiam faciant, expetunt. Senatores vero vel reliqui meliores loci fanaticis erant tunc cultibus obligati; qui vero crediderant ex pauperibus erant, iuxta illud Domini quod Iudaeis exprobrat, dicens: Quia meretrices et publicani praecedunt vos in regno Dei. Hi vero, non obtentam a quo petierant domum, Leocadium quendam et primum Galliarum senatorem, qui de stirpe Vecti Epagati fuit, quem Lugduno passum pro Christi nomine superius memoravimus, repererunt. Cui cum petitionem suam et fidem pariter intimassent, ille respondit: 'Si enim domus mea, quam apud Bituricam urbem habeo, huic operi digna esset, praestare non abnuebam'. Illi autem audientes, pedibus eius prostrati, oblatis tricentis aureis cum disco argenteo, dicunt eam huic ministerio esse condignam. Quod ille, acceptis de his tribus aureis pro benedictione, clementer indulgens reliqua, cum adhuc esset in errore idolatriae inplicitus, christianus factus, domum suam fecit aecclesiam. Haec est nunc ecclesia apud Bituricas urbem prima, miro opere conposita et primi martyres Stephani reliquiis inlustrata.

## 32. De Chroco et de delubro Arverno.

<a id="hist-01-la-034"></a>
[**[034]**](#hist-01-en-034) Vicinsimo septimo loco Valerianus et Gallienus Romanum imperium sunt adepti, qui gravem contra christianus persecutionem suo tempore conmoverunt. Tunc Romae Cornilius, Cyprianus Cartaginem felici sanguinem inlustrarunt. Horum tempore et Chrocus ille Alamannorum rex, commoto exercito, Gallias pervagavit. Hic autem Chrocus multae adrogantiae fertur fuisse. Qui cum nonnulla inique gessisset, per consilium, ut aiunt, matris iniquae, collectam, ut dixemus, Alamannorum gentem, universas Gallias pervagatur cunctasque aedes, quae antiquitus fabraecatae fuerant, a fundamentis subvertit. Veniens vero Arvernus, delubrum illud, quod Gallica lingua Vasso Galate vocant, incendit, diruit atque subvertit. Miro enim opere factum fuit atque firmatum. Cuius paries duplex erat, ab intus enim de minuto lapide, a foris vero quadris sculptis fabricatum fuit. Habuit enim paries ille crassitudinem pedes triginta. Intrinsecus vero marmore ac museo variatum erat, pavimentum quoque aedes marmore stratum, desuper vero plumbo tectum.

## 33. De martiribus qui circa Arvernum passi sunt.

<a id="hist-01-la-035"></a>
[**[035]**](#hist-01-en-035) Iuxta hanc urbem Liminius Antolianusque martyres requiescunt. Ibi Cassius ac Victorinus in dilectione Christi fraterno affectu sociati, per effusionem cruoris proprii caelorum regna pariter sunt adepti. Nam refert antiquitas, Victorinum servum fuisse ante dicti templi sacerdotis. Qui dum plerumque vicum, quem christianorum vocant, ad persequendos christianos adit, Cassium repperit christianum. Cuius praedicationibus atque miraculis motus, credidit Christo, relictisque fanaticis sordibus ac baptismo consecratus, magnus in virtutum operatione enituit. Nec multo post per martyrium, ut diximus, in terris scciati, ad caelestia pariter regna venerunt.

## 34. De sancto Privato martyre.

<a id="hist-01-la-036"></a>
[**[036]**](#hist-01-en-036) Inruentibus autem Alamannis in Gallias, sanctus Privatus Gabalitanae urbis episcopus in criptam Memmatinsis montis, ubi ieiuniis orationibusque vacabat, reperitur, populum Gredonensis castri monitione conclusum. Sed dum oves suas ut bonus pastor lupis tradere non consentit, daemoniis immolare conpellitur. Quod spurcum ille tam exsecrans quam refutans, tamdiu fustibus caeditur, quoadusque putaretur exanimis. Sed ex ipsa quassatione, interpositis paucis diebus, spiritum exalavit. Chrocus vero apud Arelatinsim Galliarum urbem conprehensus, diversis adfectus suppliciis, gladio verberatus interiit, non inmerito poenas, quas sanctis Dei intulerat, luens.

## 35. De Quirino episcopo et martyre.

<a id="hist-01-la-037"></a>
[**[037]**](#hist-01-en-037) Sub Diocliciano, qui tricesimo tertio loco Romanum rexit imperium, gravis contra christianos per annos quattuor persecutio exagitata est, ita ut quadam vice in ipso sacratissimo die paschae magni christianorum populi ob veri Dei cultum interficerentur. Eo tempore Quirinus Sisciensis ecclesiae sacerdos gloriosum pro Christi nomine martyrium tulit, quem, ligato ad collum molare saxo, in fluminis gurgite sevitia inpulit paganorum. Igitur cum cecidisset in gurgite, diu super aquas divina virtute ferebatur, nec sorbebant aquae, quem pondus criminis non praemebat. Quod factum admirans multitudo populi circumstantes, dispecto furore gentilium, ad liberandum properant sacerdotem. Haec ille cernens, non passus est, se a martyrio subtrahi, sed erectis ad caelum oculis ait: 'Iesu domine, qui gloriosus resedis ad dexteram Patris, ne patiaris me ab hoc stadio removeri, sed suscipiens animam meam, coniungere me tuis martyribus in requiae sempiterna dignare'. Et his dictis reddidit spiritum. Cuius corpus a christianis susceptum venerabiliter sepulturae mandatum est.

## 36. De nativitate sancti Martini et crucis inventione.

<a id="hist-01-la-038"></a>
[**[038]**](#hist-01-en-038) Romanorum tricesimus quartus imperium obtinuit Constantinus, annis triginta regnans feliciter. Huius imperii anno undecimo, cum post excessum Diocliciani pax reddita fuisset ecclesiis, beatissimus praesul Martinus apud Sabariam Pannoniae civitatem nascitur parentibus gentilibus, non tamen infimis. Hic Constantinus anno vicessimo imperii sui Crispum filium veneno, Faustam coniugem calentem balneo interfecit, scilicet quod proditores regni eius esse voluissent. Huius tempore venerabile crucis dominicae lignum per studium Helenae matris repertum est, prodente Iuda Hebraeo, qui post baptismum Quiriacus est vocitatus. Usque hoc tempus historiographus in chronicis scribit Eusebius. A vicessimo primo enim eius imperii anno Hieronimus praesbiter addidit, indicans, Iuvencum praesbiterum euangelia versibus conscripsisse, rogante supradicto imperatore.

## 37. De Iacobo Nisebeno episcopo.

<a id="hist-01-la-039"></a>
[**[039]**](#hist-01-en-039) Sub Constantis autem imperio Iacobus Nisebenus fuit, ad cuius praeces inclinatae aures divinae clementiae multa pericula a civitate eius reppulerunt. Sed et Maximinus Treverorum episcopus potens in omni sanctitate repperitur.

## 38. De transitu Antonii monachi.

<a id="hist-01-la-040"></a>
[**[040]**](#hist-01-en-040) Nono decimo Constantini iunioris anno Antonius monachus transiit centesimo quinto aetatis anno. Beatissimus Helarius Pictavinsis episcopus suasu hereticorum exilio deputatur, ibique libros pro fide catholica scribens, Constantio misit; qui quarto exilii anno eum absolvi iubens, ad propria redire permisit.

## 39. De adventum sancti Martini.

<a id="hist-01-la-041"></a>
[**[041]**](#hist-01-en-041) Tunc iam et lumen nostrum exoritur, novisque lampadum radiis Gallia perlustratur, hoc est eo tempore beatissimus Martinus in Gallias praedicare exorsus est, qui Christum, Dei filium, per multa miracula verum Deum in populis declarans, gentilium incredulitatem avertit. Hic enim fana distruxit, heresem oppraessit, eclesias aedificavit et, cum aliis multis vertutibus refulgeret, ad consummandum laudes suae titulum tres mortuos vitae restituit. Quarto Valentiniani et Valentis anno sanctus Helarius apud Pictavus, plenus sanctitate et fide, multis virtutibus aeditus, migravit ad caelos; nam et ipsi legitur mortuos suscitasse.

## 40. De Melanea matrona.

<a id="hist-01-la-042"></a>
[**[042]**](#hist-01-en-042) Melania vero matrona nobilis et incula urbis Romanae Hierusolimis ob devotionem abiit, Urbano filio Romae relecto. Quae ita se in cuncta bonitate ac sanctitate omnibus praebuit, ut Thecla vocaretur ab incolis.

## 41. De interitu Valentis imperatoris.

<a id="hist-01-la-043"></a>
[**[043]**](#hist-01-en-043) Post mortem autem Valentiniani Valens integri successor imperii, monachus ad militiam cogi iubet, nolentes fustibus praecipit verberari. Post haec bellum saevissimum in Thracias Romani gessire, in quo tanta stragisfuit, ut Romani, amisso equorum praesidio, pedebus fugirent. Cumque a Gotis internitione maxima caederentur et Valens sagitta fugiret sauciatus, parvum tugurium adgressus, inminentibus hostibus, super se incensam casulam, optatam caruit sepulturam. Sicque ultio divina ob sanctorum effuso sanguinem tandem emissa processit. Hucusque Hieronimus; ab hoc vero tempore Horosius presbiter plus scripsit.

## 42. De imperio Theudosi.

<a id="hist-01-la-044"></a>
[**[044]**](#hist-01-en-044) Igitur cum Gratianus imperator distitutam cernerit esse rem publicam, Theodosio collegam imperii facit. Hic Theodosius omnem spem suam atque fidutiam in Dei misericordiam ponit; qui multas gentes non tam gladio quam vigiliis et oratione conpescuit, rem publicam confirmavit, Constantinopuli urbem victor ingressus est.

## 43. De interitum Maximi tiranni.

<a id="hist-01-la-045"></a>
[**[045]**](#hist-01-en-045) Maximus vero cum per tyrannidem oppraessis Brittanis sumsisset victuriam, a militibus imperator creatus est. In urbe Treverica sedem instituens, Gratianum imperatorem circumventum dolis interfecit. Ad hunc Maximum beatus Martinus iam episcopus venit. In loco ergo Gratiani Theodosius ille, qui totam spem in Deum posuerat, totum suscepit imperium. Qui deinceps divinis affatibus fretus, Maximum spoliatum imperio interfecit.

## 44. De Orbico Arvernorum episcopo.

<a id="hist-01-la-046"></a>
[**[046]**](#hist-01-en-046) Apud Arvernus vero primus episcopus post Stremonium episcopum praedicatoremque Urbicus fuit, ex senatoribus conversus, uxorem habens, quae iuxta consuetudinem ecclesiasticam, remota a consortio sacerdotis, religiose vivebat. Vacabant enim ambo orationem, elemosinis atque operibus bonis. Cumque haec agerent, libor inimici, quae semper est aemula sanctitate, conmovetur in femina; quam in concupiscentiam viri succendens, novam Evam effecit. Nam succensa mulier a libidine, operta peccati tenebris, pergit ad domum ecclesiae per tenebras noctis. Cumque obserata omnia repperisset, pulsare fores ecclesiasticae domus coepit hac voce huiusmodi dare: 'Quousque sacerdos dormis? Quousque hostia clausa non reseras? Cur satellitem spernes? Cur obduratis auribus Pauli praecepta non audis? Scripsit enim: Revertimini ad alterutrum, ne temptet vos Satanas. Ecce! ego ad te revertor, nec ad extraneum, sed ad proprium vas recurro'. Haec et his similia diu declamanti, tandem sacerdotis tepescit religio. Iubet eam cubiculo intromitti, ususque concubitum eius, discedere iubet. Dehinc tardius ad se reversus et de perpetrato scelere condolens, acturus paenitentiam, diocesis suae monasterium appetit, ibique cum gemitu ac lacrimis quae commiserat diluens, ad urbem propriam est reversus. Qui, impleto vitae cursu, migravit a saeculo. Nata est enim filia ab hoc conceptu, quae in religione permansit. Ipse quoque sacerdos cum coniuge et filia in cripta Cantabennensis iuxta aggerem publicum est sepultus. In cuius loco Legonus episcopus subrogatur.

## 45. De sancto Hillidio episcopo.

<a id="hist-01-la-047"></a>
[**[047]**](#hist-01-en-047) Quo defuncto, sanctus Illidius successit, vir eximiae sanctitatis ac praeclarae virtutis, qui in tanta sanctitate emicuit, ut fama eius etiam extraneos fines adiret. Unde factum est, ut imperatoris Treverici filiam expetitus ab spiritu inmundo curaret, quod in libro illo, quem de eius vita conscripsimus, memoravimus. Fuit autem, ut fama refert, valde senex et plenus dierum plenusque bonis operibus; qui felici consummatione vitae huius perfunctus tramitem, migravit ad Christum, sepultusque in cripta suburbano civitatis illius. Habuit autem et archidiaconum nomine et merito Iustum; qui et cursum vitae bonis consumans operibus, magistri tumulo sociatur. Iam vero post transitum beati Illidi confessoris ad gloriosum eius sepulchrum tante virtutes apparent, ut nec scribi integre queant nec memoriae retineri. Huic sanctus Nepotianus successit.

## 46. De Nepotiano atque Arthemio episcopis.

<a id="hist-01-la-048"></a>
[**[048]**](#hist-01-en-048) Igitur apud Arvernus sanctus Nepotianus quartus habebatur episcopus. A Treveris vero legati in Spaniam mittebantur, ex quibus Artemius quidam admirabilis sapientiae atque pulchritudinis et prima aetate florens, vi febrium est correptus. Praecedentibus vero aliis, hic apud Arvernus aegrotus relinquitur; nam eo tempore apud Treverus sponsali erat vinculo nexus. A sancto autem Nepotiano visitatus atque oleo sancto perunctus, tribuente Domino, redditur sanitati. Qui cum ab eodem sancto verbum praedicationis accepisset, oblitus tam sponsam terrenam quam facultates proprias, sanctae copulatur eclesiae, factusque clericus, in tanta praelatus est sanctitate, ut beato Nepotiano successor exsisteret ad regendas dominici gregis caulas.

## 47. De castitate amantium.

<a id="hist-01-la-049"></a>
[**[049]**](#hist-01-en-049) Per idem tempus Iniuriosus quidam de senatoribus Arvernis cum magnis opibus similem sibi in coniugio puellam expetiit, datumque arrabone, diem statuit nuptiarum. Erat autem uterque unicus patri. Adveniente vero die, celebrata nuptiarum sollemnitate, in uno strato ex more locantur. Sed puella graviter contristata, aversa ad parietem, amarissime flebat. Cui ille: 'Quid', inquid, turbaris? Indica, quaeso, mihi'. Illaque silente, adiecit: 'Obsecro te per Iesum Christum, filium Dei, ut mihi quid doleas sapienter exponas'. Tunc illa conversa ad eum ait: 'Si omnibus diebus vitae meae plangam, numquid tantae erunt lacrimae, ut queant abluere tam inmensum pectoris mei dolorem? Statueram enim, ut corpusculum meum inmaculatum Christo a virili tactu servarem, sed vae mihi! qui taliter ab eo relicta sum, ut quod obtabam perficere non valerem et quod ab initio aetatis meae servavi in hac novissima die, quam videre non debueram, perdidi. Ecce enim relicta ab inmortali Christo, qui mihi dotem promittebat paradisum, mortalis hominis sum sortita consortium; et pro rosis inmarcescibilibus arentium me rosarum non ornat, sed deformat spolia. Et cum debui super quadrifluo Agni flumine puritatis stolam induere, haec mihi vestis onus exhibuit, non honorem. Sed quid amplius verba protrahimus? Infelix ego, quae debui sorte mereri polos, hodie dimergo in abyssos'. O, si mihi haec futura erant, quare non dies vitae meae ipse fuit finis, qui fuit initium! O, si ante introissem mortis ianuam, quam lactis acciperem alimenta. O, si mihi dulcium nutricum oscula in funere fuissent expensa! Horrent enim mihi terrenae species, quia pro mundi vita transfixas manus suspicio Redemptoris. Nec cerno diademas gemmis insignibus coruscantes, cum illam spineam miror mente coronam. Respuo longe lateque diffusa spatia terrae tuae, quia amaenitate concupisco paradisi. Horrent tua solaria, cum Dominum resedentem suspicio super astra'. Talia cum magno fletu iactanti commotus pietate iuvenis ait: 'Unicos nos nobilissimi Arvernorum habuere parentes et ad propagandam generationem coniungere voluerunt, ne recedentibus de mundo succederet heres extraneus'. Cui illa: 'Nihil est mundus, nihil sunt divitiae, nihil est pompa saeculi huius, nihil est vita ipsa quam fruemur, sed illa magis vita quaerenda est, quae morte terminante non clauditur, quae labe ulla non solvitur nec aliquo occasu finitur, ubi homo in beatitudine aeterna permanet, luce non occidente vivit et, quod magis est his omnibus, ipsius Domini praesentiam iugi perfruens contemplatione, in angelico translatus statu, indissolubili laetitia gaudet'. Ad haec ille: 'Dulcissimis', inquid, 'eloquiis tuis aeterna mihi vita tamquam magnum iubar inluxit, et ideo, si vis a carnali abstinere concupiscentiam, particeps tuae mentis efficiar'. Illa respondit: 'Difficile est sexum virilem mulieribus ista praestare. Tamen, si feceris, ut inmaculati permaneamus in saeculo, ego tibi partem tribuam dotis, quam promissam habeo ab sponso domino meo Iesu Christo, cui me et famulam devovi esse et sponsam'. Tunc ille armatus crucis vexillo ait: 'Faciam quae hortaris'. Et datis inter se dextris, quieverunt, multos postea in uno strato recumbentes annos, sed cum castitate laudabili, quod postea in eorum transitu declaratum est. Nam cum, impleto certamine, puella migraret ad Christum, peracto vir funeris officio cum puellam in sepulchro deponeret, ait: Gratias tibi ago, domine Iesu Christe, aeternae domine deus noster, quia hunc thesaurum, sicut a te commendatum accepi, ita inmaculatum pietati tuae restituo'. Ad haec illa subridens: 'Quid', inquid, 'loqueris quod non interrogaris?' Illamque sepultam ipse non post multum insequitur. Porro cum utriusque sepulchrum e diversis parietibus collocatum fuisset, miraculi novitas, quae eorum castitatem manifestaret, apparuit. Nam facto mane cum ad locum populi accederent, invenerunt sepulchra pariter, quae longe inter se distare reliquerant, scilicet ut, quos tenet socios caelum, sepultorum hic corporum non separet monumentum. Hos usque hodie Duos Amantes vocitare loci incolae voluerunt. Meminimus de his in libro Miraculorum.

## 48. De transitu sancti Martini.

<a id="hist-01-la-050"></a>
[**[050]**](#hist-01-en-050) Arcadi vero et Honori secundo imperii anno sanctus Martinus Turonorum episcopus, plenus virtutibus et sanctitate, praebens infirmis multa beneficia, octuaginsimo et primo aetatis suae anno, episcopatum autem vicissimo sexto, apud Condatinsem diccisis suae vicum excedens a saeculo, filiciter migravit ad Christum. Transiit autem media nocte, quae dominica habebatur, Attico Caesarioque consolibus. Multi enim in eius transitum psallentium audierunt in caelum, quod in libro virtutum eius primo plenius exposuemus. Nam cum primitus sanctus Dei apud Condatinsem, ut dixemus, vicum aegrotare coepisset, Pectavi populi ad eius transitum sicut Toronici convenerunt. Quo migrante, grandis altercatio in utrumque surrexit populum. Dicebant enim Pectavi: 'Noster est monachus, nobis abba extetit, nos requiremus commendatum. Sufficiat vobis, quod, dum esset in mundo episcopus, usi fuistis eius conloquium, participastis convivio, firmati fuistis benedictionebus, insuper et virtutibus iocundati. Sufficiant ergo vobis ista omnia, nobis liciat auferre vel cadaver exanimum'. Ad haec Toronici respondebant: 'Si virtutum nobis facta sufficere dicitis, scitote, quia vobiscum positus amplius est quam hic operatus. Nam, ut praetermittamus plurimum, vobis suscitavit duos mortuos, nobis unum; et, ut ipse saepe dicebat, maior ei virtus ante episcopatum fuit, quam post episcopatum. Ergo necesse est, ut, quod nobiscum non inplevit vivens, expleat vel defunctus. Vobis est enim ablatus, nobis a Deo donatus. Virum si mus antiquitus institutus servatur, in urbe qua ordenatus est habebit Deo iubente sepulchrum. Certe si pro monasthirio privilegio cupitis vindecare, scetote, quia primum ei monasthirium cum Mediolaninsibus fuit'. His ergo litigantibus, sol ruente nox clauditur, corpusque in medio positum, firmatis serra usteis, ab utroque populo costoditur, futurum ut mane facto a Pectavensibus per violentiam aufereretur. Sed Deus omnipotens noluit urbem Toronicam a proprio frustrari patrono. Denique nocte media omnes Pectava somno falanga conpraemitur, nec ullus superfuit, qui ex hac multitudine vigilaret. Igitur ubi Toronici eos conspiciunt obdormisse, adpraehensam sanctissimi corporis glebam, alii per fenestram eiciunt, alii a foris suscipiunt, positumque in navi, cum omni populo per Vingennam fluvium discendunt, ingressique Legeris alveum, ad urbem Toronicam cum magnis laudibus psallentioque dirigunt copioso. De quorum vocibus Pectavi expergefacti, nihil de thesauro quem costodiebant habentes, cum magna confusione ad propria sunt reversi. Quod si quis requiret, cur post transitum Catiani episcopi unus tantum, id est Litorius, usque ad sanctum Martinum fuisset episcopus, noverit, quia, obsistentibus paganis, diu civitas Toronica sine benedictione sacerdotale fuit. Nam qui christiani eo tempore videbantur, occultae et per latebras divinum offitium celebrabant. Nam si qui a paganis repperti fuissent christiani, aut adficiebantur verberibus aut gladio truncabantur. A passione ergo Domini usque transitum sancti Martini anni 412 conpotantur.
