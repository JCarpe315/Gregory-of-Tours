# BOOK SEVEN

## LIBER SEPTIMUS

### 1. Of the death of the holy Salvius

<a id="hist-07-en-001"></a>
[**[001]**](#hist-07-la-001) Although my task is to continue the history at the point where the sequence of the preceding books left it, devotion first requires me to say something about the death of blessed Salvius, who is known to have died this year. As he himself used to relate, he lived for a long time in secular dress and conducted worldly cases with the judges of the age, but never entangled himself in the desires by which the minds of young men are usually ensnared. When the fragrance of the divine Spirit touched his inward parts, he abandoned secular service and sought a monastery. Already devoted to the Godhead, he understood that it was better to practice poverty with the fear of God than to pursue the profits of a perishing world. He lived for a long time in this monastery under the rule established by the fathers. When he had advanced to greater strength of both understanding and age, the abbot who presided over the monastery died, and Salvius undertook the office of nourishing the flock. Although he ought to have made himself more accessible to the brethren for their correction, after receiving the honor he became more remote. He immediately sought a more secluded cell; in his former cell, as he himself asserted, excessive abstinence had caused him to shed the skin of his body more than nine times.

After receiving the honor, content with this frugality, he devoted himself to prayer and reading, frequently reflecting that it would be better for him to remain hidden among the monks than to bear the public title of abbot. In short, he was enclosed, bidding farewell to the brethren while they bade farewell to him. In this enclosure he lived with still greater abstinence than before. Out of charity he took care to offer prayer and give the fullest grace of blessed bread to every stranger who came; these gifts often brought complete healing to many sick people.

On one occasion he lay exhausted on his little bed, consumed by a severe fever. Suddenly the cell shone with a great light and trembled. He stretched out his hands to heaven and breathed his last with thanksgiving. The monks and his mother raised a mingled cry, drew out the dead body, washed it with water, clothed it, and placed it on a bier, passing the fleeting night in psalmody and weeping. In the morning, when the funeral rites had been prepared, they began to move the body on the bier. Behold, to the horror of all, the man shook himself as though awakened from a deep sleep. Opening his eyes and raising his hands, he said, “O merciful Lord, why have you done this to me, permitting me to return to this dark dwelling of worldly habitation, when your mercy in heaven would be better for me than this world’s most wretched life?” His companions were astonished and asked what this prodigy meant, but he answered none of their questions. He rose from the bier, feeling no ill effects from the illness he had suffered, and passed three days without nourishment of food or drink.

On the third day he summoned the monks and his mother and said, “Listen, most beloved, and understand that what you see in this world is nothing; it is as the prophet Solomon sang: ‘All is vanity.’ Happy is he who can so act in the world that he merits to behold God’s glory in heaven.” After saying this, he began to doubt whether he should speak further or remain silent. When he paused, the brethren entreated him to explain what he had seen, and he said, “Four days ago, when you saw me lifeless as the cell trembled, two angels seized me and raised me to the heights of heaven, so that I seemed to have beneath my feet not only this squalid world but also the sun, moon, clouds, and stars. I was then led through a gate brighter than this light into a dwelling where the whole pavement shone like gold and silver. The light was ineffable and the expanse indescribable. A multitude of both sexes had filled it so completely that I could not see the length or breadth of the company. The angels who went before me prepared a way for me among those crowding around, and I reached the place I had already been contemplating from afar. Above it hung a cloud brighter than every light. No sun, moon, or star could be seen in it, but it shone more splendidly than all these with its own natural light, and a voice proceeded from the cloud like the voice of many waters. There men in priestly and secular garments humbly greeted me, sinner though I was. My guides told me that these were the martyrs and confessors whom I worship here with the highest devotion. Standing where I had been commanded, I was enveloped by an odor of overwhelming sweetness, and I have been so refreshed by that sweetness that until now I have desired no food or drink. I heard a voice saying, ‘Let him return to the world, for he is necessary to my churches.’ The voice was heard, but the speaker could not be seen at all. I prostrated myself on the pavement and said through my tears, ‘Alas, alas, Lord, why did you show me these things if I was to be deprived of them? Behold, today you cast me from your presence so that I return to the frail world and cannot come back here again. Do not, I beg you, Lord, take your mercy from me, but permit me to dwell here, lest I fall there and perish.’ The voice speaking to me said, ‘Go in peace. I am your guardian until I bring you back to this place.’ Then my companions left me, and I departed in tears, returning through the gate by which I had entered.”

While he spoke and all present were astonished, the holy man of God again began to say with tears, “Woe is me, for having dared to reveal such a mystery. Behold, the odor of sweetness that I drew from the holy place, by which I have been sustained for these three days without any food or drink, has left me. My tongue is covered with severe wounds and so swollen that it seems to fill my whole mouth. I know that it was not well pleasing to my Lord God that these secrets should be made public. But you know, Lord, that I did this in simplicity of heart, not in boastfulness of mind. I beg you to forgive me and not abandon me, according to your promise.” Having spoken, he was silent and accepted food and drink.

Writing these things, I fear that some reader may find them incredible, as Sallust says in writing history: “When you record the virtue and glory of good men, everyone accepts calmly what he considers easy for himself to do; anything beyond this he regards as fabricated and false.” I call almighty God to witness that I heard everything I have related from Salvius’s own lips. Much later this blessed man was drawn from his cell and elected to the episcopate, being ordained against his will. In, I believe, the tenth year of his episcopate, the inguinal plague grew stronger in the city of Albi. When the greater part of the population had died and few citizens remained, the blessed man, like a good shepherd, refused ever to leave the place. He continually exhorted those who remained to devote themselves to prayer, persevere earnestly in vigils, and occupy themselves always with good works and thoughts, saying, “Do these things so that, if God wishes you to depart from this world, you may enter not into judgment but into rest.”

When, as I believe, he recognized by the Lord’s revelation that the time of his calling had come, he prepared his own sarcophagus, washed his body, and clothed himself. Thus, with his mind always fixed on heaven, he breathed out his blessed spirit. He was of great holiness and very little greed, never wishing to possess gold; if compelled to accept it, he immediately distributed it to the poor. When the patrician Mummolus led many captives from his city, Salvius followed and ransomed them all. The Lord granted him such favor with those people that even the men who had taken the captives reduced their price for him and made additional gifts. He thus restored the captives to their former liberty in their own country. I have heard many other good things about this man, but because I wish to return to the history I have begun, I omit most of them.

### 2. Of the clash between the men of Chartres and Orléans

<a id="hist-07-en-002"></a>
[**[002]**](#hist-07-la-002) When Chilperic had died and found the death he had long sought, the men of Orléans joined those of Blois and attacked the Dunois, overthrowing them while they were off their guard. They burned houses, grain stores, and whatever could not be moved, drove off cattle, and carried away everything they could lift. When they departed, the men of the Dunois joined the rest of the Chartrain and followed on their heels, inflicting on them the same treatment they had suffered and leaving nothing inside houses, outside houses, or belonging to houses. While they continued to rage and provoke quarrels with one another and the Orléannais were preparing arms against them, the counts intervened and peace was granted until a hearing, so that on the appointed day of judgment the party that had unjustly attacked the other should make amends through the mediation of justice. Hostilities accordingly ceased.

### 3. Of the death of Vidastis, surnamed Avus

<a id="hist-07-en-003"></a>
[**[003]**](#hist-07-la-003) Vidastis, surnamed Avus, who years earlier had killed Lupus and Ambrosius for love of Ambrosius’s wife and had married the woman herself, said to be his own cousin, was committing many crimes in the territory of Poitiers. On one occasion he joined Childeric the Saxon, and while they were provoking one another with insults, one of Childeric’s servants pierced Avus with a spear. He fell to the ground, was wounded by many additional blows, and poured out his wicked soul as his blood flowed away. Divine majesty thus avenged the innocent blood he had shed with his own hand. The wretched man had often committed many thefts, adulteries, and murders, which I consider better left unmentioned. The Saxon nevertheless made compensation to Avus’s sons for his death.

### 4. How Fredegund fled to the church

<a id="hist-07-en-004"></a>
[**[004]**](#hist-07-la-004) Meanwhile Queen Fredegund, now widowed, came to Paris and took refuge in the church with the treasures she had enclosed within the walls, where Bishop Ragnemod supported her. The treasurers carried off the remaining treasures that had stayed at the villa of Chelles, including the golden ceremonial dish Chilperic had recently made, and went swiftly to King Childebert, who was then residing in the city of Meaux.

### 5. How King Guntram came to Paris

<a id="hist-07-en-005"></a>
[**[005]**](#hist-07-la-005) Queen Fredegund took counsel and sent envoys to King Guntram, saying, “Let my lord come and take up his brother’s kingdom. I have a little infant whom I desire to place in his arms, and I humble myself beneath his rule.” When King Guntram learned of his brother’s death, he wept most bitterly. After mastering his grief, he raised an army and directed it toward Paris. When he had been received within the walls, his nephew King Childebert approached from another direction.

### 6. How the same king subdued what had been Charibert's

<a id="hist-07-en-006"></a>
[**[006]**](#hist-07-la-006) When the Parisians refused to receive Childebert, he sent envoys to King Guntram, saying, “I know, most pious father, that your goodness is not unaware how the hostile party has hitherto oppressed each of us, so that neither could obtain justice concerning his rightful possessions. I therefore now beg as a suppliant that the agreements made between us after my father’s death be preserved.” King Guntram answered his envoys, “You wretched and always perfidious men, possessing no truth and never standing by your promises! Behold, you abandoned everything you promised me and wrote a new pact with King Chilperic, intending to drive me from the kingdom and divide my cities between you. Behold the pact itself; behold the subscriptions of your own hands confirming this agreement! With what face do you now ask me to receive my nephew Childebert, whom your perversity sought to make my enemy?”

The envoys replied, “If anger has so possessed your mind that you will grant your nephew nothing you promised, at least cease taking what is owed to him from Charibert’s kingdom.” Guntram answered, “Behold the pact made between me: whoever entered the city of Paris without his brother’s consent would lose his share, with the martyr Polyeuctus and the confessors Hilary and Martin as his judge and requiter. Afterward my brother Sigibert entered it, perished by God’s judgment, and lost his share. Chilperic did likewise. Through these transgressions they lost their shares. Therefore, since they have perished according to God’s judgment and the curses of the pacts, with the law’s aid I will subject the whole kingdom of Charibert with its treasures to my own rule, and I will grant no one anything from it except by my spontaneous will. Depart, therefore, you always lying and perfidious men, and report this to your king.”

### 7. How Childebert's legates demand Fredegund

<a id="hist-07-en-007"></a>
[**[007]**](#hist-07-la-007) When they departed, Childebert’s envoys again came to Guntram demanding Queen Fredegund and saying, “Hand over the murderess who strangled my aunt, killed my father and uncle, and also slew my own cousins with the sword.” He replied, “I will decide everything at the assembly I am holding, considering what ought to be done.” He protected Fredegund, frequently inviting her to his table and promising to be her mighty defender. One day while they were feasting together, the queen rose and took her leave, but the king detained her, saying, “Take a little more food.” She answered, “I beg you to excuse me, my lord, for as happens with women I must rise because of my pregnancy.” He was astonished, knowing that it was four months since she had given birth to her other son, but allowed her to rise.

The leading men formerly belonging to Chilperic’s kingdom, including Ansovald and the rest, gathered around his son, who, as I said above, was four months old and had been named Chlothar. They exacted oaths throughout the cities formerly belonging to Chilperic that they should be faithful to King Guntram and his nephew Chlothar. King Guntram restored under justice’s guidance everything that King Chilperic’s faithful servants had wrongfully taken from various people and also conferred many gifts on the churches. He restored the wills of the dead who had made churches their heirs and had been oppressed by Chilperic, showing himself kind to many and giving much to the poor.

### 8. How the king begged the people not to kill him as his brothers

<a id="hist-07-en-008"></a>
[**[008]**](#hist-07-la-008) Because Guntram did not trust the men among whom he had come, he protected himself with arms and never went to church or other places he wished to visit without a large guard. One Sunday, after the deacon had ordered the people to be silent and listen to the Mass, the king turned to the people and said, “I adjure you, men and women here present, to deign to keep inviolate faith with me and not kill me as you recently killed my brothers. Allow me three years to rear my nephews, who have become my adopted sons, lest after my death you perish together with those little ones—may the eternal Divinity never permit it—because no adult of my family survives to defend you.” When he had spoken, all the people poured out prayer for the king to the Lord.

### 9. How Rigunth was detained by Desiderius, her treasures taken away

<a id="hist-07-en-009"></a>
[**[009]**](#hist-07-la-009) While this was happening, Rigunthis, King Chilperic’s daughter, reached Toulouse with the treasures described above. Seeing herself already near the boundary of the Goths, she began to make delays. Her attendants told her that she ought to remain there because they were exhausted by the journey, their garments unkempt and their shoes torn, while the equipment for their horses and carriages was still packed separately as it had been carried in the wagons. They ought first to put everything carefully in order and then proceed on the journey, so that she might be received with complete elegance by her bridegroom; otherwise, if they appeared unkempt among the Goths, they would be ridiculed. While they were delayed for these reasons, news of King Chilperic’s death reached Duke Desiderius. He gathered his strongest men and entered the city of Toulouse. He found and removed the treasures from the queen’s control and placed them in a house protected by seals and guarded by strong men, assigning the queen a restricted allowance until he returned to the city.

### 10. How Gundovald was raised to the kingdom

<a id="hist-07-en-010"></a>
[**[010]**](#hist-07-la-010) Desiderius hastened to Mummolus, with whom he had made an alliance two years earlier. Mummolus was then residing within the walls of Avignon with Gundovald, whom I mentioned in the preceding book. Joining the dukes mentioned above, Mummolus came to Limoges and arrived at the village of Brive-la-Gaillarde, where, as they say, Saint Martin, the disciple of my Martin, reposes. Gundovald was raised as king upon a shield there. But when they carried him around for the third time, he is said to have fallen, so that the bystanders could scarcely support him with their hands. He then traveled through the surrounding cities.

Rigunthis remained in the basilica of Saint Mary at Toulouse, where Ragnovald’s wife, whom I mentioned above, had taken refuge in fear of Chilperic. Ragnovald returned from Spain and was restored to his wife and property; he had gone to Spain as an envoy sent by King Guntram. At that time the basilica of the blessed Martin at the village of Brive was burned by an approaching enemy in a great fire, so that both the altar and the columns made of various kinds of marble were dissolved by the flames. Bishop Ferreolus later restored the building so completely that it seemed to have suffered no harm. The inhabitants greatly admire and venerate this saint because they often experience his miracles.

### 11. Of the signs that appeared

<a id="hist-07-en-011"></a>
[**[011]**](#hist-07-la-011) It was the tenth month when these things happened. New shoots with misshapen grapes appeared on the vine-stocks, and flowers on the trees. A great pyramid ran across the sky and illuminated the world far and wide before daylight. Rays also appeared in the sky. In the north a fiery column, seeming to hang from heaven, was visible for two hours, with a great star poised above it. The earth shook at Angers, and many other signs appeared which, I believe, foretold Gundovald’s own destruction.

### 12. Of the burning of the region of Tours and the power of Saint Martin

<a id="hist-07-en-012"></a>
[**[012]**](#hist-07-la-012) King Guntram sent his counts to take possession of the cities that Sigibert had formerly received from his brother Charibert’s kingdom, directing them to exact oaths and subject the cities to his rule. The people of Tours and Poitiers wished to go over to Childebert, Sigibert’s son, but the men of Bourges prepared to move against them and began setting fires within the territory of Tours. They burned with fire the church of Maroiale in the Touraine, where relics of Saint Martin were kept. But the blessed saint’s power was present: in so violent a fire the little palls placed over the altar were not consumed by the flames, and even the herbs gathered long before and placed on the altar were not burned. Seeing these fires, the people of Tours sent an embassy saying that it was better for them to submit temporarily to King Guntram than to have everything devastated by fire and sword.

### 13. Of the fire and plunder of the city of Poitiers

<a id="hist-07-en-013"></a>
[**[013]**](#hist-07-la-013) Immediately after Chilperic’s death Duke Gararic had gone to Limoges and received oaths in Childebert’s name. He then came to Poitiers, was received by the inhabitants, and remained there. Hearing what the people of Tours were suffering, he sent an embassy adjuring me not to deliver me to King Guntram’s party if I wished to safeguard myself, but rather to remember Sigibert, Childebert’s father. I sent this message back to the bishop and citizens, warning that unless they submitted temporarily to King Guntram they would suffer similar treatment. I said that Guntram was now father to the two sons of Sigibert and Chilperic who had been adopted by him, and held the rule of the kingdom as King Chlothar, their father, had once done. When they refused to consent, Gararic left the city as though to bring an army, leaving Ebero, King Childebert’s chamberlain, in the city. Sicharius, with Willacharius, count of Orléans, who had then come to Tours, raised an army against Poitiers, intending that the men of Tours on one side and the men of Bourges on the other should devastate everything. When they approached the border and began burning houses, the Poitevins sent envoys to them saying, “I ask you to wait until the assembly that Kings Guntram and Childebert are holding. If it is agreed that King Guntram should receive these districts, I will not resist; otherwise, I acknowledge my own lord, whom I ought fully to serve.” They answered, “This matter is no concern of mine; I have only to fulfill the king’s commands. If you refuse, I will devastate everything as I have begun.” When the destruction of everything by fire, plunder, and captivity was imminent, Childebert’s men were expelled from the city and the Poitevins gave oaths to King Guntram, though they did not keep them for long.

### 14. Of the legates of King Childebert sent to Guntram

<a id="hist-07-en-014"></a>
[**[014]**](#hist-07-la-014) When the assembly convened, Egidius the bishop, Guntram Boso, Sigivald, and many others were sent by King Childebert to King Guntram. When they entered his presence, the bishop said, “I give thanks to almighty God, most pious king, because after many labors he has restored you to your regions and kingdom.” The king answered, “Thanks are indeed due to him who is King of kings and Lord of lords and who has deigned to accomplish these things by his compassion—not to you, by whose treacherous counsel and perjuries my regions were burned last year. You have never kept entire faith with any man; your treachery is spread everywhere; and you show yourself not a bishop but an enemy of my kingdom.” The bishop, moved to anger, was silent.

One envoy said, “Your nephew Childebert supplicates you to order the return of the cities his father held.” Guntram replied, “I have already told you that my pacts confer them on me, and therefore I will not return them.” Another envoy said, “Your nephew asks you to hand over the sorceress Fredegund, through whom many kings were killed, so that he may avenge the deaths of his father, uncle, and cousins.” Guntram answered, “She cannot be delivered into his power because she has a son who is king. Moreover, I do not believe the charges you allege against her.”

Guntram Boso then approached the king as though to make a proposal. Because the report had spread that Gundovald had openly been raised as king, the king anticipated Boso’s words and said, “Enemy of my region and kingdom! Years ago you went to the East precisely to bring a certain Ballomer”—for so the king called Gundovald—“against my kingdom. Always perfidious, you never keep your promises!” Boso answered, “You, my lord and king, sit upon the royal throne, and no one dares answer your words. I declare myself innocent in this affair. But if there is anyone like me who secretly imputes this crime to me, let him come forward openly now and speak. Then, most pious king, leave it to God’s judgment to decide when he sees us fighting on the level ground of a single field.” When everyone was silent, the king added, “Everyone’s mind ought to be inflamed by this affair to repel from my borders an intruder whose father operated mills and, to tell the truth, sat at wool-combs and arranged wool.” Although one man might possess skill in both crafts, one of the envoys answered the king’s reproach: “According to you, this man had two fathers, a wool-worker and a miller at once. God forbid, O king, that you should speak so coarsely. It has never been heard that one man can have two fathers at once, except in a spiritual sense.” When many burst into laughter, another envoy answered, “I bid you farewell, O king. Since you have refused to return your nephew’s cities, I know that the axe fixed in your brothers’ heads remains unblunted. It will swiftly be fixed in your brain.” They departed amid scandal.

Inflamed by these words, the king ordered horse dung, rotten pieces of wood, straw, hay disintegrating with decay, and the foul mud of the city itself to be thrown on their heads as they went. Foully stained by these things, they departed amid immense injury and humiliation.

### 15. Of the malice of Fredegund

<a id="hist-07-en-015"></a>
[**[015]**](#hist-07-la-015) While Queen Fredegund was residing in the church at Paris, Leonardus, a former household officer who had just come from Toulouse, entered and began recounting the humiliation and injuries inflicted on her daughter. “At your command I accompanied Queen Rigunthis,” he said, “and saw her humiliation and how she was stripped of her treasures and all her possessions. I escaped by flight and have come to report these events to my lady.” Hearing this, she was moved to fury and ordered him to be stripped in the church itself. She took away his garments and the belt he possessed as King Chilperic’s gift and ordered him out of her presence. She also had the cooks, bakers, and anyone else she knew to have returned from this journey beaten, stripped, and deprived of their hands.

She tried to overwhelm Nectarius, Bishop Badegisil’s brother, with abominable accusations before the king, asserting that he had carried off much of the dead king’s treasure. She also said that he had taken many hides and wines from the storehouses and asked that he be thrust bound into the darkness of prison. But the patience of the king and his brother’s assistance prevented it. She practiced many vain things there, not fearing God in whose church she was seeking help. At that time she had with her the judge Audo, who had consented to many of her evil deeds in the king’s time. Together with the prefect Mummolus he had subjected to public tribute many Franks who had been freeborn elders in King Childebert’s time. After the king’s death those Franks stripped and plundered him so that nothing remained except what he could carry on his person. They set his house on fire and would certainly have taken his life as well had he not sought the church with the queen.

### 16. Of the return of Bishop Praetextatus

<a id="hist-07-en-016"></a>
[**[016]**](#hist-07-la-016) Fredegund received Bishop Praetextatus with hostility. After the king’s death the citizens of Rouen had sought him from exile and restored him to their city with great acclamation. After his return he came to Paris and presented himself before King Guntram, begging him to examine his case diligently. The queen asserted that a man who had been separated from the priestly office by the judgment of forty-five bishops ought not to be received. When the king wished to convene a synod over the matter, Ragnemod, bishop of Paris, answered for everyone, saying, “Know that the bishops imposed penance on him but did not remove him entirely from the episcopate.” Praetextatus was accordingly received by the king, invited to his table, and returned to his own city.

### 17. Of Bishop Promotus

<a id="hist-07-en-017"></a>
[**[017]**](#hist-07-la-017) Promotus, who had been appointed bishop in the fortress of Dun by King Sigibert’s ordination and had been removed after the king’s death because that fortress belonged to the diocese of Chartres, and against whom judgment had been given that he should exercise only the office of presbyter, approached the king and begged to receive episcopal ordination in the fortress. But Pappolus, bishop of Chartres, objected, saying, “It belongs to my diocese,” and particularly displayed the bishops’ judgment. Promotus could obtain nothing from the king except the recovery of his own property within the territory of that fortress, where he still resides with his surviving mother.

### 18. Of that which was said to the king

<a id="hist-07-en-018"></a>
[**[018]**](#hist-07-la-018) While the king was staying in Paris, a poor man came and said, “Hear the words of my mouth, O king. Know that Faraulf, formerly your brother’s chamberlain, is seeking to kill you. I have heard his plan to attack you with a knife or pierce you with a spear as you go to church for morning prayer.” The astonished king sent for Faraulf. Faraulf denied it, but the king, fearing for his life, protected himself strongly with arms and never proceeded to holy places or anywhere else except surrounded by armed guards. Faraulf died not long afterward.

### 19. How the queen was bidden to go to a villa

<a id="hist-07-en-019"></a>
[**[019]**](#hist-07-la-019) When a great outcry arose against those who had been powerful under King Chilperic because they had taken estates and other property belonging to others, the king ordered everything unjustly taken to be restored, as I have already mentioned. He also ordered Queen Fredegund to go to the villa of Rodoiale, situated in the territory of Rouen. All the highest-born men of Chilperic’s kingdom followed her. There they left her with Bishop Melanius, who had been removed from Rouen, and transferred themselves to her son, promising that he would be reared by them with the greatest care.

### 20. How he sent one who should wound Brunhilda

<a id="hist-07-en-020"></a>
[**[020]**](#hist-07-la-020) After Queen Fredegund went to the villa mentioned above, she was deeply grieved that part of her power had been taken away. Considering Brunhilda superior to herself, she secretly sent a cleric in her confidence to circumvent Brunhilda with deceit and kill her. He was to insinuate himself subtly into Brunhilda’s service, gain her trust, and then strike secretly. When the cleric arrived, he commended himself to her with various devices, saying, “I am fleeing from Queen Fredegund’s presence and seeking your help.” He began to make himself humble, dear, obedient, and personally devoted to the queen in everything. Not long afterward they discovered that he had been sent treacherously. He was bound and beaten, and when he had revealed the secret plot he was allowed to return to his patroness. He disclosed what had happened and said that he had been unable to carry out her orders; she punished him by cutting off his hands and feet.

### 21. Of the flight and custody of Eberulfus

<a id="hist-07-en-021"></a>
[**[021]**](#hist-07-la-021) After these events King Guntram returned to Chalon and tried to investigate his brother’s death. The queen imputed the crime to the chamberlain Eberulf. She had asked him to remain with her after the king’s death, but he had been unable to do so. As this enmity grew, the queen asserted that Eberulf had killed the king, taken much of his treasure, and then withdrawn to the Touraine. If the king desired to avenge his brother’s death, she said, he should know that Eberulf was the standard-bearer of this crime. The king then swore before all his nobles that he would destroy not only Eberulf but his progeny to the ninth generation, so that by their slaughter the wicked custom of killing kings might be abolished.

When Eberulf learned this, he sought refuge in Saint Martin’s basilica, whose property he had often seized. An arrangement was made to guard him, and the men of Orléans and Blois came alternately to keep watch. After fifteen days they returned with much plunder, driving off pack animals, cattle, and whatever else they could seize. Men who had driven off blessed Martin’s beasts quarreled and pierced one another with lances. Two others who were stealing mules approached a neighbor’s house and began demanding drink. When he said he had none, they raised their lances to pierce him, but he drew his sword and ran both through; both fell dead. Saint Martin’s animals were nevertheless returned. The men of Orléans committed so many evils there that they cannot be described.

### 22. Of his malice

<a id="hist-07-en-022"></a>
[**[022]**](#hist-07-la-022) While this was happening, Eberulf’s property was granted to various persons. He displayed in public the gold, silver, and other valuable goods that he retained with him, while what he had entrusted to others was confiscated. His herds of horses, pigs, and pack animals were plundered. His house within the walls, which he had taken from the Church’s dominion and filled with grain, wine, hides, and many other things, was completely stripped, and nothing remained there except empty walls. He regarded me with particular suspicion because I faithfully supported his interests and often promised that, if he ever regained the king’s favor, he would avenge on me what he was now suffering. God, to whom the secrets of the heart are revealed, knows that I offered him assistance with a pure heart as far as I could. Although he had previously laid many snares for me concerning Saint Martin’s property, there was reason for me to forget them because I had received his son from the holy font. But I believe it was a great obstacle to the unfortunate man that he showed no reverence to the holy bishop.

He often held banquets in the very atrium at the blessed saint’s feet, continually practicing drunkenness and vanity. When a priest delayed to give him wine, though he already appeared drunk, Eberulf crushed him against a stool and beat him with his fists and various blows until the priest seemed about to give up his soul. He might indeed have done so had the physicians’ cupping vessels not come to his aid. Out of fear of the king, Eberulf had his lodging in the reception room of the blessed basilica. When the priest who kept the door key had closed the other doors and departed, girls and Eberulf’s other servants entered through the reception-room door, looked at the wall paintings, and examined the ornaments of the blessed tomb, which religious people considered highly criminal. When the priest learned this, he fixed bolts on the door and fitted bars inside. After dinner Eberulf, soaked in wine, noticed this while I was chanting psalms in the basilica at the beginning of the night for prayer. He entered in a fury and began to assail me with insults and curses, reproaching me among other things with wishing to separate him from the holy bishop’s fringes. Astonished at the madness that had seized the man, I tried to soothe him with gentle words. Unable to overcome his fury with soft speech, I resolved to be silent. Seeing me silent, he turned to the priest and vomited out many insults against him, attacking him with shameless words and me with various reproaches. Seeing that he was, so to speak, driven by a demon, I left the holy basilica and ended the scandalous vigil. What I found hardest to bear was that he had stirred up this quarrel without reverence for the saint, before the bishop’s very tomb.

In those days I saw a dream that I related to him in the holy basilica: “I dreamed that I was celebrating the solemn Mass in this basilica. When the altar with its offerings had been covered with a silk pall, I suddenly saw King Guntram enter and shout loudly, ‘Drag out the enemy of my family; tear the murderer away from God’s holy altar.’ Hearing this, I turned to you and said, ‘Wretched man, seize the altar pall that covers the sacred gifts so that you may not be cast out.’ You seized it but held it with a slack hand, not manfully. With outstretched arms I pressed my breast against the king’s, saying, ‘Do not drive this man from the holy basilica, or you will suffer mortal danger and the holy bishop will strike you with his power. Do not kill yourself with your own javelin; if you do, you will lose both present and eternal life.’ While the king resisted me, you released the pall and came behind me. I was deeply troubled by you. When you returned to the altar, you seized the pall but again let it go. While you held it feebly and I resisted the king vigorously, I awoke terrified, not knowing what the dream meant.”

When I had told him this, he said, “The dream you saw is true and agrees exactly with my own thoughts.” I asked, “What have your thoughts contemplated?” He answered, “I had resolved that if the king ordered me dragged from this place, I would hold the altar palls with one hand and, drawing my sword with the other, first kill you and then strike down as many clergy as I could find. After that it would be no injury for me to perish in death if I had taken vengeance on this saint’s clergy.” Hearing this in astonishment, I marveled that the devil was speaking through his mouth. He never had any fear of God. While he was free, his horses and cattle were let loose in the poor people’s crops and vineyards. If the owners whose labor they destroyed drove them off, Eberulf’s men immediately beat them. In the straits in which he found himself he often recalled that he had unjustly taken the blessed bishop’s property. The previous year he had induced a certain fickle citizen to bring an action against the church’s agents. Setting justice aside, he took away under the appearance of purchase property the Church had long possessed and gave that man a gold section of his own belt. He did many other perverse things until the end of his life, which I shall describe later.

### 23. Of the Jew slain with his companions

<a id="hist-07-en-023"></a>
[**[023]**](#hist-07-la-023) This year Armentarius the Jew came to Tours with one adherent of his own sect and two Christians to collect the bonds that Injuriosus, formerly vicarius, and Eunomius, formerly count, had deposited with him on account of public taxes. When he had called upon these men, he received a promise of repayment of the money lent with interest. They further told him, “If you come to my house, I will pay what is owed and honor you with other gifts as you deserve.” He went, was received by Injuriosus, and was seated at a banquet. When the feast was over and night approached, they moved from that place to another. It is said that Injuriosus’s men then killed the Jews and the two Christians and threw them into a well near his house. Their relatives heard what had happened and came to Tours. Receiving information from certain men, they found the well and drew out the bodies, although Injuriosus strongly denied being implicated in the affair. He afterward appeared in judgment. Since he vigorously denied the charge and they had no means of proving it, judgment was given that he should clear himself by oath. When they refused to accept this, a hearing was appointed in King Childebert’s presence. Neither the dead Jew’s money nor his bonds were found. Many people then said that the tribune Medard had been involved in the crime because he too had borrowed money from the Jew. Injuriosus came to the hearing before King Childebert and waited for three days until sunset. When the accusers did not come and no one charged him in the case, he returned home.

### 24. Of the plunder of the city of Poitiers

<a id="hist-07-en-024"></a>
[**[024]**](#hist-07-la-024) In the tenth year of King Childebert’s reign, King Guntram summoned the peoples of his kingdom and assembled a great army. The larger part, with the men of Orléans and Bourges, went to Poitiers because the Poitevins had broken the faith they had promised the king. They first sent an embassy to learn whether they would be received. Maroveus, the local bishop, received the messengers harshly. The army entered the territory and committed plunder, arson, and murder. Those returning with booty passed through the Touraine and treated in the same way people who had already given oaths, even burning churches and plundering whatever they could find. This happened repeatedly while the others were being returned unwillingly to the king. When the army drew nearer the city and the greater part of the region was seen to be devastated, the Poitevins sent messengers acknowledging themselves faithful to King Guntram. The soldiers were received within the city walls and then attacked the bishop, calling him faithless. Seeing himself hard pressed, he broke up a golden chalice from the sacred vessels, converted it into coin, and ransomed himself and the people.

### 25. Of the spoiling of Marileif

<a id="hist-07-en-025"></a>
[**[025]**](#hist-07-la-025) They also surrounded Marileif, who had been regarded as the chief physician in King Chilperic’s household, with great violence. Duke Gararic had already plundered him severely, and these men now stripped him again until no substance remained. They carried off his horses, gold, silver, and best valuables and subjected him to ecclesiastical servitude. His father’s service had been to attend the church mills, and his brothers, cousins, and other relatives were subject to the royal estates and bakery.

### 26. How Gundovald went about the cities

<a id="hist-07-en-026"></a>
[**[026]**](#hist-07-la-026) Gundovald wished to approach Poitiers but feared to do so because he had heard that an army was already being raised against him. In the cities that had belonged to King Sigibert he received oaths in King Childebert’s name; in the others that had belonged to Guntram or Chilperic they swore in his own name to keep faith with him. He then came to Angoulême, received oaths, rewarded the leading men, and proceeded to Périgueux. He left its bishop gravely injured because he had not received him honorably.

### 27. Of the injury to Bishop Magnulf

<a id="hist-07-en-027"></a>
[**[027]**](#hist-07-la-027) From there Gundovald went to Toulouse and sent messengers to Magnulf, bishop of the city, asking to be received by him. Remembering the earlier injury he had suffered through Sigulf, who had once wished to raise himself to the kingdom, Magnulf told his citizens, “I know that Guntram and his nephew are kings, but I do not know where this man comes from. Be prepared, therefore, and if Duke Desiderius wishes to bring this calamity upon me, let him perish in the same way as Sigulf. Let this be an example to everyone that no foreigner may dare violate the kingdom of the Franks.” While they resisted and prepared for war, Gundovald arrived with a great army. Seeing that they could not withstand him, they received him.

Afterward the bishop sat at dinner with Gundovald in the church house and said, “You assert that you are King Chlothar’s son, but I do not know whether that is true. Even if you can justify your undertaking, my mind finds it incredible.” Gundovald replied, “I am King Chlothar’s son, and I shall now receive my part of the kingdom. I will swiftly go to Paris and establish the seat of my kingdom there.” The bishop asked, “Is it true, then, that no one of the stock of the Frankish kings remains, if you accomplish what you say?” When Mummolus overheard these exchanges, he raised his hand and struck the bishop with blows, saying, “Are you not ashamed to answer so great a king like a degenerate fool?” When Desiderius learned what had been said on the bishop’s advice, he too was moved to anger and laid hands on him. Together they beat him with spear-shafts, fists, and heels, bound him with a rope, and condemned him to exile, taking away both his own property and the Church’s in full. Waddo, who was Queen Rigunthis’s mayor of the palace, joined them. The others who had accompanied him escaped by flight.

### 28. That the Army Advanced Farther

<a id="hist-07-en-028"></a>
[**[028]**](#hist-07-la-028) Afterward the army withdrew from Poitiers and proceeded farther after Gundovald. Many men of Tours followed him for the sake of gain, but when the Poitevins attacked them some were killed and most returned stripped. Those who had joined the army earlier went on together. The army reached the river Dordogne and began waiting to learn what would become of Gundovald. Duke Desiderius and Bladast, together with Waddo, Queen Rigunthis’s mayor of the palace, had already joined him, as I said above. Bishop Sagittarius and Mummolus were among his foremost adherents, Sagittarius having already received a promise of the episcopate of Toulouse.

### 29. Of the death of Eberulfus

<a id="hist-07-en-029"></a>
[**[029]**](#hist-07-la-029) While this was happening, King Guntram sent a man named Claudius, saying, “If you go and either kill Eberulf with the sword after driving him from the basilica or bind him in chains, I will enrich you with great gifts; but I strictly warn you not to injure the holy basilica.” Claudius, devoted to vanity and avarice, flew swiftly to Paris, for his wife came from the territory of Meaux. He began considering whether to see Queen Fredegund, thinking, “If I see her, I can obtain some gift from her. I know that she is an enemy of the man to whom I am being sent.” He approached her, immediately received great gifts, and elicited many promises that he would either kill Eberulf after dragging him from the basilica, deceive and bind him in chains, or slaughter him in the atrium itself.

Claudius returned to the fortress of Dun and induced the count to assign him three hundred men, ostensibly to guard the gates of Tours, so that with their help he could overwhelm Eberulf when he arrived. While the local count was assembling these men, Claudius proceeded to Tours. On the journey he began, as is the barbarian custom, to observe omens and say that they were unfavorable to him. He also asked many people whether blessed Martin’s power was still manifested against the perfidious, and whether vengeance followed immediately when anyone injured those who hoped in him. Leaving behind the men who were to come to his aid, as I have said, he approached the holy basilica. He immediately joined the unfortunate Eberulf and began giving oaths, swearing by everything sacred and by the power of the bishop present there that no one would be more faithful in Eberulf’s cause or better able to plead it with the king. The wretched man had privately resolved, “Unless I deceive him by perjury, I will not overcome him.” When Eberulf saw Claudius making such promises with oaths in the basilica itself and throughout the porticoes and each venerable part of the atrium, the wretch believed the perjurer.

The next day, while I was staying at a villa about thirty miles from the city, Eberulf was invited with Claudius and other citizens to a banquet at the holy basilica. Claudius wished to strike him there with a sword, provided Eberulf’s servants stood far enough away, but Eberulf, vain as he was, never noticed. When the banquet ended, Eberulf and Claudius began walking together through the atrium of the basilica house, repeatedly promising each other fidelity and affection with oaths. As they spoke, Claudius said to Eberulf, “I would enjoy taking a drink at your lodging, if you have wines mixed with spices, or if your energy would provide a libation of stronger wine.” Eberulf gladly answered that he had some: “You will find everything you wish at my lodging, provided only that my lord deigns to enter the hut where I dwell.” He sent one servant after another to obtain the stronger wines, namely Laticinian and Gazitinian. When Claudius saw Eberulf left alone by the servants, he raised his hand toward the basilica and said, “Most blessed Martin, let me soon see my wife and parents.” The unfortunate man was in a dilemma: he intended to kill Eberulf in the atrium, yet feared the holy bishop’s power.

Then one of Claudius’s stronger servants seized Eberulf from behind and held him in powerful arms, preparing to expose his chest for slaughter. Claudius drew a sword from his belt and approached him. But Eberulf too, though held, prepared a weapon drawn from his belt to strike. Claudius raised his right hand and drove his knife into Eberulf’s chest, while Eberulf for his part fixed a dagger beneath Claudius’s armpit. Eberulf then drew it back and, aiming another blow, cut off Claudius’s thumb. Claudius’s servants gathered at this and wounded Eberulf with various sword blows. He slipped from their hands and tried to flee, already exhausted. Drawing their swords, they struck his head most violently; his brain poured out, and he fell dead. He did not deserve to be saved by the saint whom he had never understood faithfully to invoke.

Terrified, Claudius sought the abbot’s little cell, wishing to be protected by the abbot although he had not known how to show reverence to the abbot’s patron. As he sat there he said, “An immense crime has been committed, and unless you help me, I will perish.” While he spoke, Eberulf’s servants rushed in with swords and lances. Finding the door barred, they broke the cell’s windows and hurled spears through the openings in the wall, piercing Claudius and leaving him half alive. His attendants hid behind doors and under beds. The abbot was seized by two clerics and with difficulty rescued alive from among the sword-blades. The doors were opened, and the crowd of swordsmen entered. Some of the registered poor and other paupers also tried to overthrow the cell’s roof because of the crime committed. Demoniacs and various needy people came with stones and clubs to avenge the violence done to the basilica, indignant that such things, which had never happened before, had been perpetrated there. In short, the fugitives were dragged from their hiding places and cruelly slaughtered, and the cell’s pavement was stained with gore. After they were killed, they were dragged outside and left naked on the cold ground. The killers seized plunder and escaped by flight the following night. God’s vengeance immediately fell upon those who had polluted the blessed atrium with human blood. Nor was Eberulf’s crime considered small, since the blessed bishop had allowed him to suffer such things. The king was greatly angered but became calm when the circumstances were known. He granted the unfortunate man’s remaining movable and immovable property to his own faithful servants. They left Eberulf’s wife, severely stripped, in the holy basilica. The nearest relatives carried away the bodies of Claudius and the others and buried them in their own region.

### 30. Of Gundovald’s Envoys

<a id="hist-07-en-030"></a>
[**[030]**](#hist-07-la-030) Gundovald sent two clerics as envoys to his friends. One, an abbot of the city of Cahors, hid the letters he had received beneath the wax in a hollowed wooden tablet. He was seized by King Guntram’s men, the letters were found, and he was brought before the king. After being most severely beaten, he was thrust into custody.

### 31. Of the relics of Saint Sergius the martyr

<a id="hist-07-en-031"></a>
[**[031]**](#hist-07-la-031) At that time Gundovald was in Bordeaux and greatly loved by Bishop Berthram. While inquiring what assistance might support his cause, he was told that a certain king in the East had taken a thumb of the holy martyr Sergius and fastened it to the right arm of his own body. When necessity required him to repel enemies, he trusted in this help: wherever he raised the forearm of his right arm, a multitude of enemies immediately fled as though overwhelmed by the martyr’s power. Hearing this, Gundovald began to inquire carefully whether anyone in that region had been privileged to obtain relics of Saint Sergius.

Meanwhile Bishop Berthram, out of enmity and greed for his property, betrayed the merchant Eufron, whom he had once tonsured against his will. Eufron had escaped by moving to another city and returned when his hair had grown. The bishop said, “There is a certain Syrian here named Eufron who made a church of his house and placed this saint’s relics in it. With the martyr’s assistance he has seen very many signs from them. When the city of Bordeaux was once burning in a great conflagration, this house, though surrounded by flames, was not burned at all.”

Immediately Mummolus went at a run with Bishop Berthram to the Syrian’s house. They surrounded the man and ordered the holy pledges to be shown to them. He refused. Thinking that this snare had been prepared out of malice, he said, “Do not weary an old man or injure the saint. Take a hundred gold pieces from me and depart.” When Mummolus insisted on seeing the holy relics, Eufron offered two hundred gold pieces, but could not even then induce him to leave unless the pledges themselves were displayed. Mummolus ordered a ladder raised against the wall—the relics were enclosed in a little casket high on the wall opposite the altar—and directed his deacon to climb. As the deacon ascended the rungs and seized the casket, he was so shaken by trembling that it seemed he would never return to the ground alive. Nevertheless, he took the casket hanging on the wall and brought it down. Mummolus examined it and found a bone from the saint’s finger, which he did not fear to strike with a knife. He laid the knife on it and struck the knife with another object. After many blows the bone could scarcely be broken; when the little bone was divided into three parts, the pieces flew in different directions. I believe this deed was not acceptable to the martyr.

While Eufron wept bitterly, they all prostrated themselves in prayer, begging God to deign to reveal what had been taken from human eyes. After the prayer the particles were found. Mummolus took one and departed, but not, I believe, with the martyr’s favor, as the sequel showed.

While they were staying in Bordeaux, they ordered the priest Faustianus to be ordained bishop of Agen. The bishop of Agen had recently died, and Nicetius, count of that place and brother of Rusticus, bishop of Vic, had obtained a command from Chilperic that, after receiving the tonsure, he should be given to that city as bishop. Gundovald, seeking to annul Chilperic’s decrees, summoned the bishops and ordered Faustianus to be consecrated. Bishop Berthram, the metropolitan, guarding against future consequences, assigned the consecration to Palladius of Saintes. Berthram’s eyes were also afflicted by soreness at the time. Orestes, bishop of Bazas, was present at this ordination but later denied it before the king.

### 32. Of other legates of Gundovald

<a id="hist-07-en-032"></a>
[**[032]**](#hist-07-la-032) Afterward Gundovald again sent two envoys to the king with consecrated staffs according to Frankish custom, so that no one would harm them and they might present their message and return with an answer. But they incautiously disclosed their demands to many people before seeing the king. Word immediately reached the king, and they were brought before him bound in chains. Not daring to deny what they sought, to whom they had been sent, or by whom, they said, “Gundovald, who recently came from the East and claims to be the son of your father, King Chlothar, sent me so that he may receive the portion of the kingdom due to him. If you do not return it, know that he will come into these parts with an army. All the strongest men of the region beyond the Dordogne belonging to Gaul have joined him. He says, ‘God will judge when we meet on the level ground of a single field whether I am Chlothar’s son or not.’”

Inflamed with fury, the king ordered them stretched on the rack and severely beaten so that they might more clearly prove whether their statements were true and the force of torture might extort any deceit still hidden in their breasts. As their torments increased, they said that Gundovald’s niece, King Chilperic’s daughter, had been condemned to exile with Magnulf, bishop of Toulouse; that Gundovald had taken all the treasures; and that he had been invited by all King Childebert’s leading men, especially when Guntram Boso went to Constantinople years earlier.

### 33. How Childebert came to his uncle Guntram

<a id="hist-07-en-033"></a>
[**[033]**](#hist-07-la-033) After the men had been beaten and thrust into prison, the king ordered his nephew Childebert to be summoned, so that together they might hear what these men had to say. When the two kings were assembled and questioned the men, they repeated in the presence of both rulers what King Guntram alone had previously heard. They also steadfastly maintained that, as we have already said, this whole affair was known to all the leading men in King Childebert’s kingdom. For this reason some of Childebert’s chief men who were thought to have participated in the plot were then afraid to leave the assembly. Afterwards King Guntram placed a spear in King Childebert’s hand and said: ‘This is the sign that I have handed over my entire kingdom to you. Go now, and bring all my cities under the dominion of your authority as though they were your own. For, because of my sins, no one of my stock remains except you alone, my brother’s son. You shall therefore succeed as heir to my whole kingdom, and all others shall be excluded from the inheritance.’ Then, leaving everyone else, he took the boy aside and spoke with him privately, first adjuring him most earnestly not to reveal their secret conversation to anyone. He told him whom he should retain as counsellors and whom he should exclude from his confidence, whom he should trust and whom avoid, whom he should honour with gifts and whom deprive of office. Among other things, he charged him on no account to trust or retain Bishop Egidius, who had always been his enemy, because he had repeatedly perjured himself against both Childebert and his father. Then, when they had assembled for a banquet, King Guntram addressed the whole army, saying: ‘See, my men, my son Childebert has now become a grown man. See to it that you no longer treat him as a little child. Abandon now the perversity and presumption which you have practised, for he is the king whom you must henceforth serve.’ After speaking these and similar words, they feasted and made merry for three days, enriched one another with many gifts, and departed in peace. King Guntram then restored to Childebert everything that his father Sigibert had possessed, adjuring him not to go to his mother, lest she be given an opportunity to write to Gundovald or receive letters from him.

### 34. How Gundovald went to Comminges

<a id="hist-07-en-034"></a>
[**[034]**](#hist-07-la-034) When Gundovald heard that the army was approaching him, deserted by Duke Desiderius, he crossed the Garonne with Bishop Sagittarius, Dukes Mummolus and Bladast, and Waddo, making for Comminges. This city stands on the summit of a mountain and is not joined to any other height. A great spring bursts forth at the foot of the mountain and is enclosed by a very strong tower; the townspeople descend to it from the city through a tunnel and draw water in secret. Gundovald entered this city at the beginning of Lent and addressed the citizens, saying: ‘Know that I have been chosen king by all who are in Childebert’s kingdom, and that I have no small support with me. But since my brother King Guntram has raised an immense army against me, you must store food and all your household goods within the protection of the walls, so that, while divine goodness increases my support, you may not perish from want.’ They believed him, placed whatever they possessed in the city, and prepared to resist. At that time King Guntram sent a letter to Gundovald in Queen Brunhild’s name, instructing him to dismiss his army and order it to return home, while he himself withdrew farther away to spend the winter at Bordeaux. Guntram had written this deceitfully in order to learn more fully what Gundovald was doing. While Gundovald remained at Comminges, he said to the inhabitants: ‘The army is now approaching; go out to resist it.’ When they went out, Gundovald’s men seized and closed the gates, shutting out the people together with the bishop of the place, and brought everything they could find in the city under their own control. Such an abundance of grain and wine was found there that, if they had held out courageously, they would not have lacked provisions for many years.

### 35. Of the basilica of Saint Vincent the martyr of Agen laid waste

<a id="hist-07-en-035"></a>
[**[035]**](#hist-07-la-035) King Guntram’s commanders had heard at that time that Gundovald had halted beyond the Garonne with a vast multitude of armed men, and that he still retained the treasures which Rigunthis had brought. Making an attack, they swam across the Garonne with their horses, though some of the army were drowned in the river. The rest came ashore and, searching for Gundovald, found camels laden with an enormous weight of gold and silver, as well as horses which he had abandoned exhausted along the roads. Hearing next that Gundovald was staying within the walls of Comminges, they left their wagons and various baggage with the less able men, while the stronger men resolved to pursue him across the Garonne as they had already done. As they hastened on, they came to the basilica of Saint Vincent within the territory of Agen, where the martyr himself is said to have completed his contest for Christ’s name, and found it filled with the inhabitants’ various treasures. The people had hoped that Christians would not violate the basilica of so great a martyr, and its doors had been barred with the greatest care. Without delay the army approached and, when it could not open the doors of the church, set them on fire. Once the doors had been consumed, the soldiers carried off all the property and furnishings they could find, together with the sacred vessels. But divine vengeance struck many of them there. The hands of several were supernaturally burned and emitted a great smoke, as usually rises from a fire. Some were seized by a demon and raved in madness, crying out upon the martyr. Many who had kept apart from the tumult wounded themselves with their own javelins. The rest of the crowd went forward in great fear. What more need be said? They assembled at Comminges—for so I have called the city—and the whole army pitched camp on the plain outside the city, remaining there with their tents spread out. The entire surrounding region was devastated; but some soldiers who strayed farther afield, driven by the sharper goad of avarice, were killed by the inhabitants.

### 36. Of the conference of Gundovald with the army

<a id="hist-07-en-036"></a>
[**[036]**](#hist-07-la-036) Many of the soldiers climbed the hill and repeatedly spoke with Gundovald, heaping abuse upon him and saying: ‘Are you that painter who, in King Chlothar’s time, used to paint the walls and ceilings of oratories? Are you the man whom the inhabitants of Gaul so often called Ballomer? Are you the man who was repeatedly tonsured and exiled by the Frankish kings because of these same pretensions which you now put forward? Tell us, most wretched of men, who brought you to this region. Who gave you such audacity that you dared to enter the territory of our lords and kings? If anyone summoned you, declare it loudly. See, death lies exposed before your eyes; see the very pit of destruction for which you have so long searched and into which you will be cast headlong. Tell the truth about your accomplices, and reveal who invited you.’ Hearing this, Gundovald stood nearer above the gate and said: ‘No one is unaware that my father Chlothar hated me, and everyone knows that I was tonsured by him and afterwards by my brothers. This affair brought me into association with Narses, the commander in Italy. There I took a wife and begot two sons. When she died, I took my children with me and went to Constantinople. The emperors received me most kindly, and I lived there until this time. Some years ago, when Guntram Boso went to Constantinople, I anxiously inquired carefully into my brothers’ affairs and learned that our line had been greatly reduced. Only Kings Childebert and Guntram remained of our stock—that is, my brother and my brother’s son. King Chilperic’s sons had died with him, leaving only one small child. My brother Guntram had no sons, and our nephew Childebert was not yet strong. Then Guntram Boso, having carefully explained all this to me, invited me, saying: “Come, for all the leading men of King Childebert’s kingdom invite you, and no one dares utter a word against you. I all know that you are Chlothar’s son, and no one remains in Gaul who can rule that kingdom unless you come.” I gave him many gifts and received oaths from him at twelve holy places, so that I might enter this kingdom in safety. I came to Marseilles, where the bishop received me with the greatest kindness, for he had letters from the leading men of my nephew’s kingdom. From there I went to Avignon in accordance with the wishes of the patrician Mummolus. Guntram, however, forgot his oath and promise, stole my treasures, and brought them under his own control. Now recognize that I am king, just as my brother Guntram is. But if your minds are so inflamed with hatred of me, let me be conducted to your king; if he acknowledges me as his brother, let him do what he wishes. If you will not agree even to this, at least permit me to return whence I came. I will depart and do no injury to anyone. But so that you may know that what I say is true, question Radegund of Poitiers and Ingitrude of Tours. They will affirm to you that my words are certain.’ As he said this, many pursued his words with abuse and reproaches.

### 37. Of the war against the city

<a id="hist-07-en-037"></a>
[**[037]**](#hist-07-la-037) The fifteenth day of the siege had dawned, and Leudegisel was preparing new engines for destroying the city: wagons fitted with battering rams and covered with wicker frames and planks, beneath which the army could advance to demolish the walls. But when they approached, they were so overwhelmed with stones that all who came near the wall fell. The defenders hurled down barrels filled with burning pitch and fat, and threw others filled with stones upon them. When night put an end to the fighting, the army returned to camp. Chariulf, a very rich and powerful man, was also with Gundovald, and the city was well stocked from his storehouses and granaries; the besieged lived chiefly upon his supplies. Bladast, seeing what was happening and fearing that Leudegisel would gain the victory and put them to death, set fire to a building belonging to the church. While those shut inside rushed to extinguish the blaze, he slipped away and escaped. At daybreak the army again rose for battle and made bundles of brushwood, intending to fill a deep ravine on the eastern side; but this device accomplished nothing. Bishop Sagittarius frequently went around the walls under arms and often threw stones at the enemy with his own hand.

### 38. Of the death of Gundovald

<a id="hist-07-en-038"></a>
[**[038]**](#hist-07-la-038) When the besiegers saw that they were accomplishing nothing, they sent secret messengers to Mummolus, saying: ‘Acknowledge your lord and at last abandon this perversity. What madness possesses you, that you submit to an unknown man? Your wife and sons have already been taken captive, and your sons have, I believe, already been killed. Where are you rushing, and what do you await, except your own destruction?’ Receiving this message, Mummolus said: ‘As I see, my kingdom has reached its end and its power is falling. One thing remains: if I learn that my life will be spared, I can relieve you of much labour.’ When the messengers departed, Bishop Sagittarius went with Mummolus, Chariulf, and Waddo to a church, where they swore that, if they were assured their lives would be spared, they would abandon their friendship with Gundovald and hand him over to the enemy. The messengers returned and again promised them safety of life. Mummolus said: ‘Let only this be done: I will deliver him into your hands, and, acknowledging the king as my lord, I will hasten into his presence.’ They promised that, if he fulfilled this, they would receive him in friendship and, if they could not obtain his pardon from the king, would place him in a church so that he would not be punished with death. Having made these promises with oaths, they departed. Mummolus then went with Bishop Sagittarius and Waddo to Gundovald and said: ‘You yourself know the oaths of fidelity which I swore to you while you were present. Now accept salutary counsel: leave this city and present yourself to your brother, as you have often requested. I have spoken with these men, and they say that the king does not wish to destroy your support, since so little of your family remains.’ Perceiving their deception, Gundovald burst into tears and said: ‘It was at your invitation that I was brought to this land of Gaul. Of my treasures, which contain an immense weight of silver, gold, and various precious objects, part is retained at Avignon and part was seized by Guntram Boso. Relying entirely upon God’s help, I placed all my hope in you, entrusted my counsel to you, and always desired to reign through you. Now God will judge between us if you have spoken any falsehood to me; let Him judge my cause.’ When he had spoken, Mummolus replied: ‘I am saying nothing deceitful to you. See, very brave men are standing at the gate awaiting your arrival. Now take off my golden belt which you are wearing, lest you appear to go forth in ostentation; gird on your own sword and restore mine to me.’ Gundovald said: ‘I do not accept these words as innocent, since you seek to take from me what I have until now used as tokens of your friendship.’ But Mummolus declared with an oath that no harm would be done to him. They therefore went out of the gate, and Gundovald was received by Ollo, count of Bourges, and Boso. Mummolus returned into the city with his followers and barred the gate most securely. When Gundovald saw that he had been delivered into his enemies’ hands, he raised his hands and eyes to heaven and said: ‘Eternal Judge and true Avenger of the innocent, O God, from whom all justice proceeds, who takes no pleasure in falsehood, and in whom there is no deceit or crafty malice, I commit my cause to You, praying that You may swiftly take vengeance upon those who have delivered me, an innocent man, into the hands of my enemies.’ Having said this and signed himself with the Lord’s cross, he began to go with the men named above. When they had gone some distance from the gate, where the whole valley around the city falls away precipitously, he was pushed by Ollo and fell. Ollo cried: ‘Behold your Ballomer, who calls himself the king’s brother and son!’ He thrust a lance at him and tried to transfix him, but it was turned aside by the rings of his mail and did him no harm. Then, as Gundovald struggled to rise and climb back up the hill, Boso hurled a stone and struck his head. He fell and died. The whole crowd came, fixed their lances in him, bound his feet with a rope, and dragged him through all the army camps. They tore out his hair and beard and left him unburied in the place where he had been killed. During the following night the leading men secretly carried off all the treasures they could find in the city, together with the church vessels. In the morning the gates were opened and the army sent in. They put all the people shut inside to the sword, slaughtering the Lord’s priests and their ministers at the very altars of the churches. After they had killed everyone, so that not a male was left, they burned the whole city with its churches and other buildings, leaving nothing there but bare ground.

### 39. Of the death of Bishop Sagittarius and of Mummolus

<a id="hist-07-en-039"></a>
[**[039]**](#hist-07-la-039) Leudegisel then returned to camp with Mummolus, Sagittarius, Chariulf, and Waddo, and secretly sent messengers to the king to ask what he wished to be done with them. The king ordered them to be executed. Waddo and Chariulf then left them, leaving their sons as hostages. When word of their intended execution arrived and Mummolus learned of it, he girded on his arms and went to Leudegisel’s hut. Seeing him, Leudegisel said: ‘Why do you come thus, as though fleeing?’ Mummolus answered: ‘As I see, none of the promised faith is being kept, for I perceive that I am faced with death.’ Leudegisel said: ‘I will go outside and calm everything.’ When he went out, the house was immediately surrounded at his command so that Mummolus might be killed. Mummolus resisted the attackers for a very long time and reached the doorway; as he was coming out, two men struck him in both sides with lances. He fell and died. Seeing this, the bishop was stricken with terror, and one of those standing nearby said: ‘See with your own eyes, bishop, what is happening. Cover your head so that you will not be recognized, make for the forest, and hide for a little while; when their anger has passed, you may escape.’ He accepted the advice and tried to flee with his head covered, but a man drew his sword and cut off his head together with the hood. Then everyone returned home, committing great plunder and murder along the way. In those days Fredegund sent Chuppa to Toulouse to rescue her daughter from there by whatever means he could. Many said that he had been sent for the additional purpose that, if he found Gundovald alive, he might entice him with many promises and bring him to her. Unable to do this, Chuppa took Rigunthis and brought her back from that place, not without great humiliation and disgrace.

### 40. Of the treasures of Mummolus

<a id="hist-07-en-040"></a>
[**[040]**](#hist-07-la-040) Duke Leudegisel then came to the king with all the treasures mentioned above, which the king afterwards distributed to the poor and the churches. The king seized Mummolus’s wife and began to question her about what had become of the treasures which these men had collected. Knowing that her husband had been killed and that all their pride had utterly collapsed into the dust, she revealed everything. She said that much gold and silver still remained at Avignon which had not come to the king’s attention. The king immediately sent men to bring it back, accompanied by a servant whom Mummolus had greatly trusted and to whom he had entrusted these things. They went and took everything that had been left in the city. It is said that there were two hundred and fifty talents of silver and more than thirty of gold, and that Mummolus had taken this from an ancient treasure which he had discovered. The king divided it with his nephew King Childebert and gave most of his own share to the poor, leaving the woman nothing beyond what she had inherited from her parents.

### 41. Of the Giant

<a id="hist-07-en-041"></a>
[**[041]**](#hist-07-la-041) At that time a member of Mummolus’s household, a man of immense stature, was also brought before the king. He was of such great height that he was thought to stand two or three feet above the tallest men. He was a carpenter and died not long afterwards.

### 42. Of the power of Saint Martin

<a id="hist-07-en-042"></a>
[**[042]**](#hist-07-la-042) Afterwards the judges issued an edict that those who had been slow to join this expedition should be punished. The count of Bourges also sent his servants to despoil men of this kind in a house of blessed Martin situated within that territory. But the steward of the house began to resist vigorously, saying: ‘These are Saint Martin’s men. Do them no injury, for they have not been accustomed to go on such expeditions.’ They replied: ‘Your Martin, whom you always invoke vainly in these affairs, is nothing to us. Both you and they shall pay the penalty for having neglected the king’s command.’ Saying this, he entered the courtyard of the house. He was immediately struck by pain, fell, and became gravely ill. Turning to the steward, he said in a tearful voice: ‘I beg you to make the sign of the Lord’s cross over me and invoke blessed Martin’s name. I now know that his power is great. For as I entered the courtyard, I saw an old man holding a tree in his hand, which soon spread its branches and covered the whole courtyard. One of its branches touched me, and I fell, overwhelmed by its blow.’ Beckoning to his men, he asked to be carried out of the courtyard. Once outside, he began to invoke blessed Martin’s name more earnestly, and thereafter he recovered and was healed.

### 43. Of Desiderius and Waddo

<a id="hist-07-en-043"></a>
[**[043]**](#hist-07-la-043) Desiderius protected himself and his property within the fortifications of the camp. Waddo, Rigunthis’s major-domo, went over to Queen Brunhild; she received him and dismissed him with gifts and favour. Chariulf sought refuge in Saint Martin’s basilica.

### 44. Of the woman with the pythonic spirit

<a id="hist-07-en-044"></a>
[**[044]**](#hist-07-la-044) At that time there was a woman who possessed a spirit of divination and brought her masters great profit by her prophecies. She advanced so far in their favour that she was freed by them and allowed to follow her own wishes. Whenever anyone suffered a theft or some other loss, she would at once declare where the thief had gone, to whom he had handed over the property, or what he had done with it. She accumulated gold and silver daily and went about in finery, so that the people thought there was something divine in her. When these things were reported to Ageric, bishop of Verdun, he sent men to arrest her. She was seized and brought before him, and, in accordance with what I read in the Acts of the Apostles, he recognized that an unclean spirit of divination was in her. While he pronounced an exorcism over her and anointed her forehead with holy oil, the demon cried out and revealed to the bishop what it was. But since it was not expelled from the woman by him, she was permitted to depart. Seeing that she could not remain in that place, she went to Queen Fredegund and hid there.

### 45. Of the famine of the present year

<a id="hist-07-en-045"></a>
[**[045]**](#hist-07-la-045) A great famine afflicted almost the whole of Gaul this year. Many people made bread from grape seeds, hazel flowers, or, in some cases, dried fern roots ground into powder and mixed with a little flour. Many cut down the green growth among the standing grain and did the same. There were also many who had no flour at all; they gathered and ate various herbs, became swollen, and died. Very many wasted away from starvation and perished. Merchants then cruelly despoiled the people, scarcely selling a measure of grain or half a measure of wine for a triens. The poor submitted themselves to servitude in order to obtain even the smallest amount of food.

### 46. Of the death of Christoforus

<a id="hist-07-en-046"></a>
[**[046]**](#hist-07-la-046) In those days the merchant Christopher went to Orléans, having heard that a great quantity of wine had been brought there. He bought wine, loaded it into boats, received a large sum of money from his father-in-law, and travelled on horseback with two Saxon servants. The servants had long hated their master and had often run away because they were frequently and severely beaten. When they came into a forest, their master rode ahead, and one servant hurled a lance with great force and transfixed him. As he fell, the other hacked at his head with a framea. Cut to pieces by both of them, he was left lifeless. They took the money and escaped. After Christopher’s brother had buried his body, he sent his men after the servants. They captured the younger, while the elder escaped with the money. As the captors were returning, they left the prisoner rather loosely bound; he seized a lance and killed one of the men leading him. The others brought him to Tours, where he was subjected to various tortures, mutilated, and hung lifeless on a gibbet.

### 47. Of the civil war among the citizens of Tours

<a id="hist-07-en-047"></a>
[**[047]**](#hist-07-la-047) A serious civil feud then arose among the citizens of Tours. Sichar, the son of the late John, was celebrating the feast of the Lord’s Nativity with Austrigisel and other local people in the village of Manthelan, when the priest of the place sent a servant to invite some men to come to his house for a drink. When the servant arrived, one of those invited did not hesitate to draw his sword and strike him. He immediately fell dead. Sichar, who was on friendly terms with the priest, heard that his servant had been killed; he took up arms and went to the church to await Austrigisel. Hearing this, Austrigisel gathered his weapons and set out against him. Both sides joined battle, and Sichar was rescued by the clergy and fled to his estate, leaving four wounded servants, together with silver and clothing, in the priest’s house. After his flight, Austrigisel attacked again, killed the servants, and carried off the gold, silver, and other property. The parties then came before the citizens’ tribunal, and it was decided that Austrigisel, who had committed murder and, after killing the servants, had seized property without a hearing, should be condemned according to law. An agreement was made, but within a few days Sichar heard that the property which Austrigisel had seized was being kept by Auno, his son, and his brother Eberulf. Disregarding the settlement and joining Audinus, he raised a disturbance and attacked them at night with armed men. Breaking into the lodging where they slept, he killed the father, brother, and son, killed the servants, and carried off their property and cattle. Hearing of this, I was greatly disturbed and, with the judge, sent envoys to them, asking them to come before me so that, after a hearing, they might depart in peace and the quarrel spread no farther. When they came and the citizens had assembled, I said: ‘Do not continue in these crimes, my men, lest the evil spread farther. I have lost sons of the Church; let us now fear that we may lose others in this strife. Be peaceful, I beg you; let him who has done evil make compensation without destroying friendship, so that you may be peaceful sons worthy to receive the kingdom of God by the Lord’s gift. For He Himself says: “Blessed are the peacemakers, for they shall be called the sons of God.” If the guilty man has insufficient means, he shall be redeemed with the Church’s silver; meanwhile, let no man’s soul perish.’ Saying this, I offered the Church’s money; but Chramnesind’s party, which sought vengeance for the death of his father, brother, and uncle, would not accept it. When they departed, Sichar prepared to go to the king and set out for Poitiers to see his wife. While urging a servant to do his work and beating him with a raised stick, the servant drew the sword at his belt and did not hesitate to wound his master. Sichar fell to the ground; his friends ran up, seized the servant, cruelly beat him, cut off his hands and feet, and condemned him to the gibbet. Meanwhile a report reached Tours that Sichar had died. When Chramnesind heard this, he summoned his relatives and friends and hastened to Sichar’s house. He plundered it, killed several servants, burned all the houses belonging both to Sichar and to the others who shared that estate, and carried off the cattle and everything movable. The judge then brought the parties to the city, where they pleaded their causes. The judges decided that the party which had refused the earlier compensation and burned the houses should forfeit half the sum awarded to it—contrary to the law, but in order to restore peace—and that Sichar should pay the other half of the compensation. The Church supplied the money which they had awarded; Sichar paid the compensation, security was given, and the parties swore mutual oaths that neither would ever again trouble the other. Thus the dispute came to an end.

*INCIPIT LIBER VII.*

## 1. De obitu sancti Salvii episcopi.

<a id="hist-07-la-001"></a>
[**[001]**](#hist-07-en-001) Licet sit studium historiam prosequi, quam priorum librorum ordo reliquid, tamen prius aliqua de beati Salvii obitu exposcit loqui devotio, qui hoc anno obisse probatur. Hic enim, ut ipse referre erat solitus, diu in habitu saeculari commoratus, cum iudicibus saeculi mundialis causas est exsecutus; numquam tamen se in his concupiscentiis oblegans, quibus adoliscentum animus solitus est inplicari0. Iam cum divini spiramenti odor interna viscerum attigisset, relicta saeculari militia, monastyrio expetivit; intellexitque vir iam tunc divinitati deditus, melius esse uti paupertatem cum Dei timore quam saeculi pereuntis lucra sectari. In quo monastyrio diu sub regula a patribus instituta versatus est. Iam vero cum in robore maiori tam intellectus quam aetatis evectus esset, defuncto abbate, qui huic monasterio praeerat, alendi gregis suscepit officium; et qui se magis fratribus publicum pro correctione reddere debuerat, fit, adsumpto honore, remutior. Ilico sibi secretiorem cellolam quaerit; nam in priore, ut ipse adserebat, amplius quam novem vicibus, nimia excesus abstinentia, pellem corporis demutavit. Denique accepto honore, cum in hac contentus parsimonia orationi et lectioni vacaret, illud plerumque revolvebat, melius sibi fieri, si esset inter monachos occultus, quam nomen acciperit abbatis in populos. Quid plura? Includitur vale dicens fratribus sibique ipsis vale dicentibus. In qua inclusione in omni abstinentia magis, quam prius egerat, commoratur, studens pro caritatis obsequio, ut, cum quisque venisset extraneus, et orationem tribuerit et eoglogias gratiam plenissimam ministraret; quae multis infirmis plerumque salutem integram detulerunt. Quodam autem tempore, febre nimia exaustus, hanillus iacebat in lectulo, et ecce! subito magno lumine cellola clarificata contremuit. Ad ille, extensis ad caelum manibus, cum gratiarum actione spiritum exalavit. Mixto quoque ululatu monachi cum ipsius genetrice corpus defuncti extrahunt, aqua diluunt, vestimentis induunt et feretro superponunt, adque in psallentio fletuque labentem exegunt noctem. Mane autem facto, funeris officio praeparato, corpus movere coepit in feretro. Et ecce! malis robiscentibus, vir, quasi de gram somno suscitatus, excutitur, apertisque oculis, manibus elevatis, ait: 'O Domine misericors, quid fecisti mihi, ut me in hunc tenebrosum mundanae habitationis locum redire permitteris, cum mihi melior esset in caelo tua misericordia quam istius mundi vita nequissima?' Stupentibus autem suis et interrogantibus, quid fuerit tale prodigium, nihil interrogantibus ille respondit. Surgens autem de feretro, nihil mali sentiens de incommodo quod laboraverat, triduo absque cibi ac poculi perstetit alimento. Die autem tertio convocatis monachis et matrem, ait: 'Audite, o dilectissimi, et intellegite, quia nihil est, quod cernitis in hoc mundo; sed sunt iuxta id quae Salomon propheta cecinit: Omnia vanitas. Felix est enim qui ea agere potest in saeculo, ut gloriam Dei cernere mereatur in caelo'. Et cum haec dicerit, dubitare coepit, utrum loqueretur amplius an sileret. Quo tacente, inplicitus fratrum praecibus, ut quid vidisset exponerit, ait: 'Cum me ante hos quattuor dies, contremiscente cellola, exanimem vidistis, adpraehensus a duobus angelis in caelorum excelsa sublatus sum, ita ut non solum hunc squalidum saeculum, verum etiam solem ac lunam, nubes et sidera sub pedibus habere potarem. Deinde per portam luce ista clariorem introductus sum in illud habitaculum, in quo omne pavimentum erat quasi aurum argentumque renitens, lux ineffabilis, amplitudo inenarrabilis; quam ita multitudo promiscui sexus obtexerat, ut longitudo ac latitudo catervae prorsus pervidere non possit. Cumque nobis via inter conpraementes ab his qui praecedebant angelis pararetur, pervenimus ad locum, quem iam de longinquo contemplabamus; in quo superpendebat nubs omne luce lucidior, in quo non sol, non luna, non astrum cerni poterat, sed super his omnibus naturali luce splendidius effulgebat, et vox procedebat e nubi, tamquam vox aquarum multarum. Ibi etiam me peccatori humiliter salutabant viri in veste sacerdotali ac saeculari; quos mihi qui praecedebant enarraverunt esse martyres ac confessores, quos hic summo excolemus famulatu. Stans igitur in loco in quo iussus sum, operuit me odor nimiae suavitatis, ita ut, ab hac suavitate refectus, nullum adhuc cybum potumque desiderarem. Et audivi vocem dicentem: „Revertatur hic in saeculo, quoniam necessarius est aeclesiis nostris". Vox enim audiebatur; nam qui loqueretur, paenitus cerni non poterat. Et ego prostratus super pavimentum cum fletu dicebam: „Heu, heu, Domine, quur mihi haec ostendisti, si ab his frustrandus eram! Ecce hodie eiecis me a facie tua, ut revertar ad saeculum fragilem et hic ultra redire non valeam. Ne, queso, Domine, auferas misericordiam tuam a me, sed depraecor, ut permittas me hic habitare, ne illic decidens peream". Et ait vox, quae loquebatur mihi: „Vade in pace. Ego enim sum custus tuus, donec reducam te in hoc loco". Tunc relictus a comitibus meis, discedens cum fletu, per portam quam ingressus fueram hic sum regressus'. Haec eo loquente, stupentibus cunctis qui aderant, coepit iterum sanctus Dei cum lacrimis dicere: 'Vae mihi, quia talem misterium ausus sum revelare. Ecce enim odor suavitatis, quam de loco sancto hauseram, et in quo per hoc triduum sine ullo cybo potuque sustentatus sum, recessit a me. Sed et lingua mea gravibus est operta vulneribus et ita tumefacta, ut omne os meum videatur implere. Et scio, quia non fuit bene placitum domino Deo meo, ut haec arcana vulgarentur. Sed tu nosti, Domine, quia in simplicitate cordis haec feci, non in iactantia mentis. Sed, quaeso, indulgeas et non me derelinquas iuxta pollicitationem tuam'. Et haec dicens siluit et accepit cybum potumque. Ego vero haec scribens vereor, ne alicui legenti sit incredibile, iuxta id quod Salustius historiam scribens ait: Ubi de virtute adque gloriam bonorum memores, quae sibi quisque facilia factu putat, aequo animo accepit; supra ea veluti ficta pro falsis ducit. Nam testor Deum omnipotentem, quia ab ipsius ore omnia quae rettuli audita cognovi. Post multum vero tempus ipse vir beatus a cellola sua extractus, ad episcopatum electus, invitus est ordinatus. In quo, ut opinor, decimo anno cum agerit, invalescente apud Albigensem urbem inguinario morbo et maxima iam parte de populo illo defuncta, cum iam pauci de civibus remanerent, vir beatus tamquam bonus pastor numquam ab illo loco recedere voluit; sed semper ortabatur eos, qui relicti fuerant, oratione incumbere ac vigiliis instanter insistere et bona semper tam in operibus quam in cogitatione versare, dicens: 'Haec agite, ut, si vos Deus de hoc mundo migrare voluerit, non in iudicium, sed in requiem introire possitis'. Cum autem, ut credo, iam revelante Domino, tempus suae vocationis agnusceret, ipse sibi sarcofagum conposuit, corpus abluit, vestem induit; et sic intentum semper caelo beatum spiritum exalavit. Fuit autem magnae sanctitatis minimaeque cupiditatis, aurum numquam habere volens. Nam, si coactus accepisset, protinus pauperibus erogabat. Cuius tempore cum Mummolus patricius multos captivos ab ea urbe duxisset, prosecutus ille omnes redemit. Tantamque ei Dominus gratiam cum populo illo tribuit, ut ipsi etiam qui captivos duxerant et de praetio ei concederent et in reliquo munerarent; et sic patriae suae captivos libertati pristinae restauravit. Multaque de hoc viro bona audivi; sed dum ad historiae ceptum reverti cupio, plurima praetermitto.

## 2. De conlisione Carnotenorum et Aurilianensium.

<a id="hist-07-la-002"></a>
[**[002]**](#hist-07-en-002) Defuncto igitur Chilperico inventamque, quam diu quaesierat, mortem, Aurilianensis cum Blesensibus iuncti super Dunenses inruunt eosque inopinantes proterunt; domos annonasque vel quae moveri habele non poterant incendio tradunt, pecora diripiunt adque res quas levare poterant sustulerunt. Quibus discedentibus, coniuncti Dunenses cum reliquis Carnotenis, de vestigio subsecuntur, simile sorte eos adficientes, qua ipsi adfecti fuerant, nihil in domibus vel extra domus vel de domibus relinquentes. Cumque adhuc inter se iurgia commoventes desevirent et Aurilianensis contra hos arma concuterent, intercedentibus comitibus, pax usque in audientia data est, scilicet ut in die, quo iudicium erat futurum, pars, quae contra partem iniuste exarserat, iusticia mediante, conponerit. Et sic a bello cessatum est.

## 3. De interitu Vidastis cognomento Avi.

<a id="hist-07-la-003"></a>
[**[003]**](#hist-07-en-003) Vidastis cognomento Avus, qui ante hos annos Lupum Ambrosiumque pro amore uxoris Ambrosii interfecerat et ipsam sibi, quae consubrina sua esse dicebatur, in matrimunio acceperat, dum multa scelera infra Pectavum terminum perpetraret, quodam loco cum Chulderico Saxone coniunctus, dum se invicem convitiis lacesserent, unus ex pueris Chulderici Avonem hasta transfixit. Qui ad terram ruens, plerisque adhuc ictibus sauciatus, iniquam animam, sanguine defluente, refudit; fuitque ultrix divina maiestas sanguinis innocentis, quem propria effuderat manu. Multa enim furta, adulteria homicidiaque miserrimus saepe conmiserat, quae silere melius poto. Conposuit tamen filiis Saxo ille mortem eius.

## 4. Quod Fredegundis in aeclesia confugit.

<a id="hist-07-la-004"></a>
[**[004]**](#hist-07-en-004) Interea Fredegundis regina iam viduata Parisius advenit et cum thesauris, quos infra murorum septa concluserat, ad aeclesiam confugit adque a Ragnemodo fovetur episcopo. Reliquos vero thesauros, qui apud villam Calam remanserant, in quibus erat missurium illud aureum quod nuper fecerat, thesaurarii levaverunt et ad Childeberthum regem, qui tunc apud Meldensem conmorabatur urbem, velociter transierunt.

## 5. Quod Gunthramnus rex Parius venit.

<a id="hist-07-la-005"></a>
[**[005]**](#hist-07-en-005) Fredegundis igitur regina, accepto consilio, legatos ad Gunthchramnum regem mittit, dicens: 'Veniat dominus meus et suscipiat regnum fratris sui. Est', inquid, 'mihi infans parvolus, quem in eius ulnis ponere desiderans, me ipsam eius humilio dicioni'. Conperto autem Gunthchramnus rex de fratris excessu amarissime flevit. Moderato quoque planctu, conmoto exercitu, Parisius dirigit. Cumque ille infra muros susceptus fuisset, Childeberthus rex, nepus eius, ab alia advenit parte.

## 6. Quod idem rex ea que de Charibertho erant sibi subegit.

<a id="hist-07-la-006"></a>
[**[006]**](#hist-07-en-006) Sed cum eum Parisiaci recipere nollent, legatos ad Gunthchramnum regem diregit, dicens: 'Scio, piissime pater, non latere pietati tuae, qualiter utrumque usque praesens tempus pars obpraesserit inimica, ut nullus de rebus sibi debitis possit invenire iustitiam. Idcirco supplex nunc depraecor, ut placita, quae inter nos post patris mei obitum sunt innexa, custodiantur'. Tunc Gunthchramnus rex legatis illius ait: 'O miseri et semper perfidi, nihil in vobis verum habentes neque in promissis permanentes, ecce omnia quae mihi polliciti estis relictis, cum Chilperico rege novam pactionem scripsistis, ut, me a regno depulso, civitates meas inter se dividerent. Ecce pactiones ipsas, ecce manus vestrae subscriptiones, quibus hanc conibentiam confirmastis! Et qua nunc fronte quaeritis, ut nepotem meum Childeberthum suscipere debeam, quem mihi vestra perversitate voluistis facere inimicum?' Cui legati dixerunt: 'Si tantum mentem iracundia coepit, ut nihil nepote tuo de his quae pollicitus es indulgeas, vel illa quae de regno Chariberthi debentur auferre desiste'. Quibus ille ait: 'Ecce pactiones, quae inter nos factae sunt, ut, quisque sine fratris voluntatem Parisius urbem ingrederetur, amitteret partem suam, essetque Polioctus martyr cum Hylario adque Martino confessoribus iudex ac retributor eius. Post haec ingressus est in ea germanus meus Sigyberthus, qui iudicio Dei interiens amisit partem suam, Similiter et Chilpericus gessit. Per has ergo transgressiones amiserunt partes suas. Ideoque, quia illi iuxta Dei iudicium et maledictionibus pactionum defecerunt, omnem regnum Chariberthi cum thesauris eius meis ditionibus, lege opitulante, subiciam nec exinde alicui quicquam nisi spontanea voluntate indulgeam. Absistete igitur, vos semper mendaces ac perfidi, et haec regi vestro referte'.

## 7. Quod legati Childeberthi Fredegundem requirunt.

<a id="hist-07-la-007"></a>
[**[007]**](#hist-07-en-007) Quibus discedentibus, legati iterum Childeberthi ad antedictum regem veniunt, Fredegundem reginam requirentes atque dicentes: 'Redde homicidam, quae amitam meam suggillavit, quae patrem interfecit et patruum, quae ipsus quoque consobrinus meus gladio interemit'. At ille: 'In placito', inquid, 'quem habemus, cuncta decernimus, tractantes, quid oporteat fieri'. Nam Fredegunde patrocinio suo fovebat, ipsamque sepius ad convivium evocans, promittens, se ei fieri maximum defensorem. Quadam vero die, dum pariter ad mensam epolarentum, regina consurgens et vale dicens, a rege detenebatur, dicente sibi: 'Adhuc aliquid cybi sume'. Cui illa: 'Indulge', inquid, 'depraecor, domini mi, quia iuxta consuetudinem mulierum contigit mihi, ut pro conceptu consurgam'. Haec ille audiens, obstipuit, sciens, quartum esse mensem, ex quo alium ediderat filium; tamen permisit eam consurgere. Prioribus quoque de regno Chilperici, ut erat Ansovaldus, et reliqui ad filium eius, qui erat, ut superius diximus, quattuor mensuum, se colligerunt, quem Chlotharium vocitaverunt, exegentes sacramenta per civitates, quae ad Chilpericum prius aspexerant, ut scilicit fidelis esse debeant Gunthchramno rege ac nepote suo Chlothario. Gunthchramnus vero rex omnia, quae fidelis regis Chilperici non recte diversis abstulerant, iusticia intercedente, restituit, multa et ipsi eclesiis conferens; testamenta quoque defunctorum, qui eclesias heredis instituerant et ad Chilperico conpressa fuerant, restauravit, multisque se benignum exhibens ac multa pauperibus tribuens.

## 8. Quod rex populum petiit, ne ut fratres eius interematur.

<a id="hist-07-la-008"></a>
[**[008]**](#hist-07-en-008) Sed quia non erat fidus ab hominibus inter quos venerat, armis se munivit, nec umquam ad eclesiam aut reliqua loca, qua ire delectabat, sine grande pergebat custodia. Unde factum est, ut quadam die dominica, postquam diaconus silentium populis, ut missae abscultarentur, indixit, rex conversus ad populum dicerit: 'Adiuro vos, o viri cum mulieribus qui adestis, ut mihi fidem inviolatam servare dignimini nec me, ut fratres meus nuper fecistis, interematis, liceatque mihi vel tribus annis nepotis meus, qui mihi adoptivi facti sunt filii, enutrire, ne forte contingat, quod Divinitas aeterna non patiatur, ut illis parvolis, me defuncto, simul pereatis, cum de genere nostro robustus non fuerit qui defensit'. Haec eo dicente, omnes populus orationem pro rege fudit ad Dominum.

## 9. Quod Rigunthis a Desiderio, thesauris ablatis, retenta est.

<a id="hist-07-la-009"></a>
[**[009]**](#hist-07-en-009) Dum haec agerentur, Rigunthis, Chilperici regis filia, cum thesauris supra scriptis usque Tholosam accessit. Et cernens se iam ad terminum Gothorum esse propinquam, moras innectire coepit, dicentibus sibi tum praeterea suis, oportere eam ibidem commorari, cum ipsi fatigati de itenere vestimenta haberent inculta, calciamenta scissa, ipsosque equorum adque carrucarum apparatos adhuc, sicut plaustris evecti erant, seorsum esse disiunctos. Oportere potius omnia haec prius diligenter stabilire et sic in itenere proficisci ac suscepi cum omni elegantia ab sponso, ne forte, si inculti inter Gothos apparerent, inriderentur ab ipsis. Dum ergo his retardarentur ex causis, mors Chilperici regis in aures Desiderii ducis inlabitur. Ipse quoque, collectis secum viris fortissimis, Tholosam urbem ingreditur repertusque thesauros abstulit de potestate reginae et in domo quadam sub sigillorum munitione ac virorum fortium custodia mancipat, deputans reginae victum artum, donec ad urbem regrederetur.

## 10. Quod Gundovaldus in regno elevatus est.

<a id="hist-07-la-010"></a>
[**[010]**](#hist-07-en-010) Ipse vero ad Mummolum, cum quo foedus ante duos annos inierat, pro peravit. Morabatur tunc Mummolus infra murus Avennicae urbis cum Gundovaldo, cui in libro superiore memimmus. Qui coniunetus cum supradictis ducibus Limovicinum accedens, Briva-Curretia vicum, in quo sanctus Martinus, nostri, ut aiunt, Martini discipulus, requiescit, advenit, ibique parmae superpositus, rex est levatus. Sed cum tertio cum eodem girarent, cecidisse fertur, ita ut vix manibus circumstantium sustentare potuisset. Deinde ibat per civitates in circuitu positas. Rigunthis vero in basilica sanctae Mariae Tholosa, in qua Ragnovaldi uxor, cui supra meminimus, Chilpericum metuens confugerat, resedebat. Ragnovaldus vero de Hispaniis rediens, uxori facultatique restituetur. Legationis enim causa Hispaniam petierat, a rege Gunthchramno directus. Magno ea tempestate incendio basilica antedicti Martini beati apud Brivam vicum ab inminente hoste cremata est, ita ut tam altarium quam columnae, quae de diversis marmorum generibus aptatae erant, ab igne dissolverentur. Sed ita haec aedes in posterum a Ferreolo episcopo reparata est, tamquam si nihil mali pertulerit. Vehementer enim admirantur veneranturque hunc sanctum incole, eo quod plerumque virtutes eius experiantur.

## 11. De signis quae apparuerunt.

<a id="hist-07-la-011"></a>
[**[011]**](#hist-07-en-011) Erat enim, cum haec agebatur, mensis decimus. Tunc apparuerunt in codicibus vinearum palmites novi cum uvis deformatis, in arboribus flores; pharus magna per caelum discurrens, quae, priusquam lux fierit in die, late mundum inluminavit. Apparuerunts etiam in caelo et radii. A parte septentrionali columna ignea, quasi de caelo pendens, per duarum horarum spatium visa est, cui stella magna superposita erat. In Andecavo enim terra tremuit, et multa alia signa apparuerunt, quae, ut opinor, ipsius Gundovaldi interitum nuntiarunt.

## 12. De incendio regionis Toronicae et virtute sancti Martini.

<a id="hist-07-la-012"></a>
[**[012]**](#hist-07-en-012) Igitur Gunthchramnus rex comites suos ad conpraehendendas civitates, quas condam Sigyberthus de regno Chariberthi fratris sui acciperat, direxit, ut exegentes sacramenta suis eas ditionibus subiugarent. Toronici vero adque Pectavi ad Childeberthum, Sigyberthi filium, transire voluerunt, sed commoti Biturigi contra eos venire disponunt adque infra terminum Toronicum incendia facere coeperunt. Tunc Maroialensim aeclesiam termini Toronici, in qua sancti Martini reliquiae habibantur, incendio concremarunt; sed virtus beati adfuit, ut in tam valido incendio pallolae, quae super altarium fuerant positae, non consumerentur ab igne. Et non solum ipse, sed etiam herbolae olim collectae altarioque locatae nequaquam exustae sunt. Qua incendia videntes Toronici legationem mittunt, dicentes, melius sibi esse ad tempus Gunthchramno rege subdi quam cuncta incendio ac ferro vastari.

## 13. De incendio et praedes Pectavi urbis.

<a id="hist-07-la-013"></a>
[**[013]**](#hist-07-en-013) Confestim autem post mortem Chilperici Gararicus dux Limovicas accesserat et sacramenta de nomine Childeberthi susceperat. Exinde Pectavis veniens, ab ipsis receptus est et ibi morabatur. Audiens vero quae Toronici patiebantur, mittit legationem, obtestans, ne nos ad partem Gunthchramni regis tradere deberimus, si nobis vellimus esse consultum; sed meminirimus potius Sigyberthi, qui quondam genitur Childeberthi fuit. Nos vero haec rursum episcopo et civibus mandata remisimus, quod, nisi se ad tempus Gunthchramno rege subderent, similia paterentur, adserentes, hunc esse nunc patrem super duos filios, Sigyberthi scilicet et Chilperici, qui ei fuerant adoptati; et sic tenere regni principatum, ut quondam Chlotharius rex fecerat, pater eius. His quoque non adquiescentibus, Gararicus de civitate egreditur, quasi exercitum adducturus, in urbe vero Eberonem cubicularium Childeberthi regis relinquens. Sicharius vero cum Willachario Aurilianense comite, qui tunc Toronus acceperat, exercitum contra Pectavos commovit, ut scilicet ab una parte Toronici, ab alia Biturigi commoti cuncta vastarent. Qui cum ad terminum propinquassent ac domus cremare coepissent, miserunt ad eos Pectavi legatos, dicentes: 'Petemus, ut usque in placito, quod inter se Gunthchramnus et Childeberthus regis habent, susteneatis. Quod si convenit, ut pagos hos rex Gunthchramnus accipiat, non resistemus; sin aliud, dominum nostrum recognuscimus, cui servire plenius debeamus'. Ad haec illi responderunt: 'Nihil nobis de hac causa pertinet, nisi tantum iussa principis adimplere. Nam si nolueritis, cuncta ut coepimus devastamus'. Cumque in hoc res ageretur, ut universa incendio, praedae adque captivitati traderentur, eiectis de civitate hominibus Childeberthi, sacramenta Gunthchramno rege dederunt, non longo tempore ea custodientes.

## 14. De legatis Childeberthi regis ad Gunthramno missis.

<a id="hist-07-la-014"></a>
[**[014]**](#hist-07-en-014) Igitur, adveniente placito, directi sunt ad Childebertho rege Egidius episcopus, Gunthchramnus Boso, Sigyvaldus et alii multi ad Gunthchramno regem; ingressique ad eum, ait episcopus: 'Gratias agimus Deo omnipotenti, o piissime rex, quod te post multos labores regionibus tuis regnoque restituit'. Cui rex ait: 'Illi enim dignae sunt gratiae referendae, qui est rex regum et dominus dominorum, qui haec sua miseratione operare dignatus est. Nam non tibi, cuius consilio doloso ac periuriis regionis meae anno superiore incensae sunt, qui numquam fidem integram cum ullo homine habuisti, cuius dolositas ubique dispergitur, qui non sacerdotem, sed inimicum regni nostri te esse declaras'. Ad haec verba episcopus iracundia commotus siluit. Unus autem ex legatis dixit: 'Supplecat nepus tuus Childeberthus, ut civitates, quas pater eius tenuit, reddi iubeas '. Ad haec ille respondit: 'Iam dixi vobis prius, quia pactiones nostrae mihi haec conferunt, ideoque eas reddere nolo'. Alius quoque legatorum ait: 'Rogat nepus tuus, ut Fredegundem maleficam, per quam multi reges interfecti sunt, reddi iubeas ad ulciscendam mortem patris, patrui vel consubrinorum suorum'. At ille: 'Tradi ei', inquid, 'in potestate non poterit, quia filium regem habet. Sed et ea quae contra illam adseretes, vera esse non credo'. Post hos Gunthchramnus Boso, quasi aliquid suggesturus, ad regem accedit. Et quia sonuerat, Gundovaldum manifeste regem levatum, antecipans rex verba eius, ait: 'O inimici regionis regnique nostri, qui propterea ante hos annos Orientem adgressus es, ut Ballomerem quendam – sic enim vocabat rex Gundovaldum – super regnum nostrum adduceris, semper perfide et numquam custodiens quae promittis!' Cui ille: 'Tu', inquid, 'dominus et rex regali in solio resedis, et nullus tibi ad ea quae loqueris ausus est respondere. Insontem enim me de hac causa profiteor. At si aliquis est similis mihi, qui hoc crimen inpingat occulte, veniat nunc palam et loquatur. Tunc, o rex piissime, ponens hoc in Dei iudicium, ut ille discernat, cum nos in unius campi planitiae viderit demicare'. Ad haec, cunctis silentibus, addedit rex: 'Omnibus autem haec causa animus accendere debet, ut repellatur a finibus nostris advena, cuius pater molinas gobernavit, et ut vere dicam, pater eius pectinibus insedit lanasque conposuit'. Et quamquam possit fieri, ut unus homo utriusque artificii magisterio subderetui, ad increpationem tamen regis quidam ex ipsis respondit: 'Ergo duos, ut adseris, patres hic homo habuit, lanarium simul molinariumque. Absit a te, o rex, ut tam inculte loquaris. Non enim auditum est, unum hominem praeter spiritalem causam duos habere posse pariter genitores'. Dehinc, cum multi solverentur in risu, respondit alius legatorum, dicens: 'Vale dicimus tibi, o rex. Nam quia reddere noluisti civitatis nepotis tui, scimus, salvam esse securem, quae fratrum tuorum capitibus est defixa. Celerius tuum libravit defixa cerebrum'. Et sic cum scandalum discesserunt. Tunc rex his verbis succensus, iussit super capita euntium proici aequorum stercora, putrefactas astulas, paleas ac faenum putridine dissolutum ipsumque foetidum urbis lutum. Quibus de rebus maculati graviter, non sine inmensa iniuria adque contumilia abierunt.

## 15. De malitia Fredegunde.

<a id="hist-07-la-015"></a>
[**[015]**](#hist-07-en-015) Resedente vero Fredegunde regina in aeclesia Parisiaca, Leonardus ex domestico, qui tunc ab urbe Tholosa ad venerat, ingressus ad eam, causas contumiliae iniuriasque filiae eius narrare coepit, dicens, quia: 'Iuxta imperium tuum accessi cum regina Rigunthe ac vidi humilitatem eius, vel qualiter expoliata est a thesauris et omnibus rebus; ego vero per fugam dilapsus, veni nuntiare dominae meae quae gesta sunt'. Haec illa audiens furore commota, iussit eum in ipsa aeclesia spoliare, midatumque vestimentis ac balteo, quod ex munere Chilperici regis habebat, discedere a sua iubet praesentia. Cocos quoque sive pistores, vel quoscumque de hoc itenere regressus esse cognovit, caesos spoliatosque ac demanicatos reliquid. Nectarium autem, Baudegysili episcopi fratrem, nefandis accusationibus cum rege temptavit obruere, adserens, eum de thesauro regis mortui multa portasse. Sed et de prumptuariis tam tergora quam vina multa eum abstulisse dicebat, petens, ut vinctus carceralibus tenebris truderetur. Sed patientia regis fratrisque auxilium fieri non permisit. Multa quidem ibi vana exercens, non metuebat Deum, in cuius aeclesiam petebat auxilium. Habebat tunc temporis secum Audonem iudicem, qui ei tempore regis in multis consenserat malis. Ipse enim cum Mummolo praefecto multos de Francis, qui tempore Childeberthi regis seniores ingenui fuerant, publico tributo subegit. Qui post mortem regis ab ipsis spoliatus ac denudatus est, ut nihil ei, praeter quod super se auferre potuit, remaneret. Domus enim eius incendio subdederunt; abstulissent utique et ipsam vitam, nisi cum regina aeclesiam expetisset.

## 16. De regressu Praetextati episcopi.

<a id="hist-07-la-016"></a>
[**[016]**](#hist-07-en-016) Praetextatum vero episcopum egre suscoepit, quem cives Rhodomaginsis post excessum regis de exilio expetentes, cum grande laude civitati suae restituerunt. Post reditum vero suum ad urbem Parisiacam advenit ac se Gunthchramno regi repraesentavit, exorans, ut causam suam diligenter inquireret. Adserebat enim regina, eum non debere recepi, qui fuisset per iudicium quadraginta quinque episcoporum a sacerdotali officio segregatus. Cumque rex pro hac causa synodum excitare vellit, Ragnemodus huius urbis episcopus pro omnibus responsum reddidit, dicens: 'Scitote ei paenitentiam indictam a sacerdotibus, non tamen eum prursus ab episcopatum remotum'. Et sic a rege susceptus adque convivio eius adscitus, ad urbem suam regressus est.

## 17. De Promoto episcopo.

<a id="hist-07-la-017"></a>
[**[017]**](#hist-07-en-017) Promotus vero, qui in Dunense castro ordinante Sigybertho rege episcopus fuerat institutus et post mortem regis amotus fuerat, eo quod castrum illud esset diocisis Carnotena, – contra quem ita iudicium latum fuerat, ut praesbiterii tantum officium fungeretur – accessit ad regem, depraecans, ut ordinationem episcopatus in antedicto castro reciperet. Sed, obsistente Pappolo Carnotenae urbis episcopo ac dicente, quia: 'Diocisis mea est', ostendente praesertim iudicium episcoporum, nihil aliud potuit obtenere cum rege, nisi ea quae sub ipsius castri termino propria habebat reciperit, in qua cum genetrice adhuc superstite moraretur.

## 18. De eo quod rege dictum est.

<a id="hist-07-la-018"></a>
[**[018]**](#hist-07-en-018) Commorante vero rege apud urbem Parisiacam, venit quidam pauper, dicens: 'Audi, rex, verba oris mei. Noveris enim, quia Faraulfus, cobicularius quondam fratris tui, quaerit te interficere. Audivi enim consilium eius, ut, eunte te matutina oratione ad aeclesiam, aut cultro adpeteret aut hasta transfoderet'. Obstupefactus autem rex, misit vocare eum. Quo negante, de his rex metuens, armis se valde munivit, nec paenitusad loca sancta vel alibi nisi vallatus armatis adque custodibus procedebat. Faraulfus autem non post multum tempus mortuus est.

## 19. Quod regina in villa abire iussa est.

<a id="hist-07-la-019"></a>
[**[019]**](#hist-07-en-019) Cum autem magnus clamor fierit adversus eos qui potentes cum rege fuerant Chilperico, scilicet quod abstulissent vel villas vel res reliquas de rebus alienis, omnia, quae iniuste ablata fuerant, rex reddi praecepit, sicut iam superius indecatum est. Fredegundem quoque reginam ad villam Rodoialensim, quae in Rhodomagensi termino sita est, abire praecepit. Secutique sunt eam omnes meliores natu regni Chilperici regis. Ibique relinquentes eam cum Melanio episcopo, qui de Rhodomago submotus fuerat, ad filium eius se transtulerunt, promittentes, quod ab his studiosissime nutriretur.

## 20. Quod idem emisit qui Brunechildem lederet.

<a id="hist-07-la-020"></a>
[**[020]**](#hist-07-en-020) Postquam autem Fredegundis regina ad supradictam villam abiit, cum esset valde maesta, quod ei potestas ex parte fuisset ablata, meliorem a se existimans Brunichildem, misit occulte clericum sibi familiarem, qui eam circumventam dolis interemere possit, videlicet ut, cum se subtiliter in eius subderet famulatum, ab ea credi possit, et sic clam percoliretur. Veniens igitur clericus, cum diversis ingeniis se eidem commendavit, dicens: 'A facie Fredegundis reginae fugio, deposcens auxilium tuum'. Coepit se etiam omnibus reddere humilem, carum, oboedientem ac reginae privatum. Sed non longo tempore interposito, intellexerunt eum dolosae transmissum; vinctusque ac caesus, cum rem patifecisset occultam, redire permissus est ad patronam. Reseransque quae acta fuerant, effatus, quod iussa patrari non potuissit, manuum ac pedum abscisione multatur.

## 21. De fuga et custodia Eberulfi.

<a id="hist-07-la-021"></a>
[**[021]**](#hist-07-en-021) His ita gestis, cum rex Gunthchramnus Cabillonno regressus mortem fratris conaretur inquirere et regina crimen super Eberulfum cobicularium inposuissit – rogatus enim fuerat ab ea, ut post mortem regis cum ipsa resederet, sed optenere non potuit – haec ergo inimicitia pullulante, adseruit regina ab eodem principem interfectum, ipsumque multa de thesauris abstulisse et sic in Toronicum abscessisse, ideoque, si rex mortem fratris desideraret ulciscere, noverit huius causae hunc esse signiferum. Tunc rex iuravit omnibus optimatibus, quod non modo ipsum, verum etiam progeniem eius in nonam generationem deleret, ut per horum necem consuetudo auferretur iniqua, ne reges amplius interficerentur. Quod cum Eberulfus conperissit, basilicam sancti Martini, cuius res saepe pervaserat, expetivit. Tunc data occansione, ut custodiretur, Aurilianensis adque Blesensis vicissim ad has excubias veniebant, impletisque quindecim diebus, cum praeda multa revertebantur, adducentis iumenta, pecora vel quodcumque derepere potuissent. Ille vero, qui beati Martini iumenta abduxerant, commota altercatione, se invicem lanceis transfixerunt. Duo, qui mulas diripiebant, ad domum vicini cuiusdam accedentes, potum rogare coeperunt. Cumque ille se habere negarit, elevatis lanceis ut eum transfoderent, hic extracto gladio utrumque perfodit, cecideruntque ambo et mortui sunt; iumenta tamen sancti Martini reddita sunt. Tantaque ibi tunc mala per hos Aurilianensis gesta sunt, ut nequeant explicari.

## 22. De malitia eius.

<a id="hist-07-la-022"></a>
[**[022]**](#hist-07-en-022) Dum haec autem agerentur, res ipsius Eberulfi diversis conceduntur. Aurum argentumque vel alias meliores species, quas secum retenebat, in medio exposuit; quod vero conmendatum habuit, publicatum est. Greges etiam aequorum, porcorum iumentorumque diripiuntur. Domus vero inframuranea, quam de dominatione aeclesiae abstulerat, referta annonis, vino adque tergoribus rebusque aliis multis, adplene spoliata est, nec ibi aliud quam parietes vacui remanserunt. Ex hoc nos maxime suspectus habebat, qui in causis eius fideliter currebamus, promittens plerumque, quod, si umquam ad regis gratiam perveniret, in nobis haec quae perferebat ulcisceretur. Deus enim novit, cui arcana pectoris revelantur, quia de puro corde, in quantum potuemus, solatium ministravimus. Et quamquam multas nobis insidias prius de rebus sancti Martini fecissit, extabat tamen causa, ut eadem obliviscerem, eo quod filium eius de sancto lavacro suscipissem. Sed credo, infilici ea res maximum fuit inpedimentum, quod nullam reverentiam sancto praestabat antestiti. Nam saepe cedes infra ipsum atrium, quod ad pedes beati erat, exegit, exercens assiduae aebrietatis ac vanitatis. Presbiterum quoque unum, pro eo quod ei vinum dare differret, cum iam crapulatus aspiceretur, elisum super scamnum pugnis ac diversis ictibus verberavit, ut paene animam reddere videretur; et fecissit forsitan, si ei medicorum ventusae non subvenissent. Habebat enim pro timore regis in ipsum salutaturium beatae basilicae mansionem. Cum autem presbiter, qui clavis ostei retenebat, clausis reliquis, recessissit, per illum salutaturii osteum introeuntes puellae cum reliquis pueris eius, suspiciebant picturas parietum rimabantque ornamenta beati sepulchri; quod valde facinorosum relegiosis erat. Quod cum presbyter cognovissit, defixis clavis super ostium, intrinsecus serras aptavit. Haec ille cum post caenam vinu maditus advertissit et nos in basilicam in initium noctis orationis gratia psallerimus, furibundus ingreditur meque convitiis ac maledictionibus urguere coepit, illud inter iurgia exprobrans, quod ego eum vellim a sancti antestitis fimbriis separare. Sed ego stupens, quae virum coepissit insania, blandis sermonibus mulcire conatus sum. Sed cum eius furias verbis lenibus superare non possim, silere decrevi. Ille vero me tacitum intendens, ad presbiterum convertitur evometque in eum multa convicia. Nam et illum verbis procacibus et me diversis obpropriis inpugnabat. Nos vero cum vidissimus eum, ut ita dicam, agi a daemone, egressi a basilica sancta, scandalum vigiliasque finivimus, illud maxime indignum ferentes, quod ad hoc iurgium absque reverentia sancti ante ipsum sepulchrum antestitis excitaverat. His diebus vidi somnium, quod ipsi in sancta basilica retuli, dicens: Tutabam me quasi in hac basilica sacrosancta missarum solemnia caelebrare. Cumque iam altarium cum oblationibus palleo syrico coopertum essit, subito ingredientem Gunthchramnum regem conspicio, qui voce magna clamabat: „Extrahite inimicum generationis nostrae, evellite homicidam a sacro Dei altario". At ego, cum haec audirem, ad te conversus dixi: „Adpraehende palleum altaris, infelix, quo sacra munera conteguntur, ne hinc abiciaris". Cumque adpraehenderis, laxa eum manu et non viriliter detenebas. Ego vero, expansis manibus, contra pectus regis meum pectus aptabam, dicens: „Noli eiecere hunc hominem de basilica sancta, ne vitae periculum patiaris, ne te sanctus antestis sua virtute confodeat. Noli te proprio iaculo interemere, quia, hoc si feceris, praesentem vitam aeternamque carebis". Sed cum rex mihi resisteret, tu laxabas palleum et post me veniebas. Ego vero valde tibi molestus eram. Cumque reverteris ad altarium, adpraehendebas palleum, sed rursum relinquebas. Dum hunc tu tepide reteneris et ego rege viriliter resisterem, evigilavi pavore conterritus, ignarus quid somnium indecaret'. Igitur cum ei ista narrassem, ait: 'Verum est somnium, quod vidisti, quod valde cogitatione meae concordat'. Cui ego: 'Et quid providit cogitatio tua?' Et ille: 'Deliberatum', inquid, 'habui, ut, si me rex ab hoc loco iuberit extrahi, ab una manu pallas altaris tenerem, ab alia vero, evaginato gladio, te prius interfectum, quantuscumque deinceps clericos repperissem, in morte prosternerem. Nec mihi post haec erat iniuria leto subcumbere, si de huius sancti clericis acciperem ultionem'. Haec ego audiens et stupens, admirabam quod erat, quia per os eius diabulus loquebatur. Numquam enim in Deum ullum timorem habuit. Nam dum essit in libertatem, equi eius ac pecora per segites pauperum vineisque dimittebantur. Quod si expellebantur ab his, quorum evertebant labores, statim a suis percolibantur. Nam in hac angustia qua erat saepe commemorabat, quod res beati antestitis abstulissit iniuste. Denique anno superiore, commotum quendam levem e civibus, aeclesiae actores fecit interpellari. Tunc, postposita iustitia, res, quas olim aeclesia possedebat, sub specie emptionis abstraxit, datam ipsi homini partem auream baltei sui. Sed et alia multa perversae egit usque ad finem vitae suae, quem in posterum explanamus.

## 23. De Iudaeo interfecto cum suis.

<a id="hist-07-la-023"></a>
[**[023]**](#hist-07-en-023) Praesenti quoque anno Armentarius Iudaeus cum uno sectae suae satillite et duobus christianis ad exegendas cautionis, quas ei propter tributa puplica Iniuriosus ex vecario, ex comite vero Eonomius deposuerant, Toronus advenit. Interpellatisque viris, promissionem accepit de reddendo pecuniae fenore cum usuris, dicentibus sibi praeterea ipsis: 'Si ad domum nostram veneris, et quae debentur exsolvimus et aliis te muneribus, sicut dignum est, honoramus'. Eo quoque eunte, ab Iniurioso suscipetur et convivio conlocatur; expletoque epulo, adpropinquante nocte, commoti ab eodem loco ad alium transeunt. Tunc, ut ferunt, Iudaei cum duobus christianis ab Iniuriosi hominibus interfecti, in puteum, qui propinquus erat domui eius, proiecti sunt. Auditis his parentes eorum quae gesta fuerant, Toronus advenerunt; datoque quibusdam hominibus inditio, puteum repperiunt virosque extrahunt, multum negante Iniurioso, quod in hac causa non fuerit inquinatus. Post haec in iuditio venit; sed cum fortiter, ut diximus, denegaret et hii non haberent, qualiter eum convincere possint, iudicatum est, ut se insontem redderet sacramento. Sed nec hoc his adquiescentibus, placitum in regis Childeberthi praesentiam posuerunt. Verumtamen neque pecunia neque cautionis Iudaei defuncti repertae sunt. Loquebantur tunc multi hominum, Medardum tribunum in hoc scelere mixtum fuisse, eo quod et ipse a ludaeo pecuniam mutuassit. Iniuriosus tamen ad placitum in conspectu regis Childeberthi advenit et per triduum usque occasum solis observavit. Sed cum hii non venissent neque de causa ab ullo interpellatus fuisset, ad propria rediit.

## 24. De praeda urbis Pectavae.

<a id="hist-07-la-024"></a>
[**[024]**](#hist-07-en-024) Anno igitur decimo Childeberthi regis rex Gunthchramnus, commotis gentibus regni sui, magnum iuncxit exercitum. Sed pars maior cum Aurilianensibus adque Biturigis Pectavum petiit. Excesserant enim de fide, quam regi promiserant. Miseruntque prius legationem, ut scirent, utrum susceperentur ab his an non. Sed episcopus loci Maroveus dure suscepit hos nuntios. At illi infra terminum ingressi, praedas, incendia adque homicidia faciebant. Hii vero qui cum praeda revertebantur, per Toronicum transeuntes, similiter illis qui iam sacramenta dederant faciebant, ita ut ipsae quoque aeclesiae incenderentur et quaecumque invenire potuissent diriperentur. Quod saepius actum est, dum illi ad regem egre converterentur. Sed cum exercitus propius ad urbem accederet et iam pars maxima regionis devastata cerneretur, tunc miserunt nuntios, fideles se rege Gunthchramno fatentes. At illi infra murus urbis recepti, super episcopum inruerunt, dicentes, eum infidelem esse. Ille vero cum se ab his cerneret coartatum, effracto unum de sacris ministeriis calicem aureum et in numisma redactum, se populumque redemit.

## 25. De spolia Marileifi.

<a id="hist-07-la-025"></a>
[**[025]**](#hist-07-en-025) Marileifum vero, qui primus medicorum in domo Chilperici regis habitus fuerat, ardentissime vallant; et qui iam a Gararico duce valde spoliatus fuerat, ab his iterum denudatur, ita ut nulla ei substantia remaneret. Equos quoque eius, aurum argentumque sive species, quas meliores habebat, pariter auferentes, ipsum ditioni aeclesiasticae subdiderunt. Servitium enim patris eius tale fuerat, ut molinas aeclesiasticas studeret, fratresque ac consubrini vel reliqui parentes colinis dominicis adque pistrino subiecti erant.

## 26. Quod Gundovaldus civitates circuit.

<a id="hist-07-la-026"></a>
[**[026]**](#hist-07-en-026) Gundovaldus vero Pectavum accedere voluit, sed timuit. Audierat enim, iam contra se exercitum commoveri. In civitatibus enim, quae Sigyberthi regis fuerant, ex nomine regis Childeberthi sacramenta suscipiebat; in reliquis vero, quae aut Gunthchramni aut Chilperici fuerant, nomine suo, quod fidem servarent, iurabant. Post haec Ecolisinam accessit, susceptaque sacramenta muneratisque prioribus, Petrocoricum adgreditur. Graviter episcopum tunc iniuriatum reliquid, pro eo quod susceptus ab eodem honorifice non fuisset.

## 27. De iniuria Magnulfi episcopi.

<a id="hist-07-la-027"></a>
[**[027]**](#hist-07-en-027) Exinde Tholosam digressus, emisit nuntius ad Magnulfum episcopum civitatis, ut ab eo susciperetur. Sed ille, non inmemor prioris iniuriae, quam per Sigulfum quondam, qui se in regno elevare voluit, pertulerat, dicit civibus suis: 'Scimus enim, regis esse Gunthchramnum ac nepotem eius; hunc autem nescimus unde sit. Estote ergo parati, et si voluerit Desiderius dux hanc calamitatem inducere super nos, simili ut Sigulfus sorte depereat; sitque omnibus exemplum, ne quis extraneorum Francorum regnum audeat violare'. His ita resistentibus et bellum parantibus, adveniente Gundovaldo cum magno exercitu, cum vidissent, quod sustenere non possint, susceperunt eum. Post haec cum ad convivium in domo aeclesiae episcopus una cum Gundovaldo resederet, ait: 'Filium te Chlothacharii regis adseris, sed utrum sit verum an non, ignoramus. Vel si possis vindecare coepta, incredibile habetur apud animos nostros'. At ille ait: Ego regis Chlothacharii sum filius et partem regni de praesenti sum percepturus; et usque Parisius velociter accedam et ibi sedem regni statuam'. Cui episcopus ait: 'Verumne est ergo, quod nullus de stirpe regum Francorum remansit, si tu haec quae dicis impleberis?' Inter has altercationes cum haec Mummolus exaudisset, elevata manu alapis cecidit episcopum, dicens: 'Non pudet, ut tam degener et stultus ita magno regi respondeat?' Verum ubi et Desiderius de consilio episcopi conperit quae fuerant dicta, ira commotus, manus in eum iniecit; eaesumque communiter hastis, pugnis et calcibus ac fune revinctum exilio damnaverunt, resque eius tam proprias quam aeclesiae integre auferentes. Waddo autem, qui erat maior domus reginae Rigunthis, se eisdem copolavit. Reliqui vero qui cum eo abierant per fugam dilapsi sunt.

## 28. Quod exercitus inante accessit.

<a id="hist-07-la-028"></a>
[**[028]**](#hist-07-en-028) Post haec exercitus ab urbe Pectava remotus inantea post Gundovaldum proficiscitur. Secutique sunt eum de Toronicis multi lucri causa; sed Pectavise super se inruentibus, nonnulli interempti, plurimi vero spoliati redierunt. Hi autem qui de his ad exercitum prius iuncxerant pariter abierunt. Itaque exercitus ad Dorononiam fluvium accedens, praestolare coepit, quid de Gundovaldo cognusceret. Cui iam, ut supra dictum est, adhaeserant dux Desiderius et Bladastis cum Waddone maiore domus Riguntnis reginae. Erant enim primi cum eo Sagittarius episcopus et Mummolus. Sagittarius enim iam repromissione de episcopatu Tholosano acciperat.

## 29. De interitu Eberulfi.

<a id="hist-07-la-029"></a>
[**[029]**](#hist-07-en-029) Dum autem haec agerentur, misit rex Gunthchramnus Claudium quendam, dicens: 'Si abieris', inquid, 'et eiectum de basilica Eberulfum aut gladio interemeris aut catenis vincxeris, magnis te muneribus locupletabo; verumtamen, ne sanctae basilicae iniuriam inferas, omnino commoneo'. Ille vero, ut erat vanitate adque avaritiae deditus, velociter Parisius advolavit. Uxor enim ei ex Meldensim terreturio erat. Volvere animo coepit, utrum Fredegundem reginam videret, dicens: 'Si eam videro, elicere ab ea aliquid muneris possum. Scio enim, eam esse homini ad quem directus sum inimicam'. Tunc accedens ad eam, de praesenti munera magna capiens, promissionis multas elicuit, ut aut extractum a basilica Eberulfum occideret aut circumventum dolis catenis vinceret aut certe in ipso eum atrio trucedaret. Regressus autem ad Dunensem castrum, comitem commovit, ut ei trecentos viros quasi ad costodiendas Toronicae urbis portas adiungeret, scilicet ut, cum venisset, per eorum solatium Eberulfum possit obpremere. Cumque comes loci viros istos commoneret, Claudius Toronus peraccessit. Et cum iter ageret, ut consuetudo est barbarorum, auspicia intendere coepit ac dicere sibi esse contraria, simulque interrogare multis, si virtus beati Martini de praesenti manifestaretur in peifidis, aut certe, si aliquis iniuriam in eum sperantibus intulisset, si protenus ultio sequeretur. Igitur postpositis, ut dixemus, viris, qui ad solatium eius venire debuerant, ipse ad basilicam sanctam accessit. Statimque infelici Eberulfo coniunctus, sacramenta dare coepit ac iurare per omnia sacrosancta vel virtutem praesentis antestitis, nullum in causis eius fore fideliorem, qui ita cum rege causas eius possit exserere. Hoc enim apud se consilium habuerat miserrimus: 'Nisi eum perierando decipero, non vincam'. Verum ubi videt Eberulfus, quod ei talia cum sacramento in ipsam basilicam ac per porticos vel singula loca atrii veneranda promitterit, crededit miser homini perieranti. Die autem altero, cum nos in villam quasi milia triginta ab urbe commoraremur, ad convivium basilicae sanctae cum eodem vel reliquis civibus est adscitus, ibique eum Claudius gladio ferire voluit, si pueri eius longius adstetissent. Verumtamen numquam haec Eberulfus, ut erat vanus, advertit. Postquam autem convivium est finitum, ipse simul ac Claudius per atrium domus basilicae deambulare coeperunt, sibi invicem fidem ac caritatem sacramentis intercurrentibus promittentes. His ita loquentibus, ait Claudius Eberulfo: 'Delectat animo adp metatum tuum haurire potum, si vina odoramentis essent inmixta aut certe potentioris vini libationem strenuetas tu a requireret'. Haec eo dicente, gavisus Eberulfus, respondit habere se, dicens: 'Et omnia quae volueris ad metatum meum repperies, tantum ut dignetur dominus meus tugurium ingredi mansionis meae'. Misitque pueros unum post alium ad requirenda potentiora vina, Laticina videlicet adque Gazitina. Cumque illum a pueris relictum sclum Claudius conspexisset, elevata contra basilica manu, ait: 'Martine beatissime, fac me uxorem cum parentibus cito videre'. Infelix enim in discrimine positus, et hunc interficere in atrio cogitabat et virtutem sancti antestitis metuebat. Tunc unus e pueris Claudiis qui erat rubustior, adpraehensum Eberulfum a tergo validioribus lacertis adstringit resupinatumque pectus eius ad iugulandum parat. At Claudius, extractu a balteo gladio, ad eum diriget. Sed et ille prolatum a cingulo ferrum se ad percutiendum, dum teneretur, adaptat. Cumque Claudius, elevata dextera, cultrum eius pectori iniecisset et ille non signiter sub ascella illius pugionem defixisset, retractumque ad se, libratu ictu pollicem Claudii intercidit. Ex hoc convenientes pueri eius cum gladiis, Eberulfum. diversis ictibus sauciant. Quorum de manu dilapsus, dum fugire iam exanimes nitiretur, extracto gladio, caput eius gravissime verberant, effusoque cerebro cecidit et mortuus est; nec promeruit ab eo salvari, quem fideliter numquam intellexit exposcere. Igitur Claudius timore perterritus, cellolam abbatis expetiit, ab eo tutari se cupiens, in cuius patronum reverentiam habere non sapuit. Illo quoque resedente, ait: 'Perpetratum est scelus inmensum, et nisi tu subveneris, periemus'. Haec eo loquente, inruerunt pueri Eberulfi cum gladiis ae lanceis, obseratumque repperientes ostium, effractis cellolae vitreis, hastas per parietes fenestras inieciunt Claudiumque iam simevivum ictu transfigunt. Satellites autem eius post ostia et sub lectis abduntur. Abba adpraehensus a duobus clericis, inter gladiorum acies vix vivus eripetur; reseratisque ostiis, turba gladiatorum ingreditur. Nonnulli etiam matricolariorum et reliquorum pauperum pro scelere commisso tectum cellolae conantur evertere. Sed et inergumini ac diversi egeni cum petris et fustibus ad ulciscendam basilicae violentiam proficiscuntur, indigne ferentes, quur talia, quae numquam facta fuerant, essent ibidem perpetrata. Quid plura? Extrahuntur fugaces ex abditis et crudeliter trucidantur; pavimentum cellolae tabo maculatur. Postquam vero interempti sunt, extrahuntur foris et nudi super humum frigidam relinquuntur. Percussores vero nocte sequenti, adpraehensis spoliis, fuga dilabuntur. Adfuit autem Dei ultio de praesenti super eos, qui beatum atrium humano sanguine polluerunt. Sed nec eius facenus parvum esse censetur, quem talia beatus antestis perferre permisit. Magnam ex hoc rex iracundiam habuit; sed, cognita ratione, quievit. Res tamen ipsius infelicis tam mobelis quam inmobelis, quod a prioribus relictum fuerat, suis fidelibus condonavit. Qui uxorem eius valde spoliatam in sanctam basilicam reliquerunt. Corpus vero Claudii vel reliquorum parentes proximi auferentes in suam regionem, sepelierunt.

## 30. De legatis Gundovaldi.

<a id="hist-07-la-030"></a>
[**[030]**](#hist-07-en-030) Igitur Gundovaldus duos ad amicos suos legatus derigit, clericus utique. Ex quibus unus abba Caturcinae urbis litteras quas acciperat, cavatam cudicis tabulam, sub cera recondidit. Sed adpraehensus ab hominibus regis Gunthchramni repertis litteris, in regis praesentia est deductus; qui caesus gravissime, in custodia est retrusus.

## 31. De reliquiis sancti Sergi marthiris.

<a id="hist-07-la-031"></a>
[**[031]**](#hist-07-en-031) Erat tunc temporis Gundovaldus in urbe Burdegalensi a Berthramno episcopo valde dilectus. Inquirens autem, quae ei causae solatium praebere possint, narravit quidam, quod aliquis in partibus Orientis rex, ablato sancti Sergi martyris pollice, in dextro brachio corporis sui seruisset. Cumque ei necessitas ad depellendum inimicos obvenisset, in hoc confisus auxilio, ubi dextri lacerti erexisset ulnam, protinus multitudo hostium, quasi martyris obpraessi virtute, labibatur in fugam. Haec audiens Gundovaldus, inquirere diligentius coepit, quisnam esset in eo loco, qui reliquias sancti Sergii martyris meruisset accipere. Interea proditur ab episcopo Berthramno Eufron neguciator per inimicitiam, quia invitum aliquando eum totunderat, inhians facultatem eius. Quod ille dispiciens, ad aliam urbem transiens, caesariae crescente, regreditur. Ait ergo episcopus: 'Est hic quidam Syrus Eufron nomine, qui de domo sua ecclesiam faciens, huius sancti reliquias collocavit et plurima ex his signa, virtute martyris opitulante, conspexit. Nam cum tempore quodam Burdegalensis civitas maximo flagraretur incendio, haec domus circumdata flammis nullatenus est adusta'. Ista eo dicente, statim Mummolus cursu rapido cum episcopo Berthramno ad domum Syri accedit, vallatumque hominem, pignera sibi sancta praecepit ostendi. Negat ille. Tamen cogitans, quod pro malitia aliqua ei haec pararetur insidia, ait: 'Noli fategare senem nec sancto inferre iniuriam; sed, acceptis a me centum aureis, abscide'. Illo quoque insistenti, ut sanctas viderit reliquias, ducentus aureos obtulit; et nec sic obtenuit eum recedere, nisi ipsa pignera viderentur. Tunc Mummolus elevari ad parietem scalam iubet – erant enim in sublime parietes contra altarium in capsola reconditae – diaconum suum scandere praecepit. Qui per gradus scandens scalae, adpraehendens capsam, ita tremore concussus est, ut nec vivens putaretur ad terram reverti. Adtamen accepta, ut diximus, capsula, quae de parieti pendebat, detulit. Quam perscrutatam, Mummolus os de sancti digito repperit, quod cultro ferire non metuit. Posito enim desuper cultro, et sic de alio percutiebat. Cumque post multos ictos vix frangi potuissit, divisum in tribus partibus ossiculum diversas in partes dilabitur. Credo, non erat acceptum martyri, ut haec ille contigerit. Tunc flente vehimentius Eufronio, prosternuntur omnes in orationem, depraecantes, ut Deus dignaretur ostendere, quae ab oculis fuerant humanis ablata. Post orationem autem repertae sunt particulae, ex quibus una Mummolus adsumpta abscessit, sed non, ut credo, cum gratia martyris, sicut in sequenti declaratum est. Dum autem in hac urbe morarentur, Faustianum presbiterum Aquinsi urbi episcopum ordinare praecipiunt. Nuper enim in Aquinsi urbe episcopus obierat, et Nicetius comes loci illius, germanus Rustici Vici Iuliensis episcopi, praeceptionem ab Chilperico elicuerat, ut tonsoratus civitati illi sacerdus daretur. Sed Gundovaldus distruere nitens eius decreta, convocatis episcopis, iussit eum benedici. Berthramnuss autem episcopus, qui erat metropolis, cavens futura, Palladium Santonicum iniungit, qui eum benedicerit. Nam et oculi ei eo tempore a lippitudine gravabantur. Fuit autem ad hanc ordinationem et Orestis Vasatinsis? episcopus; sed negavit hoc coram rege.

## 32. De aliis Gundovaldi legatis.

<a id="hist-07-la-032"></a>
[**[032]**](#hist-07-en-032) Post haec misit iterum Gundovaldus duos legatos ad regem cum virgis consecratis iuxta ritum Francorum, ut scilicet non contingerentur ab ullo, sed exposita legatione cum responsu reverterentur. Sed hi incauti, priusquam regis praesentiam cernerent, multis quae petebant explanaverunt. Extemplo sermo cucurrit ad regem; itaque vincti catenis in regis praesentiam deducuntur. Tunc illi quid quaererent, ad quem directi vel a quo fuerint missi, negare non ausi, aiunt'Gundovaldus, qui, nuper ab Oriente veniens, dicit se filium esse patris vestri regis Chlothacharii, misit nos, ut debitam portionem regni sui recipiat. Sin autem a vobis non redditur, noveritis, eum in his partibus cum exercitu esse venturum. Omnes enim viri fortissimi regionis illius, quae ultra Dorononiam sita ad Gallias pertinet, ei coniuncti sunt. Et ita infit: "Iudicavit tunc Deus, cum in unius campi planitiae iuncxerimus, utrum sim Chlothacharii filius an non".' Tunc rex furore succensus iussit eos ad trocleas extendi et fortissime caedi, ut, si vera essent quae dicerint, evidentius adprobarent, et si aliquid doli adhue intra pectorum archana retinerent, vis tormentorum extorquerit invitus. Deinde increscentibus suppliciis, aiunt neptem illius, id est regis Chilperici filiam, cum Magnulfo Tholosanorum episcopo exilio depotatam; thesauros omnes ab ipso Gundovaldo sublatus, ipsum quoque regem ab omnibus maioribus natu Childeberthi regis expetitum esse, sed praesertim, cum Gunthchramnus Boso ante hos annos Constantinopolim abissit, ipsum in Galliis invitassit.

## 33. Quod Childeberthus ad Gunthchramnum patruum suum venit.

<a id="hist-07-la-033"></a>
[**[033]**](#hist-07-en-033) Quibus caesis et in carcere trusis, rex arcessire nepotem suum Childeberthum iubet, ut scilicet coniuncti pariter homines istos audire deberent. Denique cum simul coniuncti viros interrogarent, iteraverunt ea, regis simul adstantibus, quae prius solus rex Gunthchramnus audivit. Adserebant etiam constanter, hanc causam, sicut iam supra diximus, omnibus senioribus in regno Childeberthi regis esse cognitam. Et ob hoc nonnulli tunc de prioribus regis Childeberthi in hoc placito abire timuerunt, qui in hac causa putabantur esse particepis. Post haec rex Gunthchramnus, data in manu regis Childeberthi hasta, ait: 'Hoc est indicium, quod tibi omne regnum meum tradedi. Ex hoc nunc vade et omnes civitates meas tamquam tuas proprias sub tui iuris dominatione subice. Nihil enim, facientibus peccatis, de stirpe mea remansit nisi tu tantum, qui mei fratris es filius. Tu enim heres in omni regno meo succede, ceteris exheredibus factis'. Tunc relictis omnibus, adsumpto seursum puero, clam locutus est, prius obtestans diligentissime, ne secreta conlocutio ulli hominum panderetur. Tunc indicavit ei, quos in consilio haberet aut spernerit a conloquio, quibus se crederit, quos vitarit, quos honorarit muneribus, quos ab honore depellerit, interea interdicens, ut Egidium episcopum, qui ei semper inimicus exteterat, nullo modo aut crederet aut haberet, quia et ipsi et patri suo saepius periurassit. Deinde, cum ad convivium convenissent, cohortabatur Gunthchramnus rex omnem exercitum, dicens: 'Videte, o viri, quia filius meus Childeberthus iam vir magnus effectus est. Videte et cavete, ne eum pro parvolo habeatis. Relinquite nunc peiversitates adque praesumptiones quas exercitis, quia rex est, cui vos nunc deservire debetis'. Haec et his similia locutus, per triduum epulantes adque iocundantes multisque se muneribus locupletantes, cum pace discesserunt. Tunc ei reddedit rex Gunthchramnus omnia quae pater eius Syghiberthus habuerat, obtestans, ne ad matrem accederet, ne forte aliquis daretur aditus, qualiter ad Gundovaldum scriberit aut ab eo scripta susciperit.

## 34. Quod Gundovaldus Convenas abiit.

<a id="hist-07-la-034"></a>
[**[034]**](#hist-07-en-034) Igitur Gundovaldus, cum audisset sibi exercitum propinquare, relictus a Desiderio duci, Garonnam cum Sagittario episcopo, Mummolo et Bladasti ducibus adque Waddone transivit, Convenas petentes. Est enim urbs in cacumine montes sita nullique monti contigua. Fons magnus ad radicem montes erumpens, circumdatus torre tutissima; ad quem per cuniculum discendentes ex urbe, latenter latices hauriunt. Hanc enim ingressus urbem in initium quadraginsimae, locutus est civibus, dicens: 'Noveritis, me cum omnibus, qui in regno Childeberthi habentur, electum esse regem adque habere mecum non modicum solatium. Sed quoniam frater meus Guntnchramnus rex inmensum adversus me movit exercitum, oportet vos alimenta adque cuncta supellectilem infra murorum monitione concludere, ut scilicet, dum nobis illa pietas divina augit solatium, non periatis inopia'. Haec illis credentibus, quaecumque habere potuerunt collocantes in urbe, praeparabant se ad resistendum. Eo tempore Gunthchramnus rex misit litteras ad Gundovaldum ex nomine Brunichildis reginae, in quibus erat scriptum, ut, relicto exercitu et in loca sua abire iussum, ipse remotior apud Burdegalinsem urbem hyberna deducerit. Scripserat enim haec dolose, ut de eo plenius, quid agerit, possit agnoscere. Igitur cummorante eo apud urbem Convenas, locutus est incolis, dicens: 'Ecce iam exercitus adpropinquat, egrediemini ad resistendum'. Quibus egredientibus, hii occupantes portas adque claudentes, excluso foris populo cum episcopo loci, cuncta quae in urbe invenire potuerant suis dicionibus subdiderunt. Tantaque ibi multitudo annonae adque vini reperta est, ut, si viriliter stetissent, per multorum annorum spatia victus alimenta non egerent.

## 35. De basilica sancti Vincenti marthiris Agenninsis vastata.

<a id="hist-07-la-035"></a>
[**[035]**](#hist-07-en-035) Audierant enim eo tempore ducis Gunthchramni regis, Gundovaldum ultra Garonnam in litore resedere cum ingenti hostium multitudine ipsosque thesauros, quos Rigundae tulerat, secum retenere. Tunc, impetum factum, cum equitibus Garonnam nando transire, nonnullis de exercitu in amne dimersis. Reliqui litus egressi, requirentes Gundovaldum, invenerunt camellos cum ingenti pondere auri adque argenti sive equites, quos fessus per vias reliquerat. Audientes deinceps eos infra muros urbis Convenicae commorari, relictis plaustris ac diversis inpedimentis cum populo minori, rubustioris viri ipsum, sicut iam Garonnam transierant, insequi distinant. Quibus properantibus, venerunt ad basilicam sancti Vincenti, quae est infra terminum Agenninsis urbis, ubi ipse martyr pro Christi nomine agonem dicitur consummasse, inveneruntque eam refertam a diversis thesauris incolarum. Erat enim spes incolis non esse a christianis tanti martyris basilicam violandam. Cuius ostia summo studio obserata erant. Nec mora, adpropinquans exercitus cum reserare templi regias non valeret, ignem accendit; consumptisque osteis, omnem substantiam cunctamque suppellectilem, quae in ea invenire potuerunt, cum sacris ministeriis abstulerunt. Sed multos ibi ultio divina conteruit. Nam plerisque manus divinitus urebantur, emittentis fumum magnum, sicut ex incendio surgi solet. Nonnulli arrepti a daemone, per inergiam debachantes martyrem declamabant. Plurimi vero semoti a seditione propriis se iaculis sauciabant. Relicum vero vulgus inante non sine grande metu progressum est. Quid plura? Convenitur ad Convenas – sic enim diximus nomen urbis – omnesque falanga in suburbana urbis campania castra metata est, ibique extensis tenturiis resedebat. Vastabatur in circuitu tota regio; nonnulli autem ab exercitu, quos fortior avaritiae aculeus terebrabat, longius evacantes, peremebantur ab incolis.

## 36. De conlocutione Gundovaldi cum exercitu.

<a id="hist-07-la-036"></a>
[**[036]**](#hist-07-en-036) Ascendebant enim multi per collem et cum Gundovaldo saepius loquebantur, inferentes ei convitia ac dicentes: 'Tune es pictur ille, qui tempore Chlothacharii regis per oraturia parietis adque camaras caraxabas? Tune es ille, quem Ballomerem nomine saepius Galliarum incolae vocitabant? Tune es ille, qui plerumque a regibus Francorum propter has praesumptiones quas proferis tunsoratus et exilio datus es? Vel quis te, infelicissime hominum, in his locis adduxit, edicito. Quis tibi tantam audaciam praestetit, ut dominorum ac regum nostrorum finis auderis attingere? Certe, si a quoquam es evocatus, clara voce testare. En tibi ante oculus mortem expositam, en ipsam, quam diu quaesisti, exitii foveam, in qua praeceps deiciaris. Die satellites veritim, vel a quibus inviteris enuntia'. At ille, cum haec audiret, propius super portam adstans dicebat: 'Quod me Chlothacharius pater meus exosum habuerit, habetur incognitum nulli; quod autem ab eo vel deinceps a fratribus sim tonsoratus, manifestum est omnibus. Et haec me causa Narsiti praefecto Italiae iuncxit; ibique uxorem accipiens, duos filius generavi. Qua mortua, adsumptis mecum liberis, Constantinopolim abii. Ab imperatoribus vero susceptus benignissime, usque hoc tempore vixi. Ante hos enim annos, cum Gunthchramnus Boso Constantinopolim abissit et ego sollicitus causas fratrum meorum diligenter rimarem, cognovi, generationem nostram valde adtinuatam, nec super esse de stirpe nostra nisi Childeberthum et Gunthchramnum regis, fratrem scilicet et fratris mei filium. Filii enim Chilperici regis cum ipso interierant, uno tantum parvolo derelicto. Gunthchramnus frater meus filius non habebat; Childeberthus nepus noster menime fortis erat. Tunc Gunthchramnus Boso, haec mihi diligenter exposita, invitavit me, dicens: „Veni, quia ab omnibus regni regis Childeberthi principibus invitaris, nec quisquam contra te muttire ausus est. Scimus enim omnes, te filium esse Chlothacharii, nec remansit in Galliis qui regnum illum regere possit, nisi tu advenias". At ego, datis ei multis muneribus, per duodecim loca sancta ab eo suscipio sacramenta, ut securus in hoc regnum accederim. Veni enim Massilia, ibique me episcopus summa benignetate suscepit; habebat enim scripta seniorum regni nepotis mei. Ex hoc enim Avenione accessi iuxta placita patricii Mummoli. Gunthchramnus vero inmemor sacramenti ac promissiones suae, thesauros meos abstulit et in sua dicione subegit. Nunc autem recognuscite, quia ego sum rex, sicut et frater meus Gunthchramnus. Tamen, si tanto odio nostro mens vestra crassatur, vel. ad regem vestrum deducar, et si me cognuscit fratrem, quod voluerit faciat. Certe, si nec hoc volueritis, vel liceat mihi regredi, unde prius egressus sum. Abibo enim et nulli quicquam iniuriae inferam. Tamen, ut sciatis vera esse quod dico, Radegundem Pectavam et Ingutrudem Torcnicam interrogate. Ipsae enim vobis adfirmabunt, certa esse quae loquor'. Haec eo dicente, multi cum convitiis et inproperiis haec verba prosequebantur.

## 37. De bello contra urbem.

<a id="hist-07-la-037"></a>
[**[037]**](#hist-07-en-037) Quintus et decimus in hac obsidione effulserat dies, et Leudeghiselus novas ad distruendam urbem machinas praeparabat, plaustra enim cum arietibus, cletellis et axebus tecta, sub qua exercitus properaret ad distruendos muros. Sed cum adpropinquassent, ita lapidibus obruebantur, ut omnes adpropinquantes muro conruerint. Cupas cum pice et adipe accensas super eos proicientes, alias vero lapidibus plenas super eos deiciebant. Sed cum nox certamina prohiberit, hostis ad castra regressi sunt. Erat autem Gundovaldo et Chariulfus valde dives ac praepotens, cuius aphotecis ac prumptuariis urbs valde referta erat; de cuius substantia hi maxime alebantur. Bladastis autem haec cernens quae gerebantur, metuens, ne Leudeghyselus, obtenta victuria, eos morti traderet, inposito igne in eclesiae domo, concurrentibus ad incendium mitigandum inclusis, ille fuga dilapsus abscessit. Mane autem facto, exercitus iterum ad bella consurgit, ac ex virgis fasces faciunt, quasi ad conplendam vallem profundam, quae a parte orientis sita erat; sed nocere haec machina nihil potuit. Sagittarius vero episcopus frequentius muros cum arma circuibat et saepius lapides contra hostem manu propria eiecit e muro.

## 38. De interitu Gundovaldi.

<a id="hist-07-la-038"></a>
[**[038]**](#hist-07-en-038) Denique hii qui urbem inpugnabant, cum viderint, quod nihil proficere possint, nuntios occultos ad Mummolum dirigunt, dicentes: 'Recognosce dominum tuum et a perversitate ista tandem aliquando desiste. Quae enim te amentia vallat, ut ignoto homine subiungaris? Uxor enim tua iam cum filiis captivata est, filii tui utpute iam interfecti sunt. Quo rues, quidve praestolaris, nisi ut corruas?' Haec ille mandata accipiens, dixit: 'Iam, ut video, regnum nostrum finem accepit, et potentia cadit. Unum superest, si securitatem vitae me habere cognuscerim, de multo vos labore poteram removere'. Discendentibus nuntiis, Sagittarius episcopus cum Mummolo, Chariulfo atque Waddone ad eclesiam pergit, ibique sibi sacramenta dederunt, ut, si de vitae promissione certiores fierint, relicta amicitia Gundovaldi, ipsum hostibus traderent. Reversi iterum nuntii promiserunt eis vitae securitatem. Mummolus vero dixit: 'Hoc tantum fiat; ego hunc in manu vestra tradam, et ego recognuscens dominum meum regem, ad eius praesentiam properabo'. Tunc ille promittunt, quod, si haec impleret, ipsum in caritate susceperint, et si cum rege excusare non possint, in aeclesia ponerent, ne vitae amissione multaretur. Haec cum sacramenti interpositione polliciti, discesserunt. Mummolus vero cum Sagittario episcopo et Waddone ad Gundovaldum pergentes, dixerunt: 'Sacramenta fidelitatis, qualia tibi dedimus, ipse qui praesens es nusti. Nunc autem accipe salubre consilium: discende ab hac urbe et repraesentare fratri tuo, sicut saepe quaesisti. Iam enim cum his hominibus conlocuti sumus, et ipse dixerunt, quia non vult rex perdere solatium tuum, eo quod parum de generatione vestra remanserit'. At ille intellegens dolum eorum, lacrimis perfusus, ait: 'Invitationem vestram in his Galliis sum delatus, thesauros vero meos, in quibus inmensum pondus argenti continetur et auri ac diversarum specierum, aliquid in Avennica urbe retenetur, aliquid Gunthchramnus Boso diripuit. Ego vero, iuxta Dei auxilium spem omnem in vobis positam, vobis consilium meum credidi, per vos regnare semper obtavi. Nunc cum Deo vobis sit actio, si quid mihi mendacii dixeritis; ipse enim iudicet causam meam'. Haec eo dicente, respondit Mummolus; 'Nihil tibi fallaciter loquimur; sed ecce! viros fortissimos stantes ad portam tuum opperientes adventum. Nunc autem depone balteum meum aureum, quod cingeris, ne videaris in iactantia procedere; et tuum adcinge gladium meumque restituae'. Et ille: 'Non simpliciter, inquid, 'haec verba suscipio, ut ea quae de tuis usque nunc in caritate usus sum a me auferantur'. Mummolus vero adserebat cum iuramento, nihil ei molesti fieri. Egressi igitur portam, ab Ollone Biturigum comite et Bosone susceptus est. Mummolus autem cum satellitibus in urbe regressus, portam firmissime obseravit. Hic autem cum se in manibus inimicorum cerneret traditum, elevatis ad caelum manibus et oculis, ait: 'Iudex aeterne et ultio vera innocentium, Deus, a quo omnes iustitia procedit, cui mendacium non placet, in quo nullus dolus neque versutia malitiae continetur, tibi commendo causam meam, dipraecans, ut sis velociter ultor super eos, qui me insontem in manibus tradiderunt inimicorum'. Haec cum dixisset, consignans se cruce dominica, abire coepit cum hominibus supradictis. Cumque a porta elongassint, sicut est circa urbem vallis tota in praecipitio, inpulsus ab Ollone caecidit, illo quoque clamante: 'En vobis Ballomerem vestrum, qui se regis et fratrem dicit et filiumä. Et inmissa lancia voluit eum transfigere, sed repulsa a circulis hiricae nihil nocuit. Denique cum elevatus ad montem regredi niteretur, Boso, emisso lapide, capud eius libravit. Qui caecidit et mortuus est. Venitque omne vulgus, et defixis in eo lanceis, pedes eius fune legantes, per omnia exercituum castra traxerunt; evellentesque caesariem ac barbam eius, insepultum ipso quo interfectus fuerat loco reliquerunt. Nocte vero sequenti hii, qui primi erant, omnes thesauros, quos in urbe repperire potuerunt, cum ministeriis eclesiae clam abstulerunt. Mane vero, reseratis portarum valvis, emisso exercitu, omne vulgus inclusum in ore gladii tradiderunt, sacerdotis quoque Domini cum ministris ad ipsa aeclesiarum altaria trucidantes. Postquam autem cunctus interfecerunt, ut non remaneret mingens ad parietem, omnem urbem cum eclesiis reliquisquae aedificiis succenderunt, nihilque ibi praeter umum vacuum relinquentes.

## 39. De interitu Sagittarii episcopi et Mummoli.

<a id="hist-07-la-039"></a>
[**[039]**](#hist-07-en-039) Igitur Leudeghyselus rediens ad castra cum Mummolo et Sagittario, Chariulfo vel Waddone, nuntios occulte ad regem diregit, quid de his fieri vellit. At ille capitali eos iussit finire sententiam. Waddo tunc cum Chariulfo, relictis filiis obsedibus, discesserunt ab eis. Delato quoque nuntio de horum interitu, cum hoc Mummolus advertissit, accinctus arma ad tugurium Leudeghysili petit. At ille videns eum, ait: 'Quid sic', inquid, 'quasi fugiens venis?' Cui ille: 'Ninil, ut video, de fide promissa servatur; nam cerno me in mortis exitio positum'. Cui ille. 'Ego egrediar foris et omnia mitigabo'. Quo egrediente, confestim ex iusso eius vallata est domus, ut hic interficeretur. Sed et ille, cum diutissime contra bellantes restitisset, venit ad osteum; cumque egrederetur, duo cum lanceis utraque ei latera feriunt. Sicque caecidit et mortuus est. Quod viso episcopus dum timore consternatus paveret, ait ad eum quidam de adstantibus: 'Inspice propriis oculis, episcope, quae gerentur, et tecto capite, ne agnoscaris, silvam pete, ut abscondaris paulolum adque, ira labente, possis evadere'. At ille, accepto consilio, dum obtecto capite fugire niteretur, extracto quidam gladio capud eius cum cocullo decidit. Deinde unusquisque ad propria rediens, magnas per viam praedas et homicidia fecit. Fredegundis autem his diebus Chuppanem in Tholosano direxit, ut scilicet filiam suam exinde quoeumque modo possit eruere. Ferebant enim plerique ob hoc enm transmissum, ut, si Gundovaldum repperisset vivum, multis inlectum promissionibus ad eam transduceret. Sed cum hoc facere nequivissit, acceptam Rigundem a loco illo reduxit, non sine grande humilitate adque contumilia.

## 40. De thesauris Mummoli.

<a id="hist-07-la-040"></a>
[**[040]**](#hist-07-en-040) Igitur Leudeghiselus dux cum thesauris omnibus, quos superius nominavimus, ad regem venit; quos postea rex pauperibus et aeclesiis erogavit. Adpraehensam vero uxorem Mummoli inquirere rex coepit, quid thesauri, quos hii congregaverunt, devenissent. Sed illa cognoscens virum suum interfectum fuisse et omnem iactantiam eorum prorsus in terram conruisse, omnia pandit, dixitque, multum adhuc apud urbem Avennecam auri adque argenti esse, quae ad regis notitiam non venissent. Statimque misit rex viros, qui haec deferre deberint, cum uno puero, quem valde creditum Mummolus habens, haec ei commendaverat. Abeuntes autem acceperunt omnia quae in urbe relicta fuerant. Ferunt autem ducenta quinquaginta talenta argenti fuisse, auri vero amplius quam triginta. Sed haec, ut ferunt, de reperto antiquo thesauro abstulit. Quod rex diviso cum Childebertho rege, nepote suo, partem suam maxime pauperibus est largitus, mulieri autem nihil amplius quam ea, quae de parentibus habuerat, derelinquens.

## 41. De gigante.

<a id="hist-07-la-041"></a>
[**[041]**](#hist-07-en-041) Tunc et homo ille inmensi corporis ad regem de Mummoli familiaribus adductus est, ita magni corporis elatus, ut duos aut tres pedes super longissimus hcminess putaretur magnus, lignarius faber, qui non multo post obiit.

## 42. De virtute sancti Martini.

<a id="hist-07-la-042"></a>
[**[042]**](#hist-07-en-042) Post haec edictum a iudicibus datum est, ut qui in hac expeditione tardi fuerant damnarentur. Biturigum quoque comes misit pueros suos, ut in domo beati Martini, quae in hoc termino sita est, huiusmodi homines spoliare deberent. Sed agens domus illius resistere fortiter coepit, dicens: 'Sancti Martini homines hii sunt. Nihil eis quicquam inferatis iniuriae, quia non habuerunt consuetudinem in talibus causis abire'. At illi dixerunt: 'Nihil nobis et Martino tuo, quem semper in causis inaniter proferis; sed et tu et ipsi pretia dissolvitis, pro eo quod regis imperium neglexistis'. Et haec dicens ingressus est atrium domus. Protinus dolore percussus caecidit et graviter agere coepit. Conversusque ad agentem voce flebili ait: 'Rogo, ut facias super me crucem Domini et invoces nomen beati Martini. Nunc autem cognovi, quod magna est virtus eius. Nam ingrediente me atrium domus, vidi virum senem exhibentem arborem in manu sua, quae mox extensis ramis omne atrium texit. Ex ea enim unus me adtigit ramus, de cuius ictu turbatus corruis'. Et innuens suis rogabat, ut eieceretur de atrio. Egressus autem invocare nomen beati Martini attentius coepit. Ex hcc enim commodius agens, sanatus est.

## 43. De Desiderio et Waddone.

<a id="hist-07-la-043"></a>
[**[043]**](#hist-07-en-043) Desiderius vero infra castrorum munitione se resque suas tutavit. Waddo maior domus Rigundis ad Brunichildem reginam transiits, et ab ea susceptus, cum muneribus et gratia est demissus. Chariulfus basilicam sancti Martini expetiit.

## 44. De muliere phitonissa.

<a id="hist-07-la-044"></a>
[**[044]**](#hist-07-en-044) Fuit tunc temporis mulier, quae spiritum phitonis habens multum praestabat dominis divinando questum eoque in gratia proficit, ut, ab his libera facta, suis voluntatibus laxaretur. Si quis enim aut furtum aut aliquid mali perferret, statim haec, quo fur abiit, cui tradedit vel quid ex hoc fecerit, edicebat. Congregabat cotidie aurum argentumque, procedens in ornamentis, ita ut putaretur esse aliquid divinum in populis. Sed cum Agerico Veridunense episcopo haec nuntiata fuissent, misit ad conpraehendendum eam. Quam adprehensam et ad se adductam, iuxta id quod in Actibus legimus apostolorum, cognovit in eam inmundum spiritum esse phitonis. Denique cum exorcismum super eam diceret ac frontem oleo sancto perungueret, exclamavit daemonium et quid esset prodidit sacerdoti. Sed cum per eum a puella non extruderetur, abire permissa est. Cernens vero puella, quod in loco illo habitare non possit, ad Fredegundem reginam abiit ibique et latuit.

## 45. De fame anni praesentis.

<a id="hist-07-la-045"></a>
[**[045]**](#hist-07-en-045) Magna hoc anno famis paene Gallias totas obpressit. Nam plurimi uvarum semina, flores avellanorum, nonnulli radices herbae filicis arefactas redactasque in pulvere, admiscentes parumper farinae, panem conficiebant. Multi enim herba segitum decidentes, similiter faciebant. Fuerunt etiam multi, quibus non erat aliquid farinae, qui diversas colligentes herbas et comedentes, tumefacti deficiebant. Plurimi enim tunc ex inaedia tabescentes, mortui sunt. Graviter tunc negutiatores populum spoliaverunt, ita ut vix vel modium annonae aut semodium vini uno triante venundarent. Subdebant pauperes servitio, ut quantulumcumque de alimenta porregerent.

## 46. De interitu Christofori.

<a id="hist-07-la-046"></a>
[**[046]**](#hist-07-en-046) His diebus Cristoforus negutiatur ad Aurilianensem urbem abiit. Audierat enim, quod ibidem multum vini delatum fuisset. Abiens ergo, conparato vino et lintribus invecto, accepto a sccero pecunia multa, cum duobus pueris Saxonibus viam equitando terebat. Pueri vero diu dominum exosum habentes et plerumque fuga labentes, eo quod crebrius gravissime verberarentur, cum venissent in quadam silva, praecedente domino, puer unus iaculata valide lancea dominum suum transfixit. Quo ruente, alius cum framea capud eius dilaceravit. Et sic ab utroque in frustra decisus, exanimis est relictus. Hii vero accipientes pecuniam, fuga dilapsi sunt. Frater vero Cristofori, sepulto corpuscolo, homines suos post pueros dirigit. Iuniore quoque conpraehenso legant, seniore cum pecunia fugiente. Quibus redeuntibus, cum vinctum laxius reliquissent, accepta lancea, unum ex his a quibus ducebatur interemit. Sed deductus ab aliis usque Toronus, diversis suppliciis adfectus detruncatusquae, patibolo valde exanimis est adpensus.

## 47. De bello civile inter cives Toronicus.

<a id="hist-07-la-047"></a>
[**[047]**](#hist-07-en-047) Gravia tunc inter Toronicos cives bella civilia surrexerunt. Nam Sicharius, Iohannis quondam filius, dum ad natalis dominici solemnia apud Montalomaginsim vicum cum Austrigbyselo reliquosque pagenses caelebraret, presbiter lcci misit puerum ad aliquorum hominum invitationem, ut ad domum eius bibendi gratia venire deberint. Veniente vero puero, unus ex his qui invitabantur, extracto gladio, eum ferire non metuit. Qui statim cecidit et mortuus est. Quod cum Sicharius audisset, qui amicitias cum presbitero retinebat, quod scilicet puer eius fuerit interfectus, arrepta arma ad eclesiam petit, Austrighyselum opperiens. Ille autem haec audiens, adpraehenso armorum apparatu, contra eum diregit. Mixtisque omnibus, cum se pars utraque conliderit, Sicharius inter clericos ereptus, ad villam suam effugit, relictis in domo presbiteri cum argento et vestimentis quattuor pueris sauciatis. Quo fugiente, Austrighyselus iterum inruens, interfectis pueris, aurum argentumque cum reliquis rebus abstulit. Dehinc cum in iudicio civium convenissent et praeceptum esset, ut Austrighyselus, qui homicida erat et, interfectis pueris, res sine audientia diripuerat, censura legali condempnaretur, inito placito, paucis infra diebus Sicharius audiens, quod res, quas Austrighyselus direpuerat, cum Aunone et filio adque eius fratre Eberulfo retinerentur, postposito placito, coniunctus Audino, mota seditione, cum armatis viris inruit super eos nocte, elisumque hospicium, in quo dormiebant, patrem cum fratre et filio interemit resque eorum cum pecoribus, interfectisque servis, abduxit. Quod nos audientes, vehimenter ex hoc molesti, adiuncto iudice, mittimus ad eos legationem, ut in nostri praesentia venientes, accepta ratione, cum pace discederent, ne iurgium in amplius pulularet. Quibus venientibus coniunctisque civibus, ego aio: 'Nolite, o viri, in sceleribus proficere, ne malum longius extendatur. Perdidimus enim aeclesiae filios; metuemus nunc, ne et alios in hac intentione careamus. Estote, quaeso, pacifici; et qui malum gessit, stante caritate, conponat, ut sitis filii pacifici, qui digni sitis regno Dei, ipso Domino tribuente, percipere. Sic enim ipse ait: Beati pacifici, quoniam filii? Dei vocabuntur. Ecce enim! etsi illi, qui noxae subditur, minor est facultas, argento aeclesiae redemitur; interim anima viri non pereat'. Et haec dicens, optuli argentum aeclesiae; sed pars Chramnesindi, quae mortem patris fratrisque et patrui requirebat, accepere noluit. His discedentibus, Sicharius iter, ut ad regem ambularet, praeparat, et ob hoc Pectavum ad uxorem cernendam proficiscitur. Cumque servum, ut exerceret opera, commoneret elevatamque virgam ictibus verberaret, ille, extracto baltei gladio, dominum sauciare non metuit. Quo in terra ruente, currentes amici adpraehensum servum crudeliter caesum, truncatis manibus et pedibus, patibolo damnaverunt. Interim sonus in Toronicum exiit, Sicharium fuisse defunctum. Cum autem haec Chramnesindus audisset, commonitis parentibus et amicis, ad domum eius properat. Quibus spoliatis, interemptis nonnullis servorum, domus omnes tam Sichari quam reliquorum, qui participes huius villae erant, incendio concremavit, abducens secum pecora vel quaecumque movere potuit. Tunc partes a iudice ad civitatem deductae, causas proprias prolocuntur; inventumque est a iudicibus, ut, qui nollens accepere prius conpositionem domus incendiis tradedit, medietatem praetii, quod ei fuerat iudicatum, amitteret – et hoc contra legis actum, ut tantum pacifici redderentur – aliam vero medietatem conpositionis Sicharius redderet. Tunc datum ab aeclesia argentum, quae iudicaverant, accepta securitate, conposuit, datis sibi partes invicem sacramentis, ut nullo umquam tempore contra alterum pais alia musitaret. Et sic altercatio terminum fecit. EXPLICIT LIBER VII.
