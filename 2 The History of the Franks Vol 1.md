### Preface

<a id="hist-02-en-001"></a>
[**[001]**](#hist-02-la-001) Following the order of time, I record in mixed and interwoven fashion both the virtues of the saints and the slaughter of the peoples. I do not think it should be considered unreasonable if I mention the blessed life of the saints amid the destruction of the wretched, since the sequence of time, not any arbitrary arrangement of the writing, has produced this order. If the attentive reader inquires closely, he finds in the histories of the kings of Israel that under the just Samuel Phinehas the sacrilegious man perished, and under David, whom they call strong of hand, Goliath the Philistine fell. Let him also remember, in the time of the distinguished prophet Elijah—who withheld rain when he wished and poured it upon the parched lands when he pleased, and enriched the widow’s poverty by prayer—how great were the slaughters of peoples, what famine and what drought afflicted the wretched earth. Under Ezekiel too, to whom God added fifteen years of life, what evils Jerusalem suffered. Under the prophet Elisha, who restored the dead to life and performed many other miracles among the peoples, what destruction and what miseries afflicted the Israelite people itself. Thus Eusebius, Severus, and Jerome in their chronicles, and Orosius likewise, wove together both the wars of kings and the virtues of martyrs. I too have written in this way so that the order of the ages and the calculation of the years down to my own time may be found more readily as a whole. Coming therefore through the histories of the aforesaid authors, I shall relate at the Lord’s command the things done afterward.

### 1. Of Bricius, the second bishop of Tours after Martin

<a id="hist-02-en-002"></a>
[**[002]**](#hist-02-la-002) After the death of blessed Martin, bishop of Tours, that supreme and incomparable man whose miracles are preserved in great volumes among me, Bricius succeeded to the bishopric. While still a very young man, Bricius had often laid snares for the saint during his lifetime because Martin frequently rebuked him for pursuing frivolous things. One day a sick man seeking a cure from blessed Martin met Bricius, then still a deacon, in the street and innocently said: “I am waiting for the blessed man, but I do not know where he is or what he is doing.” Bricius answered: “If you are looking for that madman, look into the distance: there he is, staring at the sky as usual like a lunatic.” When the poor man had met Martin and obtained his request, the blessed man addressed Deacon Bricius: “So, Bricius, do I seem mad to you?” Confused, Bricius denied having said it, but the holy man said: “Were my ears not at your mouth when you spoke from afar? Amen, I tell you, I have obtained from God that you shall attain the honour of the episcopate after me, but know that you will suffer many adversities in your bishopric.” Hearing this, Bricius laughed and said: “Did I not say truly that he utters mad words?” Even after receiving the honour of the priesthood, he frequently provoked the blessed man with insults.

When Bricius attained the episcopal office with the citizens’ consent, he devoted himself to prayer. Although proud and vain, he was chaste in body. In the thirty-third year of his ordination a lamentable charge arose against him. A woman who wore religious dress and to whom his chamberlains brought garments for washing conceived and bore a child. All the people of Tours rose in anger, laid the whole blame on the bishop, and unanimously wished to stone him. They said: “The saint’s piety long concealed your wantonness, but God no longer permits me to pollute myself by kissing your unworthy hands.” Bricius manfully denied the charge and said: “Bring the infant to me.” The child, thirty days old, was brought. The bishop said: “I adjure you by Jesus Christ, Son of almighty God, to declare before everyone if I begot you.” The infant answered: “You are not my father.” When the people asked him to inquire who the father was, the bishop said: “That is not my business. I have dealt with what concerned me; if you wish to know more, ask for yourselves.” Declaring that this had been done by magic arts, they rose against him in a conspiracy and dragged him away, saying: “You shall no longer lord it over me under the false name of shepherd.” To satisfy the people further, he put burning coals in his cloak, drew it around himself, and walked with the crowds to blessed Martin’s tomb. When he cast the coals before the tomb, his garment appeared unburned. He said: “Just as you see this garment unharmed by the fire, so my body is unpolluted by any woman’s touch.” They still did not believe him but contradicted him, dragged him, falsely accused him, and drove him out, fulfilling the saint’s words: “Know that you will suffer many adversities in your bishopric.”

Having expelled Bricius, they appointed Justinian to the bishopric. Bricius sought the pope of Rome, weeping and lamenting: “I deserve to suffer this because I sinned against the holy man of God, often called him mad and demented, and did not believe when I saw his miracles.” After Bricius’s departure the people of Tours said to their bishop: “Go after him and pursue your case; if you do not follow him, you will be humbled before the contempt of us all.” Justinian left Tours and reached Vercelli in Italy, where, struck by God’s judgment, he died in a foreign land. Hearing of his death and persisting in their malice, the people of Tours appointed Armentius in his place. Bishop Bricius came to Rome and told the pope everything he had suffered. He remained at the apostolic see and often celebrated mass there, weeping for every sin he had committed against the holy man of God.

In the seventh year he returned from Rome and prepared to go back to Tours with that pope’s authority. He came to the village called Laudiacum, six miles from the city, and took lodging. Armentius was seized by fever and died at midnight. This was immediately revealed to Bishop Bricius in a vision. He told his companions: “Rise quickly so that I may go to meet my brother, the bishop of Tours, for burial.” As they entered one city gate, Armentius was being carried out dead through another. After burying him, Bricius returned to his see and lived happily for seven more years. He died in the forty-seventh year of his episcopate, and Saint Eustochius, a man of magnificent holiness, succeeded him.

### 2. Of the Vandals and the persecution under them

<a id="hist-02-en-003"></a>
[**[003]**](#hist-02-la-003) After this the Vandals left their own territory and, led by King Gunderic, rushed into the Gauls. After laying them waste, they made for the Spains. The Suebi—that is, the Alamanni—followed them and seized Galicia. Not long afterward a quarrel arose between the two peoples, because they were neighbors. When they had advanced armed for battle and were on the point of engaging, the king of the Alamanni said: “How long will war be stirred up against the whole people? I beg you, do not let the armies of both peoples perish. Instead, let two from our sides come forward onto the field with weapons of war and fight each other. The side whose champion wins will possess the region without a battle.” The whole people agreed, so that the entire multitude would not fall by the sword. Gunderic had died in those days, and Trasamund had obtained the kingdom in his place. When the champions fought, the Vandal side was defeated; its champion was killed, and Trasamund promised to carry out the agreed departure. Thus, after preparing what was needed for the journey, he would remove himself from the territory of Spain.

At the same time Trasamund persecuted the Christians and tried to force all Spain, by tortures and various forms of death, to consent to the perfidy of the Arian sect. A certain religious girl was therefore brought to this trial. She was very wealthy, distinguished by senatorial nobility in worldly rank and, nobler than all these things, strong in the catholic faith; she served almighty God blamelessly. When she had been brought before the king, he first tried to entice her with flattering words to be rebaptized. When she repelled his poisoned dart with the shield of faith, the king ordered her property confiscated, though her mind already possessed the kingdom of paradise; he then ordered her to be afflicted with tortures, without hope of surviving in the present life. What more need be said? After many torments and the loss of the treasure of her earthly riches, when she could not be broken into dividing the blessed Trinity, she was taken unwillingly to be rebaptized. As she was being forcibly immersed in that filthy bath, she cried out: “I believe that the Father, with the Son and the Holy Spirit, is of one substance and essence.” She stained all the waters with a fitting ointment—that is, she sprinkled them with a discharge from her bowels. She was then taken to the lawful torture; after racks, flames, and claws, she was consecrated to Christ the Lord by the cutting off of her head. Afterward the Alamanni pursued the Vandals as far as Traducta; the Vandals crossed the sea and spread through all Africa and Mauritania.

### 3. Of Cyrola the heretic, and of the holy martyrs

<a id="hist-02-en-004"></a>
[**[004]**](#hist-02-la-004) Because persecution of Christians grew strong in their time, as mentioned above, it seems right to record some of the things they inflicted on God’s churches and how they were expelled from the kingdom. After Trasamund died, following the crimes he had committed against God’s saints, Huneric, still more cruel in mind, seized the African kingdom and was placed over the Vandals by their own choice. No human being can comprehend how many Christian people were killed in his time for Christ’s most sacred name. Africa, which sent them forth, and Christ’s right hand, which crowned them with unfading jewels, are witnesses. Nevertheless, I read the passions of some of these martyrs, and certain parts must be repeated here so that I may come to the events I promised to relate.

Cyrola, falsely called a bishop, was then regarded as the greatest champion of the heretics. When the king sent agents through different regions to persecute the Christians, the persecutor found Saint Bishop Eugenius, truly a man of indescribable holiness and reputed to possess great prudence, in a suburb of his own city. He seized him so violently that he did not even permit him to leave after exhorting the flock of Christ’s worshippers. Seeing that he was being taken away by force, Eugenius sent this letter to his citizens for the preservation of the catholic faith:

“Eugenius, bishop, to my most beloved sons and daughters, most sweet in the love of Christ, of the church committed to me by God. Royal authority has gone forth, and an edict has ordered me to come to Carthage for the profession of the catholic faith. Therefore, lest by departing I should leave God’s church uncertain—that is, in suspense—or silently abandon Christ’s sheep like an unfaithful shepherd, I have thought it necessary to send this letter to your holiness in my place. In it, not without tears, I ask, exhort, and warn you, and solemnly and abundantly adjure you by God’s majesty, by the terrible day of judgment, and by the fearsome splendor of Christ’s coming, to hold the catholic faith firmly, professing that the Son has the same deity as the Father and that the Holy Spirit has the same deity as the Father and the Son. Guard the grace of the one baptism and preserve the anointing of chrism. Let no one who has been reborn from water return to the water after the water. At God’s command salt is made in water, but if it is dissolved back into water, its whole appearance is immediately lost. Therefore the Lord not undeservedly says in the Gospel: ‘If the salt has become tasteless, with what shall it be salted?’ To wish to be seasoned a second time, when once is sufficient, is indeed to become tasteless. Have you not heard Christ say: ‘He who has once been washed has no need to wash again?’ Therefore, brothers and my sons and daughters in the Lord, do not let my absence grieve you. If you adhere to catholic discipline, I shall neither forget you across any distance nor be separated from you by death. Know that, wherever my struggles cause me to be taken, the palm is with me. If I go into exile, I have the example of blessed John the evangelist; if I go to the destruction of death, ‘for me to live is Christ, and to die is gain.’ If I return, brothers, God will fulfill your desire. For now it is enough that I have not remained silent toward you. I have warned and instructed you as I could; I am innocent of the blood of all who perish, and I know that this letter will be read against them before Christ’s tribunal when He comes to repay each person according to his works. If I return, brothers, I shall see you in this life; if I do not return, I shall see you in the life to come. I say to you: farewell, pray for me, and fast, because fasting and almsgiving have always inclined Him toward mercy. Remember what is written in the Gospel: ‘Do not fear those who kill the body but cannot kill the soul; rather fear Him who, after He has killed, has power to destroy both soul and body and cast them into Gehenna.’”

When Saint Eugenius had been brought to the king, he disputed with the Arian bishop about the catholic faith. He defeated him most powerfully concerning the mystery of the Holy Trinity, and Christ moreover displayed many miracles through him; the bishop therefore burned with still greater madness as envy inflamed him. At that time the most prudent and holy bishops Vindimialis and Longinus were with Saint Eugenius, equal in rank and not unequal in virtue. Saint Vindimialis was reputed at that time to have raised the dead, while Longinus gave health to many sick people. Eugenius too drove away not only the blindness of bodily eyes but also that of minds.

Seeing this, the wicked bishop of the Arians summoned a man who shared the error in which he himself lived and said: “I cannot bear that these bishops produce many signs among the people and that everyone follows them while neglecting me. Now obey what I command. Accept fifty gold pieces and sit in the street by which I am to pass. Put your hand over your closed eyes and, as I pass with the others, cry out loudly: ‘To you, most blessed Cyrola, chief of our religion, I pray: look upon me and reveal your glory and power; open my eyes, that I may deserve to see the light I have lost.’” The man obeyed and sat in the street. As the heretic passed with God’s saints, this man who had planned to mock God cried out loudly: “Hear me, most blessed Cyrola; hear me, holy priest of God; look upon my blindness! Let me experience the remedies which other blind people have often obtained from you, which lepers have experienced, and which the dead themselves have felt. I adjure you by the very power you possess to restore the light I desire, for I have been struck by grievous blindness.” Though ignorant of the truth, he spoke truly, for greed had blinded him, and he had thought to mock almighty God’s power for money.

The heretics’ bishop turned aside a little, as though about to triumph by a miracle. Lifted up by vanity and pride, he placed his hand over the man’s eyes and said: “According to our faith, by which we rightly believe in God, let your eyes be opened.” As soon as this blasphemy burst from him, laughter changed to lamentation and the bishop’s deceit was exposed in public. Such pain invaded the wretched man’s eyes that he pressed them forcibly with his fingers to keep them from bursting. The miserable man began to cry out: “Woe to me in my misery, for I have been seduced by the enemy of divine law! Woe to me, for I wished to mock God for money and accepted fifty gold pieces to perpetrate this crime!” To the bishop he said: “Here is your gold; restore my sight, which I lost through your deceit! I beg you also, most glorious Christians, do not despise a wretch, but come quickly to one who is perishing! I have truly learned that God is not mocked.”

God’s saints, moved by mercy, said: “If you believe, all things are possible to one who believes.” He cried aloud: “May whoever has not believed that Christ the Son of God and the Holy Spirit possess substance and deity equal to the Father suffer today what I endure.” He added: “I believe in God the Father almighty; I believe in Jesus Christ, the Son of God, equal to the Father; I believe in the Holy Spirit, consubstantial and coeternal with the Father and the Son.” Hearing this and outdoing one another in mutual honor, the saints began a holy contention over who should place the sign of the blessed cross on his eyes. Vindimialis and Longinus urged Eugenius, while he begged them to lay hands on the blind man. When they had done so and held their hands over his head, Saint Eugenius made Christ’s cross over the blind man’s eyes and said: “In the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, the true God, whom I confess as three in one equality and omnipotence, let your eyes be opened.” The pain was immediately removed, and he returned to his former health.

Through this man’s blindness it became most plain how the heretics’ bishop veiled the eyes of wretched hearts with the cloak of his assertions, lest anyone be permitted to contemplate the true light with the eyes of faith. Miserable man! He had not entered through the door—that is, through Christ, who is the true door—and had become a wolf to the flock rather than its guardian. The torch of faith which he ought to have kindled in believers’ hearts he tried instead to extinguish in the depravity of his own heart.

God’s saints performed many other signs among the people, and the people spoke with one voice: “True God the Father, true God the Son, true God the Holy Spirit: to be worshipped with one faith, feared with one fear, and venerated with the same honor. It is now plain to everyone that all Cyrola’s assertions are false.” King Huneric saw that his assertions were being overthrown by the saints’ glorious faith, that the sect of error was not being raised up but destroyed, and that his bishop’s disgrace in this crime had been exposed. After many tortures—racks, flames, and claws—he ordered God’s saints to be killed. He ordered blessed Eugenius to be beheaded, but on this condition: if, at the very hour when the sword rested on his neck, he did not return to the heretics’ sect, he would not be killed, lest the Christians venerate him as a martyr, but would be condemned to exile. This is clearly what happened. Questioned as death approached whether he was resolved to die for the catholic faith, he answered: “This indeed is eternal life: to die for righteousness.” The sword was stayed, and he was sent into exile at Albi, a city of Gaul, where he ended his present life. Many miracles are now frequently manifested at his tomb.

He ordered Saint Vindimialis to be struck with the sword, and this was carried out. In this struggle Archdeacon Octavian and many thousands of men and women professing this faith were killed or maimed. Yet for love of glory these torments were nothing to the holy confessors. Troubled for a little while, they knew well that abundant glory was being prepared for them, according to the apostle: “The sufferings of this time are not worthy to be compared with the future glory which shall be revealed in the saints.” Many who then strayed from the faith and accepted riches plunged themselves into many sorrows, as did that wretched bishop named Revocatus, who was called back from the catholic faith. At that time the sun also appeared so dark that scarcely a third part of it shone, I believe because of such great crimes and the shedding of innocent blood.

After so great a crime Huneric was seized by a demon which had long fed on the saints’ blood, and he tore himself with his own teeth. In this torment he ended an unworthy life by a just death. Childeric succeeded him; when Childeric died, Gelimer took the kingdom. He too was defeated by the republic and ended his life and rule. Thus the kingdom of the Vandals fell.

### 4. Of the persecution under Athanaric

<a id="hist-02-en-005"></a>
[**[005]**](#hist-02-la-005) Many heresies were attacking God’s churches at that time, and divine vengeance was often exacted for them. Athanaric, king of the Goths, stirred up a great persecution. He afflicted many Christians with various punishments and cut them down with the sword; others he sent into exile and destroyed by hunger and various torments. Therefore, as God’s judgment drew near, he who had attacked God’s churches was driven from his kingdom and became an exile from his homeland in retribution for the shedding of righteous blood. But let me return to earlier events.

### 5. Of Aravatius the bishop, and of the Huns

<a id="hist-02-en-006"></a>
[**[006]**](#hist-02-la-006) A rumor spread that the Huns intended to burst into the Gauls. At that time Aravatius, a bishop of exceptional holiness, lived in the town of Tongres. Devoting himself to vigils and fasts and repeatedly drenched in showers of tears, he begged the Lord’s mercy never to permit this unbelieving nation, always unworthy of Him, to come into the Gauls. But he perceived in the Spirit that this had not been granted him because of the people’s offenses. He therefore resolved to seek the city of Rome, so that, with the patronage of apostolic power joined to his petition, he might more readily obtain what he humbly asked of the Lord’s mercy.

Coming to the blessed apostle’s tomb, he prayed for the help of his goodness. He consumed himself with great abstinence and extreme fasting, remaining for two or three days without any food or drink, and there was no interval in which he ceased from prayer. When he had remained there in such affliction for many days, he is said to have received this answer from the blessed apostle: “Why do you trouble me, most holy man? It has already been decreed in the Lord’s counsel that the Huns are to come into the Gauls and lay them waste in a mighty storm. Now therefore take counsel: hurry quickly, set your house in order, prepare your burial, and seek clean linen! For you will depart from the body, and your eyes will not see the evils which the Huns are to commit in the Gauls, as the Lord our God has spoken.”

On receiving this answer from the holy apostle, the bishop hastened his journey and swiftly returned to the Gauls. Coming to the city of Tongres, he quickly gathered what was necessary for his burial. Taking leave of the clergy and the other citizens of the city, he announced with weeping and lamentation that they would see his face no more. They followed him with great wailing and tears, entreating him with humble prayers: “Do not abandon us, holy father; do not forget us, good shepherd!” When they could not call him back with their tears, they received his blessing and kisses and returned home.

He then came to the city of Maastricht. Afflicted by a slight fever, he departed from the body; the faithful washed him, and he was buried beside the public highway. I have written in the Book of Miracles how his blessed body was translated after a long interval of time.

### 6. Of the oratory of Saint Stephen at Metz

<a id="hist-02-en-007"></a>
[**[007]**](#hist-02-la-007) The Huns then set out from Pannonia and, as some report, arrived at the city of Metz on the very vigil of holy Easter, devastating everything else as they came. They gave the city to fire, slaughtered the people by the sword, and killed the Lord’s priests before the most holy altars. No place in it remained unburned except the oratory of blessed Stephen, the first martyr and deacon. I have not hesitated to relate what I have heard from certain people about this oratory.

They say that before these enemies came, a faithful man saw in a vision the blessed deacon Stephen conferring, as it were, with the holy apostles Peter and Paul about this destruction and saying: “I beg you, my lords, do not permit the city of Metz to be burned by the enemy through your intercession, for there is a place in it where the relics of my lowliness are kept. Rather, let the people perceive that I have some power with the Lord. But if the people’s crime has grown so great that nothing else can be done and the city must be given to fire, at least let this oratory not be burned.” The apostles answered him: “Go in peace, most beloved brother; your oratory alone will escape the fire! I shall not obtain this for the city, because the sentence of the Lord’s decree has already gone forth against it. The people’s sin has prevailed, and the cry of their wickedness has ascended before God; therefore this city will be burned with fire.” There is no doubt, therefore, that through their intercession the oratory remained unharmed when the city was laid waste.

### 7. Of the wife of Aetius

<a id="hist-02-en-008"></a>
[**[008]**](#hist-02-la-008) Attila, king of the Huns, left the city of Metz and, while overwhelming many cities of the Gauls, attacked Orléans and tried to capture it with powerful blows from battering rams. The most blessed Anianus was then bishop of that city, a man of exceptional prudence and praiseworthy holiness; faithful accounts of his miracles are preserved among us. When the people shut within the city cried out to their bishop, asking what they should do, he trusted in God and instructed everyone to prostrate themselves in prayer and with tears implore the Lord’s help, which is always present in times of need. When they had prayed as he directed, the priest said: “Look from the city wall to see whether God’s mercy is already coming to our aid.” He hoped through the Lord’s mercy that Aetius was coming; he had previously gone to him at Arles because he suspected what was to happen. They looked from the wall but saw no one. He said: “Pray faithfully, for the Lord will deliver you today!” While they prayed, he said: “Look again!” They looked but saw no one bringing help. He told them a third time: “If you ask faithfully, the Lord will quickly be at hand.” With weeping and great wailing they implored the Lord’s mercy. When the prayer was finished, they looked from the wall a third time at the old man’s command and saw in the distance something like a cloud rising from the earth. When they reported this, the priest said: “It is the Lord’s help.”

Meanwhile the walls were already shaking under the rams’ blows and were about to fall, when Aetius, Theodoric king of the Goths, and his son Thorismud came running to the city with their armies and drove out and repelled the enemy. The city was liberated through the blessed bishop’s intercession, and they put Attila to flight. Attila went to the Mauriac plain and prepared for battle. When Aetius and his allies heard this, they prepared themselves bravely against him.

In those days a report reached Rome that Aetius was struggling in the greatest danger amid the enemy’s ranks. His wife, anxious and sorrowful at the news, went continually to the basilicas of the holy apostles and prayed to receive her husband back safe from this campaign. While she did this day and night, a poor man, drunk with wine, fell asleep one night in a corner of the basilica of blessed Peter the apostle. The doors were closed according to custom, and the guards did not find him. Rising in the night, with lamps shining throughout the building, he was terrified and looked for a way out. But when he tried the doors, first one and then another, and found everything barred, he lay down on the floor and waited anxiously until the people assembled for morning hymns and he could leave freely.

Meanwhile he saw two figures greeting each other with veneration and asking solicitously about one another’s welfare. The elder began: “I can no longer endure the tears of Aetius’s wife. She continually asks me to bring her husband back safe from the Gauls, although something different had been determined by divine judgment. Nevertheless, I have obtained boundless mercy for his life, and now I am hurrying to bring him back from there alive. But I adjure you: let whoever hears this keep silent and not dare to make God’s secret known, lest he quickly perish from the earth.”

The man who heard this could not keep silent. As soon as daylight came, he disclosed everything he had heard to the wife. When he finished speaking, he lost the sight of his eyes. Aetius then joined the Goths and Franks in battle against Attila. Seeing his army being destroyed, Attila escaped in flight. Theodoric, king of the Goths, fell in this struggle. No one should doubt that the Hunnish army was put to flight through the intercession of the bishop already mentioned. Aetius the patrician and Thorismod obtained the victory and destroyed the enemy.

When the battle was over, Aetius said to Thorismod: “Hurry back quickly to your homeland, lest your brother press on and you be deprived of your father’s kingdom.” Hearing this, Thorismod departed swiftly, as though to anticipate his brother and obtain his father’s throne first. Aetius drove away the king of the Franks by a similar trick. After they had withdrawn, Aetius stripped the battlefield and returned victoriously to his own country with great spoils. Attila returned with only a few men. Not long afterward Aquileia was captured, burned, and razed by the Huns, and Italy was overrun and devastated. Thorismod, whom I mentioned above, subdued the Alans in war; afterward, following many quarrels and battles, he was overcome by his brothers and died by slaughter.

### 8. What the historians wrote of Aetius

<a id="hist-02-en-009"></a>
[**[009]**](#hist-02-la-009) Now that these events have been related and completed in order, I have thought it wrong to pass over in silence what the history of Renatus Frigeridus records about the Aetius mentioned above. In the twelfth book of his Histories he relates that after the death of the divine Honorius, the little boy Valentinian, only five years old, was made emperor by his cousin Theodosius; that the tyrant John rose to imperial power in the city of Rome; and that his envoys were treated with contempt by the Caesar. Renatus adds:

“While these things were happening, the envoys returned to the tyrant bearing harsh instructions. Moved by them, John sent Aetius, then keeper of the palace, to the Huns with an immense weight of gold. Aetius had been known to them and bound to them in close friendship since the time of his own service as a hostage. John sent instructions that, as soon as the opposing forces entered Italy, Aetius should attack them from the rear while John advanced against their front. Because many things about this man must be related in sequence, it is fitting to begin with his family and character. His father Gaudentius, a man of leading rank in the province of Scythia, began his military service among the domestics and advanced to the highest office of master of the cavalry. His mother was an Italian, a noble and wealthy woman. Their son Aetius belonged to the praetorians from boyhood; he was a hostage for three years with Alaric and then with the Huns. Afterward he became Carpilio’s son-in-law, having served as count of the domestics and John’s keeper of the palace. He was of medium height and manly build, handsomely formed so as to be neither weak nor heavy, alert in mind and vigorous in limb, a most ready horseman, skilled in shooting arrows, and tireless with the spear. He was most suited to war and renowned for the arts of peace. He had no avarice and very little cupidity; he was endowed with good qualities of mind and did not turn from his purpose even under evil persuasion. He was most patient of injuries, eager for labor, fearless amid dangers, and most enduring of hunger, thirst, and sleeplessness. It is clear that from his earliest years it was foretold how great a power he was destined by fate to attain, to be celebrated in his own times and places.”

This is what the aforesaid historian relates about Aetius. When Valentinian grew up, he feared that Aetius would overthrow him by usurpation and killed him although no charges existed. Afterward, while the emperor Augustus himself was seated on a tribunal in the Campus Martius addressing the people, Occila, Aetius’s bodyguard, came from the opposite direction and ran him through with a sword. Such was the end of both men.

### 9. What the same writers say of the Franks

<a id="hist-02-en-010"></a>
[**[010]**](#hist-02-la-010) Many people do not know who was the first king of the Franks. Although Sulpicius Alexander’s history says much about them, it nowhere names their first king, but says that they had dukes. It seems right to record what he relates about them. After saying that Maximus remained inside Aquileia, having lost all hope of empire and as though out of his mind, he adds:

“At that time the Franks, led by the dukes Genobaudes, Marcomer, and Sunno, burst into Germany. They broke through the border, killed many people, devastated especially the fertile districts, and struck fear even into Colonia Agrippina. When this news reached Trier, the military commanders Nanninus and Quintinus, to whom Maximus had committed his son’s infancy and the defense of the Gauls, assembled an army and met at Agrippina. But the enemy, laden with plunder after stripping the richest parts of the provinces, crossed the Rhine. They left many of their men on Roman soil, prepared to renew the devastation. An engagement with these men proved advantageous to the Romans, and many Franks were killed by the sword at Carbonaria. When the angry soldiers debated whether they ought to cross into Francia, Nanninus refused, because he knew that the Franks would not be unprepared and would undoubtedly be stronger in their own territory. Since this displeased Quintinus and the other military men, Nanninus returned to Mainz, while Quintinus crossed the Rhine with the army near the fortress of Neuss. After two days’ march from the river, he found huts empty of inhabitants and great villages deserted. The Franks had feigned fear and withdrawn into the remoter woodlands, constructing barricades of felled trees at the forests’ edges. The soldiers burned every house; their cowardly certainty of victory made them think that raging against buildings completed their triumph, and they passed an anxious night under the burden of their arms. At first light they entered the woods with Quintinus as commander in the battle. Entangling themselves in the errors of the paths, they wandered through the whole area almost until midday. At last they found everything enclosed by enormous solid barricades. As they tried to break out into the marshy fields adjoining the woods, a few of the enemy appeared. Standing together on tree trunks or on the felled trees, they poured down arrows as though from the tops of towers, in the manner of artillery. The arrows were smeared with poisonous herbs, so that wounds inflicted only on the surface of the skin and not in lethal places were followed by an uncertain death. Then a greater multitude of the enemy surrounded the army, which poured eagerly into the open fields that the Franks had left free. The first cavalry were swallowed in the abysses; the bodies of men and beasts mingled, and the falling men crushed their own comrades. The infantry too, though not trampled by the weight of horses, became entangled in the mud and could scarcely free their steps; those who had barely emerged a little earlier hid themselves again, trembling, in the woods. The ranks were thrown into confusion and the legions slaughtered. Heraclius, tribune of the Jovinians, and nearly all who commanded the soldiers were killed; for only a few did night and the hiding places of the forests provide a complete escape.”

Sulpicius related this in the third book of his History. In the fourth book, while describing the killing of Victor, son of the tyrant Maximus, he says: “At that time Carietto and Syrus, appointed in Nanninus’s place, were staying in Germany with an army stationed opposite the Franks.” A little later, when the Franks had taken plunder from Germany, he adds: “Arbogastes, unwilling to delay, warned the Caesar that he must exact the penalties owed by the Franks unless they immediately restored everything they had seized in the previous year when the legions were slaughtered, and surrendered the authors of the war, on whom would rest the perfidy of having violated the peace.” Sulpicius related that these things happened while they were dukes. He then says: “After a few days he held a hurried conference with Marcomer and Sunno, the regales of the Franks and, having received hostages according to custom, withdrew to Trier to spend the winter.” Since Sulpicius calls them regales, I do not know whether they were kings or held the royal power in turn.

The same writer, when recording the difficulties of the emperor Valentinian, adds: “While various events were occurring in the East throughout Thrace, the condition of the state in Gaul was disturbed. The emperor Valentinian was shut within the palace buildings at Vienne and reduced almost below the condition of a private person; the conduct of military affairs was entrusted to Frankish retainers, and civil offices also passed into Arbogastes’s conspiracy. None of those bound by military oaths was found who dared obey the emperor’s private speech or commands.” He then relates: “In the same year Arbogastes, pursuing Sunno and Marcomer, the petty kings of the Franks, with hereditary hatred, attacked Agrippina at the height of winter. He knew that every recess of all Francia could now be penetrated and burned, since with the leaves fallen the bare and dry forests could not conceal ambushers. He assembled an army, crossed the Rhine, and devastated the Bructeri nearest the bank and also the district inhabited by the Chamavi. No one ever opposed him, except that a few of the Ampsivarii and Chatti appeared with Marcomer as their leader on the ridges of the more distant hills.”

Again, after this passage in which he has abandoned the terms “dukes” and “regales,” Sulpicius plainly indicates that the Franks had a king. Omitting the king’s name, he says: “Afterward the tyrant Eugenius undertook a military expedition and sought the Rhine frontier, so that, after renewing ancient treaties with the kings of the Alamanni and Franks according to custom, he might display an immense army to the savage nations at that time.” This is what the historian mentioned above wrote about the Franks.

Renatus Profuturus Frigeridus, whom I have already mentioned, says in relating the capture and destruction of Rome by the Goths: “Meanwhile Respendial, king of the Alans, after Goar had gone over to the Romans, turned his army away from the Rhine. The Vandals were struggling in war against the Franks: their king Godigisel had been destroyed and nearly twenty thousand of their army killed by the sword, and all the Vandals would have been annihilated if the force of the Alans had not come to their aid in time.” This passage troubles me: since he names the kings of other nations, why does he not also name the king of the Franks?

When Renatus says that Constantine, having assumed the tyranny, ordered his son Constans to come to him from the Spains, he writes: “The tyrant Constantine summoned from the Spains his son Constans, likewise a tyrant, so that they might consult together in person about the highest affairs. Constans therefore left the apparatus of the court and his wife at Caesaraugusta, entrusted everything within the Spains to Gerontius, and hastened without interruption to his father. After they met and many days had passed, with no danger arising from Italy, Constantine, devoted to gluttony and his belly, advised his son to return to the Spains. Constans sent his columns ahead and remained with his father, but messengers arrived from Spain reporting that Gerontius had invested Maximus, one of his clients, with imperial power and had prepared him by surrounding him with an escort of barbarian nations. Terrified by this, they sent Edobec ahead to the German nations. Constans and Decimius Rusticus, formerly master of offices and now prefect, made for the Gauls, intending to return immediately to Constantine with the Franks, Alamanni, and the entire body of soldiers.”

Likewise, when Renatus describes the siege of Constantine, he says: “The fourth month of Constantine’s siege had scarcely begun when messengers suddenly arrived from farther Gaul reporting that Jovinus had assumed royal ornaments and, with the Burgundians, Alamanni, Franks, Alans, and the whole army, was threatening the besiegers. The customary measures were hastened; the city was opened and Constantine surrendered. He was at once sent toward Italy, but executioners dispatched by the emperor met him, and he was beheaded above the river Mincius.” A little later the same writer relates: “In those same days Decimius Rusticus, prefect of the tyrants, Agroetius, formerly chief of Jovinus’s notaries, and many nobles were captured in the Auvergne by Honorius’s commanders and cruelly killed. The city of Trier was plundered and burned by the Franks in a second invasion.” When Asterius had received the patriciate by imperial letters, Renatus adds: “At the same time Castinus, count of the domestics, was sent into the Gauls after undertaking an expedition against the Franks.” These writers said this about the Franks.

The historian Orosius likewise records in the seventh book of his work: “Stilicho assembled the nations, crushed the Franks, crossed the Rhine, traversed the Gauls, and went as far as the Pyrenees.” This is the information about the Franks which these historians have left me, without naming their kings. Many people relate that the Franks set out from Pannonia and first inhabited the banks of the river Rhine; afterward they crossed the Rhine and passed through Thuringia. There, in each district and city, they established over themselves long-haired kings drawn from their first and, so to speak, nobler family. Clovis’s later victories proved this, as I shall explain in what follows.

I also read in the Consular Lists that Theudomer, king of the Franks and son of the late Richimer, was killed by the sword together with his mother Ascyla. They further say that Clodio was then an able king of the Franks and the most noble among his people. He lived at the fortress of Dispargum, which is on the border of the Thuringians. In these regions—that is, toward the south—the Romans lived as far as the river Loire. Beyond the Loire the Goths ruled. The Burgundians too, following the Arian sect, lived across the Rhône, which adjoins the city of Lyon. Clodio sent scouts to the city of Cambrai and explored everything; he then followed, crushed the Romans, and captured the city. After remaining there for a short time, he occupied the country as far as the river Somme. Some assert that King Merovech was of this man’s stock, and that Childeric was Merovech’s son.

### 10. What the prophets of the Lord wrote of the idols of the nations

<a id="hist-02-en-011"></a>
[**[011]**](#hist-02-la-011) But this nation always seemed to have devoted itself to pagan cults and knew nothing of God. They fashioned images for themselves of woods and waters, birds and beasts and other elements, and were accustomed to worship these as God and offer sacrifices to them. Oh, if only that terrible voice which spoke to the people through Moses had touched the fibres of their hearts, saying: “You shall have no other gods besides me. You shall not make for yourself a graven image, nor worship any likeness of the things in heaven, on earth, or moving in the waters; you shall not make them or serve them.” And again: “You shall adore the Lord your God, serve him alone, and swear by his name.” What if they could have understood what vengeance overtook and crushed the people of Israel for worshipping the molten calf, when after feasting and singing, after wantonness and dancing, they proclaimed with unclean mouths concerning that image: “These are your gods, Israel, who brought you out of the land of Egypt”? Twenty-four thousand of them fell. What of those initiated into the worship of Baal-peor who committed fornication with the Moabites and were slaughtered and laid low by their nearest kin? In that plague Phinehas the priest killed the adulterers, appeased God’s fury, and it was reckoned to him as righteousness. What if those words which the Lord thunders through David had reached their ears: “All the gods of the nations are demons, but the Lord made the heavens”? And: “The idols of the nations are silver and gold, the work of human hands. Let those who make them become like them, and all who trust in them.” Or this: “Let all who adore graven images and glory in their idols be confounded.” And again what the prophet Habakkuk testifies: “What use is the graven image they have carved? They have made it a molten image, a false phantom. It is a casting of silver and gold, and there is no breath in it. But the Lord is in his holy temple; let all the earth keep silence before him.” Another prophet says: “Let the gods who did not make heaven and earth perish from the earth and from beneath the heavens.” And again: “Thus says the Lord, who created the heavens, the very God who formed the earth and all that is in it. He is its maker; he did not create it in vain but formed it to be inhabited. I am the Lord; that is my name. I shall not give my glory to another or my power to graven images, which have passed away from the beginning.” And elsewhere: “Are there any among the idols of the nations who can send rain?” Through Isaiah he says again: “I am the first and the last, and besides me there is no god formed whom I do not know. All makers of idols are nothing, and their most beloved works shall profit them nothing. They themselves are witnesses that they neither see nor understand, and they shall be confounded. Behold, all who share in them shall be confounded, for their makers are only men. He shaped it with burning coals and hammers and worked it with the strength of his arm. Likewise the carpenter turned it with a compass and made the image of a man like a handsome human being dwelling in a house. He cut down wood, worked it, made an idol, and adored it as god; he fastened it with nails and hammers so that it would not fall apart. When carried, idols must be lifted because they cannot walk. The rest of the wood men make into a fire and warm themselves. From another piece he makes a god and an idol for himself. He bows before it, adores it, and prays: ‘Deliver me, for you are my god. I burned half of it in the fire and baked bread on its coals; I roasted meat and ate, and shall I make an idol of what remains? Shall I adore a block of wood? Part of it is ashes.’ A foolish heart adored it and did not deliver its soul. Nor does it say: ‘Perhaps there is a lie in my right hand.’” This nation of the Franks did not understand at first, but afterwards they understood, as the following history relates.

### 11. Of the emperor Avitus

<a id="hist-02-en-012"></a>
[**[012]**](#hist-02-la-012) Avitus was one of the senators and, as is very well known, a citizen of the Auvergne. After seeking the Roman empire, he wished to live dissolutely and was cast out by the senators; he was then ordained bishop in the city of Piacenza. Learning that the Senate, still indignant, wished to deprive him of life, he sought the basilica of Saint Julian, martyr of the Auvergne, bringing many gifts. But the course of his life was completed on the journey, and he died. He was carried to the village of Brioude and buried at the feet of the aforesaid martyr. Marcian succeeded him. In the Gauls, Aegidius, a Roman, was appointed master of the soldiers.

### 12. Of King Childeric and Egidius

<a id="hist-02-en-013"></a>
[**[013]**](#hist-02-la-013) Childeric ruled over the nation of the Franks but was excessively dissolved in wantonness and began to dishonor their daughters by seduction. Indignant at this, the Franks drove him from the kingdom. Learning that they also intended to kill him, he went to Thuringia. He left behind a man dear to him who could soften the minds of angry men with smooth words, and gave him a sign for when he could return to his homeland. Together they divided a gold solidus: Childeric took one part with him, while his friend kept the other and said: “When I send you this part and the joined pieces make one whole solidus, then return to your homeland with a secure mind.” Going into Thuringia, Childeric hid with King Bisinus and his wife Basina.

After expelling Childeric, the Franks unanimously chose as their king Aegidius, whom I mentioned above as having been sent by the state as master of the soldiers. When Aegidius had ruled over them for eight years, that faithful friend secretly pacified the Franks and sent a messenger to Childeric with the part of the divided solidus which he had kept. Recognizing the sure token that the Franks desired his return, Childeric came back from Thuringia at their request and was restored to his kingdom.

While these men were ruling, that same Basina whom I mentioned above left her husband and came to Childeric. When he asked carefully why she had come to him from so distant a region, she is said to have answered: “I know your ability and that you are very energetic; therefore I have come to live with you. Know that if I had known anyone beyond the sea more capable than you, I would certainly have sought to live with him.” Rejoicing, he joined her to himself in marriage. She conceived and bore a son, whom she named Clovis. He was a great man and an outstanding warrior.

### 13. Of the bishops Venerandus and Rusticus of the Auvergne

<a id="hist-02-en-014"></a>
[**[014]**](#hist-02-la-014) In the Auvergne, after Saint Artemius’s death, Venerandus, a member of the senatorial order, was ordained bishop. Paulinus bears witness to his character: “If today you would see priests worthy of the Lord—Exsuperius of Toulouse, Simplicius of Vienne, Amandus of Bordeaux, Diogenianus of Albi, Dinamius of Ecolisna, Venerandus of the Auvergne, Alithius of Cahors, and now Pegasius of Périgueux—you will see, whatever evils the age brings, men most worthy to guard the whole faith and religion.” Venerandus is said to have died on the vigil of the Lord’s Nativity itself; when morning came, the procession formed the solemn service of his funeral.

After his death, a shameful dispute over the bishopric agitated the citizens. The parties divided, each wishing to elevate a different man, and the people clashed violently. While the bishops were seated on the Lord’s day, a veiled woman devoted to God boldly entered and said: “Hear me, priests of the Lord! Know that God is not pleased with those whom these men have chosen for the priesthood. Today the Lord Himself will provide a bishop. Do not disturb or crush the people, but be patient for a little while; the Lord will now send the man who is to govern this church.” While they were marveling at her words, a priest named Rusticus, from the diocese of the city of Clermont itself, suddenly arrived. He had already been indicated to the woman in a vision. Seeing him, she said: “Here is the man whom the Lord has chosen! This is the bishop He has appointed for you! Let him be ordained!” As she spoke, all the people set aside their disputes and cried out that this was right and just. He was placed in the episcopal chair and, amid the people’s rejoicing, received the honor of the pontificate as the seventh bishop of that see.

### 14. Of Eustochius of Tours and Perpetuus, and of the basilica of Saint Martin

<a id="hist-02-en-015"></a>
[**[015]**](#hist-02-la-015) At Tours, Bishop Eustochius died in the seventeenth year of his priesthood, and Perpetuus was ordained as the fifth bishop after blessed Martin. Perpetuus saw that miracles occurred continually at Martin’s tomb and judged the little cell built over it too small and unworthy of such wonders. He removed it and built on the site the great basilica which remains to this day, 550 paces from the city. It is 160 feet long, 60 feet wide, and 45 feet high up to the vault. It has 32 windows in the sanctuary, 20 in the nave, and 41 columns; in the entire building there are 52 windows, 120 columns, and eight doors—three in the sanctuary and five in the nave.

The basilica’s solemnity is distinguished by a threefold significance: the dedication of the church, the translation of the saint’s body, and his ordination as bishop. You will keep it on the fourth day before the Nones of July; know that his deposition is on the third day before the Ides of November. If you celebrate these faithfully, you will merit the blessed bishop’s patronage both in the present world and in the world to come.

Because the vault of the earlier cell had been made with elegant workmanship, the bishop considered it wrong for that work to perish. He therefore built another basilica in honor of the blessed apostles Peter and Paul and installed the vault there. He built many other basilicas as well, which stand to this day in Christ’s name.

### 15. Of the Basilica of Saint Symphorian

<a id="hist-02-en-016"></a>
[**[016]**](#hist-02-la-016) At the same time the priest Eufronius built the basilica of blessed Symphorian, martyr of Autun. Eufronius himself later obtained the episcopate of Tours. With great devotion he also sent the marble which is placed over Saint Martin’s holy tomb.

### 16. Of Namatius the bishop and the church of the Auvergne

<a id="hist-02-en-017"></a>
[**[017]**](#hist-02-la-017) After Bishop Rusticus’s death, Saint Namatius was the eighth bishop of the Auvergne in those days. Through his own efforts he built the church which now stands and is regarded as the oldest within the city walls. It is 150 feet long, 60 feet wide within the nave, and 50 feet high up to the vault. It has a rounded apse at the front and side chambers constructed with elegant workmanship on either side; the whole building is laid out in the form of a cross. It has 42 windows, 70 columns, and eight doors. A great fear of God and a great splendor are perceived there, and religious people often truly sense a most sweet fragrance, as of spices, arising within it. The walls around the altar are adorned with inlaid work made from many kinds of marble.

When the blessed bishop had completed the building in the twelfth year, he sent priests to the city of Bologna in Italy to obtain for him relics of Saints Agricola and Vitalis, whom I know were plainly crucified for the name of Christ our God.

### 17. Of his wife and the basilica of Saint Stephen

<a id="hist-02-en-018"></a>
[**[018]**](#hist-02-la-018) Namatius’s wife built the basilica of Saint Stephen outside the city walls. Wishing to adorn it with colored pigments, she held a book in her lap and read the histories of ancient deeds, indicating to the painters what they should depict on the walls. One day, while she sat reading in the basilica, a poor man came to pray. Seeing that she wore black clothing and was already advanced in age, he thought she was one of the needy. He drew out a quarter-loaf of bread, placed it in her lap, and departed. She did not disdain the gift of a poor man who did not recognize her. She accepted it with thanks and set it aside, preferring it to her own meals and receiving a blessing from it each day until it was consumed.

### 18. How Childeric came to Orléans and Angers, and Odoacer

<a id="hist-02-en-019"></a>
[**[019]**](#hist-02-la-019) Childeric fought battles at Orléans, while Odoacer came with the Saxons to Angers. A great plague then devastated the population. Aegidius died, leaving a son named Syagrius. After Aegidius’s death, Odoacer received hostages from Angers and other places. The Goths drove the Britons from Bourges, and many were killed at the village of Déols. Count Paul, with Romans and Franks, waged war against the Goths and took plunder. When Odoacer came to Angers, King Childeric arrived on the following day; Count Paul was killed, and Childeric took possession of the city. On that day the great church building was consumed by fire.

### 19. Of the war between the Saxons and the Romans

<a id="hist-02-en-020"></a>
[**[020]**](#hist-02-la-020) After these events war was waged between the Saxons and the Romans. The Saxons turned their backs and, as the Romans pursued, left many of their own men to the sword. The Franks captured and destroyed their islands, killing a great many people. In the ninth month of that year there was an earthquake. Odoacer made a treaty with Childeric, and together they subdued the Alamanni who had overrun part of Italy.

### 20. Of the duke Victorius

<a id="hist-02-en-021"></a>
[**[021]**](#hist-02-la-021) In the fourteenth year of his reign, Euric, king of the Goths, placed Duke Victorius over seven cities. Victorius came immediately to the Auvergne and wished to extend the city; the crypts from that work remain to this day. He ordered columns to be supplied for the basilica of Saint Julian, and they are set in the building. He also ordered the construction of the basilica of Saint Lawrence and that of Saint Germanus in the village of Licaniacum. He remained in the Auvergne for nine years.

He brought false charges against the senator Eucherius. After imprisoning him, Victorius ordered him to be brought out at night and bound beside an old wall, and then ordered the wall itself to be thrown down upon him. Victorius was excessively dissolute in his love of women. Fearing that the people of the Auvergne would kill him, he fled to Rome; there he tried to practice similar wantonness and was stoned to death. After Victorius’s departure Euric reigned for four more years and died in the twenty-seventh year of his reign. There was also a great earthquake at that time.

### 21. Of Eparchius the bishop

<a id="hist-02-en-022"></a>
[**[022]**](#hist-02-la-022) When Bishop Namatius died in the Auvergne, Eparchius succeeded him, a most holy and religious man. Because the church possessed only a small property within the city walls at that time, the bishop’s residence was in the building now called the Salutatorium, and he would rise during the night to give thanks to God at the church’s altar.

One night he entered and found the church full of demons, with their prince seated on the chair of his throne in the likeness of an adorned woman. The bishop said: “Accursed prostitute, is it not enough for you to infect every place with your various pollutions? Must you also defile the chair consecrated to the Lord with the foul approach of your sitting? Depart from God’s house, so that it may no longer be polluted by you.” The demon answered: “Because you call me a prostitute, I shall prepare many snares for you through the desire of women.” Saying this, it vanished like smoke. The priest was indeed tempted by bodily stirrings of concupiscence, but protected by the sign of the holy cross, he could not be harmed by the enemy.

People also relate that Eparchius established a monastery on the height of Mount Chantoin, where an oratory now stands, and that he shut himself in there during the days of holy Lent. On the day of the Lord’s Supper he would return to his church with great psalmody, accompanied by clergy and citizens. When he died, Sidonius, a former prefect, was appointed in his place. Sidonius was most noble in worldly rank and one of the leading senators of the Gauls; he had married a daughter of the emperor Avitus.

In Sidonius’s time, while Victorius, whom I mentioned above, was still staying in the city of Clermont, an abbot named Abraham lived in the monastery of Saint Cyricus in that city. He shone with the grace of the earlier Abraham in faith and works, as I have written in the book of his Life.

### 22. Of the bishop Sidonius

<a id="hist-02-en-023"></a>
[**[023]**](#hist-02-la-023) Saint Sidonius possessed such eloquence that he could often compose extemporaneously, with the greatest brilliance and without any halting delay, whatever he wished to say. One day he came by invitation to a festival at the basilica of the monastery mentioned above. His booklet, from which he customarily conducted the most sacred rites, had been maliciously taken from him; yet, prepared by the occasion, he performed the whole service of the festival so well that everyone marveled, and those present thought that an angel, rather than a man, had spoken there. I explained this more fully in the preface to the book in which I collected the masses he composed.

He was of magnificent holiness and, as I have said, one of the leading senators. Without his wife’s knowledge, he often carried silver vessels away from the house and gave them to the poor. When she discovered this, she was angry with him; he then paid the needy the price and restored the vessels to the house.

### 23. Of the holiness of Sidonius

<a id="hist-02-en-024"></a>
[**[024]**](#hist-02-la-024) After Sidonius had been consecrated to the Lord’s service and was living a holy life in the world, two priests rose against him. They took away all his authority over the church’s property, left him only a scanty and meager diet, and reduced him to the deepest humiliation. But divine mercy would not allow the injury done to him to remain unavenged for long.

One of these most wicked men, unworthy to be called a priest, had threatened during the previous night to drive Sidonius out of the church. When he heard the signal for matins, he rose, boiling with gall against God’s holy man and intending to carry out the wicked plan he had discussed on the preceding day. He entered his privy and, while trying to empty his bowels, breathed his last. His servant waited outside with a candle for his master to come out. Daylight had already arrived, and his accomplice—that is, the other priest—sent a message: “Come without delay, so that we may together carry out what we agreed yesterday.” When the dead man gave no answer, the servant lifted the curtain over the door and found his master dead on the privy seat. There can be no doubt that he was guilty of no less a crime than Arius, whose bowels likewise poured out through his lower parts in a privy. Nor can it be accepted without heresy that a priest of God to whom the sheep have been committed for feeding should be disobeyed in the church, while another intrudes into an authority committed to him neither by God nor by men.

Afterward the blessed bishop was restored to his authority, although one enemy still remained. Later Sidonius fell ill with a fever and asked his people to carry him into the church. When he had been brought there, a multitude of men and women and even infants gathered, weeping and saying: “Why do you abandon us, good shepherd? To whom do you leave us as orphans? Shall we have life after your departure? Will anyone afterward season us so with the salt of wisdom or instruct us in the fear of the Lord’s name with such prudent reasoning?” As the people said these and similar things with loud weeping, the bishop at last answered under the influence of the Holy Spirit within him: “Do not be afraid, my people. Behold, my brother Aprunculus is alive, and he shall be your bishop.” They did not understand and thought he was speaking in ecstasy.

When Sidonius died, the other wicked priest who remained immediately seized all the church’s property with greedy desire, as though he were already bishop, saying: “At last God has looked upon me, recognized that I am more righteous than Sidonius, and granted me this authority.” He went proudly through the whole city. On the Lord’s day following the holy man’s death, he prepared a banquet and ordered all the citizens to be invited to the church house. Disregarding the elders, he reclined first on the couch.

The cupbearer offered him a cup and said: “My lord, I have seen a dream which I will relate if you permit. I saw this night that it was the Lord’s day, and there was a great house. In the house stood a throne, on which someone like a judge was seated, greater in power than all others. Many priests in white garments stood beside him, and also an exceedingly great and innumerable mixed multitude of peoples. As I contemplated these things in fear, I saw blessed Sidonius standing among them at a distance, earnestly disputing with that priest who was most dear to you and who departed from this world a few days ago. When the priest had been defeated, the king ordered him to be thrust back into the deepest confines of prison. After that man had been removed, Sidonius brought another charge against you, saying that you had shared in the crime for which the earlier man had been condemned. When the judge began to inquire carefully whom he should send to you, I tried to hide among the others and stood at the back, reasoning to myself that I, whom the man knows, might be sent. While I silently turned this over in my mind, everyone else was removed, and I remained alone in the open. Called by the judge, I approached closer. Looking upon his power and splendor, I became faint and began to stagger with fear. He said: ‘Do not be afraid, boy, but go and tell that priest: Come to answer the charge, because Sidonius has prayed that you be summoned. Do not delay in going, for that king commanded me to say this to you under a solemn warning, saying: If you remain silent, you will die a most wretched death.’”

As the cupbearer spoke, the terrified priest dropped the cup from his hand and gave up his spirit. He was carried dead from his couch and committed to burial, to possess hell together with his accomplice. The Lord executed such judgment upon obstinate clerics in this world that one received the death of Arius, while the other, like Simon Magus at the holy apostle’s prayer, was hurled headlong from the lofty height of pride and crushed. Those who acted wickedly together against their holy bishop are undoubtedly in possession of hell together.

Meanwhile the terror of the Franks was already resounding in these regions, and everyone desired with longing love that they should rule. Saint Aprunculus, bishop of the city of Langres, therefore began to be regarded with suspicion among the Burgundians. As hatred grew from day to day, an order was given that he should be struck down with the sword. When this news reached him, he was lowered over the wall of the fortress of Dijon by night and came to the Auvergne. There, according to the word which the Lord had placed in Saint Sidonius’s mouth, he was appointed the eleventh bishop.

### 24. Of the famine in Burgundy and of Ecdicius

<a id="hist-02-en-025"></a>
[**[025]**](#hist-02-la-025) In Bishop Sidonius’s time a severe famine oppressed Burgundy. The people scattered through different regions, and there was no one to give alms to the poor. A certain senator named Ecdicius, a kinsman of Sidonius, is said then to have done a great deed in trust in God. As the famine intensified, he sent his servants with horses and wagons through the neighboring cities to bring to him those afflicted by want. They went and brought to his house all the poor they could find. Feeding them there throughout the whole period of barrenness, he saved them from death by famine. Many say that there were more than four thousand persons of both sexes. When abundance returned, he again arranged transport and restored each person to his own place.

After they had departed, a voice descended from heaven to him: “Ecdicius, Ecdicius, because you have done this, bread shall never be lacking to you and your seed, for you have obeyed my words and satisfied my hunger by feeding the poor.” Many record that Ecdicius was remarkably swift: on one occasion he is described as having put a multitude of Goths to flight with only ten men. Saint Patiens, bishop of Lyon, is said to have shown the people a similar kindness in the same famine. A letter of blessed Bishop Sidonius on this subject survives among me to this day, in which he praised him in a declamation.

### 25. Of the persecutor Euric

<a id="hist-02-en-026"></a>
[**[026]**](#hist-02-la-026) At this time Euric, king of the Goths, crossed the Spanish frontier and inflicted a severe persecution upon the Christians in Gaul. In his perversity he mutilated those who did not agree with him, imprisoned clergy, exiled some bishops, and slaughtered others with the sword. He even ordered the entrances to the sacred temples to be blocked with thorns, intending that infrequent access should cause the faith to be forgotten. At that time especially the cities of Novempopulania and the two Germanies were devastated by this storm. A letter of the noble Bishop Sidonius himself concerning this matter, addressed to Bishop Basilius, survives to this day and speaks of it. But not long afterward the persecutor was struck by divine vengeance and died.

### 26. Of the death of the holy Perpetuus

<a id="hist-02-en-027"></a>
[**[027]**](#hist-02-la-027) After this blessed Perpetuus, bishop of the city of Tours, rested in peace after completing thirty years in the episcopate. Volusianus, one of the senators, was appointed in his place. Because the Goths regarded him with suspicion, he was taken to the Spains like a captive in the seventh year of his episcopate and immediately ended his life. Verus succeeded him and was ordained bishop, the seventh after blessed Martin.

### 27. How Clovis received the kingdom, and of Syagrius

<a id="hist-02-en-028"></a>
[**[028]**](#hist-02-la-028) After these events Childeric died, and his son Clovis reigned in his place. In the fifth year of Clovis’s reign, Syagrius, king of the Romans and son of Egidius, had his seat at Soissons, the city which the aforementioned Egidius had once held. Clovis came against him with his kinsman Ragnachar, who also held a kingdom, and demanded that a field of battle be prepared. Syagrius did not delay and did not fear to resist. As the two sides fought, Syagrius saw his army crushed, turned in flight, and fled swiftly to King Alaric at Toulouse. Clovis sent to Alaric demanding that he surrender Syagrius, otherwise he should know that war would be made upon him for harbouring him. Fearing that he would incur the Franks’ anger on Syagrius’s account, as the Goths are easily terrified, Alaric handed him over in chains to the envoys. Clovis ordered him to be imprisoned and, having taken his kingdom, commanded that he be secretly struck with the sword.

At that time Clovis’s army plundered many churches, because he was still entangled in pagan errors. The enemy had taken from one church a ewer of remarkable size and beauty together with the other ornaments of the church’s ministry. The bishop of that church sent a messenger to the king, asking that, if he could not recover any other sacred vessel, his church might at least receive back the ewer. Hearing this, the king told the messenger: “Follow me to Soissons, where all the captured goods are to be divided. If the lot gives me that vessel, I shall fulfil what the bishop asks.” When he came to Soissons and the whole mass of plunder had been placed in the centre, the king said: “I ask you, bravest warriors, not to refuse to grant me this vessel in addition to my share”—he meant the ewer mentioned above. Those of sounder mind answered: “Glorious king, everything I see is yours, and I myself am subject to your dominion. Do now whatever seems good to you, for no one can resist your power.” But one frivolous, envious, and impulsive man raised his axe and with a loud cry struck the ewer, saying: “You shall receive nothing from this except what the true lot grants you.” All were astonished. The king restrained his resentment with patient gentleness, returned the ewer to the church messenger, and kept the wound hidden in his breast.

A year later he ordered the whole army to assemble with its equipment and display the brightness of its arms on the March field. While reviewing everyone, he came to the man who had struck the ewer and said: “No one has kept his arms as neglected as you; your spear, sword, and axe are all unfit for use.” He seized the man’s axe and threw it to the ground. When the man bent down to pick it up, the king raised his own axe and buried it in his head, saying: “Thus did you treat that ewer at Soissons.” He then ordered the others to depart, having established great fear of himself by this deed. He fought many wars and won many victories. In the tenth year of his reign he made war upon the Thuringians and subjected them to his authority.

### 28. How Clovis took Chrodigildis

<a id="hist-02-en-029"></a>
[**[029]**](#hist-02-la-029) Gundevech, king of the Burgundians, belonged to the family of the persecuting King Athanaric, whom I mentioned above. He had four sons: Gundobad, Godigisel, Chilperic, and Godomar. Gundobad killed his brother Chilperic with the sword and drowned Chilperic’s wife by tying a stone to her neck and casting her into the water. He condemned Chilperic’s two daughters to exile. The elder, who had changed her clothing for the religious habit, was called Crona; the younger was named Chrotchildis.

While Clovis was frequently sending embassies into Burgundy, his envoys discovered the girl Chrotchildis. They saw that she was elegant and wise and learned that she was of royal birth, and they reported this to King Clovis. Without delay he sent an embassy to Gundobad, requesting her in marriage. Not daring to refuse, Gundobad handed her over to the men. They received the girl and presented her to the king with all speed. Clovis rejoiced greatly at seeing her and joined her to himself in marriage. He already had a son named Theuderic by a concubine.

### 29. Of their first son

<a id="hist-02-en-030"></a>
[**[030]**](#hist-02-la-030) Queen Clotilde bore Clovis his firstborn son. When she wished to consecrate him by baptism, she continually exhorted her husband, saying: “The gods you worship are nothing; they can help neither themselves nor others. They are carved from stone, wood, or some metal. The names you have given them belonged to men, not gods: Saturn, who is said to have fled to avoid being driven from his kingdom by his son; and Jupiter himself, the foulest perpetrator of every debauchery, a defiler of men and a mocker of his relatives, who could not even refrain from intercourse with his own sister, as the verse says: ‘Jupiter’s sister and wife.’ What power did Mars and Mercury have? They were endowed with magical arts rather than with the power of the divine name. He alone ought to be worshipped who by his word created heaven and earth, the sea and everything in them out of nothing; who made the sun shine and adorned the heaven with stars; who filled the waters with creeping things, the earth with animals, and the air with flying creatures; at whose command the earth is adorned with crops, the trees with fruit, and the vines with grapes; by whose hand the human race was created; and by whose bounty all creation serves the man he created, both in obedience and in benefit.” Although the queen said these things, the king’s mind was in no way moved to belief. He said: “Everything is created and comes forth by the command of our gods. Your God is shown to have no power and, what is more, is proved not even to belong to the race of gods.”

Meanwhile the faithful queen presented her son for baptism. She ordered the church to be adorned with hangings and curtains, hoping that the man who could not be moved by preaching might at least be led to belief by this mystery. The boy was baptized and named Ingomer, but died in the white robes in which he had been regenerated. Enraged by this, the king rebuked the queen severely: “If the boy had been dedicated in the name of my gods, he would surely have lived. But because he was baptized in the name of your God, he could not live at all.” The queen answered: “I give thanks to almighty God, the Creator of all, who did not judge me altogether unworthy but deigned to receive into his kingdom the child born of my womb. My heart is not touched by grief over this, because I know that one called from this world in white robes will be nourished in God’s sight.” Afterwards she bore another son, whom she named Chlodomer at his baptism. When he fell ill, the king said: “Nothing else can happen to him than happened to his brother: now that he has been baptized in the name of your Christ, he will die immediately.” But at his mother’s prayer and by the Lord’s command he recovered.

### 30. The war against the Alamanni

<a id="hist-02-en-031"></a>
[**[031]**](#hist-02-la-031) The queen did not cease exhorting him to acknowledge the true God and abandon idols. But he could in no way be moved to believe these things, until at last war against the Alamanni arose, in which he was compelled by necessity to confess what he had previously refused by choice. When the two armies engaged, they slaughtered one another violently, and Clovis’s army began to rush toward utter destruction. Seeing this, he raised his eyes to heaven; stricken in heart and moved to tears, he said: “Jesus Christ, whom Chrotchildis proclaims to be the Son of the living God, and who is said to give help to those in trouble and grant victory to those who hope in You, I devoutly implore the glory of Your aid. If You grant me victory over these enemies, and I experience that power which the people dedicated to Your name claim to have proved in You, I will believe in You and be baptized in Your name. For I have invoked my gods, but, as I now experience, they have withdrawn from helping me. I therefore believe that they possess no power, since they do not come to the aid of those who obey them. Now I invoke You; I desire to believe in You; only let me be delivered from my adversaries.”

While he was saying this, the Alamanni turned their backs and began to flee. Seeing that their king had been killed, they submitted to Clovis’s rule, saying: “I beg you, let the people perish no further; we are now yours.” Clovis stopped the battle, exhorted the people, and returned in peace. He told the queen how he had merited victory by invoking Christ’s name. **[This occurred in the fifteenth year of his reign.]**

### 31. Of the baptism of Clovis

<a id="hist-02-en-032"></a>
[**[032]**](#hist-02-la-032) The queen then secretly ordered Saint Remigius, bishop of the city of Reims, to be summoned, begging him to impart the word of salvation to the king. When the bishop had come, he began privately to urge the king to believe in the true God, the maker of heaven and earth, and to abandon idols, which could benefit neither themselves nor others. Clovis answered: “Most holy father, I would gladly hear you; but one difficulty remains: the people who follow me will not consent to abandon their gods. I will go and speak to them according to your word.” When he met with his men, before he could speak, God’s power went before him, and all the people cried out together: “We reject the mortal gods, pious king, and we are ready to follow the immortal God whom Remigius preaches.”

When this was reported to the bishop, he was filled with great joy and ordered the baptismal bath to be prepared. The streets were shaded with decorated awnings; the churches were adorned with white curtains; the baptistery was set in order; balsam was spread about; gleaming candles gave forth fragrance; and the whole baptistery was sprinkled with a divine odor. God bestowed such grace on those present that they imagined themselves situated amid the fragrances of paradise.

The king asked to be baptized by the bishop before anyone else. Like a new Constantine, he advanced to the bath, to wash away the disease of his ancient leprosy and remove with fresh water the filthy stains he had borne from of old. As he entered the baptismal water, God’s saint addressed him with eloquent speech: “Gently bow your neck, Sicambrian; adore what you have burned, and burn what you have adored.” Saint Bishop Remigius was a man of outstanding learning and thoroughly trained in rhetorical studies; he was also so eminent in holiness that he equaled Sylvester in miracles. A book of his Life now exists which relates that he raised a dead man.

The king confessed almighty God in the Trinity, was baptized in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, and was anointed with sacred chrism bearing the seal of Christ’s cross. More than three thousand of his army were baptized. His sister Albofled was also baptized and not long afterward departed to the Lord. When the king grieved for her, Saint Remigius sent a letter of consolation, which began: “The cause of your sadness distresses me, and distresses me greatly: your sister Albofled of good memory has died. But I can be consoled, because she has departed from this world in such a condition that she ought rather to be looked up to than mourned.” His other sister, named Lanthechild, who had fallen into the Arian heresy, was also converted; she confessed the Son equal to the Father and the Holy Spirit, and received the chrism.

### 32. The war against Gundobad

<a id="hist-02-en-033"></a>
[**[033]**](#hist-02-la-033) At that time the brothers Gundobad and Godegisel held the kingdom around the Rhône and Saône together with the province of Marseille. Both they and their people belonged to the Arian sect. When they began attacking each other, Godegisel heard of King Clovis’s victories and secretly sent an embassy to him, saying: “If you give me help against my brother so that I can kill him in battle or drive him from the region, I shall pay you every year whatever tribute you wish to impose.” Clovis gladly accepted and promised help whenever necessity required. At the appointed time he moved his army against Gundobad. Hearing this and unaware of his brother’s treachery, Gundobad sent to Godegisel: “Come to my aid, for the Franks are moving against me and approaching my region to capture it. Let me be of one mind against this hostile people, lest, separated, I suffer what other nations have suffered.” Godegisel answered: “I shall come with my army and give you help.” The three armies moved: Clovis against Gundobad, and Godegisel with his whole war equipment. They reached the fortress called Dijon. When battle was joined on the river Ouche, Godegisel joined Clovis, and together their armies destroyed Gundobad’s people. Gundobad then perceived his brother’s unsuspected treachery, turned in flight, hurried along the banks of the Rhône, and entered Avignon. Godegisel, having obtained victory, departed in peace after promising Clovis part of his kingdom. He entered Vienne in triumph as though he already possessed the whole kingdom.

King Clovis increased his forces and pursued Gundobad, intending to drag him from the city and kill him. Hearing this, Gundobad was terrified and feared sudden death. He had with him an illustrious man named Aridius, shrewd and wise. Summoning him, Gundobad said: “Difficulties surround me on every side, and I do not know what to do. These barbarians have come upon me to kill me and overthrow the whole region.” Aridius answered: “You must soothe this man’s ferocity so that you do not perish. If it pleases you, I shall pretend to flee from you and go over to him. When I reach him, I shall arrange matters so that they harm neither you nor this region. Only be careful to fulfil whatever he demands through my counsel until the Lord in his mercy deigns to make your cause prosper.” Gundobad said: “I shall do whatever you command.” Aridius took leave, departed, and went to King Clovis, saying: “Behold, most pious king, I humbly come to your power, abandoning that most miserable Gundobad. If your kindness deigns to receive me, you and your posterity will have in me a whole and faithful servant.” Clovis received him readily and kept him with him, for Aridius was agreeable in conversation, shrewd in counsel, just in judgment, and faithful in what was entrusted to him.

When Clovis had settled with his whole army around the city walls, Aridius said: “If the glory of your highness would graciously deign, king, to hear a few words of my humility, I shall offer counsel with complete faithfulness, although you do not need it. It will benefit both you and the cities through which you intend to pass. Why do you keep your army here while your enemy sits in an impregnable place? You devastate the fields, graze the meadows, cut down the vines, fell the olive groves, and destroy all the fruits of the region, while you cannot harm him at all. Instead send an embassy and impose a tribute for him to pay each year, so that the region may be preserved and you may rule for ever over the tribute-payer. If he refuses, then do as you please.” The king accepted this counsel and ordered the army to return home. He sent an embassy to Gundobad commanding him to pay the imposed tribute every year. Gundobad paid it for the present and promised to pay thereafter.

### 33. Of the death of Godigisil

<a id="hist-02-en-034"></a>
[**[034]**](#hist-02-la-034) Afterward Gundobad recovered his strength and, now disdaining to pay King Clovis the promised tribute, moved his army against his brother Godigisel and besieged him inside the city of Vienne. When food began to fail the common people, Godigisel feared that the famine would reach even him and ordered the common people to be expelled from the city. Among the others expelled was the workman responsible for the care of the aqueduct. Indignant at having been cast out of the city with the rest, he went furiously to Gundobad and showed him how to break into the city and take vengeance on his brother. The army was then led through the aqueduct with him as guide, many men going ahead with iron bars. The outlet was closed by a great stone, but this was driven back with the bars under the workman’s direction, and they entered the city. While the defenders shot arrows from the wall, these attackers took them from behind. A trumpet sounded from the middle of the city; the besiegers captured the gates, which were opened, and both forces entered together. As the people of the city were slaughtered between the two armies, Godigisel fled to a church of the heretics and was killed there together with the Arian bishop.

The Franks who had been with Godigisel gathered in a single tower. Gundobad ordered that none of them should be harmed; he seized them and sent them into exile at Toulouse to King Alaric. The senators and Burgundians who had supported Godigisel were killed. Gundobad restored the whole region now called Burgundy to his own dominion. He also established milder laws for the Burgundians, so that they would not oppress the Romans.

### 34. How Gundobad wished to be converted

<a id="hist-02-en-035"></a>
[**[035]**](#hist-02-la-035) When Gundobad had learned that the heretics’ assertions were worthless, he confessed to Saint Avitus, bishop of Vienne, that Christ the Son of God and the Holy Spirit were equal to the Father, and secretly asked to be chrismated. The bishop said: “If you truly believe, you ought to carry out what the Lord himself taught me. He says: ‘Whoever confesses me before men, him shall I also confess before my Father who is in heaven; but whoever denies me before men, him shall I also deny before my Father who is in heaven.’ When he taught his holy and beloved blessed apostles about the temptations of future persecution, he likewise said: ‘Beware of men. They will deliver you to councils and scourge you in their synagogues, and you shall stand before kings and governors for my sake as a testimony to them and to all nations.’ Though you are a king and fear no physical arrest, you dread a popular disturbance and therefore will not confess the Creator of all in public. Abandon this folly and proclaim before the people with your mouth what you say you believe in your heart. The blessed apostle says: ‘With the heart one believes unto righteousness, and with the mouth confession is made unto salvation.’ The prophet likewise says: ‘I shall confess you, Lord, in the great church; I shall praise you among the mighty people.’ And again: ‘I shall confess you among the peoples, Lord; I shall sing a psalm to your name among the nations.’ You fear the people, king, not knowing that it is better for the people to follow your faith than for you to indulge popular weakness. You are the head of the people, not the people your head. When you go to war, you lead the ranks against the enemy, and they follow where you go. It is therefore better that they learn the truth under your leadership than that they remain in error when you perish. God is not mocked, and he does not love the man who refuses to confess him before the world for the sake of an earthly kingdom.”

Confounded by this reasoning, Gundobad nevertheless persisted in his madness until the end of his life and would not publicly confess the equality of the Trinity. Blessed Avitus was a man of great eloquence at that time. When heresy arose at Constantinople—both Eutyches’s heresy and that taught by Sabellius, namely that my Lord Jesus Christ possessed no divinity—Avitus wrote against them at King Gundobad’s request. Admirable letters of his survive among me today; just as they suppressed heresy then, so now they build up God’s Church. He wrote one book of homilies, six books in verse on the beginning of the world and various other creations, and nine books of letters, among which the letters mentioned above are contained.

In one homily on the Rogations he relates that these very Rogations, which I celebrate before the triumph of the Lord’s Ascension, were instituted by Mamertus, bishop of Vienne, over whom Avitus himself then presided, when that city was terrified by many prodigies. It was shaken by frequent earthquakes, and wild deer and wolves entered the gates and wandered fearlessly through the whole city, as he wrote. While these things occurred throughout the year and the days of the Easter solemnity approached, all the people devoutly awaited God’s mercy, hoping that the great feast would end their terror. But during the vigil of that glorious night, while mass was being celebrated, the royal palace within the walls was suddenly kindled by divine fire. Everyone fled the church in terror, believing either that the whole city would be consumed by the fire or that it would collapse as the earth split open. The holy bishop prostrated himself before the altar and implored the Lord’s mercy with groans and tears. What more need I say? The illustrious bishop’s prayer penetrated the heights of heaven, and the river of flowing tears extinguished the fire in the house. As these things happened and the Ascension of the Lord’s majesty approached, he proclaimed a fast to the people and instituted a form of prayer, an order of eating, and a cheerful distribution of alms. When the terrors ceased, the report of the deed spread through every province and prompted all bishops to imitate what the bishop had done in faith. It is celebrated to this day in God’s name in every church with compunction of heart and contrition of spirit.

### 35. How Clovis and Alaric saw each other

<a id="hist-02-en-036"></a>
[**[036]**](#hist-02-la-036) When Alaric, king of the Goths, saw King Clovis continually defeating nations, he sent envoys to him, saying: “If my brother is willing, let it be his desire that we meet together with God’s favour.” Clovis did not refuse and came to him. They met on an island in the Loire near the village of Amboise in the territory of Tours. They talked, ate, and drank together, pledged friendship, and departed in peace. Many in Gaul even then desired with the deepest longing to have the Franks as their lords.

### 36. Of the bishop Quintianus

<a id="hist-02-en-037"></a>
[**[037]**](#hist-02-la-037) Because of this hatred Quintianus, bishop of Rodez, was driven from his city. The citizens told him: “Your desire is for Frankish dominion to possess this land.” A few days later discord arose between him and the citizens, and suspicion fell upon the Goths living in the city, while the citizens reproached him for wishing to subject himself to Frankish rule. Taking counsel, the Goths planned to run him through with the sword. When this was reported to the man of God, he rose at night and left Rodez with his most faithful servants, coming to Clermont. Saint Bishop Eufrasius, who had succeeded Aprunculus of Dijon, received him kindly, gave him houses, lands, and vineyards, and kept him with him, saying: “This church’s resources are sufficient to support us both; only let the love which the blessed apostle preaches remain between priests of God.” The bishop of Lyon also gave him part of his church’s property in Auvergne. The other things concerning Saint Quintianus, both the plots he suffered and the works the Lord deigned to perform through him, are written in the book of his Life.

### 37. The war with Alaric

<a id="hist-02-en-038"></a>
[**[038]**](#hist-02-la-038) King Clovis said to his men: “It is a great grief to me that these Arians hold part of Gaul. Let me go with God’s help, defeat them, and bring the land under my authority.” When this speech pleased everyone, he mobilized the army and marched towards Poitiers, where Alaric was then staying. Because part of his army was passing through the territory of Tours, in reverence for blessed Martin he issued an edict that no one should take anything from that region except fodder and water. A soldier found a poor man’s hay and said: “Did the king not command that only grass and nothing else be taken? This is grass. I shall not transgress his command if I take it.” He used violence against the poor man and carried off the hay by force. The deed reached the king, who immediately killed him with the sword and said: “Where will my hope of victory be if I offend blessed Martin?” It was enough to keep the army from taking anything further from that region.

The king sent messengers to the blessed church, saying: “Go, and perhaps you will receive some omen of victory from the holy building.” Giving them gifts to present at the holy place, he said: “If you, Lord, are my helper and have decreed to deliver this unbelieving people, ever hostile to you, into my hands, deign graciously to reveal it as I enter Saint Martin’s church, so that I may know that you will be favourable to your servant.” The servants hurried and, arriving at the place by the king’s command, entered the holy church. The precentor unexpectedly began this antiphon: “You have girded me with strength for battle; you have subdued under me those who rose against me, you have given me the backs of my enemies, and you have destroyed those who hated me.” Hearing the singers, they thanked God, made vows to the blessed confessor, and joyfully reported to the king.

When Clovis came with his army to the river Vienne, he did not know at all where he ought to cross, for it had swollen with rainwater. That night he prayed to the Lord to deign to show him a ford. In the morning a doe of wonderful size entered the river before them by God’s command, and as it crossed the people learned where they could pass. When the king came to Poitiers and was staying in tents at a distance, he saw a fiery beacon come out of Saint Hilary’s church and seem to approach above him. Aided by the blessed confessor Hilary’s light, he was to defeat more freely the heretical ranks against which that bishop had often contended for the faith. Clovis charged the whole army not to despoil anyone or seize anyone’s property either there or on the march.

In those days there was a man of praiseworthy holiness, Abbot Maxentius, who for fear of God lived as a recluse in his monastery in the territory of Poitiers. I have not given the monastery’s name in this account because the place is still called the Cell of Saint Maxentius today. When his monks saw a troop of the enemy approaching the monastery, they begged the abbot to come out of his cell and console them. When he delayed, they were so terrified that they opened the door and brought him out. He went intrepidly to meet the enemy as though to ask for peace. One of them drew his sword to strike off Maxentius’s head, but his hand, raised to his ear, became rigid, and the sword fell backwards. The man prostrated himself at the blessed man’s feet and begged pardon. Seeing this, the others returned to the army in great fear, afraid that they too would perish. The blessed confessor touched the man’s arm with blessed oil, made the sign of the cross, and restored it to health. Through his intercession the monastery remained unharmed. Maxentius performed many other miracles; anyone who inquires diligently and reads the book of his Life will find them all.

Meanwhile, in the twenty-fifth year of Clovis, King Clovis met Alaric, king of the Goths, on the field of Vouillé at the tenth milestone from Poitiers. Clovis’s men fought at a distance, while the Goths resisted at close quarters. When the Goths turned their backs according to their custom, King Clovis obtained victory with the Lord’s help. He had as his helper Chloderic, son of Sigibert the Lame. This Sigibert had been wounded in the knee while fighting the Alamanni at Zülpich and limped. After Clovis had put the Goths to flight and killed King Alaric, two enemies suddenly came up and struck him on both sides with lances, but the help of his armour and his swift horse saved him from death. A great multitude of Auvergne people who had come with Apollinaris fell there, including the leading senators. From this battle Alaric’s son Amalaric fled to Spain and skilfully secured his father’s kingdom. Clovis sent his son Theuderic through Albi and Rodez to Clermont. Going there, Theuderic subjected those cities from the Gothic frontiers to the Burgundian border to his father’s authority. Alaric had reigned for twenty-two years. Clovis spent the winter at Bordeaux, took all Alaric’s treasures from Toulouse, and came to Angoulême. The Lord granted him such grace that the walls fell of their own accord at his appearance. He drove out the Goths and subjected the city to his dominion. After winning this victory he returned to Tours, offering many gifts to the holy church of blessed Martin.

### 38. Of the patriciate of King Clovis

<a id="hist-02-en-039"></a>
[**[039]**](#hist-02-la-039) Clovis received codicils granting him the consulship from the emperor Anastasius. In the basilica of blessed Martin he put on a purple tunic and a military cloak and placed a diadem on his head. Then he mounted a horse and, with most generous intent, scattered gold and silver with his own hand among the people present along the route between the gate of the atrium and the city church. From that day he was addressed as consul or Augustus. He left Tours and came to Paris, where he established the seat of his kingdom. Theuderic also came to him there.

### 39. Of the bishop Licinius

<a id="hist-02-en-040"></a>
[**[040]**](#hist-02-la-040) When Eustochius, bishop of Tours, died, Licinius was ordained bishop of Tours, eighth after Saint Martin. The war described above took place in his time, and King Clovis came to Tours in his time. Licinius is said to have been in the East, visited the places of the saints, gone to Jerusalem itself, and often seen the places of the Lord’s Passion and Resurrection which I read of in the Gospels.

### 40. Of the death of Sigibert the elder, and of his son

<a id="hist-02-en-041"></a>
[**[041]**](#hist-02-la-041) While King Clovis was staying at Paris, he secretly sent to Sigibert’s son, saying: “Behold, your father has grown old and limps from a lame foot. If he were to die, his kingdom would rightly be delivered to you together with my friendship.” Seduced by greed, Chloderic plotted to kill his father. When Sigibert left Cologne, crossed the Rhine, and prepared to travel through the forest of Buchonia, he fell asleep in his tent at noon. Chloderic sent assassins against him, and they killed him there, intending that Chloderic should possess his kingdom. But by God’s judgment he fell into the pit he had hostilely dug for his father. He sent messengers to King Clovis to report his father’s death and say: “My father is dead, and I have his treasures and kingdom. Send your men to me, and I shall willingly send you whatever pleases you from his treasures.” Clovis answered: “I thank you for your goodwill and ask that you show everything to my men when they come; you yourself shall possess it thereafter.” When they came, Chloderic displayed his father’s treasures. While they examined various objects, he said: “My father used to heap his gold coins in this little chest.” They said: “Put your hand in to the very bottom and find everything.” When he did so and was bent far over, one of them raised his hand and struck his brain with a battle-axe. Thus the unworthy son suffered what he had inflicted on his father.

Hearing that Sigibert and his son had been killed, Clovis came to the same place and summoned all the people, saying: “Hear what has happened. While I was sailing on the river Scheldt, Chloderic, my kinsman’s son, pursued his father, claiming that I wished to kill him. As Sigibert fled through the forest of Buchonia, Chloderic sent bandits against him, delivered him to death, and killed him. Chloderic himself was struck by someone unknown while opening his father’s treasures and died. I have no part in any of this. I cannot shed my kinsmen’s blood, which would be sacrilege. But since these things have happened, I offer you advice, if it seems acceptable: turn to me and place yourselves under my protection.” Hearing this, they applauded with shields and voices, raised Clovis on a shield, and established him as king over them. He accepted Sigibert’s kingdom and treasures and brought the people themselves under his authority. For God daily cast down his enemies beneath his hand and increased his kingdom because he walked with an upright heart before him and did what was pleasing in his sight.

### 41. Of the death of Chararic and his son

<a id="hist-02-en-042"></a>
[**[042]**](#hist-02-la-042) After this Clovis moved against Chararic. When Clovis had fought Syagrius, Chararic, though summoned to assist him, stood at a distance and aided neither side. He waited for the outcome, intending to form friendship with whichever side won. Angered by this, Clovis went against him. He trapped Chararic by deceit and captured him with his son. He bound them and had their hair cut off; he ordered Chararic to be ordained a priest and his son a deacon.

While Chararic lamented and wept over his humiliation, his son is said to have answered: “These leaves have been cut from a green tree, but it has not withered completely. The branches will quickly spring forth and grow again. Would that the man who did this might perish just as quickly!” These words reached Clovis’s ears: they were threatening to let their hair grow and to kill him. He therefore ordered both to be struck on the head and killed. After their deaths he acquired their kingdom, together with its treasures and people.

### 42. Of the death of Ragnachar and his brothers

<a id="hist-02-en-043"></a>
[**[043]**](#hist-02-la-043) At that time Ragnachar, who ruled at Cambrai, was so unrestrained in debauchery that he scarcely showed indulgence even to his nearest relatives. His counselor was Farro, a man defiled by similar filth. It is said that whenever food, a gift, or anything else was brought to the king, Farro would say that it was enough for him and for his Farro. The Franks swelled with the greatest indignation at this.

Clovis therefore gave gold ornaments, armlets, and belts to Ragnachar’s leudes so that they would invite him to attack their king. But all the supposed gold was counterfeit: the objects were made of gilded bronze by deceit. When Clovis moved his army against Ragnachar, Ragnachar repeatedly sent scouts to learn about the force. When the messengers returned, he asked how strong the army was. They answered: “It is a very great reinforcement for you and your Farro.” Clovis approached and drew up his army for battle. Seeing his own army defeated, Ragnachar prepared to escape in flight, but the soldiers captured him, bound his hands behind his back, and brought him before Clovis together with his brother Richarius.

Clovis said to him: “Why have you humiliated our family by allowing yourself to be bound? It would have been better for you to die.” He raised his axe and buried it in Ragnachar’s head. Turning to the brother, he said: “If you had given your brother help, he would not have been bound.” He likewise struck Richarius with the axe and killed him.

After their deaths the traitors discovered that the gold they had received from the king was false. When they told the king, he is said to have answered: “A man who willingly leads his own lord to death deserves to receive such gold.” He said that it should be enough for them to remain alive, so that they would not perish under torments in wretched expiation of betraying their lords. Hearing this, they begged to obtain his favor, declaring that it would be enough if they were permitted to live.

The kings mentioned above were Clovis’s relatives. Their brother, named Rignomer, was killed at the city of Le Mans on Clovis’s orders. After their deaths Clovis took their whole kingdom and treasures. He also killed many other kings and close relatives whom he feared might take the kingdom from him, and extended his rule through all the Gauls. Nevertheless, when he had once assembled his men, he is said to have spoken of the relatives whom he himself had destroyed: “Woe to me, who have remained like a pilgrim among strangers and have no relative who can help me if adversity comes.” He said this not out of grief for their deaths, but by deceit, hoping to discover whether anyone still remained whom he could kill.

### 43. Of the death of Clovis

<a id="hist-02-en-044"></a>
[**[044]**](#hist-02-la-044) After these events Clovis died at Paris and was buried in the basilica of the holy apostles which he himself had built with Queen Chrotchildis. He died in the fifth year after the battle of Vouillé. His whole reign comprised thirty years, and his entire life forty-five years. From Saint Martin’s death to the death of King Clovis—which occurred in the eleventh year of the episcopate of Licinius, bishop of Tours—112 years are reckoned. **[FLAG — Gregory’s received chronology.]**

After her husband’s death Queen Chrotchildis came to Tours. She remained there all the days of her life, serving at blessed Martin’s basilica with the utmost chastity and kindness, and rarely visiting Paris.

Here ends Book Two.

## Praefatio

<a id="hist-02-la-001"></a>
[**[001]**](#hist-02-en-001) Prosequentes ordinem temporum, mixte confusequae tam virtutes sanctorum quam strages gentium memoramus. Non enim inrationabiliter accipi puto, se filicem beatorum vitam inter miserorum memoremus excidia, cum idem non facilitas scripturis, sed temporum series praestitit. Nam sullicitus lector, si inquirat strinue, invenit inter illas regum Israheliticorum historias sub Samuhel iustum Fineen interisse sacrilegum ac sub David, quem Fortem manu dicunt, Golian alophilum conruisse. Meminiat etiam sub Heliae eximii vatis tempore, qui pluvias cum voluit abstulit et cum libuit arentibus terris infudit, qui viduae paupertatem oratione locopletavit, quantae populorum strages fuere, quae famis vel quae siccitas miseram oppraesserit humum; quae sub Ezechie tempore, cui Deus ad vitam quindecim annos auxit, Hierusolima mala pertulerit. Sed et sub Heliseum prophetam, qui mortuos vitae restituit et alia in populis multa miracula fecit, quantae internitiones, quae miseriae ipsum Israheliticum populum oppraesserunt. Sic et Eusebius, Severus Hieronimusquae in chronicis atque Horosius et bella regum et virtutes martyrum pariter texuerunt. Ita et nos idcircum sic scripsemus, quod facilius saeculorum ordo vel annorum ratio usque nostra tempora tota repperiatur. Venientes ergo per antedictorum auctorum historias, ea quae in posterum acta sunt Domino iubente disseremus.

## 1. De episcopatu Brictii.

<a id="hist-02-la-002"></a>
[**[002]**](#hist-02-en-002) Igitur post excessum beati Martini Turonicae civitatis episcopi, summi et inconparabilis viri, de cuius virtutibus magna apud nos volumina retinentur, Brictius ad episcopatum succedit. At vero hic Brictius, cum esset primaevae aetatis iuvenis, sancto adhuc viventi in corpore multas tendebat insidias, pro eo quod ab eodem plerumque, cur faciles res sequeretur, arguebatur. Quadam tamen die dum quidam infirmus medicinam a beato Martino expeteret, Brictium adhuc diaconum in platea convenit; cui simpliciter ait: 'Ecce! ego praestolor beatum virum et nescio, ubi sit vel quid operis agat'. Cui Brictius: 'Si', inquid, 'delerum illum quaeris, prospice eminus; ecce! caelum solite sicut amens respicit'. Cumque pauper ille, occursu reddito, quod petierat inpetrasset, Brictionem diaconem vir beatus adloquitur: 'En ego, Brictio, delerus tibi videor?' Cumque ille confusus haec audiens dixisse se denegaret, ait vir sanctus: 'Nonne aures meae ad os tuum erant, cum haec eminus loquebaris? Amen dico tibi, quia obtinui apud Deum, ut post me ad pontificatus honorem accedas, sed noveris te in episcopatu multa adversa passurum'. Brictius haec audiens inridebat, dicens: 'Nonne verum dixi, istum delera verba proferre?' Sed et praesbiterii honore praeditus, saepius beatum virum conviciis lacessivit. Adeptum ergo consentientibus civibus pontificatus officium, orationi vacabat. Quia quamquam esset superbus et vanus, castus tamen habebatur in corpore. Tricesimo tertio vero ordinationis suae anno oritur contra eum lamentabilis causa pro crimine. Nam mulier, ad quam cubicularii eius vestimenta deferebant ad abluendum, quae sub specie religionis erat veste mutata, concepit et peperit. Qua de re surrexit omnis populus Toronorum in ira, et totum crimen super episcopum referunt, volentes eum unanimiter lapidare. Aiebant enim: 'Diu pietas sancti tuam celavit luxoriam, nec nos Deus diutius sinit manus tuas indignas osculando pollui'. Illo quoque e contrario viriliter haec negante: 'Adferte', inquid, 'infantem ad me'. Cumque oblatus fuisset infans, triginta dies ab ortu habens, ait ad eum episcopus: 'Adiuro te per Iesum Christum, filium Dei omnipotentis, ut, si ego te generavi, coram cunctis edicas'. Et ille: 'Non es', inquid, 'tu pater meus'. Populis autem rogantibus, ut quis esset pater interrogaret, ait sacerdos: 'Non est hoc meum. Quod ad me pertinuit, sollicitus fui; vobis si aliquid suppetit, per vos quaerite'. Tunc illi magicis haec artibus factum adseverantes, insurgunt contra eum in una conspiratione, et trahentes eum, dicebant: 'Non diutius nobis falso pastoris nomine dominaberis'. Ille autem ad satisfaciendo adhuc populo prunas ardentes in byrrum suum posuit, et ad se stringens, usque ad sepulchrum beati Martini una cum populorum turbis accedit, proiectasque ante sepulchrum prunas, vestimentum inustum apparuit. Illo quoque sic prosequente: 'Sicut istud vestimentum ab his ignibus videtis inlesum, ita et corpus meum a tactu muliebris coiti est inpollutum', illis vero non credentibus, sed contradicentibus, trahitur, calumniatur, eicitur, ut sermo sancti adimpleretur: 'Noveris, te in episcopatu multa adversa passurum'. Hunc enim eiectum, Iustinianum in episcopatu constituunt. Denique Brictius Romanae urbis papam expetiit, flens et eiulans atque dicens: 'Merito haec patior, quia peccavi in sanctum Dei et eum delerum et amentem saepe vocavi; cuius videns virtutes non credidi'. Post cuius abscessum aiunt Toronici sacerdoti suo: 'Vade post eum et exere negotium tuum, quia, si eum prosecutus non fueris, a nostro omnium contemptu humiliaberis' Iustinianus vero egressus a Turonus, Vircellis Italiae civitatem adgressus, iudicio Dei percussus, obiit peregrinus. Toronici eius obitum audientes et in sua malitia perdurantes, Armentium in eius loco constituunt. At Brictius episcopus Romam veniens, cuncta quae pertulerat papae refert. Qui ad sedem apostolicam resedens, plerumque missarum solemnia caelebravit ibi, quicquid in sanctum Dei deliquerat deflens. Septimo igitur regressus anno a Roma, eum auctoritate papae illius Toronus redire disponit; et veniens ad vicum cui nomen est Laudiacum, sexto ab urbe miliario, mansionem accepit. Armentius vero febre corripitur, media autem nocte spiritum exalavit. Quod protinus Brictio episcopo per visum revelatum est; qui ait suis: 'Surgite velocius, ut ad tumulandum fratrem nostrum, Turonicum pontificem, occurramus'. Cumque illi venientes portam civitatis ingrederent, ecce! istum per aliam portam mortuum efferebant. Quo sepulto, Brictius in cathedram suam regressus est, septem postea feliciter vivens annos. Cui post quadragesimo septimo episcopatus anno defuncto sanctus Eustochius successit magnificae sanctitatis.

## 2. De Wandalis et persecutionem christianorum sub ipsis.

<a id="hist-02-la-003"></a>
[**[003]**](#hist-02-en-003) Post haec Wandali a loco suo degressi, cum Gunderico rege in Gallias ruunt. Quibus valde vastatis, Spanias adpetunt. Hos secuti Suebi, id est Alamanni, Gallitiam adpraehendunt. Nec multo pos scandalum inter utrumque oritur populum, quoniam propinqui sibi erant. Cumque ad bellum armati procederent ac iamiamque in conflicto parati essent, ait Alamannorum rex: 'Quousque bellum super cunctum populum commovetur? Ne pereant, quaeso, populi utriusque falangae, sed procedant duo de nostris in campum cum armis bellicis, et ipse inter se confligant. Tunc ille, cuius puer vicerit, regione sine certamine obtenebit'. Ad haec cunctus consensit populus, ne universa multitudo in ore gladii rueret. His enim diebus Gundericus rex obierat, in cuius loco Trasamundusobtenueratregnum. Confligentibus vero pueris, pars Wandalorum victa succubuit; interfectoque puero, placitum egrediendi Trasamundus spondit, ut scilicet, praeparatis itineris necessariis, se a finibus Hispaniae removerit. Per idem vero tempus persecutionem in christianus Trasamundus exercuit ac totam Hispaniam, ut perfidiam Arrianae sectae consentiret, tormentes ac diversis mortibus inpellebat. Unde factum est, ut puella quaedam relegiosa, praedives opibus ac secundum saeculi dignitatem nobilitate senatoria florens et, quod his omnibus est nobilius, fide catholica pollens, quae Deo omnipotenti inreprehensibiliter serviebat, ad hac quaestionem adduceretur. Cumque regis fuisset oblata conspectibus, coepit eam primum ad rebaptizandum blandis sermonibus inlicire. Quae cum venenosum eius iaculum fidei parma propelliret, praecipit rex facultates eius auferre, quae iam mente possedebat regna paradisi, deinde suppliciis adplecatam sine spe praesentes vitae torquiri. Quid plura? Post multas quaestiones, post ablatusto terrenarum divitiarum thesaurus, cum ad hoc frangi non possit, ut beatam scinderit Trinitatem, ad rebaptizandum invita deducitur. Cumque in illud caenosum lavacrum vi cogeretur inmergi ac proclamaret: 'Patrem cum Filio ac Spiritum sanctum unius credo esse substantiae essentiaeque', digno aquas unguine cunctas infecit, id est fluxu ventris aspersit. Exhinc ad legitimam deductam quaestionem, post aeculeos, post flammas et ungulas, Christo domino capitis decisione decatur. Post haec prosequentibus Alamannis usque Traductam, transito mare, Wandali per totam Africam ac Mauritaniam sunt dispersi.

## 3. De Cyrola hereticorum episcopum et de sanctis martyribus.

<a id="hist-02-la-004"></a>
[**[004]**](#hist-02-en-004) Sed quoniam eorum tempore persecutio in christianis invaluit, sicut superius mentio facta est, videtur, ut aliqua ex his quae circa Dei eclesias intulerunt vel quemadmodum de regno expulsi sint, memorare. Defuncto igitur Trasamundo post scelera, quae in sanctis Dei exercuit, Honericus mente crudilior Africanum occupat regnum atque ex electione Wandalorum ipsis praeponitur. Cuius sub tempore quanti christianorum populi pro ipso Christi sacratissimum nomine caesi sint, ab hominibus non potest conpraehendi. Testes est tamen Africa, quae misit, et Christi dextera, quae gemmis inmarciscibilibus coronavit. Legimus tamen quorundam ex ipsis martyrum passiones, ex quibus quaedam replicanda sunt, ut ad ea quae spondimus veniamus. Igitur Cirola, falso vocatus episcopus, hereticorum tunc maximus habebatur assertor. Cumque ad persequendum christianus rex per diversa transmitteret, sanctum Eugenium episcopum, verum inenarrabili sanctitate, qui tunc ferebatur magnae prudentiae esse, in suburbano civitates suae repperit persecutor. Quem ita violenter rapuit, ut neccohortandumgregem christicolum abire permitterit. Ille vero cum se vi viderit abduci, epistolam civibus pro custodiendam fidem catholicam hoc modo transmisit: Dilectissimis et in Christi amore dulcissimis filiis et filiabus ecclesiae mihi a Deo commissae Eugenius episcopus. Regalis emanavit auctoritas, pro exercenda fide catholica edicto nos ad Cartaginem venire praecepit. Et ideo ne abiens a vobis ecclesiam Dei in ambiguo, hoc est in suspenso, dimitterem, aut oves Christi non verus pastor silentio reliquissem, necessarium duxi has pro me vicarias vestrae dirigere sanctitati, in quibus non sine lacrimis peto, hortor et moneo et satis abundeque obtestor per Dei maiestatem et per tremendum iudicii diem atque adventus Christi terribilem claritatem, ut fixius teneatis catholicam fidem, adserentes Filium Patri et Spiritum sanctum eandem habere cum Patre et Filio deitatem. Servate itaque unici baptismatis gratiam, custodientes crismatis unctionem. Nemo post aquam revertatur ad aquam, renatus ex aqua. Nutu enim Dei sal in aqua conficitur, sed si in aqua redactum fuerit, omnis species eius confestim evacuatur. Unde non inmerito Dominus in euangelio ait: 'Si sal infatuatum fuerit, in quo salietur?' Et utique hoc est infatuari velle secundo condiri, cum semel factum sufficiat. Non audistis Christum dicentem: 'Qui semel lotus est, non habet necessitatem iterum lavandi?' Ideoque, fratres et filii filiaeque meae in Domino, non vos contristet absentia mea, quia, si catholicae disciplinae adhaereatis, ego vos nec longinquitate aliqua obliviscor nec morte a vobis divellor. Scitote, quia, quocumque me fecerint dividi agones, mecum est palma: si ad exilium abiero, beati Iohannis euangelistae exemplum; si ad mortis exitium, 'vivere mihi Christus est, et mori lucrum'. Si rediero, fratres, implebit Deus desiderium vestrum. Attamen sufficit modo, quod vobis non tacui; monui, instruxi, quomodo potui, inmunis sum a sanguine omnium pereuntium, et scio, quia adversus eos legentur litterae istae ante tribunal Christi, cum venerit reddere unicuique secundum opera sua. Si reversus fuero, fratres, videbo vos in hac vita; si non reversus fuero, videbo vos in futuro. Dico vobis: valete, orate pro nobis et ieiunate, quia ieiunium et elemosina semper ad misericordiam deflexerunt. Mementote scriptum in euangelio: 'Nolite timere eos qui occidunt corpus, animam autem non possunt occidere; timete autem eum, qui, postquam occiderit, habet potestatem et animam et corpus perdere et mittere in gehennam'. Ductus itaque sanctus Eugenius ad regem, cum illo Arrianorum episcopum pro fide catholica decertavit. Cumque eum de sanctae Trinitatis mistirium potentissime devicissit et insuper multas per eum virtutes Christus ostenderit, in maiore insania idem episcopus invidia inflammante succenditur. Erant enim tunc temporis cum sancto Eugenio viri prudentissimi atque sanctissimi Vindimialis et Longinus episcopi, pares gradu et virtute non inpares. Nam sanctus Vindimialis eo tempore ferebatur mortuus suscetasse; Longinus autem multis infirmis salutem tribuit. Eugenius quoque non solum visibilium oculorum caecitatem, sed etiam mentium depellebat. Quod cernens ille nequam Arrianorum episcopus, vocatum ad se quendam hominem ab illo quo ipse vivebat errore, ait: 'Non patior, quod hi episcopi multa in populos signa depromunt illosque cuncti, me neglecto, secuntur. Adquiesce nunc his quae praecipio, et acceptis quinquaginta aureis, sede in platea, per qua nobis est transitus, et manum super clausus oculus ponens, me praetereunte cum reliquis, exclama in magna virtute, dicens: „Ad te, beatissime Cirola, nostrae relegiones antestes, depraecor, ut respiciens manefestis gloriam ac virtutem tuam, ut oculos meos aperiens merear lucem videre quam perdedi" ' Qui iussa conplens resedensque in platea, transeunte heretico cum sanctis Dei, iste qui Deum inridere cogitabat exclamat in magna virtute, dicens: 'Audi me, beatissime Cyrola, audi me, sanctae sacerdus Dei, respice caecitatem meam! Experear ego medicamenta, quae saepe caeci reliqui a te meruerunt, quae lebrosi experti sunt, quae ipsi mortui praesenserunt. Adiuro te per ipsam virtutem quam habes, ut mihi desideratam restituas lucem, quia gravi sum caecitate percussus'. Veritatem enim nesciens, verum dicebat, quia caecaverat eum cupiditas, et virtutem Dei omnipotentis inridere per pecuniam aestimabat. Tunc hereticorum episcopus paulolum se divertit, quasi in virtute triumphaturus, elatus vanitate atque superbia, posuit manum super oculos eius, dicens: 'Secundum fidem nostram, qua recte Deum credimus, aperiantur oculi tui'. Et mox ut hunc nefas erupit, risus mutatur in planctum, et dolus episcopi patefactus in publico; nam tantus dolor oculos miseri illius invasit, ut eos digitis vi comprimeret, ne creparent. Denique clamare coepit miser ac dicere: 'Vae mihi misero, quia seductus sum ab inimico legis divinae! Vae mihi, quia Deum per pecuniam inridere volui et quinquaginta aureos accepi, ut hoc facinus perpetrarem!' Ad episcopum autem aiebat: 'Ecce aurum tuum, redde lumen meum, quod dolo tuo perdidi! Vos quoque rogo, gloriosissimi christiani, ne despiciatis miserum, sed velociter occurrite pereunti! Vere enim cognovi, quia Deus non inridetur'. Tunc sancti Dei misericordia moti: 'Si', inquiunt, 'credis, omnia possibilia sunt credenti'. At ille clamabat voce magna: 'Qui non credederit Christum Filium Dei et Spiritum sanctum aequalem habere substantiam atque deitate cum Patre, hodie quae ego perfero patiatur'. Et adiecit: 'Credo Deum Patrem omnipotentem, credo Filium Dei Christum Iesum aequalem Patri, credo Spiritum sanctum Patri et Filio consubstantialem atque coaeternum'. Haec illi audientes et se invicem honore mutuo praevenientes, oritur inter eos sancta contentio, quis oculis eius signum beatae crucis inponeret. Vindimialis vero ac Longinus Eugenium, ille autem eos exorat, ut manus inponerent caeco. Quod cum fecissent et manus suas super capud eius tenirent, sanctus Eugenius crucem Christi super oculos caeci faciens, ait: 'In nomine Patris et Fili et Spiritus sancti, veri Dei, quem trinum in unam aequalitatem atque omnipotentiam confitemur, aperiantur oculi tui'. Et statim ablata dolore, ad pristinam rediit sanitatem. Manefestissime autem patuit per huius caecitatem, qualiter hereticorum episcopus oculos cordium misero adsertiones suae velabat amictu, ne veram lucem ulli liceret fidei oculis contemplare. O miserum, qui non ingressus per ianuam, id est per Christo, qui est ianua vera, lupus magis gregi quam custus effectus est et facem fidei, quam in credentium cordibus debuerat accendere, in pravitate cordis sui conabatur extinguere! Sancti vero Dei alia signa in populis multa fecerunt, et erat vox una populi dicentes: 'Verus Deus Pater, verus Deus Filius, verus Deus Spiritus sanctus, una fide colendus, uno timore metuendus, eodem honore venerandus; nam quae Cyrola asserit falsa esse cunctis est manefestum'. Videns autem Honoricus rex, assertiones suas per sanctorum fidem gloriosam taliter denutari, nec erigi sectam erroris, sed potius distrui, famamquae pontefices sui in hoc scelere fuisse detectam, sanctus Dei post multa tormenta, post eculeos, post flammas, post ungulas, iussit interfici; beatum vero Eugenium decollari iussit, sub ea speciae, ut, si in ea hora, qua ensis super cervicem eius incumbebat, non reverteretur ad hereticorum sectam, non occideretur, ne eum pro martyrem excolerent christiani, sed exilio damnaretur. Quod ita factum esse, manefestum est. Nam cum imminente morte interrogatus fuisset, se mori pro fide catholica distinaret, respondit: 'Haec enim est sempiterna vita, pro iustitia mori'. Tunc, suspenso gladio, apud Albiginsem Galliarum urbem exilio depotatus est; ubi et finem vitae praesentis fecit. Ad cuius nunc sepulchrum multae virtutis et creberrimae ostenduntur. Sanctum vero Vindimialem gladio percuti praecipit; quod ita impletum est. In hoc certamine et Octavianus archidiaconus et multa milia virorum ac mulierum hanc fidem asserentes interempta atque debilitata sunt. Sed pro amore gloriae nihil erant haec supplicia confessoribus sanctis, qui in paucis vexati, in multis bene noverant disponendus iuxta illud apostoli: Quia non sunt condignae passiones huius tempores ad futuram gloriam, quae revelatur in sanctis. Multi tunc errantes a fide, accipientes divitias, inseruerunt se doloribus multis, sicut infilex ille episcopus nomine Revocatus est revocatus a fide catholica. Tunc et sol teter apparuit, ita ut vix ab eo pars vel tertia eluceret; credo pro tantis sceleribus et effusione sanguinis innocentes. Honoricus vero post tantum facinus arreptus a daemone, qui diu de sanctorum sanguine pastus fuerat, propriis se morsibus laniabat, in quo cruciatu vitam indignam iusta morte finivit. Huic Childericus successit; quo defuncto, Grelesimeris regnum suscipit. Ipse quoque a re publica superatus, vitam principatumque finivit. Et sic regnum decidit Wandalorum.

## 4. De persecutionem sub Athanarico agitata.

<a id="hist-02-la-005"></a>
[**[005]**](#hist-02-en-005) Multae enim heresis eo tempore Dei eclesias inpugnabant, de quibus plerumque ultio divina data est. Nam et Athanaricus Gothorum rex magnam excitavit persecutionem; qui multus christianorum diversis poenis adfectus gladio detruncabat; sed et nonnullus exilio datus fame variisque cruciatibus enecabat. Unde factum est, ut, inminente iudicio Dei, pro effusione sanguinis iusti a regno depelleretur et esset exsul a patria, qui Dei eclesias inpugnabat. Sed ad superiora redeamus.

## 5. De Aravatio episcopo et Chunis.

<a id="hist-02-la-006"></a>
[**[006]**](#hist-02-en-006) Igitur rumor erat, Chunos in Galliis velle prorumpere. Erat autem tunc temporis apud Tungrus oppidum Aravatius eximiae sanctitatis episcopus, qui vigiliis ac ieiuniis vacans, crebro lacrimarum imbre perfusus, Domini misericordiam praecabatur, ne umquam gentem hanc incredulam sibique semper indignam in Galliis venire permitterit. Sed sentiens per spiritum, pro dilictis populi sibi hoc non fuisse concessum, consilium habuit expetendi urbem Romanam, scilicet ut, adiunctam sibi apostolicae virtutis patrocinia, quae humiliter ad Domini misericordiam flagitabat, mereretur facilius obtinere. Accedens ergo ad beati apostoli tumolum, depraecabatur auxilium bonitatis eius, in multa abstinentia, maximae inaedia se consumens, ita ut bidui triduique sine ullo cibo putuque maneret, nec esset intervallum aliquod, in quo ab oratione cessaret. Cumque ibidem per multorum dierum spatia in tali adflictione moraretur, fertur hoc a beato apostolo accepisse responsum: 'Quid me, vir sanctissime, inquietas? Ecce! enim apud Domini deliberationem prursus sanccitum est, Chunos in Gallias advenire easque maxima tempestate debere depopulari. Nunc igitur sume consilium, accelera velociter, ordenadomum tuam, sepulturamconpone, require lentiaminamunda! Ecce! enim migraberis a corpore, nec videbunt oculi tui mala, quae facturi sunt Chuni in Galliis, sicut locutus est dominus Deus noster'. Hoc a sancto apostolo pontifex responso suscepto, iter accelerat Galliasque velociter repetit, veniensque ad urbem Tungrorum, quae erant necessaria sepulturae secum citius levat, valedicensque clericis ac reliquis civibus urbis, denuntiatcum fletu et lamentatione, quia non visuri essent ultra faciem illius. At ille cum heiulato magno et lacrimis prosequentes supplecabant humili praece, dicentes: 'Nederelinquasnos, pater sanctae, ne obliviscaris nostri, pastor bonae!' Sed cum eum fletibus revocare non possent, accepta benedictione cum osculis, redierunt. Hic vero ad Treiectinsemurbem accedens, modica pulsatusfebre, recessita corpore, ablutusque fidelibus, iuxta ipsum agerempublicum est sepultus. Cuius beatum corpus qualiter post multorum temporum spatia sit translatum, in libro Miraculorumscripsimus.

## 6. De basilica sancti Stefani apud Metensim orbem.

<a id="hist-02-la-007"></a>
[**[007]**](#hist-02-en-007) Igitur Chuni a Pannoniis egressi, ut quidam ferunt, in ipsa sanctae paschae vigilia ad Mettinsem urbem reliqua depopulando perveniunt, tradentes urbem incendium, populum in ore gladii trucidantes ipsusque sacerdotes Domini ante sacrosancta altaria perimentes. Nec remansit in ea locus inustus praeter oraturium beati Stefaniprimi martyresac levitae. De quo oraturioquae a quibusdam audivi narrare non distuli. Aiunt enim, quia, priusquam hi hostes venerent, vidisse virum fidelem in visu quasi conferentem cum sanctis apostolis Petro ac Paulo beatum levitam Stefanum de hoc excidio ac dicentem: 'Oro, domini mi, ut non permittatis obtentu vestro Mettensim urbem ab inimicis exuri, quia locus in ea est, in quo parvitatis meae pignera contenentur; sed potius sentiant se populi aliquid me posse cum Domino. Quod si tantum facinus populi supercrevit, ut aliut fieri non possit, nisi civitas tradatur incendio, saltim vel hoc oraturium non cremetur'. Cui ille aiunt: 'Vade in pace, dilectissime frater, oraturium tantum tuum caribit incendium! Pro urbe vero non obtinebimus, quia dominicae sanctionis super eam sententia iam processit. Invaluit enim peccatum populi, et clamor malitiae eorum ascendit coram Deo; ideo civitas haec cremabitur incendio'. Unde proculdubium est, quod horum obtentu, urbe vastata, oraturium permansit inlaesum.

## 7. De uxore Aetii.

<a id="hist-02-la-008"></a>
[**[008]**](#hist-02-en-008) Attela vero Chunorum rex a Mittense urbe egrediens, cum multas Galliarum civitates oppraemeret, Aurilianis adgreditur eamque maximo arietum inpulsu nititur expugnare. Erat autem eo tempore beatissimus Annianus in supradicta urbe episcopus, vir eximiae prudentiae ac laudabilis sanctitatis, cuius virtutum gesta nobiscum fideliter retenentur. Cumque inclusi populi suo pontefice, quid agerent, adclamarent, ille confisus in Deo, monet omnes in oratione prosterni et cum lacrimis praesentem semper in necessitatibus Domini auxilium inplorare. Denique his ut praeciperat depraecantibus, ait sacerdus: 'Aspicite de muro civitatis, si Dei miseratio iam succurrat'. Suspicabatur enim per Domini misericordiam Aetium advenire, ad quem et Arelate abierat prius suspectus futuri. Aspicientes autem de muro, niminem viderunt. Et ille: 'Orate' inquid, 'fideliter; Dominus enim liberavit vos hodie!' Orantibus autem illis, ait: 'Aspicite iterum!'. Et cum aspexissent, niminem viderunt qui ferret auxilium. Ait eis tertio: 'Si fideliter petitis, Dominus velociter adest'. Ad ille cum fletu et heiulatu magno Domini misericordiam inplorabant. Exactam quoque orationem, tertio iuxta senis imperium aspicientes de muro, viderunt a longe quasi nebolam de terra consurgere. Quod renuntiantes, ait sacerdus: 'Domini auxilium est'. Interea iam trementibus ab impetu arietum muris iamque ruituris, ecce! Aetius et Theudor Gothorum rex ac Thorismodus, filius eius, cum exercitibus suis ad civitatem adcurrunt adversumque hostem eieciunt repelluntque. Itaque liberatam obtentu beati antestites civitatem, Attilanem fugant. Qui Mauriacum campum adiens, se praecingit ad bellum. Quod hi audientes, se contra eum viriliter praeparant. His diebus Romam sonus adiit, Aetium in maximo discrimine inter falangas hostium laborare. Quo auditu uxor eius anxia atque maesta, assiduae basilicas sanctorum apostolorum adibat atque, ut virum suum de hac via reciperet sospitem, praecabatur. Quae cum die noctuque haec agerit, quadam nocte homo pauperculus, crapulatus a vino, in angulo basilicae beati Petri apostoli obdormivit. Clausis autem ex more usteis, a custodibus non est nanctus. De nocte vero consurgens, relucentibus per tota aedis spatia lychinis, pavore territus, aditum, per quem foris evaderit, quaerit. Verum ubi primi atque alterius ustei claustra pulsat et obserata cuncta cognoscit, solo decubuit, trepidus praestolans locum, ut, convenientibus ad matutinis hymnis populis, hic liber abscederit. Interea vidit duas personas se invicem venerabiliter salutantes sollicitusque de suis esse prosperetatibus. Tunc qui erat senior ita exorsus est: 'Uxoris Aeti lacrimas diutius sustenire non patior. Petit enim assiduae, ut virum suum de Galliis reducam incolomem, cum aliud exinde fuisset apud divinum iuditium praefinitum, sed tamen obtenui inmensam pietatem pro vita illius. Et ecce nunc illum propero viventem exinde reducturus! Verumtamen obtestcr, ut qui haec audierit sileat arcanumque Dei vulgare non audeat, ne pereat velociter a terra'. Ille autem haec audiens, silire non potuit; sed mox inluciscente caelo omnia quae audierat matrisfamiliae pandit, expletisque sermonibus, lumen caruit oculorum. Igitur Aetius cum Gothis Francisque coniunctus adversus Attilanem confligit. At ille ad internitionem vastari suum cernens exercitum, fuga delabitur. Theodor vero Gothorum rex huic certamine subcubuit Nam nullus ambigat, Chunorum exercitum obtentu memorati antestites fuisse fugatum. Verum Aetius patritius cum Thorismodo victuriam obtinuit hostesque delivit. Expletoque bello, ait Aetius Thorismodo: 'Festina velociter redire in patriam, ne insistente germano a patris regno priveris'. Haec ille audiens, cum velocitate discessit, quasi antecipaturus fratrem et prior patris cathedram adepturus. Simili et Francorum regem dolo fugavit. Illis autem recedentibus, Aetius, spoliato campo, victor in patriam cum grande est reversus spolia. Attila vero cum paucis reversus est, nec multo post Aquileia a Chunis capta, incensa atque deruta, Italia pervagata atque subversa est. Thorismodus cui supra meminimus, Alanos bello edomuit, ipsi deinceps post multas lites et bella a fratribus oppraessus ac iugulatus interiit.

## 8. Quid de Aetio historiograffi scripserint.

<a id="hist-02-la-009"></a>
[**[009]**](#hist-02-en-009) Igitur his ita degestis ac per ordinem expletis, quid de Aetio supra memorato Renati Frigiredi narret historia, tacere nefas putavi. Nam cum in duodecimo historiarum libro referat, post divi Honori excessum Valentinianum puerolum, uno tantum lustro peracto, a consubrino Theodosio imperatorem fuisse creatum et apud urbem Romam tyrannum Iohannem in imperium surrexisse, legatusque eius a caesare dicat fuisse dispectus, adiecit: Dum haec ita gererentur, legati ad tyrannum reversi sunt, mandata atrocia reportantes. Quibus permotus Iohannis Aetium, id temporis curam palatii, cum ingenti auri pondere ad Chunus transmittit, notus sibi obsidatus sui tempore et familiari amicicia divinctos, cum mandatis huiusmodi: cum primum partes adversae Italiam ingressae forent, ipse a tergo adoriretur, se ad fronte venturum. Et quia de hoc viro consequenter plura memoranda sunt, genus moresque ordire placet. Gaudentius pater, Scyciae provintiae primoris loci, a domesticatu exorsus militiam, usque ad magisterii equitum culmen profectus. Mater Itala, nobilis ac locuplex faemina. Aetius filius a puero praetorianus, tribus annis Alarici obsessus, dehinc Chunorum; post haec Carpilionis gener, ex comite domesticorum et Iohannis cura palatii. Medii corporis, virilis habitudinis, decenter formatus, quo neque infirmitudini esset neque oneri, animo alacer, membris vegitus, eques prumptissimus, sagittarum iactu peritus, contu inpiger, bellis aptissimus, pacis artibus celebris, nullius avaritiae, minimae cupiditatis, bonis animi praeditus, ne inpulsoribus quidem pravis ab instituto suo devians, iniuriarum patientissimus, laboris adpetens, inpavidus periculorum, famis, sitis, vigiliarum tolerantissimus. Cui ab ineunte aetate praedictum liquet, quantae potentiae fatis distinaretur, temporibus suis locisque celebrandus. Haec supradictus historiograffus de Aetio narrat. Adultus autem Valentinianus imperator, metuens, ne se per tyrannidem Aetius oppraemeret, eum nullis causis extantibus interimit. Ipse postmodum augustus dum in campo Martio pro tribunali resedens concionaretur ad populum, Occila, buccellarius Aeti, ex adverso veniens, eum gladio perfodit. Talis utrisque extitit finis.

## 9. Quid de Francis idemque dicant.

<a id="hist-02-la-010"></a>
[**[010]**](#hist-02-en-010) De Francorum vero regibus, quis fuerit primus, a multis ignoratur. Nam cum multa de eis Sulpici Alexandri narret historia, non tamen regem primum eorum ullatinus nominat, sed duces eos habuisse dicit. Quae tamen de eisdem referat, memorare videtur. Nam cum dicit, Maximum intra Aquileiam, amissam omnem spem imperii, quasi amentem resedere, adiungit: Eo tempore Genobaude, Marcomere et Sunnone ducibus Franci in Germaniam prorupere, ac pluribus mortalium limite inrupto caesis, fertiles maxime pagus depopulati, Agrippinensi etiam Coloniae metum incusserunt. Quod ubi Treverus perlatum est, Nanninus et Quintinus militaris magistri, quibus infantiam filii et defensionem Galliarum Maximus conmiserat, collecto exercitu, apud Agripinam convenerunt. Sed onusti praeda hostes, provinciarum opima depopulati, Rhenum transierunt, pluribus suorum in Romano relictis solo, ad repetendam depopulationem paratis, cum quibus congressus Romanis adcomodus fuit, multis Francorum apud Carbonariam ferro perimptis. Cumque consultaretur succensu, an in Franciam transire deberit, Nannenus abnuit, quia non inparatus et in locis suis indubiae fortiores futurus sciebat. Quod cum Quintino et reliquis viris militaribus displicuisset, Nanneno Mogontiacum reverso, Quintinus cum exercitu circa Nivisium castellum Rhenum transgressus, secundis a fluvio castris, casas habitatoribus vacuas atque ingentes vicos distitutos offendit. Franci enim simulatu metu se in remotiores saltus reciperant, concidibus per extrema silvarum procuratis. Itaque universis domibus exustis, in quas saevire soliditas ignava victoriae consummationem reponebat, nocte sollicita militis sub armorum onere duxerant. Ac primo diluculo Quintino proelii duci ingressi saltus, in medium fere diem inplicantes se erroribus viarum, toto pervagati sunt. Tandem cum ingentibus saeptis omnia a solido clausa offendissent, in palustres campus, qui silvis iungebantur, prorumpere molientibus, kostium rare apparuere, qui coniuncti arborum truncis vel concidibus superstantes, velut e fastigiis turrium sagittas turmentorum ritu effudere inlitas herbarum venenis, ut summe cutis neque letalibus inflicta locis vulnera aut dubiae mortis sequerentur. Dehinc maiore multitudine hostium circumfusus exercitus, in aperta camporum, quae libera Franci reliquerant, avide effusus est. Ac primi equites voraginibus inmersi, permixtis hominum iumentorumque corporibus, ruinam invicem suorum oppraessi sunt. Pedites etiam, quos nulla onera equorum calcaverant, inplicati limo, egre explecantes gressum, rursus se, qui paulo ante vix emerserant, silvis trepidantes occulebant. Perturbatis ergo ordinibus, caesae legionis. Heraclio Iovinianorum tribuno ac paene omnibus qui militibus praeerant extinctis, paucis effugium totum nox et latibula silvarum praestiterunt. Haec in tertic Historiae libro narravit. In quarto vero libro, cum de interfectione Victuris, fili Maximi tyranni, narraret, ait: Eo tempore Carietto et Sirus in locum Nanneni subrogati, in Germania cum exercitu opposito Francis diversabantur. Et post pauca, cum Franci de Germania praedas tulissent, adiecit: Nihil Arbogastis differre volens, conmonet caesarem poenas debetas a Francis exigendas, nisi universa, quae superiore anno caesis legionibus diripuerant, confestim restituerent auctorisque belli traderent, in quos violatae pacis perfidia poneretur. Haec acta, cum duces essent, retulit; et deinceps ait: Post dies paucolus, Marcomere et Sunnone Francorum regalibus transacto cursim conloquio imperatisque ex more obsidibus, ad hiemandum Treverus concessit. Cum autem eos regales vocet, nescimus, utrum reges fuerint, an in vices tenuerunt regnum. Idem tamen scriptor, cum necessitates Valentiniani agusti commemorat, haec adiungit: Dum diversa in Oriente per Thracias geruntur, in Gallia status publicus perturbabatur. Clauso apud Viennam palatii aedibus principe Valentiniano paene infra privati modum redacto, militaris rei cura Francis satellitibus tradita, civilia quoque officia transgressa in coniurationem Arbogastis; nullusque ex omnibus sacramentis militiae obstrictis repperiebatur, qui familiari principis sermoni aut iussis obsequi auderet. Dehinc refert: Eodem anno Arbogastis Sunnonem et Marcomere subregolus Francorum gentilibus odiis insectans, Agrepinam regentem maxime hieme petiit, gnarus toto omnes Frantiae recessus penetrandus urendusque, cum decursis foliis nudae atque arentes silvae insidiantes oceulere non possent. Collicto ergo exercitu, transgressus Rhenum, Bricteros ripae proxiwos, pagum etiam quem Chamavi incolunt depopulatus est, nullo umquam occursante, nisi quod pauci ex Ampsivariis et Catthis Marcomere duce in ulterioribus collium iugis apparuere. Iterum hic, relictis tam ducibus quam regalibus, aperte Francos regem habere designat, huiusque nomen praetermissum, ait: Dehinc Eugenius tyrannus, suscepto expeticionale procincto, Rheni limitem petit, ut, cum Alamannorum et Francorum regibus vetustis foederibus ex more initis, inmensum ea tempestate exercitum gentibus feris ostentaret. Haec suprascriptus historiograffus de Francis exseruit. Renatus Profuturus Frigiretus, cui iam supra meminimus, cum Romam refert a Gothis captam atque subversam, ait: Interea Respendial rex Alanorum. Goare ad Romanos transgresso, de Rheno agmen suorum convertit, Wandalis Francorum bello laborantibus, Godigyselo rege absumpto, aciae viginti ferme milibus ferro peremptis, cunctis Wandalorum ad internitionem delendis, nisi Alanorum vis in tempore subvenisset. Movet nos haec causa, quod cum aliorum gentium regis nominat, cur non nominet et Francorum. Tamen cum ait, quod Constantinus, adsumpta tyrannide, Constantium filium ad se de Hispaniis venire iussisset, ita disseruit: Accito Constantinus tyrannus de Hispaniis Constante filio idemque tyranno, quo de summa rerum consultarent praesentes; quo? factum est, ut Constans, instrumento aulae et coniuge sua Caesaraugustae dimissis, Gerontio intra Hispanias omnibus creditis, ad patrem continuato itinere decurreret. Qui ubi in unum venere, interiectis diebus plurimis, nullo ex Italia metu, Constantinus gulae et ventre deditus redire ad Hispanias filium monet. Qui, praemissis agminibus, dum cum patre resederet, ab Hispania nuntii commeant, a Gerontio Maximum unum e clientibus suis imperio praeditum atque in se cometatu gentium barbararum accinctum parari. Quo exterreti, Edobecco ad Germanias gentes praemisso, Constans et praefectus iam Decimius Rusticus ex officiorum magistro petunt Gallias, cum Francis et Alamannis omnique militum manu ad Constantinum iam iamque redituri. Item cum Constantinum obsedire scribit, ita dicit: Vixdum quartus obsidionis Constantini mensis agebatur, cum repente ex ulteriore Gallia nuntii veniunt, Iovinum adsumpsisse ornatus regius et cum Burgundionibus, Alamannis, Francis, Alanis omnique exercitu inminire obsedentibus. Ita adceleratis moris, reserata urbe, Constantinus deditur, confestimque ad Italiam directus, missis a principe obviam percussoribus, supra Mintium flumine capite truncatus est. Et post pauca idem refert: Hisdem diebus praefectus tyrannorum Decimius Rusticus, Agroetius ex primicirio notariorum Iovini multique nobiles apud Arvernus capti a ducibus Honorianis et crudeliter interempti sunt. Treverorum civitas a Francis direpta incensaque est secunda inruptione. Cum autem Asterius codicillis imperialibus patriciatum sortitus fuisset, haec adiungit: Eodem tempore Castinus domesticorum comes, expetitionem in Francos suscepta, ad Gallias mittitur. Haec hi de Francis dixire. Horosius autem et ipse historiograffus in septimo operis sui libro ita commemorat: Stilico, congregatis gentibus, Francos proteret, Rhenum transit, Gallias pervagatur et ad Pyrenius usque perlabitur. Hanc nobis notitiam de Francis memorati historici reliquere, regibus non nominatis. Tradunt enim multi, eosdem de Pannonia fuisse degressus, etv primum quidem litora Rheni amnes incoluisse, dehinc, transacto Rheno, Thoringiam transmeasse, ibique iuxta pagus vel civitates regis crinitos super se creavisse de prima et, ut ita dicam, nobiliore suorum familia. Quod postea probatum Chlodovechi victuriae tradedirunt, itaque in sequenti digerimus. Nam et in Consolaribus legimus, Theudomerem regem Francorum, filium Richimeris quondam, et Ascylam, matrem eius, gladio interfectus. Ferunt etiam, tunc Chlogionem utilem ac nobilissimum in gente sua regem fuisse Francorum, qui apud Dispargum castrum habitabat, quod est in terminum Thoringorum. In his autem partibus, id est ad meridianam plagam, habitabant Romani usque Ligerem fluvium. Ultra Ligerem vero Gothi dominabantur. Burgundiones quoque, Arrianorum sectam sequentes, habitabant trans Rhodanum, quod adiacit civitate Lugdunense. Chlogio autem, missis exploratoribus ad urbem Camaracum, perlustrata omnia, ipse secutus, Romanus proteret, civitatem adpraehendit, in qua paucum tempus resedens, usque Sumenam fluvium occupavit. De huius stirpe quidam Merovechum regem fuisse adserunt, cuius fuit filius Childericus.

## 10. Quid de simulacris gentium prophetae Domini scribant.

<a id="hist-02-la-011"></a>
[**[011]**](#hist-02-en-011) Sed haec generatio fanaticis semper cultibus visa est obsequium praebuisse, nec prursus agnovere Deum, sibique silvarum atque aquarum, avium bestiarumque et aliorum quoque elementorum finxere formas, ipsasque ut Deum colere eisque sacrifitium delibare consueti. O! si eorum fibras cordium vox illa terribilis attigisset, qui per Moyse populo locuta est, dicens: Non sint tibi dii alii praeter me. Non facies tibi sculptile neque adorabis omnem similitudinem quae in caelo sunt et quae in terra et quae versantur in aquis; non facis et non coles ea. Et illud: Dominum Deum tuum adorabis et ille soli servies ac per nomen eius iurabis. Quid si intellegere potuissent, quae pro vituli conflatilis veneratione Israheliticum populum ultio subsecuta conpraesseret, cum post epolum et cantica, post luxorias atque saltationes cum ore inmundo proferrent de eodem sculptile: Hii sunt du tui, Israhel, qui te eduxerunt de terra Aegypti? Ceciderunt ex eis viginti quattuor milia hominum. Quid de his qui initiati Belphegor cum Moabitibus scorto commixti, a proximis caesi, prostrati sunt? In qua plaga Finees sacerdus, interemptis adulteris, sedabit furorem Dei, et repotatum est illi ad iustitiam. Quid, si et illa eorum aurebus inlata fuisset, quod Dominus per David intonat, dicens: Quia omnes du gentium daemonia, Dominus autem caelos fecit? Et: Simulacra gentium argentum et aurum, opera manuum hominum. Similis illis fiant qui faciunt ea, et omnes qui confidunt in eis. Vel illud: Confundantur omnes qui adorant sculptile, qui gloriantur in simulacris suis. Et iterum quod Abbacuc profeta testatur, dicens: Quid prodest sculptile quod sculpserunt illud? Finxerunt illud cumflatile, fantasma mendum. Est hoc autem productio argenti et auri, et omnes spiritus non est in eis. Dominus autem in templo sancto suo: timeat a faciae eius universa terra. Sed et alius propheta dicit: Dii, qui caelum et terram non fecerunt, pereant a terra et de his qui sub caelis sunt. Item illic: Haec dicit Dominus, creans caelus, ipse Deus formans terram et quae in ea sunt; ipse plastis eius, non in vanum fundavit eam, ut habitaretur creavit eam. Ego Dominus, hoc mihi nomen est, gloriam meam alteri non dabo neque virtutem meam sculptilibus, qui ab initio transierunt. Et alibi: Numquid sunt in sculptilibus gentium qui pluant? Et per Isaiam iterum dicit: Ego primus et novissimus, et absque me non est deus et formatur, quem ego non noverim. Plastae idoli omnes nihil sunt, et amantissima eorum non proderunt eis. Ipse sunt testis eorum, quia non vident neque intellegent, et confundentur in eis. Ecce! omnes participes eius confundentur, fabri enim sunt ex hominibus. In prunis et malleis furmavit illud, et operatus est in brachio fortitudinis suae. Similiter et artifex lignarius in circino turnavit illud et fecit imaginem viri quasi spetiosi hominis habitantis in domo. Succidit lignum, operatus est, fecit sculptile et adoravit ut deum, clavis et malleis conpaginavit, ut non dissolvatur. Portata tollentur, quia incidere non valent; reliquum vero ligni factus est hominibus in focum, et calefacti sunt. Ex aliud vero deum fecit et sculptile sibi. Curvatur ante illud et adoravit illud et obsecrat, dicens: 'Libera me, quia deus meus es tu. Medietatem eius igni conbussi et coxi super carbones eius panes; coxi carnes et comedi, et de reliquum eius idolum faciam. Ante truncum ligni adorabo; pars eius cinis est'. Cor insipiens adoravit illud et non liberavit animam suam. Neque dicit: Forte mendatium est in dextera mea. Haec autem generatio Francorum non intellexit primum; inteliexerunt autem postea, sicut sequens historia narrat.

## 11. De Avito imperatore.

<a id="hist-02-la-012"></a>
[**[012]**](#hist-02-en-012) Avitus enim unus ex senatoribus et – valde manefestum est – civis Arvernus, cum Romanum ambisset imperium, luxoriosae agere volens, a senatoribus proiectus, apud Placentiam urbem episcopus ordenatur. Conperto autem, quod adhuc indignans senatus vita eum privari vellit, basilica sancti Iuliani Arverni martyres cum multis muneribus expetivit. Sed impleto in itenere vitae cursu, obiit, delatusque ad Brivatinsem vicum, ad pedes antedicti martyres est sepultus. Cui Martianus successit. In Galliis autem Egidius ex Romanus magister militum datus est.

## 12. De Childerico rege et Egidio.

<a id="hist-02-la-013"></a>
[**[013]**](#hist-02-en-013) Childericus vero, cum esset nimia in luxoria dissolutus et regnaret super Francorum gentem, coepit filias eorum stuprose detrahere. Illique ob hoc indignantes, de regnum eum eieciunt. Conperto autem, quod eum etiam interficere vellent, Thoringiam petiit, relinquens ibi hominem sibi carum, qui virorum furentium animus verbis linibus mollire possit, dans etiam signum, quando redire possit in patriam; id est diviserunt simul unum aureum, et unam quidem partem secum detulit Childericus, aliam vero amicus eius retenuit, dicens: 'Quando quidem hanc partem tibi misero, partesque coniunctae unum efficerent solidum, tunc tu securo animo in patriam repedabis'. Abiens ergo in Thoringiam, apud regem Bysinum uxoremque eius Basinam latuit. Denique Franci, hunc eiectum, Egidium sibi, quem superius magistrum militum a re publica missum diximus, unanimiter regem adsciscunt. Qui cum octavo anno super eos regnaret, amicus ille fidelis, pacatis occultae Francis, nuntius ad Childerico cum parte illa divisi solidi quam retenuerat mittit. Ille vero certa cognoscens inditia, quod a Francis desideraretur, ipsis etiam rogantibus, a Thoringia regressus, in regno suo est restitutus. His ergo regnantibus, simul Basina illa, quam supra memoravimus, relicto viro suo, ad Childericum venit. Qui cum sollicite interrogaret, qua de causa ad eum de tanta regione venisset, respondisse fertur: 'Novi', inquid, 'utilitatem tuam, quod sis valde strinuus, ideoque veni, ut habitem tecum. Nam noveris, si in transmarinis partibus aliquem cognovissem utiliorem tibi, expetissem utique cohabitationem eius'. At ille gaudens eam sibi in coniugio copulavit. Quae concipiens, peperit filium vocavitque nomen eius Chlodovechum. Hic fuit magnus et pugnatur egregius.

## 13. De episcopatu Venerandi ac Rustici Arvernis.

<a id="hist-02-la-014"></a>
[**[014]**](#hist-02-en-014) Apud Arvernus vero post transitum sancti Artemi Venerandus ex senatoribus episcopus ordenatur. Qualis autem fuerit hic pontifex, testatur Paulinus dicens: Si enim hodie videas dignos Domino sacerdotes, vel Exsuperium Tholosae, vel Simplicium Viennae, vel Amando Burdigale, vel Diogenianum Albigae, vel Dinamium Ecolisnae, vel Venerandum Arvernus, vel Alithium Cadurcis, vel nunc Pegasium Petrocoris, utcumque se habent saeculi mala, videbis profectu dignissimus totius fidei relegionesque custodes. Hic in ipsa dominici natalis vigilia transisse refertur. Mane autem facto processio solemnitates funeris eius obsequium fuit. Post cuius obitum foeda apud civis pro episcopatu intentio vertebatur. Cumque partes inter se divisae alium aliumque eregere vellent, magna conlisio erat populi. Resedentibus tamen episcopis die dominico, mulier quaedam velata atque devota Deo audenter ad eos ingreditur, qui ait: 'Audite me, sacerdotes Domini! Scitote enim, quod non est in his bene placitum Deo, quos hi ad sacerdotium elegerunt. Ecce enim Dominus hodie ipsi sibi providebit antestitem. Itaque nolite conturbare neque conlidere populum, sed pacientes estote parumper! Dominus enim nunc diriget, qui regat ecclesiam hanc'. His itaque mirantibus haec verba, subito Rusticus nomine, qui erat ex ipsa urbis Arvernae diocese presbiter, advenit. Ipsi enim iam mulieri per visionem fuerat indicatus. Quo viso, ait: 'En ipsum quem elegit Dominus! Ecce qualem vobis pontificem distinavit! Hic ordenetur episcopus!' Haec ea loquente, omnis populus, cuncta intentione postposita, clamavit, dignum ac iustum esse. Qui in cathedra positus, septimus in eodem loco pontificatus honorem, populo gaudente, suscepit.

## 14. De episcopatu Eustochi Turonici.

<a id="hist-02-la-015"></a>
[**[015]**](#hist-02-en-015) Apud urbem vero Turonicam, defuncto Eustochio episcopo septimo decimo sacerdotii sui anno, quintus post beatum Martinum Perpetuus ordinatur. Qui cum virtutes assiduas ad sepulchrum eius fieri cerneret, cellulam, quae super eum fabricata fuerat, videns parvulam, indignam talibus miraculis iudicavit. Qua submota, magnam ibi basilicam, quae usque hodie permanet, fabricavit, quae habetur a civitate passus 550. Habet in longo pedes 160, in lato 60, habet in alto usque ad cameram pedes 45; fenestras in altario 32, in capso 20; columnas 41; in toto aedificio fenestras 52, columnas 120; ostia 8, tria in altario, quinque in capso. Sollemnitas enim ipsius basilicae triplici virtute pollet: id est dedicatione templi, translatione corporis sancti vel ordinatione eius episcopati. Hanc enim quarto Nonas Iulias observabis; depositionem vero eius tertio Idus Novembris esse cognoscas. Quod si fideliter celebraveris, et in praesenti saeculo et in futuro patrocinia beati antistitis promereberis. Et quoniam camera cellulae illius prioris eleganti opere fuerat fabricata, indignum duxit sacerdos, ut opera eius deperiret, sed in honore beatorum apostolorum Petri et Pauli aliam construxit basilicam, in qua cameram illam adfixit. Multas et alias basilicas aedificavit, quae usque hodie in Christi nomine constant.

## 15. De basilica sancti Simphoriani.

<a id="hist-02-la-016"></a>
[**[016]**](#hist-02-en-016) Eo tempore et basilica beati Simphoriani Agustidunensis martiris ab Eufronio presbitero aedificata est. Et ipse Eufronius huius deinceps urbis episcopatum sortitus est. Hic enim marmorem, qui super sanctum sepulchrum beati Martini habetur, cum grandi devotione transmisit.

## 16. De Namatio episcopo et ecclesia Arverna.

<a id="hist-02-la-017"></a>
[**[017]**](#hist-02-en-017) Sanctus vero Namatius post obitum Rustici episcopi apud Arvernus in diebus illis octavus erat episcopus. Hic ecclesiam, qui nunc constat et senior infra murus civitatis habetur, suo studio fabricavit, habentem in longo pedes 150, in lato pedes 60, id est infra capso, in alto usque cameram pedes 50, inante absidam rotundam habens, ab utroque latere ascellas eleganti constructas opere; totumque aedificium in modum crucis habetur expositum. Habet fenestras 42, columnas 70, ostia 8. Terror namque ibidem Dei et claritas magna conspicitur, et vere plerumque inibi odor suavissimus quasi aromatum advenire a religiosis sentitur. Parietes ad altarium opere sarsurio ex multa marmorum genera exornatos habet. Exactum ergo in duodecimo anno beatus pontifex aedificium, Bononiae civitatem Italiae sacerdotes dirigit, ut ei reliquias sanctorum Agricolae et Vitalis exhibeant, quos pro nomine Christi Dei nostri manifestissime crucifixos esse cognovimus.

## 17. De coniuge eius et basilica sancti Stephani.

<a id="hist-02-la-018"></a>
[**[018]**](#hist-02-en-018) Cuius coniux basilicam sancti Stephani suburbano murorum aedificavit. Quam cum fucis colorum adornare velit, tenebat librum in sinum suum, legens historias actionis antiquae, pictoribus indicans, quae in parietibus fingere deberent. Pactum est autem quadam die, ut, sedente ea in basilica ac legente, adveniret quidam pauper ad orationem, et aspiciens eam in veste nigra, senio iam provecta, putavit esse unam de egentibus protulitque quadram panis et posuit in sinu eius et abscessit. Illa vero non dedignans munus pauperis, qui personam eius non intellexit, accepit et gratias egit reposuitque, hanc suis epulis anteponens et benedictionem ex ea singulis diebus sumens, donec expensa est.

## 18. Quod Childericus Aurilianus et Andecavo venit Odovacrius.

<a id="hist-02-la-019"></a>
[**[019]**](#hist-02-en-019) Igitur Childericus Aurilianis pugnas egit, Adovacrius vero cum Saxonibus Andecavo venit. Magna tunc lues populum devastavit. Mortuus est autem Egidius et reliquit filium Syagrium nomine. Quo defuncto, Adovacrius de Andecavo vel aliis locis obsedes accepit. Brittani de Bituricas a Gothis expulsi sunt, multis apud Dolensim vicum peremptis. Paulos vero comes cum Romanis ac Francis Gothis bella intulit et praedas egit. Veniente vero Adovacrio Andecavus, Childericus rex sequenti die advenit, interemptoque Paulo comite, civitatem obtinuit. Magnum ea die incendio domus aeclesiae concremata est.

## 19. Bellum inter Saxones ac Romanus.

<a id="hist-02-la-020"></a>
[**[020]**](#hist-02-en-020) His ita gestis, inter Saxones atque Romanos bellum gestum est; sed Saxones terga vertentes, multos de suis, Romanis insequentibus, gladio reliquerunt; insolae eorum cum multo populo interempto a Francis captae atque subversi sunt. Eo anno minse nono terra tremuit. Odovacrius cum Childerico foedus iniit, Alamannusque, qui partem Italiae pervaserant, subiugarunt.

## 20. De Victorio duce.

<a id="hist-02-la-021"></a>
[**[021]**](#hist-02-en-021) Eoricus autem Gothorum rex Victorium ducem super septem civitatis praeposuit anno XIIII. regni sui. Qui protinus Arvernus adveniens, civitatem addere voluit, unde et criptae illae usque hodie perstant. Ad basilicam sancti Iuliani colomnas, quae sunt in aede positae, exhibere iussit. Basilicam sancti Laurenti et sancti Germani Licaniacensis vici iussit aedificare. Fuit autem Arvernus annis novem. Super Euchirium vero senatorem calumnias devolvit; quem in carcere positum nocte extrahi iussit, ligatumque iuxta parietem antiquum, ipsum parietem super eum elidi iussit. Ipse vero dum nimium esset in amore mulierum luxoriosus et ab Arvernus veriritur interfeci, Romam aufugit, ibique similem timptans exercere luxoriam, lapidibus est obrutus. Post cuius excessum regnavit Euricus annus IIII; obiit autem anno vicissimo septimo regni sui. Fuit etiam et tunc terrae motus magnus.

## 21. De Eparchio episcopo.

<a id="hist-02-la-022"></a>
[**[022]**](#hist-02-en-022) Defuncto autem apud Arvernus Namatio episcopo, Eparchius successit, vir sanctissimus atque religiosus. Et quia eo tempore ecclesia parvam infra muros urbis possessionem habebat, ipsi sacerdoti in ipso, quod modo salutatorium dicitur, mansio erat, atque ad gratias Deo tempore nocturno reddendas ad altarium ecclesiae consurgebat. Factum est autem, ut nocte quadam Ingrediens, plenam ecclesiam a demonibus repperiret ipsumque principem in modum ornatae mulieris in throni illius cathedra resedentem. Cui ait pontifex: 'O meretrix execranda, non sufficit tibi loca cuncta variis pollutionibus infecire, adhuc et cathedram a Domino consecratam fetida sessionis tuae accessione coinquinas? Abscede a domo Dei, ne a te amplius polluatur'. Cui ait: 'Et quia mihi meretricis nomen inponis, multas tibi parabo insidias ob desideria mulierum'. Et haec dicens, sicut fumus evanuit. Verumtamen sacerdos temptatus est per commotionem corporis a concupiscentia; sed signo crucis sanctae munitus, nihil ei inimicus nocere potuit. Ferunt etiam, ipsum in arce Cantobennici montis monasterium collocasse, ubi nunc oratorium est, et ibi se diebus quadragesimae sanctae recludere; die autem caenae dominicae cum magno psallentio, comitantibus clericis civibusque, ad ecclesiam suam revertebatur. Quo migrante, Sidonius ex praefecto substituetur, vir secundum saeculi dignitatem nobilissimus et de primis Galliarum senatoribus, ita ut filiam sibi Aviti imperatoris in matrimonio sociaret. Huius tempore, cum adhuc apud urbem Arvernam Victorius, cui supra meminimus, moraretur, fuit in monasterio beati Quirici urbis ipsius abba Abraham nomine, qui fide atque operibus Abrahae illius prioris gratia refulgebat, sicut in libro vitae eius scripsimus.

## 22. De Sidonio episcopo.

<a id="hist-02-la-023"></a>
[**[023]**](#hist-02-en-023) Sanctus vero Sidonius tantae facundiae erat, ut plerumque ex inproviso luculentissime quae voluisset, nulla obsistente mora, conponeret. Contigit autem quadam die, ut ad festivitatem basilicae monasterii, cui supra meminimus, invitatus accederet, ablatoque sibi nequiter libello, per quem sacrosancta sollemnia agere consueverat, ita paratus a tempore cunctum festivitatis opus explicuit, ut ab omnibus miraretur nec putaretur ab adstantibus, ibidem hominem locutum fuisse, sed angelum. Quod in praefatione libri, quem de missis ab eo conpositis coniunximus, plenius declaravimus. Cum autem esset magnificae sanctitatis atque, ut diximus, ex senatoribus primis, plerumque nesciente coniuge vasa argentea auferebat a domo et pauperibus erogabat. Quod illa cum cognosceret, scandalizabatur in eum, sed tamen, dato egenis pretio, species domi restituebat.

## 23. De sanctitate Sidonii episcopi, et de iniuriis eius ultione divina moderatis.

<a id="hist-02-la-024"></a>
[**[024]**](#hist-02-en-024) Cumque ad officium dominicum fuisset mancipatus et sanctam ageret in saeculo vitam, surrexerunt contra eum duo presbiteri, et ablatam ei omnem potestatem a rebus ecclesiae, artum ei victum et tenuem relinquentes, ad summam eum contumeliam redigerunt. Sed non longi temporis spatio inultam eius iniuriam divina voluit sustinere clementia. Nam unus ex his nequissimis et indignum dici presbiteris, cum ante nocte minatus fuisset eum de ecclesia velle extrahere, signum ad matutinis audiens fuisset commotum, fervens felle contra sanctum Dei surrexit, hoc iniquo corde explere cogitans, quod die praecedente tractaverat. Ingressus autem in secessum suum, dum ventrem purgare nititur, spiritum exalavit. Expectat enim eum puer a foris cum cereo dominum egressurum. Iamque advenerat lux, et satelles eius, id est alius presbiter, mittit nuntium, dicens: 'Veni, ne tarderis, ut quae nobis die hesterna convenit pariter expleamus'. Sed cum responsa dare differret exanimis, elevato puer velo ostii repperit dominum super sellula secessi defunctum. Unde indubitatum est, non minoris criminis hunc reum esse quam Arrium illum, cui similiter in secessum fuerunt interna deposita per partis inferioris egestum, quia nec istud sine heresi potest accipi, ut in ecclesiam non obaudiatur sacerdos Dei, cui ad pascendum oves commissae sunt, et ille se ingerat potestati, cui neque a Deo neque ab hominibus aliquid est commissum. Dehinc beatus sacerdos, uno adhuc manente nihilominus inimico, suae restituetur potestati. Factum est autem post haec, ut accedente febre aegrotare coepisset. Qui rogat suos, ut eum in ecclesiam ferrent. Cumque ibidem inlatus fuisset, conveniebant ad eum multitudo virorum ac mulierum simulque etiam et infantium plangentium atque dicentium: 'Cur nos deseres, pastor bone, vel cui nos quasi orphanos derelinquis? Numquid erit nobis post transitum tuum vita? Numquid erit postmodum, qui nos sapientiae sale sic condiat aut ad dominici nominis timorem talis prudentiae ratione redarguat?' Haec et his similia populis cum magno fletu dicentibus, tandem sacerdos, Spiritu in se sancto influente, respondit: 'Nolite timere, o populi, ecce! frater meus Aprunculus vivit, et ipse erit sacerdos vester'. Qui non intellegentes, putabant, eum loqui aliquid in extasi. Quo migrante, presbiter ille nequam, alter ex duobus qui remanserat, protinus omnem facultatem ecclesiae, tamquam si iam episcopus esset, inhians cupiditate, praeoccupat, dicens: 'Tandem respexit in me Deus, cognoscens, me iustiorem esse Sidonio, largitusque est mini hanc potestatem'. Cumque per totam urbem superbus feritur, adveniente die dominico, qui inminebat post transitum sancti viri, praeparato epulo, iussit cunctos cives in domo ecclesiae invitari, dispectisque senioribus, primus recumbit in toro. Cui oblatum pincerna poculum ait: 'Domine mi, vidi somnium, quem, si permittis, edicam: Videbam hac nocte dominica, et ecce! erat domus magna, et in domo erat thronus positus, in quo quasi iudex resedebat cunctis potestate praestantior, cui adsistebant multi sacerdotum in albis vestibus, sed et promiscuae populorum turbae valde innumerabiles. Verum cum haec trepidus contemplarem, conspicio eminus inter eos beatum adstare Sidonium, et cum presbitero illo tibi carissimo, qui ante hos paucos annos de hoc mundo migravit, adtente litigantem. Quo devicto, iubet rex, ut in imis carceris angustiis retrudatur; ablatoque isto, contra te iterum suggeret, dicens, te in eo scelere, propter quod prior ille damnatus fuerat, fuisse participem. Verum ubi iudex, quem ad te transmitteret, sollicite coepit inquirere, ego me inter reliquos occolere coepi et a tergo statui, tractans apud memet ipsum, ne forte ego, qui notus sum homini, mittar. Dum haec mecum tacitus volverem, amotis omnibus, remansi solus in publico vocatusque a iudice propius accedo. Cuius virtutem atque splendorem contuens, coepi hebes effectus titubare prae metu. Et ille: „Ne timeas, puer", inquid, „sed vade, die presbitero illi: Veni ad respondendam causam, quia Sidonius te arcersiri deprecatus est. Tu vero ne moram facias ad eundum, quia sub grandi testificatione mihi praecepit rex ille haec loqui, dicens: Si tacueris, morte pessima morieris"'. Haec eo loquente, exterritus presbiter, elapsum de manu calicem, reddidit spiritum; ac de recubitu ablatus mortuus sepulturae mandatus est, possessurus infernum cum satellite suo. Tale iudicium super contumaces clericos Dominus in hunc praetulit mundum, ut unus Arrii sortiretur mortem, alius tamquam Simon Magus apostoli sancti oratione ab excelsa arce superbiae praeceps allideretur. Qui non ambiguntur pariter possidere tartarum, qui simul egerunt nequiter contra sanctum episcopum suum. Interea cum iam terror Francorum resonaret in his partibus et omnes eos amore desiderabili cupirent regnare, sanctus Abrunculus Lingonicae civitatis episcopus apud Burgondiones coepit haberi suspectus. Cumque odium de die in diem cresceret, iussum est, ut dam gladio feriretur. Quo ad eum perlato nuntio, nocte a castro Divionensi per murum dimissus Arvernus advenit, ibique iuxta verbum Domini, quod posuit in ore sancti Sidonii, undecimus datur episcopus.

## 24. De fame Burgundiae et Ecdicio.

<a id="hist-02-la-025"></a>
[**[025]**](#hist-02-en-025) Sed tempore Sidoni episcopi magna Burgundiam famis oppressit. Cumque populi per diversas regiones dispergerentur, nec esset ullus qui pauperibus alemoniam largeretur, Ecdicius quidam ex senatoribus, huius propinquos, magnam tunc rem in Deo confisus fecisse perhibitur. Nam invaliscente fame, misit pueros suos cum equitibus et plaustris per vicinas sibi civitates, ut eos qui hac inopia vexabantur sibi adducerent. At illi euntes, cunctus pauperes, quodquod invenire potuerunt, adduxerunt ad domum eius, ibique eos per omnem tempus sterelitates pascens, ab interitu famis eximit. Fueruntque, ut multi aiunt, amplius quam quattuor milia promiscui sexus. Adveniente autem ubertate, ordinata iterum evectionem, unumquemque in loco suo restituit. Post quorum discessum vox ad eum e caelis lapsa pervenit, dicens: 'Ecdici, Ecdici, quia fecisti rem hanc, tibi et simine tuo panis non deerit in sempiternum, eo quod obaudieris verbis meis et famem meam refectione pauperum saciaberis'. Quem Ecdicium mirae velocitatis fuisse, multi commemorant. Nam quadam vice multitudinem Gothorum cum decim viris fugasse perscribitur. Sed et sanctos Patiens Lugdunensis episcopus simile huic in ipsa fame populis prestetisse perhibetur benefitium. Extat exinde hodieque apud nos beati Sidoni epistola, in qua eum declamaturiae conlaudavit.

## 25. De Euvarege persecutore.

<a id="hist-02-la-026"></a>
[**[026]**](#hist-02-en-026) Huius temporis et Euarix rex Gothorum, excidens Hispanum limitem, gravem in Galliis super christianis intulit persecutionem. Truncabat passim perversitate suae non consentientis, clericus carceribus subegebat, sacerdotis vero alius dabat exilio, alius gladio trucidabat. Nam et ipsus sacrorum templorum aditus spinis iusserat obserari, scilicet ut raritas ingrediendi oblivionem facerit fidei. Maxime tunc Novimpopulanae geminaeque Germaniae urbes ab hac tempestate depopulatae sunt. Extat hodieque et pro ac causa ad Basilium episcopum nobilis Sidoni ipsius epistola, quae haec ita loquitur. Sed persecutor non post multum tempus ultione divina percussus interiit.

## 26. De obitu sancti Perpetui et episcopatu Volosiani ac Viri.

<a id="hist-02-la-027"></a>
[**[027]**](#hist-02-en-027) Post haec beatus Perpetuus Turonicae civitatis episcopus, impletis triginta in episcopatu annis, in pace quievit. In cuius loco Volusianus, unus ex senatoribus, subrogatus est. Sed a Gothis suspectus habitus, episcopatus sui anno septimo in Hispaniis est quasi captivus adductus, sed protinus vitam finivit. In cuius loco Virus succedens, septimus post beatum Martinum ordinatur episcopus.

## 27. Quod Chlodovechus regnum accepit.

<a id="hist-02-la-028"></a>
[**[028]**](#hist-02-en-028) His ita gestis, mortuo Childerico, regnavit Chlodovechus, filius eius, pro eo. Anno autem quinto regni eius Siacrius Bomanorum rex, Egidi filius, apud civitatem Sexonas, quam quondam supra memoratus Egidius tenuerat, sedem habebat. Super quem Chlodovechus cum Ragnechario, parente suo, quia et ipse regnum tenebat, veniens, campum pugnae praeparare deposcit. Sed nec iste distolit ac resistere metuit. Itaque inter se utrisque pugnantibus, Syagrius elisum cernens exercitum, terga vertit et ad Alaricum regem Tholosa curso veluci perlabitur. Chlodovechus vero ad Alarico mittit, ut eum redderit; alioquin noverit, sibi bellum ob eius retentationem inferri. Ad ille metuens, ne propter eum iram Francorum incurrerit, ut Gothorum pavere mos est, vinctum legatis tradedit. Quem Chlodovechus receptum custodiae mancipare praecipit; regnoque eius acceptum, eum gladio clam feriri mandavit. Eo tempore multae aeclesiae a Chlodovecho exercitu depraedatae sunt, quia erat ille adhuc fanaticis erroribus involutus. Igitur de quadam eclesia urceum mirae magnitudinis ac pulchritudinis hostes abstulerant, cum reliqua eclesiastici ministeriiornamenta. Episcopus autem eclesiae illius missus ad regem dirigit, poscens, ut, si aliud de sacris vasis reciperenon meretur, saltim vel urceum aeclesia sua reciperit. Haec audiens rex, ait nuntio: 'Sequere nos usque Sexonas, quia ibi cuncta que adquisita sunt dividenda erunt. Cumque mihi vas illud sors dederit, quae papa poscit, adimpleam'. Dehinc adveniens Sexonas, cunctum onus praedae in medio positum, ait rex: 'Rogo vos, o fortissimi proeliatores, ut saltim mihi vas istud' – hoc enim de urceo supra memorato dicebat, – 'extra partem concidere non abnuatis'. Haec regi dicente, illi quorum erat mens sanior aiunt: 'Omnia, gloriose rex, quae cernimus, tua sunt, sed et nos ipsi tuo sumus dominio subiugati. Nunc quod tibi bene placitum viditur facito; nullus enim potestati tuae resistere valet' Cum haec ita dixissent, unus levis, invidus ac facilis, cum voce magna elevatam bipennem urceo inpulit, dicens: 'Nihil hinc accipies, nisi quae tibi sors vera largitur'. Ad haec obstupefactis omnibus, rex iniuriam suam patientiae lenitate coercuit, acceptumque urceum nuntio eclesiastico reddidit, servans abditum sub pectore vulnus. Transacto vero anno iussit omnem cum armorum apparatu advenire falangam, ostensuram in campo Marcio horum armorum nitorem. Verum ubi cunctus circuire diliberat, venit ad urcei percussorem; cui ait: 'Nullus tam inculta ut tu detulit arma; nam neque tibi hasta neque gladius neque securis est utilis'. Et adpraehensam securem eius terrae deiecit. At ille cum paulolum inclinatus fuisset ad collegendum, rex, elevatis manibus, securem suam capite eius defixit. 'Sic', inquid, 'tu Sexonas in urceo illo fecisti'. Quo mortuo, reliquos abscedere iubet, magnum sibi per hanc causam timorem statuens. Multa bella victuriasque fecit. Nam decimo regni sui anno Thoringis bellum intulit eosdemque suis diccionibus subiugavit.

## 28. Quod Chlodovechus Chrodigilde accepit.

<a id="hist-02-la-029"></a>
[**[029]**](#hist-02-en-029) Fuit igitur et Gundevechus rex Burgundionum ex genere Athanarici regis persecutoris, cui supra meminimus. Huic fuerunt quattuor filii: Gundobadus, Godigisilus, Chilpericus et Godomarus. Igitur Gundobadus Chilpericum fratrem suum interfecit gladio uxoremque eius, ligatu ad collum lapidem, aquis inmersit. Huius duas filias exilio condemnavit; quarum senior mutata veste Crona, iunior Chrotchildis vocabatur. Porro Chlodovechus, dum legationem in Burgundiam saepius mittit, Chrotchildis puella repperitur a legatis eius. Qui cum ea vidissent elegantem atque sapientem et cognovissent, quod de regio esset genere, nuntiaverunt haec Chlodovecho regi. Nec moratus ille ad Gundobadum legationem dirigit, eam sibi in matrimonio petens. Quod ille recusare metuens, tradidit eam viris; illeque accipientes puellam, regi velotius repraesentant. Qua visa, rex valde gavisus, suo eam coniugio sociavit, habens iam de concubina filium nomine Theudericum.

## 29. De primo eorum filio baptizato in albis defuncto.

<a id="hist-02-la-030"></a>
[**[030]**](#hist-02-en-030) Igitur ex Chrotchilde regina habuit filium primogenitum. Quem cum mulier baptismo consecrare vellit, praedicabat assiduae viro, dicens: 'Nihil sunt dii quos colitis, qui neque sibi neque aliis potuerunt subvenire. Sunt enim aut ex lapide aut ex ligno aut ex metallo aliquo sculpti. Nomina vero quae eis indedistis homines fuere, non dii, ut Saturnus, qui a filio ne a regno depelleretur, per fugam elapsus adseritur, ut ipse Iovis omnium stuprorum spurcissimus perpetratur, incestatur virorum, propinquarum derisor, qui nec ab ipsius sororis propriae potuit abstenere concubitum, ut ipsa ait: Iovisque et soror et coniux. Quid Mars Mercuriusque potuere? Qui potius sunt magicis artibus praediti, quam divini nominis potentiam habuere. Sed ille magis coli debit, qui caelum et terram, mare et omnia quae in eis sunt verbo ex non extantibus procreavit, qui solem lucere fecit et caelum stillis ornavit, qui aquas reptilibus, terras animantibus, aera volatilibus adimplivit, cuius nutu terrae frugibus, pomis arbores, uvis vineae decorantur, cuius manu genus humanum creatum est, cuius largitione ipsa illa creatura omnes homini suo, quem creavit, et obsequio et benefitio famulatur'. Sed cum haec regina dicerit, nullatinus ad credendum regis animus movebatur, sed dicebat: 'Deorum nostrorum iussione cuncta creantur ac prudeunt, Deus vero vester nihil posse manefestatur, et quod magis est, nec de deorum genere esse probatur'. Interea regina fidelis filium ad baptismum exhibet, adornare eclesiam velis praecipit atque curtinis, quo facilius vel hoc misterio provocaretur ad credendum, qui flecti praedicationem non poterat. Baptizatus autem puer, quem Ingomerem vocitaverunt, in ipsis, sicut regeneratus fuerat, albis obiit. Qua de causa commotus feile rex, non signiter increpabat regina, dicens: 'Si in nomine deorum meorum puer fuisset decatus, vixisset utique; nunc autem, quia in nomine Dei vestri baptizatus est, vivere omnino non potuit'. Ad haec regina: 'Deo', inquid, 'omnipotenti, creatori omnium, gratias ago, qui me non usquequaque iudicavit indigna, ut de utero meo genitum regno suo dignaretur adscire. Mihi autem dolore huius causae animus non attingitur, quia scio, in albis ab hoc mundo vocatus Dei obtutibus nutriendus'. Post hunc vero genuit alium filium, quem baptizatum Chlodomere vocavit; et hic cum egrotare coepisset, dicebat rex: 'Non potest aliud, nisi et de hoc sicut et de fratre eius contingat, ut baptizatus in nomine Christi vestri protinus moriatur'. Sed orante matre, Domino iubente convaluit.

## 30. Bellum contra Alamannus.

<a id="hist-02-la-031"></a>
[**[031]**](#hist-02-en-031) Regina vero non cessabat praedicare, ut Deum verum cognusceret et idola neglegerit. Sed nullo modo ad haec credenda poterat commoveri, donec tandem aliquando bellum contra Alamannos conmoveretur, in quo conpulsus est confiteri necessitate, quod prius voluntate negaverat. Factum est autem, ut confligente utroque exercitu vehementer caederentur, atque exercitus Chlodovechi valde ad internitionem ruere coepit. Quod ille videns, elevatis ad caelum oculis, conpunctus corde, commotus in lacrimis, ait: 'Iesu Christi, quem Chrotchildis praedicat esse filium Dei vivi, qui dare auxilium laborantibus victuriamque in te sperantibus tribuere diceris, tuae opis gloriam devotus efflagito, ut, si mihi victuriam super hos hostes indulseris et expertus fuero illam virtutem, quam de te populus tuo nomine dicatus probasse se praedicat, credam tibi et in nomine tuo baptizer. Invocavi enim deos meos, sed, ut experior, elongati sunt ab auxilio meo; unde credo, eos nullius esse potestatis praeditos, qui sibi oboedientibus non occurrunt. Te nunc invoco, tibi credere desidero, tantum ut eruar ab adversariis meis', Cumque haec dicerit, Alamanni terga vertontes, in fugam labi coeperunt. Cumque regem suum cernirent interemptum, Chlodovechi se ditionibus subdunt, dicentes: 'Ne amplius, quaesumus, pereat populus, iam tui sumus'. Ad ille, prohibito bello, cohortato populo cum pace regressus, narravit reginae, qualiter per invocationem nominis Christi victuriam meruit obtenire. [Actum anno 15. regni sui.]

## 31. De baptismo Chlodovechi.

<a id="hist-02-la-032"></a>
[**[032]**](#hist-02-en-032) Tunc regina arcessire clam sanctum Remedium Remensis urbis episcopum iubet, depraecans, ut regi verbum salutis insinuaret. Quem sacerdos arcessitum secritius coepit ei insinuare, ut Deum verum, factorem caeli ac terrae, crederit, idola neglegerit, quae neque sibi neque aliis prodesse possunt. At ille ait: 'Libenter te, sanctissime pater, audiebam; sed restat unum, quod populus, qui me sequitur, non patitur relinquere deus suos; sed vado et loquor eis iuxta verbum tuum'. Conveniens autem cum suis, priusquam ille loqueretur, praecurrente potentia Dei, omnes populus pariter adclamavit: 'Mortalis deus abigimus, pie rex, et Deum quem Remegius praedicat inmortalem sequi parati sumus'. Nuntiantur haec antestiti, qui gaudio magno repletus, iussit lavacrum praeparari. Velis depictis adumbrantur plateae, eclesiae curtinis albentibus adurnantur, baptistirium conponitur, balsama difunduntur, micant flagrantes odorem cerei, totumque templum baptistirii divino respergetur ab odore, talemque ibi gratiam adstantibus Deus tribuit, ut aestimarent se paradisi odoribus collocari. Rex ergo prior poposcit, se a pontifeci baptizare. Procedit novos Constantinus ad lavacrum, deleturus leprae veteris morbum sordentesque maculas gestas antiquitus recenti latice deleturus. Cui ingresso ad baptismum sanctus Dei sic infit ore facundo: 'Mitis depone colla, Sigamber; adora quod incendisti, incende quod adorasti'. Erat autem sanctus Remegius episcopus egregiae scientiae et rethoricis adprimum inbutus studiis, sed et sanctitate ita praelatus, ut Silvestri virtutebus equaretur. Est enim nunc liber vitae eius, qui eum narrat mortuum suscitasse. Igitur rex omnipotentem Deum in Trinitate confessus, baptizatus in nomine Patris et Filii et Spiritus sancti delebutusque sacro crismate cum signaculo crucis Christi. De exercito vero eius baptizati sunt amplius tria milia. Baptizata est et soror eius Albofledis, quae non post multum tempus migravit ad Dominum. Pro qua cum rex contristaretur, sanctus Remegius consolaturiam misit epistolam, quae hoc modo sumpsit exordium: Anget me et satis me anget vestrae causa tristitiae, quod bonae memoriae germana vestra transiit Albofledis. Sed consolare possumus, quia talis de hoc mundo migravit, ut suspici magis debeat quam lugere. Conversa est enim et alia soror eius Lantechildis nomen, quae in haeresim Arrianorum dilapsa fuerat, quae confessa aequalem Filium Patri et Spiritum sanctum, crismata est.

## 32. Bellum contra Gundobadum.

<a id="hist-02-la-033"></a>
[**[033]**](#hist-02-en-033) Tunc Gundobadus et Godegisilus fratres regnum circa Rhodanum aut Ararem cum Massiliensem provintiam retinebant. Erant autem tam ille quam populi eorum Arrianae sectae subiecti. Cumque se invicem inpugnarent, auditas Godigisilus Chlodovechi regis victurias misit ad eum legationem occulte, dicens: 'Si mihi ad persequendum fratrem meum praebueris solatium, ut eum bello interficere aut de regione eiecere possim, tributum tibi, quale tu ipse vellis iniungere, annis singulis dissolvam'. Quod ille libenter accipiens, auxilium ei, ubicumque necessitas poposcerit, repromisit, et statuto tempore contra Gundobadum exercitum commovit. Quo audito, Gundobadus ignorans dolum fratris, misit ad eum, dicens: 'Veni in adiuturium meum, quia Franci se conmovent contra nos et regionem nostram adeunt, ut eam capiant. Ideoque simus unianimis adversus gentem inimicam nobis, ne separati invicem quod aliae gentes passi sunt perferamus'. Ad ille: 'Vadam', inquid, 'cum exercitu meo et tibi auxilium praebeam'. Moventesque simul hii tres exercitum, id est Chlodovechus contra Gundobadum et Godigiselo, cum omni instrumento belli ad castrum cui Divione nomen est pervenerunt. Confligentesque super Oscaram fluvium, Godigiselus Chlodovecho coniungetur, ac uterque exercitus Gundobadi populum adteret. At ille dolum fratres, quem non suspecabatur, advertens, terga dedit fugamque iniit, Rhodanitidesqueripaspercurrens, Avinionem urbem ingreditur. Godigisilus vero, obtenta victuria, promissam Chlodovecho aliquam partem regni sui, cum pace discessit Viennamque triumphans, tamquam si iam totum possederit regnum, ingreditur. Auctis adhuc Chlodovechus rex viribus post Gundobadum abiit, ut eum de civitate extractum interemeret. Quod ille audiens, pavore perterritus, metuebat, ne ei mors repentina succederet. Habebat tamen secum virum inlustrem Aredium, strinuum atque sapientem, quem ad se arcessitum, ait: 'Vallant me undique angustiae, et quid faciam ignoro, quia venerunt hi barbari super nos, ut, nobis interemptis, regionem totam evertant'. Ad haec Aredius ait: 'Oportit te lenire feritatem hominis huius, ne pereas. Nunc ergo, si placit in oculis tuis, ego a tefugire et ad eum transire consimulo, cumque ad eum accessero, ego faciam, ut neque te neque hanc noceant regionem. Tantum ut quod tibi per meum consilium demandaverit implere studias, donec causam tuam Dominus prosperam facere sua pietate dignetur'. Et ille: 'Faciam', inquid, 'quaecumque mandaveris'. Haec eo dicente, vale dicens Aredius discessit, et ad Chlodovechum regem abiens, ait: 'Ecce! ego humilis tuos, piissime rex, ad tuam potentiam venio, relinquens illum miserrimum Gundobadum. Quod si me pietas tua recipere dignatur, integrum in me famulum atque fidelem et tu et posteri tui habebitis'. Quem ille prumptissime colligens secum retinuit. Erat enim iocundus in fabulis, strinuus in consiliis, iustus in iuditiis et in conmisso fidelis. Denique Chlodovecho cum omni exercito circa murus urbis resedente, ait Aredius: 'Si dignanter, o rex, gloria celsitudinis tuae paucos humilitatis meae sermones vellit accepere, consilium licet non egeatis, tamen fide integra ministrabam; idemque vel tibi congruum vel civitatibus erat, per quas transire diliberas. Cur', inquid, 'retines exercitum, cum loco firmissimo tuus resedeat inimicus? Depopularis agros, prata depascis, vineas dissecas, olivita succidis omnesque regiones fructus evertis; interim et ille nocere nihil praevalis. Mitte potius legationem et tributum, quod tibi annis singulis dissolvat, inpone, ut et regio salva sit et tu tributa dissolventi perpetuo domineris. Quod si noluerit, tunc quod libuerit facies'. Quo consilio rex accepto hostem patriae redire iubet ad propria. Tunc missa legationem ad Gundobadum, ut ei per singulos annos tributa inposita reddere debeat, iubet. Ad ille et de praesenti solvit et deinceps solviturum se esse promittit.

## 33. De interitu Godigisili.

<a id="hist-02-la-034"></a>
[**[034]**](#hist-02-en-034) Post haec resumptis viribus, iam dispiciens rege Chlodovecho tributa promissa dissolvere, contra Godigisilo fratrem suum exercitum conmovet eumque apud Viennam civitatem inclusum obsidit. Verum ubi minori populo alimenta dificere coeperunt, timens Godigiselus, ne ad se usque fames extenderetur, iussit expelli minoris populi ab urbe. Quo facto, expulsus est inter citeros artifex ille, cui de aquaeducto cura manebat. Ille vero indignans, cur fuerit eiectus ab urbem cum ceteris, ad Gundobadum furibundus vadit, indicans, qualiter civitatem inrumpens ultionem exerceret in fratre. Illo quoque duci exercitum per aquaeducto directum, multis cum ferreis vectibus praecidentibus, erat autem spiraculum illius lapide magno conclusum; quo cum vectibus illis per magisterium artefecis repulso, civitatem introeunt, illisque de muro sagittantibus, hi terga praeveniunt. Datum autem de medio civitatis sonum bucinae, obsedentes portas capiunt apertisque pariter ingrediuntur. Cumque inter duas has acies populus urbis ab utroque exercitu caederetur, Godegiselus ad eclesiam hereticorum confugit ibique cum episcopo Arriano interfectus est. Denique Franci, qui apud Godigiselo erant, in unam se turrem congregant. Gundobadus autem iussit, ne unus quidem ex ipsis aliquid noceretur; sed adpraehensus eos Tholosae in exilium ad Alaricum regem transmisit, interfectis senatoribus Burgundionibusque, qui Godigiselo consenserant. Ipse vero regionem omnem, quod nunc Burgundia dicitur, in suo dominio restauravit. Burgundionibus leges mitiores instituit, ne Romanos obpraemerent.

## 34. Quod Gundobadus converti voluit.

<a id="hist-02-la-035"></a>
[**[035]**](#hist-02-en-035) Cum autem cognovisset, assertiones hereticorum nihil esse, a sancto Avito episcopo Viennense, Christum, filium Dei, et Spiritum sanctum aequalis Patri confessus, clam ut crismaretur expetiit. Cui ait sacerdos: 'Si vere credis hoc, quod nos ipse Dominus edocuit, debes exsequere. Ait autem: Si quis me confessus fuerit coram hominibus, confitebor et ego eum coram Patre meo, qui est in caelis; qui autem negaverit me coram hominibus, negabo et ego eum coram Patre meo, qui est in caelis. Sic et ipsis sanctis ac dilictis suis beatis apostolis, cum de futurae persecutiones temptationibus doceret, insinuavit, dicens: Adtendete vobis ab hominibus. Tradent enim vos in conciliis, et in sinagogis suis flagellabunt vos, et ante regis et praesides stabitis propter me in testimonium illis et omnibus gentibus. Tu vero cum sis rex et ad nullo adpraehendi formidas, seditionem paviscis populi, ne Creatorem omnium in publico fateares. Relinque hanc stultitiam, et quod corde te dicis credere, ore profer in plebe. Sic enim et beatus apostolus ait: Corde creditur ad iustitiam, ore autem confessio fit ad salutem. Sic et propheta ait: Confitebor tibi, Domine, in eclesia magna, in populo gram laudabo te. Et iterum: Confitebor tibi in populis, Domine, psalmum dicam nomini tuo inter gentes. Metuens enim populum, o rex, ignorans, quia satius est, ut populus sequatur fidem tuam, quam tu infirmitate faveas populari. Tu enim es capud populi, non populus capud tuum. Si enim ad bellum proficiscaris, tu praecedis catervas hostium, et ille quo abieris subsequuntur. Unde melius est, ut te praecedente cognoscant veritatem, quam pereunte permaneant in errorem. Nam Deus non inriditur, nec enim diligit illum qui propter terrenum regnum eum non confitetur in saeculum'. Ista ille ratione confusus, usque ad exitum vitae suae in hac insania perduravit, nec publice aequalitatem Trinitatis voluit confiteri. Magnae enim facundiae erat tunc temporis beatus Avitus; namque insurgente heresim apud urbem Constantinopolitanam tam illam quam Eutices quam quae Sabellius docuit, id est nihil divinitatis habuisse dominum nostrum Iesum Christum, rogante Gundobado rege, ipse contra eas scripsit. Extant exinde nunc apud nos epistolae admirabilis, quae sicut tunc heresim oppraesserunt, ita nunc eclesiam Dei aedificant. Scripsit enim humiliarum librum unum, de mundi principio et de diversis aliis conditionibus libros sex versu conpaginatus, epistolarum libros novem, inter quas supradictae contenentur epistolae. Refert enim in quadam omilia, quam de rogationibus scripsit, has ipsas rogationes, quas ante ascensionis dominicae triumphum caelebramus, a Mamerto ipsius Viennensis urbis episcopo, cui et hic eo tempore praeerat, instatutas fuisse, dum urbis illa multis terreretur prodigiis. Nam terrae moto frequenti quatiebatur, sed et cervorum atque luporum feritas portas ingressa, per totam, ut scripsit, urbem nihil metuens oberrabat. Cumque haec per anni circulum gererentur, advenientibus paschalis sollemnitatis diebus, expectabat misericordiam Dei plebs tota devote, ut vel hic magnae sollemnitatis dies huic terrori terminum darent. Sed in ipsa gloriosae noctis vigilia, dum missarum celebrarentur sollemnia, subito palatium regale intramuraneum divino igne succenditur. Pavore omnibus perterritis et eclesiam egressis, credentibus, ne aut hoc incendio urbs tota consumeretur aut certe disrupta tellore degiscerit, sanctus sacerdus prostratus ante altare, cum gemitu et lacrimis Domini misericordiam inpraecatur. Quid plura? Penetravit excelsa poli oratio pontefecis incliti, restinxitque domus incendium flumen profluentium lacrimarum. Cumque haec agerentur, adpropinquante ascensione, ut iam diximus, maiestatis dominicae, indixit populis ieiunium, instituit orandi modum, edendi seriem, erogandi helarem dispensationem. Cessantebus quoque exinde terroribus, per cunctas provintias dispersa facti fama cunctus sacerdotes imitare conmonuit, quod sacerdus fecit ex fide. Quae usque nunc in Dei nomine per omnes eclesias in conpunctione cordis et contritione spiritus caelebratur.

## 35. Quod Chlodovechus et Alaricus se viderunt.

<a id="hist-02-la-036"></a>
[**[036]**](#hist-02-en-036) Igitur Alaricus rex Gothorum cum viderit, Chlodovechum regem gentes assiduae debellare, legatus ad eum dirigit, dicens: 'Si frater meus vellit, insederat animo, ut nos Deo propitio pariter videremus'. Quod Chlodovechus non respuens, ad eum venit. Coniunctique in insula Ligeris, quae erat iuxta vicum Ambaciensim terreturium urbis Toronicae, simul locuti, comedentes pariter ac bibentes, promissa sibi amicitia, pacifici discesserunt. Multi iam tunc ex Galleis habere Francos dominos summo desiderio cupiebant.

## 36. De Quintiano episcopo.

<a id="hist-02-la-037"></a>
[**[037]**](#hist-02-en-037) Unde factum est, ut Quintianus Rutenorum episcopus per hoc odium ab urbe depelleretur. Dicebant enim ei: 'Quia desiderium tuum est, ut Francorum dominatio possideat terram hanc'. Post dies autem paucos, orto inter eum et cives scandalum, Gothos, qui in hac urbe morabantur, suspitio attigit, exprobrantibus civibus, quod velit se Francorum ditionibus subiugare; consilioque accepto, cogitaverunt eum perfodere gladio. Quod cum viro Dei nuntiatum fuisset, de nocte consurgens, cum fidelissimis ministris suis ab urbe Rutena egrediens, Arvernus advenit. Ibique a sancto Eufrasio episcopo, qui quondam Aprunculo Divionensi successerat, benigne susceptus est, largitisque ei tam domibus quam agris et vineis, secum retenuit, dicens: 'Sufficit huius facultas ecclesiae, ut utrumque susteneat: tantum caritas, quam beatus praedicat apostolus, permaneat inter sacerdotes Dei'. Sed et Lugdunensis episcopus largitus est ei aliqua possessionis ecclesiae suae, quam in Arverno habebat. Reliqua vero de sancto Quintiano, tam insidiae quas pertulit quam illa quae per eum Dominus operare dignatus est, scripta sunt in libro Vitae eius.

## 37. Bellum cum Alarico.

<a id="hist-02-la-038"></a>
[**[038]**](#hist-02-en-038) Igitur Chlodovechus rex ait suis: 'Valde molestum fero, quod hi Arriani partem teneant Galliarum. Eamus cum Dei adiutorium, et superatis redegamus terram in ditione nostra'. Cumque placuisset omnibus hic sermo, conmoto exercitu, Pectavus dirigit. Ibi tunc Alaricus commorabatur. Sed quoniam pars hostium per territurium Turonicum transiebat, pro reverentia beati Martini dedit edictum, ut nullus de regione illa aliud quam herbarum alimenta aquamque praesumeret. Quidam autem de exercitu, inventum cuiusdam pauperis faenum, ait: 'Nonne rex herbam tantum praesumi mandavit, nihil aliud? Et hoc, inquid, 'herba est. Non enim erimus transgressores praecepti eius, si eam praesumimus'. Cumque vim faciens pauperi faenum vertute tulisset, factum pervenit ad regem. Quem dicto citius gladio peremptum, ait: 'Et ubi erit spes victuriae, si beato Martino offendimus?' Satisque fuit exercitui nihil ulterius ab ac regione praesumere. Ipsi vero rex direxit nuntius ad beatam basilicam, dicens: 'Ite et forsitan aliquod victuriae auspicium ab aedae sancta suscipitis'. Tunc datis muneribus, quod loco sancto exhiberent, ait: 'Si tu, Domine, adiutor mihi es et gentem hanc incredulam semperque aemulam tibi meis manibus tradere decrevisti, in ingressu basilicae sancti Martini dignare propitius revelare, ut cognuscam, quia propitius dignaberis esse famulo tuo'. Maturantibus autem pueris et ad locum accedentibus iuxta imperium regis, dum sanctam ingrederentur basilicam, hanc antefanam ex inproviso primicirius, qui erat, inposuit: Praecinxisti me, Domine, virtutem ad bellum, subplantasti insurgentes in me subtus me et inimicorum meorum dedisti mihi dorsum et odientes me disperdedisti. Quod psallentium audientes, Domino gratias agentes et vota beato confessori promittentes, laeti nuntiaverunt regi. Porro ille cum ad fluvium Vigennam cum exercitu advenisset, in quo loco eum transire deberit, paenitus ignorabat. Intumuerat enim ab inundationem pluviarum. Cumque illa nocte Dominum depraecatus fuisset, ut ei vadum quo transire possit dignaretur ostendere, mane facto cerva mirae magnitudinis ante eos nuto Dei flumine ingreditur, illaque vadante, populus quo transire possit agnovit. Veniente autem rege apud Pictavus, dum eminus in tenturiis commoraret, pharus ignea, de basilica sancti Helari egressa, visa est ei tamquam super se advenire, scilicet ut, lumine beati confessoris adiutus Helarii, liberius hereticas acies, contra quas saepe idem sacerdos pro fide conflixerat, debellaret. Contestatus est autem omni exercitu, ut nec ibi quidem aut in via aliquem expoliarent aut res cuiusquam direperent. Erat in his diebus vir laudabilis sanctitatis Maxentius abba, reclausus in monastyrio suo ob Dei timore infra terminum Pictavensim. Cuius monastyrio nomen lectioni non indidimus, quia locus ille usque hodie Cellula sancti Maxenti vocatur. Cuius monachi cum unum hostium cuneum ad monastyrium cernerent propinquare, abbatem exorant, ut de cellola sua egrederetur ad consolandum eos. Illoque demorante, hii timore perculsi, eum aperto ustio de cellola sua producunt. At ille in occursum hostium, quasi pacem rogaturus, perget intrepidus. Unus autem ex his evaginato gladio, ut capud eius libraret, manus ad aurem erecta diriguit, gladiusque retrursum ruit. Ad ipse ad pedes beati viri veniam deposcens sternitur. Quod videntes reliqui, cum timore maximo ad exercitum redierunt, timentes, ne et ipse pariter interirent. Huius vero brachium beatus confessor cum oleo benedicto contrectans, inposito signo crucis, restituit sanum, eiusque obtentu monastyrio permansit inlaesum. Multasque et alias virtutes operatus est, quas si quis diligenter inquiret, librum Vitae illius legens cuncta repperiet. [Anno 25. Chlodovechi.] Interea Chlodovechus rex cum Alarico rege Gothorum in campo Vogladense decimo ab urbe Pictava miliario convenit, et confligentibus his eminus, resistunt comminus illi. Cumque secundum consuetudinem Gothi terga vertissent, ipse rex Chlodovechus victuriam, Domino adiuvante, obtinuit. Habebat autem in adiuturium suum filium Sygiberthi Claudi nomen Chlodericum. Hic Sygiberthus pugnans contra Alamannos apud Tulbiacensim oppidum percussus in genuculum claudicabat. Porro rex, cum, fugatis Grothis, Alaricum regem interfecisset, duo ex adverso subito advenientes, cum contis utraque ei latera feriunt. Sed auxilio tam luricae quam velocis equi, ne periret, exemptus est. Maximus ibi tunc Arvernorum populus, qui cum Apollinare venerat, et primi qui erant ex senatoribus corruerunt. De hac pugna Amalaricus, filius Alarici, in Spaniam fugit regnumque patris sagaciter occupavit. Chlodovechus vero filium suum Theudoricum per Albigensim ac Rutinam civitatem ad Arvernus dirigit. Qui abiens, urbes illas a finibus Gothorum usque Burgundionum terminum patris sui dicionibus subiugavit. Regnavit autem Alaricus annos 22. Chlodovechus vero apud Burdigalinsi urbe hiemem agens, cunctos thesauros Alarici a Tholosa auferens, Ecolisnam venit. Cui tantam Dominus gratiam tribuit, ut in eius contemplatione muri sponte corruerent. Tunc, exclusis Gothis, urbem suo dominic subiugavit. Post haec, patrata victuria, Turonus est regressus, multa sanctae basilicae beati Martini munera offerens.

## 38. De patriciato Chlodovechi regis.

<a id="hist-02-la-039"></a>
[**[039]**](#hist-02-en-039) Igitur ab Anastasio imperatore codecillos de consolato accepit, et in basilica beati Martini tunica blattea indutus et clamide, inponens vertice diademam. Tunc ascenso equite, aurum argentumque in itinere illo, quod inter portam atrii et eclesiam civitatis est, praesentibus populis manu propria spargens, voluntate benignissima erogavit, et ab ea die tamquam consul aut augustus est vocitatus. Egressus autem a Turonus Parisius venit ibique cathedram regni constituit. Ibi et Theudericus ad eum venit.

## 39. De Licinio episcopo.

<a id="hist-02-la-040"></a>
[**[040]**](#hist-02-en-040) Denique migrante Eustochio Turonorum episcopo, VIII. post sanctum Martinum Licinius Turonicis episcopus ordinatur. Huius tempore bellum superius scriptum gestum est. Et huius tempore Chlodovechus rex Turonus venit. Hic fertur in Oriente fuisse ac loca visitasse sanctorum ipsamque adisse Hierusolimam et loca passionis ac resurrectionis dominicae, quae in euangeliis legimus, saepe vidisse.

## 40. De interitu Sigiberthi senioris et fili eius.

<a id="hist-02-la-041"></a>
[**[041]**](#hist-02-en-041) Cum autem Chlodovechus rex apud Parisius moraretur, misit clam ad filium Sigyberthi, dicens: 'Ecce! pater tuus senuit et pede dibile claudicat. Si illi', inquid, 'moreretur, recte tibi cum amicitia nostra regnum illius reddebatur'. Qua ille cupiditate seductus, patrem molitur occidere. Cumque ille egressus de Colonia civitate, transacto Rheno, per Buconiam silvam ambulare disponeret, meridiae in tenturia sua obdormiens, inmissis super eum filius percussoribus eum ibidem interfecit, tamquam regnum illius possessurus. Sed iuditio Dei in foveam, quam patri hostiliter fodit, incessit. Misit igitur nuntius ad Chlodovechum regem de patris obito nuntiantes atque dicentes: 'Pater meus mortuos est, et ego thesaurus cum regnum eius paenes me habeo. Dirige tuos ad me, et ea quae tibi de thesauris illius placent bona voluntate transmittam'. Et illi: 'Gratias', inquid, 'tuae voluntate ago et rogo, at venientibus nostris patefacias, cuncta ipse deinceps possessurus'. Quibus venientibus iste patris thesauros pandit. Qui dum diversa respicerent, ait: 'In hanc arcellolam solitus erat pater meus numismata auri congerere'. – 'Inmitte', inquiunt illi, 'manum buam usque ad fundum et cuncta reppereas'. Quod cum fecisset et esset valde declinus, imus elevata manu bipinnem cerebrum eius inlisit, et sic quae in patre egerat indignus incurrit. Quod audiens Chlodovechus, quod scilicet interfectus esset Sygibertus vel filius eius, in eodem loco adveniens, convocavit omnem populum illum, dicens: 'Audite, quid contingerit. Dum ego', inquid, 'per Scaldem fluvium navigarem, Chlodericus, filius parentis mei, patrem suum insequebatur, verbo ferens, quod ego eum interficere vellim. Cumque ille per Buconiam silvam fugiret, inmissis super eum latruncolis, morti tradidit et occidit. Ipse quoque dum thesaurus eius aperit, a nescio quo percussus interiit. Sed in his ego nequaquam conscius sum. Nec enim possum sanguinem parentum meorum effundere, quod fieri nefas est. Sed quia haec evenerunt, consilio vobis praebeo, si videtur acceptum: convertimini ad me, ut sub meam sitis defensionem'. At ille ista audientes, plaudentes tam parmis quam vocibus, eum clypeo evectum super se regem constituunt. Regnumque Sigyberthi acceptum cum thesauris, ipsos quoque suae ditioni adscivit. Prosternebat enim cotidiae Deus hostes eius sub manu ipsius et augebat regnum eius, eo quod ambularet recto corde coram eo et facerit quae placita erant in oculis eius.

## 41. De interitu Chararici et fili eius.

<a id="hist-02-la-042"></a>
[**[042]**](#hist-02-en-042) Post haec ad Chararicum dirigit. Quando autem cum Siagrio pugnavit, hic Chararicus, evocatus ad solatium Chlodovechi, eminus stetit, neutre adiuvans parti; sed eventum rei expectans, ut cui evenerit victuriam, cum illo et hic amicitia conligaret. Ob hanc causam Chlodovechus indignans, contra eum abiit. Quem circumventum dolis coepit cum filio vinctusque totondit et Chararicum quidem presbiterum, filio vero eius diaconem ordinari iubet. Cumque Chararicus de humilitate sua conquireret et fleret, filius eius dixisse fertur: 'In viridi', inquid, 'lignum hae frondis succisae sunt nec omnino ariscunt, sed velociter emergent, ut crescere queant; utinam tam velociter qui haec fecit intereat!' Quod verbum sonuit in aures Chlodovechi, quod scilicet minarentur sibi caesariem ad crescendum laxare ipsumque interficere. At ille iussit eos pariter capite plecti. Quibus mortuis, regnum eorum cum thesauris et populis adquesivit.

## 42. De interitu Ragnachari et fratrum eius.

<a id="hist-02-la-043"></a>
[**[043]**](#hist-02-en-043) Erat autem tunc Ragnacharius rex apud Camaracum tam effrenis in luxoria, ut vix vel propinquis quidem parentibus indulgeret. His habebat Farronem consiliarium simili spurcitia lutolentum, de quo fertur, cum aliquid aut cibi aut muneris vel cuiuslibet rei regi adlatum fuisset, dicere solitum, hoc sibi suoque Farroni sufficere. Pro qua re Franci maxima indignatione tumibant. Unde factum est, ut, datis aureis sive armellis vel baltheis, Chlodovechus, sedtotum adsimilatum auro – erat enim aereum deauratum sub dolo factum–haec dedit leudibus eius, ut super eum invitaretur. Porro cum exercitum contra eum commovisset, et ille speculatores plerumque ad cognuscendum transmitteret, reversis nuntiis, interrogat, quam valida haec manus foret. Qui responderunt: 'Tibi tuoque Farroni est maximum supplimentum'. Veniens autem Chlodovechus, bellum contra eum instruit. At ille devictum cernens exercitum suum, fuga labi parat, sed ab exercitum conpraehensus ac ligatis postergum manibus in conspectu Chlodovechi una cum Richario fratre suo perducetur. Cui ille: 'Cur', inquid, 'humiliasti genus nostrum, ut te vincere permitteris? Melius enim tibi fuerat mori'. Et elevatam securem capite eius defixit, conversusque ad fratrem eius, ait: 'Si tu solatium fratri tribuissis, allegatus utique non fuisset'; similiter et hunc secure percussum interfecit. Post quorum mortem cognuscent proditores eorum, aurum, quod a regi acceperant, esse adulterum. Quod cum rege dixissent, ille respondisse fertur: 'Merito', inquid, 'tale aurum accepit, qui domino suo ad mortem propria voluntate deducit'; hoc illis quod viverent debere sufficere, ne male proditionem dominorum suorum luituri inter tormenta deficerent. Quod ille audientes, optabant gratiam adipisci, illud sibi adserentes sufficere, si vivere mererentur. Fuerunt autem supradicti regis propinqui huius; quorum frater Rignomeris nomen apud Cinomannis civitatem ex iusso Chlodovechi est interfectus. Quibus mortuis, omnem regnum eorum et thesaurus Chlodovechus accepit. Interfectisque et aliis multis regibus vel parentibus suis primis, de quibus zelum habebat, ne ei regnum auferrent, regnum suum per totas Gallias dilatavit. Tamen, congregatis suis quadam vice, dixisse fertur de parentibus, quos ipse perdiderat: 'Vae mihi, qui tamquam peregrinus inter extraneus remansi et non habeo de parentibus, qui mihi, si venerit adversitas, possit aliquid adiuvare'. Sed hoc non de morte horum condolens, sed dolo dicebat, si forte potuisset adhuc aliquem repperire, ut interficeret.

## 43. De obitu Chlodovechi.

<a id="hist-02-la-044"></a>
[**[044]**](#hist-02-en-044) His ita transactis, apud Parisius obiit, sepultusque in basilica sanctorum apostolorum, quam cum Chrodechilde regina ipse construxerat. Migravit autem post Vogladinse bellum anno quinto. Fueruntque omnes dies regni eius anni triginta; [aetas tota XLV anni]. A transitu ergo sancti Martini usque ad transitum Chlodovechi regis, qui fuit XI. annus episcopatus Licini Toronici sacerdotes, supputantur anni CXII. Chrodechildis autem regina post mortem viri sui Toronus venit, ibique ad basilica beati Martini deserviens, cum summa pudititia atque benignitate in hoc loco commorata est omnibus diebus vitae suae, raro Parisius visitans.
