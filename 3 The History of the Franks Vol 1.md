### Preface

<a id="hist-03-en-001"></a>
[**[001]**](#hist-03-la-001) I should like, if you please, to compare briefly the prosperity that has attended Christians confessing the blessed Trinity and the ruin that has befallen heretics dividing it. Let me pass over how Abraham venerated it at the oak, Jacob preached it in blessing, Moses knew it in the bush, the people followed it in the cloud and trembled before it on the mountain; how Aaron bore it on his breastplate, or David prophesied of it in the psalm, praying to be renewed by the right spirit, that the Holy Spirit not be taken from him, and that he be strengthened by the principal Spirit. Here too I see a great mystery: the prophetic voice called “principal” Him whom heretics assert to be inferior. But leaving these things, as I said, let me return to my own times. Arius, the first and wicked inventor of this wicked sect, voided his bowels in a privy and was subjected to infernal fires. Blessed Hilary, defender of the undivided Trinity, was given over to exile because of it and restored both to his country and to paradise. King Clovis confessed it, overcame the heretics themselves by its aid, and extended his kingdom through all the Gauls. Alaric denied it and was deprived of kingdom, people, and—what is greater—eternal life. The Lord restores a hundredfold here to those who truly believe in Him, even if by the enemy’s plotting they lose something; but heretics do not acquire anything better, and even what they seem to have is taken from them. The deaths of Godigisil, Gundobad, and Godomar, who lost both country and souls together, proved this. I confess one Lord, invisible, immense, incomprehensible, glorious, everlasting, and perpetual: one in Trinity because of the number of persons, Father, Son, and Holy Spirit; and threefold in unity because of the equality of substance, deity, omnipotence, and power. He is the one highest and almighty God, reigning forever and ever.

### 1. Of the sons of Clovis

<a id="hist-03-en-002"></a>
[**[002]**](#hist-03-la-002) After King Clovis’s death, his four sons—Theuderic, Chlodomer, Childebert, and Chlothar—received his kingdom and divided it among themselves in equal portions. Theuderic already had a son named Theudebert, a handsome and capable young man. Because the brothers possessed great strength and a vigorous desire for military action, Amalaric, son of Alaric and king of Spain, asked for their sister in marriage. They graciously granted his request and sent her themselves into the region of Spain with a great array of ornaments.

### 2. Of the bishops Dinifius, Apollinaris, and Quintianus

<a id="hist-03-en-003"></a>
[**[003]**](#hist-03-la-003) When Licinius, bishop of the city of Tours, died, Dinifius ascended the episcopal chair. In the Auvergne, after blessed Aprunculus’s death, Saint Eufrasius was regarded as the twelfth bishop. He lived for four years after Clovis’s death and died in the twenty-fifth year of his episcopate.

The people chose Saint Quintianus, who had been expelled from Rodez. Alchima and Placidina, the wife and sister of Apollinaris, came to Saint Quintianus and said: “Holy lord, let it suffice your old age that you have been ordained a bishop. May your piety permit your servant Apollinaris to obtain the place of this honor. When he ascends to this height, he will obey whatever pleases you; you will command, and he will submit to your every order. Only lend a kindly ear to our humble petition.” He answered: “What can I bestow, when nothing is subject to my authority? It is enough that, while I devote myself to prayer, the church supplies my daily food.”

Hearing this, they sent Apollinaris to the king. He went, offered many gifts, and succeeded to the bishopric. After abusing it for four months, he departed from the world. When this was reported to Theuderic, he ordered Saint Quintianus to be established there and all authority over the church to be entrusted to him, saying: “He was expelled from his own city because of his zeal for love of me.” Messengers were dispatched immediately; the bishops and people assembled and placed him in the chair of the church of the Auvergne. He was the fourteenth bishop set over that church. The rest of his deeds, his miracles, and the time of his death are written in the book I composed about his Life.

### 3. How the Danes sought the Gauls

<a id="hist-03-en-004"></a>
[**[004]**](#hist-03-la-004) After these events the Danes, with their king named Chlochilaichus—that is, Hygelac—came by sea in ships against the Gauls. Going ashore, they devastated one district of Theuderic’s kingdom and took captives. They loaded their ships with captives and other spoils and wished to return to their homeland. Their king remained on the shore until the ships reached the deep sea, intending to follow afterward. When Theuderic was told that foreigners had devastated his region, he sent his son Theudebert into those parts with a powerful army and a great array of weapons. Theudebert killed the king, defeated and overwhelmed the enemy in a naval battle, and restored all the plunder to the land.

### 4. Of the kings of the Thuringians

<a id="hist-03-en-005"></a>
[**[005]**](#hist-03-la-005) At that time three brothers—Baderic, Hermanfrid, and Berthachar—held the kingdom of the Thuringian people. Hermanfrid overpowered and killed his brother Berthachar. Dying, Berthachar left an orphaned daughter, Radegund, and also a son; I shall write about them later.

Hermanfrid’s wife, a wicked and cruel woman named Amalaberga, sowed civil war among these brothers. One day her husband came to a banquet and found half the table uncovered. When he asked his wife what this meant, she answered: “A man despoiled of half his kingdom deserves to have half his table stripped.” Moved by these and similar words, he rose against his brother and secretly invited King Theuderic by messengers to attack him, saying: “If you kill him, we shall divide this region in equal shares.” Theuderic rejoiced at the message and directed his army against Baderic. The allies joined together and pledged faith to one another before going out to war. Engaging Baderic, they destroyed his army and cut him down with the sword. After obtaining the victory, Theuderic returned home. Hermanfrid immediately forgot his pledge and disdained to give King Theuderic what he had promised. A great enmity arose between them.

### 5. How Sigismund killed his son

<a id="hist-03-en-006"></a>
[**[006]**](#hist-03-la-006) After Gundobad’s death his son Sigismund obtained the kingdom. With skillful care he built the monastery of Agaune, together with its houses and basilicas. His first wife, a daughter of Theodoric, king of Italy, had died; by her he had a son named Sigeric. He married another wife, who began, as is the custom of stepmothers, to malign his son vehemently and stir up trouble.

On a feast day the boy recognized his mother’s garments on her. Moved with bitterness, he said: “You were not worthy to have these garments cover your back, for they are known to have belonged to your lady—that is, my mother.” Inflamed with fury, she incited her husband with deceitful words: “This wicked boy desires to possess your kingdom. He intends to kill you and extend his rule as far as Italy, so that he may also possess the kingdom which his grandfather Theodoric held in Italy. He knows that he cannot accomplish this while you live, and that unless you fall he cannot rise.”

Incited by these and similar words, Sigismund followed his wicked wife’s counsel and became a criminal parricide. He ordered his son, heavy with wine, to sleep after midday. While the boy slept, a napkin was placed beneath his neck and tied under his chin; two servants pulled its ends in opposite directions and strangled him. When this had been done, the father repented too late. Falling upon the lifeless corpse, he began to weep most bitterly. An old man is said to have told him: “Weep now for yourself, who through evil counsel have become a most savage parricide. There is no need to mourn this boy, who was innocent when he was strangled.”

Nevertheless Sigismund went to the holy place of Agaune and remained for many days in weeping and fasting, begging for pardon. He established continual psalmody there and returned to Lyon, with divine vengeance following close behind. King Theuderic received Sigismund’s daughter.

### 6. Of the death of Chlodomer

<a id="hist-03-en-007"></a>
[**[007]**](#hist-03-la-007) Queen Chrotchildis addressed Chlodomer and her other sons: “My dearest sons, let me not regret having nurtured you so tenderly. I beg you, be indignant at the injury done to me and avenge the deaths of my father and mother with resolute care.” Hearing this, they made for the Burgundies and marched against Sigismund and his brother Godomar. Their enemies’ army was defeated, and Godomar fled. Sigismund, while trying to escape to the holy place of Agaune, was captured by Chlodomer and led away captive with his wife and sons. He was placed in custody within the territory of the city of Orléans and held there.

After these kings departed, Godomar recovered his strength, gathered the Burgundians, and regained the kingdom. Chlodomer prepared to march against him again and resolved to kill Sigismund. Blessed Abbot Avitus, a great priest of that time, told him: “If you look to God and amend your plan so that you do not permit these people to be killed, God will be with you, and you will go forth and obtain victory. But if you kill them, you yourself will be delivered into your enemies’ hands and perish by a similar fate. What you do to Sigismund, his wife, and his children will be done to you, your wife, and your sons.”

Disdaining to heed this counsel, Chlodomer said: “I think it foolish counsel to leave enemies at home and march against the others, so that I rush between two enemy formations, one rising behind me and the other in front. Victory will be achieved more surely and easily if one is separated from the other. Once one has been killed, the other can easily be marked out for death.” He immediately killed Sigismund together with his wife and sons, ordered them thrown into a well at the village of Colomna in the territory of Orléans, and made for the Burgundies, calling King Theuderic to his aid. Unwilling to avenge the injury done to his father-in-law, Theuderic nevertheless promised to go.

They joined forces at Vézeronce, a place near the city of Vienne, and fought Godomar. Godomar and his army turned in flight, and Chlodomer pursued until he was separated from his own men by a considerable distance. The enemy imitated his signal and called to him: “Turn this way, this way! We are your men.” Believing them, he went and rushed into the midst of the enemy. They cut off his head, fixed it on a pike, and raised it high. When the Franks saw this and knew that Chlodomer had been killed, they recovered their strength, put Godomar to flight, overwhelmed the Burgundians, and brought their country under their own power.

Without delay Chlothar married Guntheuca, his brother’s widow. After the days of mourning were completed, Queen Chrotchildis received Chlodomer’s sons and kept them with her. They were named Theudoald, Gunthar, and Chlodovald. Godomar regained the kingdom.

### 7. The war against the Thuringians

<a id="hist-03-en-008"></a>
[**[008]**](#hist-03-la-008) After this Theuderic, remembering King Hermanfrid’s perjury, called his brother Chlothar to his aid and prepared to march against him. He promised King Chlothar a share of the plunder if God granted them the gift of victory. He assembled the Franks and said: “I beg you, be indignant both at my injury and at the destruction of your kinsmen. Remember that the Thuringians once violently attacked our ancestors and inflicted many evils upon them. Our ancestors gave hostages and wished to make peace, but the Thuringians destroyed the hostages by various deaths. Rushing upon our ancestors, they took all their substance. They hung boys from trees by the sinews of their thighs. They cruelly killed more than two hundred girls: they bound their arms over horses’ necks, goaded the horses into sharp movement in different directions, and tore the women apart. They stretched others over the ruts of the roads, fixed stakes in the ground, and drove loaded wagons over them. Then, with their bones broken, they gave them to dogs and birds for food. Now Hermanfrid has broken his promise to me and altogether refuses to fulfill it. I have a clear cause: let us go against them with God’s help!”

Hearing this and indignant at such great crimes, they made for Thuringia with one mind and one resolve. Theuderic took his brother Chlothar and his son Theudebert as allies and set out with the army. The Thuringians prepared traps for the approaching Franks. They dug ditches in the field where the battle was to be fought and covered their openings with dense turf, making them resemble level ground. When the fighting began, many Frankish horsemen fell into these pits, which greatly impeded them; but once they recognized the trick, they began to watch for it.

When the Thuringians saw themselves being slaughtered violently and their king Hermanfrid put to flight, they turned their backs and came as far as the river Unstrut. Such slaughter of the Thuringians occurred there that the riverbed was filled with heaps of corpses, and the Franks crossed over them to the farther bank as though over a bridge. Having won the victory, they occupied the region and brought it under their own power.

On his return Chlothar took with him as a captive Radegund, daughter of King Berthachar, and joined her to himself in marriage. Later her brother was unjustly killed by wicked men. Radegund turned to God, changed her clothing for the religious habit, and built herself a monastery within the city of Poitiers. Distinguished by prayers, fasts, and almsgiving, she became so eminent that she was regarded as great among the people.

While the aforesaid kings were still in Thuringia, Theuderic wished to kill his brother Chlothar. He secretly prepared armed men and summoned Chlothar as though to discuss something privately. He stretched a curtain across part of the house from one wall to the other and ordered the armed men to stand behind it. Because the curtain was too short, the armed men’s feet were visible. Recognizing this, Chlothar entered the house armed and accompanied by his own men. Theuderic understood that he had discovered the plot, invented a story, and changed from one subject to another. Finally, not knowing how to disguise his treachery, he gave Chlothar a great silver dish as a token of goodwill. Chlothar took leave, thanked him for the gift, and returned to his camp.

Theuderic then complained to his men that he had lost his dish for no reason and told his son Theudebert: “Go to your uncle and ask him willingly to grant you the gift I gave him.” Theudebert went and obtained what he asked. Theuderic was exceedingly skilled in such deceptions.

### 8. Of the death of Hermanfrid

<a id="hist-03-en-009"></a>
[**[009]**](#hist-03-la-009) After Theuderic returned home, he gave Hermanfrid a pledge of safety and ordered him to come to him without fear. He also enriched him with honorable gifts. One day, while they were talking together on the wall of the city of Zülpich, Hermanfrid was pushed by someone unknown, fell from the height of the wall to the ground, and there breathed out his spirit. I do not know who threw him down, but many assert that Theuderic’s treachery was plainly revealed in this deed.

### 9. How Childebert went to the Auvergne

<a id="hist-03-en-010"></a>
[**[010]**](#hist-03-la-010) While Theuderic was still in Thuringia, a report spread through the Auvergne that he had been killed. Archadius, one of the senators of the Auvergne, invited Childebert to take possession of the region. Childebert went there without delay. So dense a fog covered that day that nothing could be discerned beyond two acres. The king used to say: “I wish I could one day see with my own eyes the Limagne in the Auvergne, which is said to shine with such delightful charm.” But God did not grant it to him. The city gates were barred, and he had no open passage by which to enter. Archadius sawed through the bar of one gate and admitted him into the city. While these things were happening, news arrived that Theuderic had returned alive from Thuringia.

### 10. Of the death of Amalaric

<a id="hist-03-en-011"></a>
[**[011]**](#hist-03-la-011) Certain of this, Childebert returned from the Auvergne and made for Spain on account of his sister Chrotchildis. She had suffered many injuries from her husband Amalaric because of the catholic faith. When she went to the holy church, he often ordered dung and various kinds of filth to be thrown over her. Finally he is said to have beaten her so cruelly that she sent her brother a handkerchief stained with her own blood. Greatly moved by this, Childebert made for the Spains.

Hearing of his approach, Amalaric prepared ships for flight. As Childebert drew near and Amalaric was about to board a ship, he remembered that he had left a multitude of precious stones in his treasury. When he returned to the city to fetch them, the army cut him off from the harbor. Seeing that he could not escape, he tried to take refuge in a Christian church. Before he could touch the sacred threshold, one man hurled a spear and wounded him with a mortal blow; he fell there and gave up his spirit.

Childebert then wished to bring his sister back with him, together with great treasures, but by some chance she died on the journey. She was afterward carried to Paris and buried beside her father Clovis. Among the other treasures Childebert brought back very precious church vessels: sixty chalices, fifteen patens, and twenty Gospel cases, all made of pure gold and adorned with precious gems. He would not allow them to be broken up, but distributed and delivered them all to churches and basilicas of the saints.

### 11. How Childebert and Chlothar went to Burgundy

<a id="hist-03-en-012"></a>
[**[012]**](#hist-03-la-012) After this Chlothar and Childebert resolved to make for Burgundy. Theuderic was summoned to assist them but refused to go. The Franks who followed him said: “If you disdain to go into Burgundy with your brothers, we shall leave you and prefer to follow them.” Considering the people of the Auvergne disloyal to him, Theuderic answered: “Follow me, and I shall lead you into a country where you may take as much gold and silver as your greed can desire, and from which you may carry away cattle, slaves, and clothing in abundance. Only do not follow them!” Enticed by these promises, they undertook to do his will. He prepared to cross into the Auvergne, repeatedly promising the army permission to carry all the region’s plunder, together with its people, into their own territories. Meanwhile Chlothar and Childebert made for Burgundy, besieged Autun, put Godomar to flight, and occupied all Burgundy.

### 12. Of the devastation of the Auvergne

<a id="hist-03-en-013"></a>
[**[013]**](#hist-03-la-013) Theuderic came with his army into the Auvergne, devastated the whole region, and crushed it. Meanwhile Archadius, the author of the crime through whose cowardice the region was devastated, went to the city of Bourges, which was then in King Childebert’s kingdom. His mother Placidina and his father’s sister Alchima were seized in the city of Cahors, deprived of their property, and condemned to exile.

King Theuderic advanced as far as the city of Clermont and pitched camp in its suburbs. Blessed Quintianus was bishop in those days. The army ranged through all that wretched region, destroying and overwhelming everything. Some came to Saint Julian’s basilica, broke the doors, removed the bars, and plundered the poor people’s property which had been gathered there, committing many evils in that place. But the perpetrators were seized by an unclean spirit and tore themselves with their own ravenous teeth, crying out: “Why do you torment me so, holy martyr?” I have written about this in the book of Julian’s Virtues.

### 13. Of Lovolautrum and the castle of Meroliacum

<a id="hist-03-en-014"></a>
[**[014]**](#hist-03-la-014) The enemy stormed the fortress of Lovolautrum and wretchedly killed the priest Proculus at the church altar—the same Proculus who had once injured Saint Quintianus. I believe that the fortress was delivered into wicked men’s hands on his account, though it had been defended until that day. When the enemy could not capture it, they prepared to return home. Hearing this, the besieged became joyful and felt secure, but they were deceived, as the apostle says: “When they say, ‘Peace and security,’ then sudden destruction will come upon them.” Through the priest Proculus’s own servant, the people, now secure, were delivered into the enemy’s hands. When the fortress had been devastated and the captives were being led away, an immense rain, which had been withheld for thirty days, descended.

The besieged in the fortress of Meroliacum paid a ransom and were freed, lest they be led away as captives. Their cowardice caused this, although the fortress was protected by its own natural position. It is enclosed by a sheer rock face one hundred feet high or more, without any construction of walls. In the middle is a great pool of very pleasant water, and on another side are most abundant springs, so that a stream of living water flows through the gate. The fortification extends over so great an area that those living within its enclosures cultivate the land and gather crops in abundance. Elated by this protection, the besieged went outside to seize some plunder and then hide themselves again within the fortress, but the enemy captured them. They were fifty men. With their hands bound behind their backs, they were brought forward before their parents’ faces. With the sword already threatening them, the besieged agreed to pay thirty trientes for each man as his ransom, so that they would not be killed.

When Theuderic departed from the Auvergne, he left his kinsman Sigivald there as a guard. At that time a certain commoner named Litigius was preparing great snares against Saint Quintianus. Although the holy bishop prostrated himself at Litigius’s feet, he was never moved to submit to him; on one occasion he even pointed this out as a joke to his wife, who revered the saint. She, moved by better understanding, said: “If he is so cast down today, you will never rise.” On the third day messengers came from the king’s presence and led Litigius away bound, together with his wife and children. He departed and never returned to the Auvergne.

### 14. Of the death of Munderic

<a id="hist-03-en-015"></a>
[**[015]**](#hist-03-la-015) Munderic, who claimed kinship with the royal family, was greatly puffed up with pride and said: “What is there between me and King Theuderic? The throne of the kingdom is due to me just as it is to him. I shall go out, gather my people, and exact an oath from them, so that Theuderic may know that I am a king just as he is.” He went out and began to seduce the people, saying: “I am a prince. Follow me, and it will be well with you.” A rustic multitude followed him, as often happens with human frailty, swearing fidelity and honoring him as king.

When Theuderic learned this, he sent Munderic a command: “Come to me, and if any portion of the rule of my kingdom is due to you, take it.” Theuderic said this deceitfully, intending to kill him when he came. Munderic refused: “Go and tell your king that I am a king just as he is.” The king then ordered the army to move so that Munderic could be crushed by force. Realizing that he could not defend himself, Munderic sought the walls of the fortress of Vitry with all his possessions. Gathering those whom he had seduced, he tried to establish himself there. The army surrounded the fortress and besieged it for seven days. Munderic and his men resisted, and he said: “Let us stand bravely, fight together even to death, and not submit to the enemy.” The army hurled javelins from every side but accomplished nothing, and reported this to the king.

Theuderic sent one of his men, named Aregisilus, and told him: “You see how this perfidious man prevails in his obstinacy. Go and swear to him that he may come out safely. When he has come out, kill him and blot out his memory from my kingdom.” Aregisilus went and did as commanded. First he gave the people a signal: “When I say such and such, rush in immediately and kill him.” Entering, Aregisilus said to Munderic: “How long will you remain here like a fool? Can you resist the king for long? Your food has been cut off! When hunger overwhelms you, you will come out of your own accord, be delivered into the enemy’s hands, and die like a dog. Listen instead to my counsel and submit to the king, so that you and your sons may live.”

Softened by these words, Munderic said: “If I go out, the king will seize and kill me, my sons, and all the friends gathered with me.” Aregisilus answered: “Do not be afraid. If you wish to go out, receive an oath concerning this offense and stand secure before the king. Do not fear; you will be with him as you were before.” Munderic replied: “If only I could be certain that I shall not be killed!” Aregisilus placed his hands on the holy altar and swore that he would come out safely.

After the oath had been given, Munderic went out through the fortress gate holding Aregisilus’s hand, while the people watched from a distance. As the signal, Aregisilus said: “Why are you staring so intently, my people? Have you never seen Munderic before?” The people immediately rushed at Munderic. Understanding, he said: “I recognize very plainly that by these words you gave the people the signal to kill me. Nevertheless, because you deceived me by perjury, I tell you that no one will see you alive again.” He hurled his lance into Aregisilus’s shoulders and pierced him through; Aregisilus fell dead. Munderic then drew his sword and, with his men, inflicted great slaughter on the people. Until he breathed his last, he did not cease killing everyone he could overtake. When he had been killed, his property was taken into the royal treasury.

### 15. Of the captivity of Attalus

<a id="hist-03-en-016"></a>
[**[016]**](#hist-03-la-016) Theuderic and Childebert made a treaty. They swore that neither would move against the other and exchanged hostages to confirm their agreement more firmly. Many sons of senators were given as hostages at that time. When discord arose between the kings again, these hostages were condemned to public service, and whoever received one for custody made him his own slave. Many escaped by flight and returned to their homeland, but some were retained in servitude.

Among them was Attalus, nephew of blessed Gregory, bishop of Langres. He was assigned to public service and appointed a keeper of horses, serving a certain barbarian in the territory of Trier. Blessed Gregory sent servants to find him. When they had found him, they offered the man gifts, but he refused, saying: “A youth of such a family ought to be redeemed for ten pounds of gold.” When they returned, a certain Leo from his master’s kitchen said: “If only you would permit me, perhaps I could bring him back from captivity.” His master rejoiced and sent him.

Leo came to the place and tried to withdraw the youth secretly, but could not. He then hired a man and said: “Come with me and sell me into that barbarian’s household. Let my price be your profit; only let me have freer access to do what I have resolved.” The man accepted an oath, went, sold Leo for twelve gold pieces, and departed. When the purchaser asked his raw servant what work he knew, Leo answered: “I know very well how to prepare everything that ought to be served at lords’ tables, and I do not fear that anyone equal to me in this skill can be found. Indeed, even if you wished to prepare a feast for a king, I could compose royal dishes, and no one could do it better.” The barbarian said: “Sunday is coming”—for barbarians customarily call the Lord’s day the day of the sun—“and on that day my neighbors and relatives will be invited to my house. I ask you to prepare a meal that will make them marvel and say that they have seen nothing better in a king’s house.” Leo replied: “Let my master order a great number of chickens to be collected, and I shall do what you command.”

When everything Leo had requested had been prepared, Sunday dawned, and he made a great banquet filled with delicacies. Everyone feasted and praised the meal, and the relatives departed. The master gave favor to the youth and granted him authority over everything in his store. He loved Leo greatly, and Leo himself distributed food and dishes to everyone in the household.

After a year, when Attalus’s master was completely confident in him, Leo went with Attalus, the horse keeper, into a meadow near the house. They lay on the ground at a distance from the others, with their backs turned away, so that no one would know they were speaking together. Leo told the youth: “It is time for us to think about our homeland. I advise you not to be overcome by sleep tonight when you have brought the horses in to be shut up. As soon as I call you, be ready, and let us go.”

The barbarian had invited many relatives to a banquet, among them his son-in-law, who had married his daughter. When the guests rose from the feast at midnight and went to rest, Leo accompanied his master’s son-in-law to his lodging with drink and offered him a cup. The man said jokingly: “Tell me, you who have won my father-in-law’s confidence, when will you decide to take his horses and go to your own country?” Leo was delighted and asked this as though in jest. The other, likewise answering the joke in kind, said: “I intend to do it tonight, if it is God’s will.” Leo replied: “I hope my servants will guard me, so that you do not take any of my property!” They departed laughing.

When everyone was asleep, Leo called Attalus. After saddling the horses, he asked whether Attalus had a sword. Attalus answered: “I have nothing but a small lance.” Leo entered his master’s chamber and took his shield and spear. When the master asked who he was and what he wanted, Leo answered: “I am your servant Leo, and I am waking Attalus so that he may rise quickly and take the horses to pasture; he is held down by sleep as though drunk.” The master said: “Do as you please,” and fell asleep again.

Leo went out, gave the youth the weapons, and found the courtyard doors divinely opened, although at the beginning of the night he had barred them with wedges struck by a hammer to guard the horses. Giving thanks to God, they took the remaining horses and departed, carrying also one hawk and the clothing. When they came to the river Moselle to cross and were detained by certain men, they abandoned the horses and clothing. Supporting themselves on their shields, they swam across the river and came out on the farther bank. They entered the woods in the darkness of night and hid.

It was now the third night of their journey without tasting food. By God’s guidance they found a tree full of the fruit commonly called plums. They ate, gained a little strength, and entered the road through Champagne. As they proceeded, they heard the hoofbeats of running horses and said: “Let us throw ourselves on the ground so that the approaching men do not see us.” Suddenly a great bramble bush appeared. They passed behind it and threw themselves on the ground with drawn swords, intending to defend themselves with their spears like desperate men if they were discovered.

The riders came to that place and stopped in front of the thorny bush. While the horses urinated, one man said: “Woe to me, for these detestable men are escaping and cannot be found! By my own salvation, if they were found, I would condemn one to the gallows and order the other cut to pieces by sword blows.” The barbarian doing this was their master, who had come from the city of Reims in search of them. He would certainly have found them on the road if the night had not hindered him. He then moved his horses and departed.

That same night the fugitives reached the city. Entering it, they found a man and asked where the priest Paulellus’s house was; he showed them. As they passed through the street, the signal for matins sounded, for it was Sunday. They knocked at the priest’s door and entered, and the youth explained about his master. The priest said: “My vision was true. This night I saw two doves fly down and settle on my hand; one was white and the other black.” The youth said: “May the Lord reward you for His holy day. We ask you to give us something to eat, for the fourth day is dawning since we tasted bread or any dish.” The priest hid the youths, gave them wine mixed with water and bread, and went to matins. The barbarian followed, again searching for the youths, but the priest deceived him and he returned. The priest had an old friendship with blessed Gregory.

The youths recovered their strength with food and remained in the priest’s house for two days. They then departed and were brought safely to Saint Gregory. The bishop rejoiced at seeing them and wept upon the neck of his nephew Attalus. He freed Leo and all his descendants from the yoke of servitude and gave him land of his own, on which Leo lived freely with his wife and children all the days of his life.

### 16. Of Sigivald

<a id="hist-03-en-017"></a>
[**[017]**](#hist-03-la-017) While Sigivald was living in the Auvergne, he did many evil things there. He seized other people’s property, and his servants did not cease from thefts, murders, attacks, and various crimes; no one dared utter a word against them. Thus it happened that he rashly dared to seize the estate of Bulgiatensis, which the blessed bishop Tetradius had formerly left to the basilica of Saint Julian. But as soon as he entered that house, he was driven out of his mind and took to his bed. Then his wife, advised by a priest, had him carried on a litter to another estate, and he recovered his health. She went to him and explained everything that he had suffered. When he heard this, he made vows to the blessed martyr and restored twofold what he had taken by force. We have also recorded this miracle in the book of the Miracles of Saint Julian.

### 17. Of the bishops of Tours

<a id="hist-03-en-018"></a>
[**[018]**](#hist-03-la-018) When Bishop Dinifius died at Tours, Ommatius governed the church for three years. He had been ordained by command of King Chlodomer, whom we mentioned above. When Ommatius also died, Leo served for seven months. He was an energetic man and skilled in woodworking. After his death, the bishops Theodorus and Proculus, who had come from the region of Burgundy, governed the church of Tours for three years, having been ordained by Queen Chrotchildis. When they died, Francilio, one of the senators, was appointed in their place. In the third year of his episcopate, when the holy night of the Lord’s Nativity had shone upon the people, the bishop, before going down to the vigil, ordered a cup to be brought to him. A servant boy came and immediately handed it to him. As soon as he had drunk, he breathed out his spirit. It is therefore certain that he was killed by poison. Upon his death, Injuriosus, one of the citizens, obtained the episcopal chair, the fifteenth after blessed Martin.

### 18. Of the death of the sons of Chlodomer

<a id="hist-03-en-019"></a>
[**[019]**](#hist-03-la-019) While Queen Chrotchildis was staying in Paris, Childebert saw that his mother loved the sons of Chlodomer, whom we mentioned above, with undivided affection. Moved by envy and fearing that they might be admitted to the kingdom through the queen’s favor, he sent secretly to his brother King Chlothar, saying: “Our mother is keeping our brother’s sons with her and wishes to give them the kingdom. You must come quickly to Paris. We must take counsel together and consider what should be done with them: whether their hair should be cut and they should live like the rest of the people, or whether they should be killed and our brother’s kingdom divided equally between us.” Chlothar was greatly pleased by these words and came to Paris. Childebert had spread word among the people that the kings were meeting for the purpose of raising the little boys to the kingdom. When they had joined forces, they sent to the queen, who was then staying in that city, saying: “Send the little boys to us, so that they may be raised to the kingdom.” Rejoicing and unaware of their treachery, she gave the boys food and drink and sent them off, saying: “I shall not feel that I have lost my son if I see you established in his kingdom.” As they went, they were immediately seized. Their servants and attendants were separated from them, and both groups were guarded apart, the attendants in one place and the little princes in another.

Then Childebert and Chlothar sent Archadius, whom we mentioned above, to the queen with a pair of shears and a drawn sword. He came and showed her both, saying: “Your sons, our lords, ask your decision, most glorious queen. What do you think should be done with the boys? Do you order them to live with their hair cut, or to have their throats cut?” Terrified by the message and deeply embittered, especially when she saw the drawn sword and the shears, she was overcome by anguish and, not knowing in her grief what she was saying, answered without reflection: “If they are not to be raised to the kingdom, I would rather see them dead than shorn.” He gave little thought to her grief and did not wait to learn what she might say after fuller consideration. He quickly returned and reported: “The queen favors the work you have begun; complete your plan, for she herself wishes it carried out.”

Without delay Chlothar seized the older boy by the arm, threw him to the ground, drove a knife beneath his armpit, and cruelly killed him. As the boy cried out, his brother threw himself at Childebert’s feet, clasped his knees, and begged with tears: “Help me, most merciful father, so that I too may not perish like my brother.” Then Childebert, his face wet with tears, said: “I beg you, dearest brother, grant me this boy’s life as a kindness. I will give whatever you command in return for his life; only let him not be killed.” But Chlothar answered with abuse: “Either push him away from you, or you will surely die in his place. You are the instigator of this affair, and are you going back on your word so quickly?” When Childebert heard this, he thrust the boy away and cast him toward Chlothar. Chlothar caught him, pierced his side with a knife, and killed him as he had killed his brother. Then they put the servants and attendants to death.

After these killings, Chlothar mounted his horse and departed, caring little about the murder of his nephews; Childebert withdrew to the suburbs. The queen had the little bodies placed on a bier and followed them with great chanting of psalms and immense grief to the basilica of Saint Peter, where she buried them side by side. One was ten years old and the other seven. They were unable to seize the third boy, Chlodovald, because he was rescued with the help of brave men. He later renounced an earthly kingdom and turned to the Lord. Cutting his hair with his own hand, he became a cleric; devoted to good works, he departed from this world as a priest. The two kings divided Chlodomer’s kingdom equally between themselves.

Queen Chrotchildis conducted herself so worthily and greatly that she was honored by everyone. She was constant in almsgiving, spent whole nights in vigils, and always kept herself pure in chastity and every honorable practice. She provided the lands needed by churches, monasteries, and other holy places, distributing them with generous and ready goodwill. Thus she seemed at that time not so much a queen as a handmaid of God, serving him devotedly. Neither her sons’ kingdom, nor worldly ambition, nor wealth raised her up only to bring her to ruin; humility lifted her to grace.

### 19. Of the holy Gregory and the site of the castle of Dijon

<a id="hist-03-en-020"></a>
[**[020]**](#hist-03-la-020) The blessed Gregory was then at the city of Langres, a great priest of God, renowned for signs and miracles. Since we have mentioned this bishop, I have thought it well to include in this account a description of the site of Dijon, where he especially used to reside. The fortress stands in the middle of a plain and is pleasantly situated, with very strong walls and extremely fertile and productive land. The fields need be cut only once by the ploughshare; then the seed is scattered, and a great abundance of crops follows. To the south it has the river Ouche, very rich in fish; from the north another small river comes, which enters through a gate, runs beneath a bridge, and goes out again through another gate, flowing with a calm current around the entire fortified place. In front of the gate it drives mills with remarkable speed. Four gates face the four quarters of the world, and thirty-three towers adorn the whole structure. Its wall is built of squared stones up to twenty feet, and above that of smaller stones; it is thirty feet high and fifteen feet thick. Why it is not called a city, I do not know. It has excellent springs within its circuit. To the west are very rich hills covered with vineyards, which supply the inhabitants with Falernian wine so noble that they despise the wine of Ascalon. The ancients relate that it was built by the emperor Aurelian.

### 20. How Theudebert Betrothed Visigard

<a id="hist-03-en-021"></a>
[**[021]**](#hist-03-la-021) Theuderic had betrothed Wisigard, the daughter of a certain king, to his son Theudebert.

### 21. How Theudebert went into Provence

<a id="hist-03-en-022"></a>
[**[022]**](#hist-03-la-022) After the death of Clovis, the Goths seized many of the places that he had acquired. Theuderic sent Theudebert, and Chlothar sent his elder son Gunthar, to recover them. Gunthar went as far as Rodez but then turned back, for what reason I do not know. Theudebert went as far as the city of Béziers, took the fortress of Dehas, and plundered it. He then sent messengers to another fortress named Capraria, declaring that unless its occupants surrendered, he would burn the whole place with fire and carry off those who lived there as captives.

### 22. How he afterward took Deoteria

<a id="hist-03-en-023"></a>
[**[023]**](#hist-03-la-023) There was then in that place a matron named Deoteria, a very capable and wise woman, whose husband had died in the city of Béziers. She sent messengers to the king, saying: “No one can resist you, most merciful lord. We acknowledge you as our lord; come and do whatever is pleasing in your eyes.” Theudebert came to the fortress and entered in peace. Seeing that the people had submitted to him, he did no harm there. Deoteria came out to meet him. Seeing that she was beautiful, he was overcome by love for her and took her to his bed.

### 23. Of the death of Sigivald, and his flight

<a id="hist-03-en-024"></a>
[**[024]**](#hist-03-la-024) In those days Theuderic killed his kinsman Sigivald with the sword. He also sent secretly to Theudebert, ordering him to put to death Sigivald’s son, likewise named Sigivald, who was then with him. But because Theudebert had received the younger Sigivald from the sacred font as his godson, he refused to destroy him. He gave him his father’s letter to read and said: “Flee from here, for I have received my father’s command to kill you. But if he dies and you hear that I am reigning, then return to me in safety.” Sigivald thanked him, bade him farewell, and departed. The Goths had then seized the city of Arles, and Theudebert was holding hostages from it; Sigivald fled there. But seeing that he was not sufficiently protected, he went to Latium and hid there.

While these things were happening, Theudebert received word that his father was gravely ill and that, unless he hurried, he would not find him alive; his uncles would exclude him, and he would never return there. On hearing this, he set everything else aside and hastened to his father, leaving Deoteria and her daughter in the Auvergne. Not many days after his departure, Theuderic died in the twenty-third year of his reign. Childebert and Chlothar then rose against Theudebert and tried to take his kingdom from him. But he appeased them with gifts, was defended by his leudes, and was firmly established in the kingdom. He later sent to the Auvergne, summoned Deoteria, and joined her to himself in marriage.

### 24. How Childebert made gifts to Theudebert

<a id="hist-03-en-025"></a>
[**[025]**](#hist-03-la-025) When Childebert saw that he could not prevail against Theudebert, he sent an embassy and summoned him to come, saying: “I have no sons, and I wish to regard you as my son.” When Theudebert came, Childebert enriched him with such gifts that everyone was astonished. He gave him three sets each of fine weapons, clothing, and other ornaments such as a king ought to possess, and likewise of horses and dishes. When Sigivald heard that Theudebert had obtained his father’s kingdom, he returned to him from Italy. Theudebert welcomed him joyfully, kissed him, and gave him a third of the gifts he had received from his uncle. He also ordered that all of Sigivald’s property which his own father had placed in the treasury should be returned to him.

### 25. Of the Goodness of Theudebert

<a id="hist-03-en-026"></a>
[**[026]**](#hist-03-la-026) Once firmly established in the kingdom, Theudebert showed himself great and outstanding in every kind of goodness. He ruled the kingdom justly, honored the priests, endowed churches, relieved the poor, and conferred many benefits on many people with pious and most kindly generosity. He graciously remitted all the tribute that the churches situated in the Auvergne used to pay to his treasury.

### 26. Of the Death of Deoteria’s Daughter

<a id="hist-03-en-027"></a>
[**[027]**](#hist-03-la-027) Deoteria saw that her daughter was now fully grown. Fearing that the king would desire her and take her for himself, she placed the girl in a carriage, harnessed untamed oxen to it, and had her thrown from a bridge. The girl died in the river. This happened at the city of Verdun.

### 27. How Theudebert took Visigard

<a id="hist-03-en-028"></a>
[**[028]**](#hist-03-la-028) Seven years had now passed since Theudebert’s betrothal to Wisigard, and he still refused to marry her because of Deoteria. The Franks joined together in strong indignation against him for abandoning his betrothed. Moved by their protest, he left Deoteria, by whom he had a young son named Theudebald, and married Wisigard. He did not have her for long: she died, and he took another wife. But he never took Deoteria back.

### 28. How Childebert went against Chlothar

<a id="hist-03-en-029"></a>
[**[029]**](#hist-03-la-029) Childebert and Theudebert called up their armies and prepared to march against Chlothar. When Chlothar heard of it, he judged that he could not withstand their forces. He fled into a forest, made great clearings among the trees, and placed all his hope in God’s mercy. Queen Chrotchildis also heard of it. She went to the tomb of blessed Martin, prostrated herself in prayer there, and kept vigil throughout the night, praying that no civil war might arise between her sons.

When Childebert and Theudebert came with their armies and besieged Chlothar, intending to kill him the next day, a storm arose at daybreak in the place where they were gathered. It scattered their tents, carried off their possessions, and overturned everything. Flashes of lightning mingled with thunder and hailstones came down upon them. They fell face down on the hail-covered ground and were severely beaten by the falling stones, for no covering remained to them except their shields. Above all, they feared being burned by fire from heaven. Their horses were scattered so widely that some could scarcely be found within twenty stades, and many were never found at all. Bruised by the hailstones and prostrate on the ground, the men repented and begged God’s pardon for having intended to do these things against their own kin.

Not a single drop of rain fell on Chlothar; no thunder was heard there, and those with him felt no blast of wind. Childebert and Theudebert sent messengers to him and asked for peace and concord. When these were granted, they returned home. Let no one doubt that this miracle of blessed Martin was obtained through the queen’s intercession.

### 29. How Childebert and Chlothar went into Spain

<a id="hist-03-en-030"></a>
[**[030]**](#hist-03-la-030) After this King Childebert went into Spain. He and Chlothar entered the country, surrounded the city of Zaragoza with their army, and besieged it. The inhabitants turned to God in such great humility that, clothed in haircloth and abstaining from food and drink, they went around the city walls singing psalms and carrying the tunic of the blessed martyr Vincent. The women followed them in mourning, dressed in black garments, their hair loosened and ashes sprinkled over them, so that one would have thought they were attending their husbands’ funerals. The whole place placed its hope in the Lord’s mercy. It was said that the fast of the Ninevites was being celebrated there, and the people believed that nothing else could avail except to move divine mercy by their prayers.

The besiegers did not know what the besieged were doing. When they saw them going around the walls in this manner, they thought that they were practicing some kind of sorcery. They seized a rustic from the city and asked him what was happening. He replied: “They are carrying the tunic of blessed Vincent and praying with it that the Lord may have mercy on them.” Fearing this, the besiegers withdrew from the city. Nevertheless, after acquiring a very large part of Spain, they returned to Gaul with great spoils.

### 30. Of the kings of the Spaniards

<a id="hist-03-en-031"></a>
[**[031]**](#hist-03-la-031) After Amalaric, Theuda was made king in Spain. When he had been killed, the Goths raised Theudegisil to the throne. While Theudegisil was dining with his friends and was in high spirits, the candles were suddenly extinguished; his enemies struck him with a sword where he reclined, and he died. Agila succeeded him. The Goths had adopted the detestable custom of attacking with the sword any king who displeased them and then setting up as king whoever pleased them.

### 31. Of the daughter of Theuderic king of Italy

<a id="hist-03-en-032"></a>
[**[032]**](#hist-03-la-032) Theoderic, king of Italy, had married King Clovis’s sister, and when he died he left a young daughter with his wife. When the girl had grown up, she acted with foolish levity. Rejecting her mother’s plan to provide her with a king’s son, she took her own servant, named Traguila, and fled with him to a city where she could be defended. Her mother was greatly enraged and begged her no longer to degrade her noble lineage, but to abandon the servant and accept the royal suitor of her own rank whom her mother had chosen. She would not consent. Her enraged mother then called out an army. The soldiers came upon the couple, killed Traguila with the sword, beat the daughter, and brought her back to her mother’s house.

They lived under the Arian sect. It was their custom for the kings, when they came to the altar, to receive from one chalice and the common people from another. The daughter put poison in the chalice from which her mother was to receive communion. As soon as her mother drank from it, she died. There can be no doubt that such an evil deed comes from the devil. What answer will the wretched heretics make to this, that the enemy should have a place in their holy things? We who confess the Trinity in one equality and omnipotence believe that, even if we drink something deadly in the name of the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit, the true and incorruptible God, it will not harm us.

The Italians were indignant at this woman. They invited Theodahad, king of Tuscany, and set him over themselves as king. When he learned what the harlot had done—how she had become her mother’s murderer because of the servant she had taken—he ordered a bath to be heated intensely and had her shut inside it with a single maid. As soon as she entered amid the overpowering vapors, she collapsed on the pavement and died, consumed by the heat.

When her cousins Kings Childebert and Chlothar, as well as Theudebert, learned that she had been killed by so shameful a punishment, they sent an embassy to Theodahad. They reproached him for her death and said: “If you do not make compensation to us for what you have done, we will take away your kingdom and condemn you to a similar punishment.” In fear, he sent them fifty thousand gold pieces. Childebert, always envious and crafty in his dealings with King Chlothar, joined with his nephew Theudebert. They divided the gold between themselves and refused to give any of it to Chlothar. Chlothar then seized Chlodomer’s treasures and took from them much more than the others had cheated him of.

### 32. How Theudebert went into Italy

<a id="hist-03-en-033"></a>
[**[033]**](#hist-03-la-033) Theudebert went into Italy and acquired much territory there. But because those regions are unhealthy, as is reported, his army was afflicted by various fevers and many of his men died there. Seeing this, Theudebert returned from Italy, with himself and his men carrying off great spoils. He is said to have advanced at that time as far as the city of Pavia, to which he again sent Buccelin.

Buccelin captured Lesser Italy and brought it under the power of the aforesaid king, then sought Greater Italy. Fighting there many times against Belisarius, he won the victory. When the emperor saw that Belisarius was repeatedly defeated, he removed him and put Narses in his place. He reduced Belisarius to his former office of count of the stable, as though to humiliate him. Buccelin fought great battles against Narses. He captured all Italy, extended its boundary to the sea, and sent great treasures from Italy to Theudebert.

When Narses reported this to the emperor, the emperor hired foreign peoples for money and sent reinforcements to Narses. Buccelin subsequently joined battle, was defeated, and withdrew. He later occupied Sicily and sent the tribute he exacted from it to the king. He enjoyed great success in these undertakings.

### 33. Of Asteriolus and Secundinus

<a id="hist-03-en-034"></a>
[**[034]**](#hist-03-la-034) Asteriolus and Secundinus were then held in high esteem by the king. Both were intelligent and trained in rhetoric. Secundinus had often been sent by the king on embassies to the emperor; this made him boastful, and he behaved in various unreasonable ways. A bitter quarrel consequently arose between him and Asteriolus. It went so far that they abandoned verbal accusations and beat each other with their own hands.

The king reconciled them, but Secundinus was still swollen with anger over the blows he had received, and strife broke out between them again. The king took Secundinus’s side and handed Asteriolus over to his power. Asteriolus was severely humiliated and deprived of his office, but he was restored through Queen Wisigard. After her death, Secundinus rose against him again and killed him.

Asteriolus left a son at his death. When the son had grown to manhood, he began to seek vengeance for the wrong done to his father. Secundinus, terrified, fled before him from estate to estate. When he saw that the other was closing in and that he could not escape, he killed himself with poison, as is said, rather than fall into his enemy’s hands.

### 34. Of the gift of Theudebert to the citizens of Verdun

<a id="hist-03-en-035"></a>
[**[035]**](#hist-03-la-035) King Theuderic had inflicted many injuries on Desideratus, bishop of Verdun. After many disasters, losses, and sufferings, Desideratus had returned by the Lord’s command to freedom and, as we have said, had recovered his episcopal office in the city of Verdun. Seeing that its inhabitants were extremely poor and destitute, he grieved for them. He himself had been stripped of his property by Theuderic and had nothing of his own with which to help them. Knowing King Theudebert’s goodness and mercy toward everyone, he sent an embassy to him, saying: “The fame of your goodness is spread throughout the whole earth, and your generosity is so great that you give help even to those who do not ask for it. I beg you, if your charity has any money available, lend it to us so that we may relieve our citizens. When their business activities have restored our city to the prosperity enjoyed by others, we will repay your money with lawful interest.”

Moved by compassion, Theudebert lent him seven thousand gold pieces. Desideratus received the money and distributed it among his citizens. They engaged in trade, became rich through it, and are held in high esteem to this day. When the bishop offered to repay the money to the king, the king replied: “I have no need to receive it back. It is enough for me that the poor who were crushed by want have been relieved through your management, at your petition and by my generosity.” He demanded nothing and thus made the citizens wealthy.

### 35. Of the death of Syrivald

<a id="hist-03-en-036"></a>
[**[036]**](#hist-03-la-036) When the bishop just mentioned died in that city, Agiricus, one of the citizens, was appointed to his see. His son Siacrius remembered the wrong done to his father: Sirivald had accused him before King Theuderic, and he had not only been stripped of his property but also subjected to punishments. Siacrius therefore attacked Sirivald with an armed band and killed him in the following manner.

At daybreak, when the fog was dense and the darkness was only just dispersing so that one could scarcely distinguish anything, he came to Sirivald’s estate, named Floriacum, in the territory of Dijon. One of Sirivald’s friends came out of the house. Thinking that he was Sirivald himself, the attackers killed him. As they were withdrawing, believing that they had overcome their enemy, a member of the household told them that they had not killed the master but a subordinate. They returned, searched for Sirivald, and attacked the small chamber in which he usually slept. They fought for a long time at its door but could not prevail against him. Then they broke through the wall on one side, entered, and killed him with the sword. This happened after Theuderic’s death.

### 36. Of the death of Theudebert and of the death of Parthenius

<a id="hist-03-en-037"></a>
[**[037]**](#hist-03-la-037) After these events, King Theudebert fell ill. Physicians devoted great care to him, but nothing availed, because the Lord was already calling him. After a long illness, he grew weaker and gave up his spirit.

The Franks hated Parthenius intensely because he had imposed taxes on them during the reign of the king just mentioned, and they began to pursue him. Seeing himself in danger, he fled from the city. He humbly begged two bishops to escort him to the city of Trier and to calm the fury of the rioting people by their preaching. While they were traveling, he was lying on his bed at night when he suddenly cried out loudly in his sleep: “Alas! Alas! Help me, you who are here; bring aid to a man who is perishing!” Those present were awakened by the cry and asked what was wrong. He answered: “My friend Ausanius and his wife Papianilla, whom I killed long ago, were summoning me to judgment. They said: ‘Come and answer, for you must plead your case with us before the Lord.’” Some years earlier, moved by jealousy, he had killed his innocent wife and his friend.

When the bishops reached Trier, they could not endure the uproar of the rioting people and decided to hide Parthenius in the church. They put him in a chest and spread over him the vestments used in the church. The people entered and searched every corner of the church. Finding nothing, they went out in a rage. Then one man said suspiciously: “There is the chest in which our enemy has not been sought.” The guards said that it contained nothing but church ornaments, but the crowd demanded the key, saying: “Unless you unlock it quickly, we will break it open ourselves.” At last the chest was opened, the linen cloths were removed, and Parthenius was found and dragged out. The people clapped their hands and cried: “God has delivered our enemy into our hands!” They beat him with their fists and spat on him. Then they bound his hands behind his back and stoned him to death against a column.

He was extremely gluttonous. He took aloes to digest his food quickly so that he might be ready to eat again sooner. He also emitted noises from his belly in public without any regard for those who heard him. Such was the end in which he perished.

### 37. Of the severe winter

<a id="hist-03-en-038"></a>
[**[038]**](#hist-03-la-038) That year there was a severe winter, harsher than usual. The streams were so bound by continuous frost that they offered people a passage like the rest of the ground. Because of the deep snow, birds weakened by cold or hunger could be caught by hand without any human snare. From the death of Clovis to the death of Theudebert, thirty-seven years are reckoned. Theudebert died in the fourteenth year of his reign, and his son Theudebald reigned in his place.

## Praefatio

<a id="hist-03-la-001"></a>
[**[001]**](#hist-03-en-001) Vellim, si placet, parumper conferre, quae christianis beatam confitentibus Trinitatem prospera successerint et quae hereticis eandem scindentibus fuerint in ruinam. Omittamus autem, qualiter illam Abraham veneratur ad elicem, Iacob praedicat in benedictionem, Moyses cognuscit in sentem, populus sequitur in nubem eandemque paviscit in montem, vel qualiter eam Aaron portat in logium, aut David vaticinatur in psalmum, orans innovari se per spiritum rectum, nec sibi auferri spiritum sanctum, atque se confirmari per spiritum principalem. Magnum et hic ego cerno mistirium, quod scilicet, quem heretici minorem adserunt, principalem vox prophetica nuntiavit. Sed haec, ut diximus, omissa, ad nostra tempora revertamur. Arrius enim, qui huius iniquae sectae primus iniquosque Inventur fuit, interiora in secessum deposita, infernalibus ignebus subditur, Hilarius vero beatus individuae Trinitatis defensor, propter hanc in exilium deditus, et patriae et paradiso restauratur. Hanc Chlodovechus rex confessus, ipsus hereticos adiuturium eius oppraesset regnumque suum per totas Gallias dilatavit; Alaricus hanc denegans, a regno et populo atque ab ipsa, quod magis est vita multatur aeterna. Dominus autem se vere credentibus, etsi insidiante inimico aliqua perdant, hic centuplicata restituit, heretici vero nec adquerunt melius, sed quod videntur habere, aufertur ab eis. Probavit hoc Godigisili, Gundobadi atque Godomari interitus, qui et patriam simul et animas perdiderunt. Nos vero unum atque invisibilem et inmensum, inconpraehensibilem, inclitum, perennem atque perpetuum Dominum confitemur, unum in Trinitate propter personarum numerum, id est Patris et Filii et Spiritus sancti; confitemur et trinum in unitate propter aequalitatem substantiae, deitatis, omnipotentiae vel virtutis; qui est unus summus atque omnipotens Deus in sempiterna saecula regnans.

## 1. De filiis Chlodovechi.

<a id="hist-03-la-002"></a>
[**[002]**](#hist-03-en-002) Defuncto igitur Chlodovecho regi, quattuor filii eius, id est Theudoricus, Chlodomeris, Childeberthus atque Chlothacharius, regnum eius accipiunt et inter se aequa lantia dividunt. Habebat iam tunc Theudoricus filium nomen Theudobertum, elegantem atque utilem. Cumque magna virtute pollerent et eis de exercitu rubor cupiosus inesset, Amalaricus, filius Alarici, rex Hispaniae sororem eorum in matrimonio postolat, quod ille clementer indulgent et eam ipse in regionem Hispaniae cum magnorum ornamentorum mole transmittunt.

## 2. De episcopatu Dinifi, Apollonaris atque Quinciani.

<a id="hist-03-la-003"></a>
[**[003]**](#hist-03-en-003) Licinio autem urbis Toronicae defuncto episcopo, Dinifius cathedram pontificalem ascendit. Apud Arvernus vero post obitum beati Aprunculi sanctus Eufrasius duodecimus episcopus habebatur. Hic quattuor annos post Chlodovechi obitum vixit, vicissimo quinto episcopatus sui anno transiens. Cumque populus sanctum Quintianum, qui de Rutino eiectus fuerat, eligisset, Alchima et Placidina, uxor sororque Apollonaris, ad sanctum Quintianum venientes, dicunt: 'Sufficiat, domne sancte, senectute tuae, quod es episcopus ordenatus. Permittat', inquiunt, 'pietas tua servo tuo Apollonari locum huius honoris adipisci. Ille vero, cum ad hunc apicem ascenderet, sicut tibi placitum fuerit obsequitur; tu quoque imperabis, et ille tuae parebit in omnibus iussioni, tantum ut humili suggestioni nostrae aurem benignitatis acommodis'. Quibus ille: 'Quid ego', inquid, 'praestabo, cuius potestati nihil est subditum? Sufficit enim, ut oratione vacans cottidianum mihi victum praestet eclesia'. Ille autem haec audientes, Apollonarem ad regem dirigunt. Qui abiens, oblatis multis muneribus, in episcopatu successit; quod quattuor abutens minsebus, migravit a saeculo. Cum autem haec Theudorico nuntiatum fuisset, iussit inibi sanctum Quintianum constitui et omnem ei potestatem tradi eclesiae, dicens: 'Hic ob nostri amoris zelo ab urbe sua eiectus est'. Et statim directi nuntii, convocatis pontificibus et populo, eum in cathedra Arverne eclesiae locaverunt; qui quartus decimus eclesiae illi praepositus est. Reliqua vero quae gessit, tam virtutes quam tempus migrationes eius, scripta sunt in libro, quem de eius vita conposuimus.

## 3. Quod Dani Gallias appetierunt.

<a id="hist-03-la-004"></a>
[**[004]**](#hist-03-en-004) His ita gestis, Dani cum rege suo nomen Chlochilaichum evectu navale per mare Gallias appetunt. Egressique ad terras, pagum unum de regno Theudorici devastant atque captivant, oneratisque navibus tam de captivis quam de reliquis spoliis, reverti ad patriam cupiunt; sed rex eorum in litus resedebat, donec navis alto mare conpraehenderent, ipse deinceps secuturus. Quod cum Theudorico nuntiatum fuisset, quod scilicet regio eius fuerit ab extraneis devastata, Theudobertum, filium suum, in illis partibus cum valido exercitu ac magno armorum apparatu direxit. Qui, interfectu rege, hostibus navali proelio superatis oppraemit omnemque rapinam terrae restituit.

## 4. De Thoringorum regibus.

<a id="hist-03-la-005"></a>
[**[005]**](#hist-03-en-005) Porro tunc apud Thoringus tres fratres regnum gentis illius retinebant, id est Badericus, Hermenefredus atque Berthacharius. Denique Hermenefredus Berthacharium, fratrem suum, vi oppraemens, interfecit. Is moriens, Radegundem filiam orfanam dereliquid; reliquid enim et alius filius, de quibus in sequente scribemus. Hermenefrede vero uxor iniqua atque crudelis Amalaberga nomen inter hos fratres bellum civile dissimenat. Nam veniens quadam die ad convivium vir eius, mensam mediam opertam repperit. Cumque uxori, quid sibi hoc vellit, interrogaret, respondit: 'Qui', inquid, 'a medio regno spoliatur, decet eum mensae medium habere nudatum'. Talibus et his similibus ille permotus, contra fratrem insurgit ac per occultus nuntius Theudoricum regem ad eum persequendum invitat, dicens: 'Si hunc interfecis, regionem hanc pari sorte dividimus'. Ille autem gavisus, haec audiens, cum exercitu ad eum dirigit. Coniunctique simul fidem sibi invicem dantis, egressi sunt ad bellum. Confligentisque cum Baderico, exercitum eius adterunt ipsumque obtruncant gladio, et obtenta victuria, Theudoricus ad propria est reversus. Protenus Hermenefredus oblitus fidei suae, quod regi Theudorico indulgere pollicitus est, implere dispexit, ortaque est inter eos grandis inimicitia.

## 5. Quod Sigimundus filium suum interfecit.

<a id="hist-03-la-006"></a>
[**[006]**](#hist-03-en-006) Igitur mortuo Gundobado, regnum eius Sigimundus filius obtenuit, monastiriumque Acaunensim sollerti cura cum domibus basilicisque aedificavit; qui, perditam priorem coniugem, filiam Theudorici regis Italici, de qua filium habebat nomen Sigiricum, aliam duxit uxorem, quae valide contra filium eius, sicut novercarum mos est, malignari ac scandalizare coepit. Unde factum est, ut una solemnitatum die, cum puer super eam vestimenta matris agnusceret, commotus felle diceret ad eam: 'Non enim eras digna, ut haec indumenta tua terga contegerent, quae dominae tuae, id est matre meae, fuisse nuscuntur'. At illa furore succensa, instigat verbis dolosis virum suum, dicens: 'Hic iniquos regnum tuum possedere desiderat, teque interfecto, eum usque Italiam dilatare disponit, scilicet ut regnum, quod avus eius Theudoricus Italiae tenuit, et iste possedeat. Scit enim, quod te vivente haec non potest adimplere, et nisi tu cadas, ille non surgat'. His et huiuscemodi ille incitatus verbis, uxoris iniquae consilium utens, iniquus extitit parricida. Nam sopitum vino dormire post meridiem filium iubet; cui dormiente orarium sub collo positum ac sub mento legatum, trahentibus ad se invicem duobus pueris, suggillatus est. Quo facto pater sero iam paenetens, super cadaver exanime ruens, flere coepit amarissimae. Ad quem senex quidam sic dixisse fertur: 'Te', inquid, 'plangeamodo, qui per consilium nequam factus es parricida saevissimus; nam hunc, qui innocens iugulatus est, necessarium non est plangi'. Nihilominus ille ad sanctus Acaunenses abiens, per multus dies in fletu et ieiuniis durans, veniam praecabatur. Psallentium ibi assiduum instituens, Lugduno regressus est, ultione divina de vestigio prosequente. Huius filiam rex Theudoricus accepit.

## 6. De interitu Chlodomeris.

<a id="hist-03-la-007"></a>
[**[007]**](#hist-03-en-007) Chrodechildis vero regina Chlodomerem vel reliquos filius suos adloquitur, dicens: 'Non me paeneteat, carissimi, vos dulciter enutrisse; indignate, quaeso, iniuriam meam et patris matrisque meae mortem sagaci studio vindecate'. Haec illi audientes, Burgundias petunt et contra Sigimundum vel fratrem eius Godomarum dirigunt. Devictumque exercitum eorum, Godomarus terga vertit. Sigimundus vero, dum ad Sanctos Acaunos fugire nititur, a Chlodomere captus cum uxore et filiis captivus abducitur atque, infra terminum Aurilianensim urbis in costodia positus, detenetur. Discedentibusque his regibus, Godomarus, resumptis viribus, Burgundionis colligit regnumque recipit. Contra quem Chlodomeris iterum ire disponens, Sigimundum interficere distinat. Cui a beato Avito abbate, magno tunc tempore sacerdote, dictum est: 'Si, inquid, 'respiciens Deum, emendaveris consilium tuum, ut hos homines interfici non patiaris, erit Deus tecum, et abiens victuriam obtenibis; si vero eos occideris, tu ipse in manibus inimicorum traditus, simili sorte peribis; fietque tibi uxorique et filiis tuis, quod feceris Sigimundo et coniugi ac liberis eius'. Quod ille abscultare dispiciens consilium eius, ait: 'Stultum consilium esse puto, ut, inimicos domui relictus, contra reliquos eam, eosque a tergo, hunc a fronte surgente, inter duos hostium cuneos ruam. Satius enim et facilius victuria patrabitur, si unus ab alio separetur; quo interfecto, facile et alius morte poterit distinare'. Statimque interfecto Sigimundo cum uxore et filiis, apud Colomnam Aurilianinsim urbis vicum in puteum iactare praecipiens, Burgundias petiit, vocans in solatium Theudoricum regem. Ille autem iniuriam soceri sui vindecare nolens, ire promisit. Cumque pariter apud Visorontiam locum urbis Viennensis coniuncti fuissent, cum Godomaro confligunt. Cumque Godomarus cum exercitu terga vertisset et Chlodomeris insequeretur ac de suis non modico spatio elongatus esset, adsimilantes illi signum eius, dant ad eum voces, dicentes: 'Huc, huc convertere! Tui enim sumus'. At ille credens, abiit inruitque in medio inimicorum. Cuius amputatum caput et conto defixum elevant in sublimi. Quod Franci cernentes atque cognuscentes Chlodomerem interfectum, reparatis viribus, Godomarum fugant, Burgundionis oppraemunt patriamque in suam redigunt potestatem. Nec moratus Chlothacharius uxorem germani sui Guntheucam nomine sibi in matrimonio scciavit. Filios quoque eius Chrodichildis regina, exactis diebus luctus, secum recipit ac tenuit; quorum unus Theudoaldus, alter Guntharius, tertius Chlodovaldus vocabatur. Godomarus iterum regnum recepit.

## 7. Bellum contra Thoringus.

<a id="hist-03-la-008"></a>
[**[008]**](#hist-03-en-008) Post Theudoricus non inmemor periurias Heimenefrede regis Thoringorum Chlothacharium fratrem suum in solatio suo vocat et adversum eum ire disponit, promittens regi Chlothachario partem praedae, si eisdem munus victuriae divinitus conferritur. Convocatis igitur Francis, dicit ad eos: 'Indignamini, quaeso, tam meam iniuriam quam interitum parentum vestrorum, ac recolite, Thoringus quondam super parentes nostros violenter advenisse ac multa illis intulisse mala. Qui, datis obsidibus, pacem cum his inire voluerunt, sed ille obsedes ipsus diversis mortibus peremerunt et inruentes super parentes nostros, omnem substantiam abstullerunt, pueros per nervos femorum ad arbores appendentes, puellas amplius ducentas crudeli nece interfecerunt, ita ut, legatis brachiis super equorum cervicibus, ipsique acerrimo moti stimulo per diversa petentes, diversis in partebus feminas diviserunt. Aliis vero super urbitas viarum extensis, sudibusque in terra confixis, plaustra desuper onerata transire fecerunt, confractisque ossibus, canibus avibusque eas in cibaria dederunt. Nunc autem Herminefredus quod mihi pollicitus est fefellit et omnino haec adimplire dissimulat. Ecce! verbum directum habemus: Eamus cum Dei adiuturio contra eos!' Quod ille audientes et de tanto scelere indignantes, uno animo eademque sententiam Thoringiam petierunt. Theudoricus autem, Chlothacharium fratrem et Theudobertum filium in solatio suo adsumptos, cum exercito abiit. Thoringi vero venientibus Francis dolos praeparant. In campum enim, quo certamen agi debebant, fossas effodiunt, quarum ora operta denso cispete planum adsimilant campum. In his ergo foveis, cum pugnare coepissent, multi Francorum equites conruerunt, et fuit eis valde inpedimentum; sed post cognitum hunc dolum, observare coeperunt. Denique cum se Thoringi caedi vehementer viderent, fugato Hermenefredo rege ipsorum, terga vertunt et ad Onestrudem fluvium usque perveniunt. Ibique tanta caedes ex Thoringis facta est, ut alveos fluminis a cadaverum congeriae repleretur et Franci tamquam per pontem aliquod super eos in litus ulteriore transirent. Patratam ergo victuriam, regionem illam capessunt et in suam redigunt potestatem. Chlothacharius vero rediens, Radegundem, filiam Bertecharii regis, secum captivam abduxit sibique eam in matrimonio sociavit; cuius fratrem postea iniuste per homines iniquos occidit. Illa quoque ad Deum conversa, mutata veste, monastyrium sibi intra Pectavensem urbem construxit. Quae orationibus, ieiuniis atque elymosinis praedita, in tantum emicuit, ut magna in populis haberetur. Cum autem adhuc supradicti regis in Thoringiam essent, Theudoricus Chlothacharium, fratrem suum, occidere voluit, et praeparatis occulte cum arma viris, eum ad se vocat, quasi secricius cum eo aliquid tractaturus, expansumque in parte domus illius tenturium de uno pariete in altero, armatus post eum stare iubet. Cumque tenturium illud esset brevior, pedes armatorum apparuere detecti. Quod cognoscens Chlothacharius, cum suis armatus ingressus est domum. Theudoricus vero intellegens, hunc haec cognovisse, fabulam fingit et alia ex aliis loquitur. Denique nesciens, qualiter dolum suum deleniret, discum ei magnum argenteum pro gratia dedit. Chlothacharius autem vale dicens et pro munere gratias agens, ad metatum regressus est. Theudoricus vero quaeritur ad suos, nullam extantem causam suum perdedisse catinum, et ad filium suum Theudobertum ait: 'Vade ad patruum tuum et roga, ut munus, quod ei dedi, tibi sua voluntate concedat'. Qui abiens, quod petiit inpetravit. In talibus enim dolis Theudoricus multum callidus erat.

## 8. De interitu Hermenifredi.

<a id="hist-03-la-009"></a>
[**[009]**](#hist-03-en-009) Idem vero regressus ad propria, Hermenefredum ad se data fidem securum praecipit venire, quem et honorificis ditavit muneribus. Factum est autem, dum quadam die per murum civitatis Tulbiacensis confabularentur, a nescio quo inpulsus, de altitudine muri ad terram corruit ibique spiritum exalavit. Sed qui eum exinde deiecerit, ignoramus; multi tamen adserunt, Theudorici in hoc dolum manifestissime patuisse.

## 9. Quod Childeberthus Arvernus abiit.

<a id="hist-03-la-010"></a>
[**[010]**](#hist-03-en-010) Cum autem adhuc Theudoricus in Thoringiam esset, Arvernus sonuit, eum interfectum fuisse. Archadius quoque, unus ex senatoribus Arvernis, Childeberthum invitat, ut regionem illam deberet accipere. Ille quoque nec moratus Arvernus vadit. Tantaque in illa die condensa fuit nebula, ut nihil super duabus iugeri partibus discerni possit. Dicere enim erat solitus rex: 'Vellim umquam Arvernam Lemanem, quae tantae iocunditatis gratia refulgere dicitur, oculis cernere'. Sed non ei a Deo concessum est. Cumque portae civitatis obseratae essent, et unde ingrederetur pervium patulum non haberet, incisam Archadius serram unius portae eum civitati intromisit. Dum haec agerentur, nuntiatur, Theudoricum vivum de Thoringiam fuisse regressum.

## 10. De interitu Amalarici.

<a id="hist-03-la-011"></a>
[**[011]**](#hist-03-en-011) Quod certissime Childeberthus cognoscens, ab Arverno rediit et Hispaniam propter sororem suam Chlotchildem dirigit. Haec vero multas insidias ab Amalarico viro suo propter fidem catholicam patiebatur. Nam plerumque procedente illa ad sanctam eclesiam, stercora et diversos fetores super eam proieci imperabat, ad extremum autem tanta eam crudilitate dicitur caecidisse, ut infectum de proprio sanguine sudarium fratri transmitteret, unde ille maxime commotus, Hispanias appetivit. Amalaricus vero haec audiens, naves ad fugiendum parat. Porro inminente Childebertho, cum Amalaricus navem deberet ascendere, ei in mentem venit, multitudinem se praetiosorum lapidum in suo thesauro reliquisse. Cumque ad eosdem petendus in civitatem regrederetur, ab exercitu a porto exclusus est. Videns autem, se non posse evadere, ad eclesiam christianorum confugire coepit. Sed priusquam limina sancta contingerit, unus emissam manum lanciam eum mortali ictu sauciavit, ibique decidens reddedit spiritum. Tunc Childeberthus cum magnis thesauris sororem adsumptam secum adducere cupiebat, quae, nescio quo casu, in via mortua est, et postea Parisius adlata, iuxta patrem suum Chlodovechum sepulta est. Childeberthus vero inter reliquos thesauros ministeria eclesiarum praetiosissima detulit. Nam sexaginta calices, quindecim patenas, viginti euangeliorum capsas detulit, omnia ex auro puro ac gemmis praetiosis ornatas. Sed hon est passus ea confringi. Cuncta enim eclesiis et basilicis sanctorum dispensavit ac tradidit.

## 11. Quod Childeberthus Burgundias abiit.

<a id="hist-03-la-012"></a>
[**[012]**](#hist-03-en-012) Post haec Chlothacharius et Childeberthus Burgundia petere distinant. Convocatusque Theudoricus in solatio eorum, ire noluit. Franci vero, qui ad eum aspiciebant, dixerunt: 'Si cum fratribus tuis in Burgundiam ire dispexeris, te relinquimus et illos sequi satius praeoptamus'. At ille infidelis sibi exhistimans Arvernus, ait: 'Me sequimini, et ego vos inducam in patriam, ubi aurum et argentum accipiatis, quantum vestra potest desiderare cupiditas, de qua pecora, de qua mancipia, de qua vestimenta in abundantiam adsumatis. Tantum hos ne sequamini!' His promissionibus hi inlecti suam voluntatem facere repromittunt. Ille vero illuc transire disponit, promittens iterum atque iterum exercitu cuncta regionis praedam cum hominibus in suis regionibus transferre permittere. Chlothacharius vero et Childeberthus in Burgundiam dirigunt, Agustidunumque obsedentes, cunctam, fugato Godomaro, Burgundiam occupaverunt.

## 12. De excidio regionibus Arvernae.

<a id="hist-03-la-013"></a>
[**[013]**](#hist-03-en-013) Theudoricus vero cum exercitu Arverno veniens, totam regionem devastat ac proterit. Interea Archadius sceleris illius auctor, cuius ignavia regio devastata est, Bituricas urbem petiit. Erat autem tunc temporis urbs illa in regno Childeberthi regis. Placidina vero, mater eius, et Alchima, soror patris eius, conpraehensae apud Cadurcum urbem, rebus ablatis, exsilio condemnatae sunt. Rex igitur Theudoricus ad urbem Arvernam usque accedens, in vici illius suburbana castra fixit. Beatus vero Quintianus his diebus erat episcopus. Interea exercitus cunctam circuit miseram regionem illam, cuncta delet, universa debellat. De quibus nonnulli ad basilicam sancti Iuliani perveniunt, confringunt ostia, seras removent resque pauperum, quae ibidem fuerant adgregatae, diripiunt et multa in hoc loco perpetrant mala. Verumtamen auctores scelerum ab spiritu inmundo correpti, infestis dentibus propriis se morsibus lacerant, clamantes atque dicentes: 'Cur nos, martyr sanctae, sic crucias?' – sicut in libro virtutum eius conscripsimus.

## 13. De Lovolautro et Meroliacensae castro.

<a id="hist-03-la-014"></a>
[**[014]**](#hist-03-en-014) Lovolautrum autem castro hostis expugnant Proculumque presbiterum, qui quondam sanctum Quintiano iniuriam intulerat, ad altarium eclesiae miserabiliter interficiunt. Et credo, ob illius causa fuerit ipsum castrum in manibus traditum iniquorum, quid usque illa die defensatum est. Nam cum eum hostes expugnare non possent, ad propria iam redire disponerent, audientes haec obsessi, iam laeti atque securi decipiuntur, sicut ait apostolus: Cum dixerint: 'Pax et securitas', tunc repentinus superveniet interitus. Denique per ipsius Proculi presbiteri servum iam securi populi traduntur in manus hostium. Cumque vastato castello ducerentur captivi, inmanis pluvia, quae per triginta dies fuerat abnegata, discendit. Tunc obsessi Meroliacensis castri, ne captivi abducerentur, redemptione data, liberantur. Sed haec ignavia eorum effecit; caster enim propria natura monitus erat. Nam centenum aut eo amplius pedum ab exciso vallatur lapide, sine murorum structione, in medio autem ingens stagnum aquae liquore gratissimum, ab alia vero parte fontes uberrimi, ita ut per portam rivus defluat aquae vivae. Sed in tam grande spatio munitio ista distenditur, ut manentes infra murorum septa terram excolant frugesque in habundantiam collegant. Huius munitionis tutamine elati qui obsessi erant, egressi foras, ut, arreptum aliquid praedae, iterum se intra castelli septa reconderent, ab hostibus conpraehensi sunt. Erant autem quinquaginta viri. Tunc ante ora parentum, vinctis postergum manibus, oblati, inminente iam gladio, adquiverunt obsessi, ne hi interfecerentur, singulos treantes dare in redemptionem suam. Theudoricus autem ab Arverno discendens, Sigivaldum, parentem suum, in ea quasi pro custodia dereliquid. Erat ibi tunc temporis quidam Lytigius ex minoribus, qui magnas sancto Quintiano parabat insidias; et cum se sanctos episcopus pedibus eius prosterneret, numquam, ut se ei subderet, movebatur, ita ut quadam vice uxori quae sanctos ficerat pro ridiculo indicaret. Quae melioris intellegentiae modo commota, ait: 'Si ita est hodie pessumdatus, numquam eregeris'. Die autem tertia advenientes nuntii de praesentia regis, vinctum cum uxore ac liberis paritei abduxerunt. Qui abiens, numquam Arverno regressus est.

## 14. De interitu Munderici.

<a id="hist-03-la-015"></a>
[**[015]**](#hist-03-en-015) Mundericusigitur, qui se parentem regium adserebat, [multa] elatus superbia, ait: 'Quid mihi et Theudorico regi? Sic enim mihi solium regni debetur, ut ille. Egrediar et collegam populum meum atque exegam sacramentum ab eis, ut sciat Theudoricus, quia rex sum ego, sicut et ille'. Et egressus coepit seducere populum, dicens: 'Princeps ego sum. Sequimini me, et erit vobis bene'. Sequebatur autem eum rustica multitudo, ut plerumque fragilitati humanae convenit, dantes sacramentum fidelitatis et honorantes eum ut regem. Quod cum Theudoricus conperisset, mandatum mittit ad eum, dicens: 'Accede ad me, et si tibi aliqua de dominatione regni nostri portio debetur, accipe'. Dolosae enim haec Theudoricus dicebat, scilicet ut, cum ad eum venissit, interficeretur. Ille vero noluit, dicens: 'Ite; renuntiate rege vestro, quia rex sum sicut et ille'. Tunc rex commovere iussit exercitum, quo oppressus vi puneretur. Quod ille cognuscens et se non praevalens defensare, Victuriaci castri murus expetens cum rebus omnibus, in eo se studuet commonere, his secum quos seduxerat adgregatis. Igitur commotus exercitus castrum vallat ac per septem dies obsedit. Mundericus autem repugnabat cum suis, dicens: 'Stemus fortes et usque ad mortem pariter demicemus et non subdamur inimicis'. Cumque exercitus a circuitu incontra iacula transmitteret nec aliquid praevaleret, nuntiaverunt haec regi. At ille misit quendam de suis Aregisilum nomine dixitque ei: 'Vidis', inquid, 'quod praevaleat hic perfedus in contumacia sua; vade et redde ei sacramentum, ut securus egrediatur. Cum autem egressus fuerit, interfice eum et dele memoriam eius a regno nostro'. Qui abiens, fecit iuxta quod ei praeceptum fuerat. Dederat tamen prius signum populo, dicens: 'Cum ego haec et haec locutus fuero, statim inruentes interficite eum'. Ingressus autem Aregisilus, ait Munderico: 'Quousque hic resedis tamquam unus ex insipientibus? Numquid poteris diu regi resistere? Ecce ablatum tibi cibum! Cum te famis oppraesserit, ultro egredieris et traderis in manus inimicorum et morieris quasi unus ex canibus. Audi potius consilium meum et subde te regi, ut vivere possis tu et filitui'. Tunc ille his mollitus sermonibus, ait: 'Si egredior, conpraehensus a regi interficior et ego et fili mei vel omnes amici, qui mecum sunt adgregati'. Cui Aregisilus ait: 'Noli timere, sed, si vis egredi, accipe sacramentum de hac culpa et sta securus coram regi. Ne timeas, sed eris cum eum, sicut prius fuisti'. Ad haec Mundericus respondit: 'Utinam securus sim, quod non interficiar!' Tunc Aregisilus, positis super altarium sanctum manibus, iuravit ei, ut securus egrederetur. Data igitur sacramenta, egredibatur Mundericus de porta castelli, tenens manum Aregisili, populus autem spectabat a longe aspiciens eum. Tunc pro signo ait Aregisilus: 'Quid aspicitis tam intenti, o populi? An numquid non vidistis prius Mundericum?' Et statim inruit populus in eum. At ille intellegens, ait: 'Evidentissime cognusco, quod feceris per hoc verbum signum populis ad me interficiendum; verumtamen dico tibi, quia periuriis me decipisti, te vivum ultra nullus aspiciet'. Et emissa lancea in scapulis eius, perfodit eum, ceciditque et mortuus est. Evaginatumque deinceps Mundericus gladium, cum suis magnam stragem de populo illo fecit, et usquequo spiritum exalavit, interficere quemcumque adsequi potuisset non distitit. Quo interfecto, res eius fisco conlatae sunt.

## 15. De captivitate Attali.

<a id="hist-03-la-016"></a>
[**[016]**](#hist-03-en-016) Theudoricus vero et Childiberthus foedus inierunt, et dato sibi sacramento, ut nullus contra alium moveretur, obsedes ab invicem acciperunt, quo facilius firmarentur, quae fuerant dicta. Multi tunc fili senatorum in hac obsidione dati sunt, sed orto iterum inter reges scandalum, ad servicium publicum sunt addicti; et quicumque eos ad costodiendum accepit, servus sibi ex his fecit. Multi tamen ex eis per fugam lapsi, in patriam redierunt, nonnulli in servitio sunt retenti. Inter quos Attalus, nepus beati Gregori Lingonici episcopi, ad publicum servitium mancipatus est custusque equorum distinatus. Erat enim intra Treverici termini territurio cuidam barbaro serviens. Denique beatus Gregorius ad inquirendum eum pueros distinavit, qui inventum, obtulerunt homini munera, sed respuit ea, dicens: 'Hic tali generatione decem auri libras redimi debet'. Quibus redeuntibus, Leo quidam ex cocina domini sui ait: 'Utinam me permitteris, et forsitan ego poteram eum reducere de captivitate'. Gaviso autem domino, directus venit ad locum voluitque puerum clam abstrahere, sed non potuit. Tunc locatum secum hominem quendam, ait: 'Veni mecum et venunda me in domo barbari illius, sitque tibi lucrum praetium meum, tantum liberiorem aditum habeam faciendi id quod decrevi'. Accepta vero sacramenta, homo ille abiit, et vinditum duodecim aureis, discessit. Sciscitatus autem emptor rudi famulo, quid opere sciret, respondit: 'In omnibus, quae mandi debent in mensis dominorum, valde scitus sum operari, nec metuo, quod repperire possit similis mei in hac scientia. Verum enim dico tibi, quia, etiam si regi epulum cupias praeparare, fercula regalia conponere possum, ne quisquam a me melius'. Et ille: 'Ecce enim dies solis adest' – sic enim barbaries vocitare diem dominecum consueta est –, 'in hac die vicini atque parentes mei invitabuntur in domo mea. Rogo, ut facias mihi prandium, quod admirentur, et dicant, quia in domo regis melius non aspeximus'. Et ille: 'Iubeat', inquid, 'dominus meus congregari pullorum gallinatiorum multitudinem, et faciam quae praecipis'. Praeparatis ergo quae dixerat puer, inluxit dominica dies, fecitque aepulum magnum diliciisque refertum. Aepulantibus autem omnibus et laudantibus prandium, parentes illius discesserunt. Dominus enim dedit gratiam puero huic, et accepit potestatem super omnia quae habebat dominus suus in prumptu, diligebatque eum valde, et omnibus qui cum eo erant ipse dispensabat cibaria et pulmenta. Post anni vero curriculum, cum iam securus esset dominus illius de eo, abiit in pratum, qui erat domi proximus, cum Attalo puero, custode equorum; et decubans in terram cum eo a longe, aversis dorsis, ut non cognuscerentur, quod loquerentur simul, dicit puero: 'Tempus est enim, ut iam cogitare de patria debeamus. Ideoque moneo te, ut hac nocte, cum equos ad claudendum adduxeris, sopore non depraemaris, sed, cum primum te vocitavero, adsis et ambulemus. Vocaverat enim barbarus ille multos parentum suorum ad aepulum, inter quos erat et gener eius, qui acceperat filiam illius. Media autem nocte a convivio surgentibus et quieti datis, prosecutus est Leo generum domini sui cum potu, porregensque ei bibere, in metatum eius. Ait ad eum homo: 'Die tu, o creditor soceri mei, sic valeas, quando enim voluntatem adhibibis, ut, adsumptis equitibus eius, eas in patriam tuam?' Hoc quasi ioco delectans dixit. Similiter et ille ioculariter respondens viritatem, ait: 'Hac nocte delibero, si Dei voluntas fuerit'. Et ille: 'Utinam', inquid, 'costodiant me famuli mei, ne aliquid de rebus meis adsumas!' Et ridentes discesserunt. Dormientibus autem cunctis, vocavit Leo Attalum, stratisque equitibus, interrogat, si haberet gladium. Respondit: 'Non est mihi nisi tantum lancea parvula'. At ille ingressus mansionem domini sui, adpraehendit scutum eius ac frameam. Quo interrogante, quis esset aut quid sibi vellit, respondit: 'Ego sum Leo servus tuus, et suscito Attalo, ut surgat velocius et deducat equos ad pastum; detenitur enim sopore quasi ebrius'. Qui ait: 'Fac ut libet. Et haec dicens, obdormivit. Ille vero egressus foris, monivit puerum arma, invenitque ianuas atrii divinitus reseratas, quas in initio noctis cum cuneis malleo percussis obseraverat pro custodia caballorum; et gratias agens Deo, sumptis reliquis equitibus secum, discesserunt, unum etiam volucrum cum vestimentis tollentes. Venientes autem ad Musellam fluvium, ut transirent, cum detenerentur a quibusdam, relictis equitibus et vestimentis, enatantes super parma positi amnem, in ulteriorem egressi sunt ripam, et inter obscura noctis ingressi silvas, latuerunt. Tertia enim nox advenerat, quod nullum cibum gustantes iter terebant. Tunc nutu Dei repertam arborem plenam pomis, qua vulgo pruna vocant, comedunt, et parumper sustentati, ingressi sunt iter Campaniae. Quibus pergentibus, audiunt pedibulum equitum currentium dixeruntque: 'Prosternamus terrae, ne appareamus hominibus venientibus'. Et ecce! ex inproviso sterps rubi magnus adfuit, post quem transeuntes proiecerunt se terrae cum evaginatis gladiis, scilicet ut, si adverterentur, confestim se quasi ab inprobis framea defensarent. Verumtamen cum venissent in loco illo, coram sterpe spineo restiterunt; dixitque unus, dum equi urinam proiecerent: 'Vae mihi, quia fugiunt hi detestabiles nec repperiri possunt; verum dico per salutem meam, quia, si invenirentur, unum patibulum condemnari et alium gladiorum ictibus in frustra discerpi iubebam'. Erat enim barbarus ille, qui haec agebat, dominus eorum, de Remense urbe veniens, hos inquirens, et repperisset utique in via, si nox obstaculum non praebuisset. Tunc, motis equitibus, discesserunt. Hi autem nocte ipsa adtigerunt ad urbem, ingressique invenerunt hominem, quem sciscitati, ubinam esset domus Paulelli presbiteri, indicavit eis. Qui dum per plateam praeterirent, signum ad matutinus motum est – erat enim dies dominica –, pulsantesque ianuam presbiteri, ingressi sunt, exposuitque puer de domino suo. Cui ait presbiter: 'Vera est enim visio mea. Nam videbam duas in hac nocte columbas advolare et consedere in manu mea, ex quibus una alba, alia autem nigra erat'. Dixitque puer presbitero: 'Indulgeat Dominus pro die sua sancta. Nam nos rogamus, ut aliquid victu praebeas; quarta enim inluciscit dies, quod nihil panis pulmentique gustavimus'. Occultatis autem pueris, praebuit eis infusum cum vino et panem et abiit ad matutinus. Secutusque est et barbarus, iterum inquirens puerus; sed inlusus a presbitero, regressus est. Presbiter enim amicitiam cum beato Gregorio antiquam habebat. Tunc resumptis pueri epulo viribus, per duos dies in domo presbiteri conmorantes, abscesserunt, et sic usque ad sanctum Gregorium perlati sunt. Gavisus autem pontifex visis pueris, flevit super collum Attali, nepotis sui; Leonem autem a iugo servitutis absolvens cum omni generatione sua, dedit ei terram propriam, in qua cum uxore ac liberis liber vixit omnibus diebus vitae suae.

## 16. De Sigivaldo.

<a id="hist-03-la-017"></a>
[**[017]**](#hist-03-en-017) Sigivaldus autem cum in Arverno habitaret, multa mala in ea faciebat. Nam et res diversorum pervadebat, et servi eius non desistebant a furtis, homicidiis ac superventis diversisque sceleribus, nec ullus muttiri ausus erat coram eis. Unde factum est, ut ipse villam Bulgiatensim, quam quondam benedictus Tetradius episcopus basilicae sancti Iuliani reliquerat, temerario auso pervaderet. Sed cum ingressus in domo illa fuisset, statim amens effectus, lecto decubuit. Tunc mulier admonita per sacerdotem, elevatum in basterna ut in aliam villam transtulit, sanum recipit. Et accedens, exposuit ei omnia, quae pertulerat. Quod ille audiens, vota beato martiri vovens, quae vi abstulerat duplicata restituit. Meminimus et huius virtutis in libro Miraculorum sancti Iuliani.

## 17. De episcopis Turonicis.

<a id="hist-03-la-018"></a>
[**[018]**](#hist-03-en-018) Igitur Dinifio episcopo apud Toronus dicedente, Ommatius tribus annis praefuit. Hic enim ex iusso Chlodomeris regis, cui supra meminimus, ordinatus est. Illo quoque migrante, Leo septem mensibus ministravit. Hic fuit vir strinuus atque utilis in fabrica operis lignarii. Quo defuncto, Theodorus et Proculus episcopi, qui de partibus Burgundiae advenerant, ordinante Chrodigilde regine, tribus annis Toronicam rexerunt eclesiam. Quibus defunctis, Francilio ex senatoribus substituitur. Anno igitur tertio episcopatus sui, cum dominici natalis nox alma populis effulsisset, idem pontifex, priusquam ad vigilias discenderet, iussit sibi poculum ministrari. Adveniens autem puer, sine mora porrexit. Quo hausto, mox spiritum fudit. Unde indubitatum est, veneno eum fuisse negatum. Quo decedente, Iniuriosus unus e civibus quintus decimus post beatum Martinum cathedram pontificalem sortitus est.

## 18. De interitu filiorum Chlodemeris.

<a id="hist-03-la-019"></a>
[**[019]**](#hist-03-en-019) Dum autem Chrodigildis regina Parisius moraretur, videns Childeberthus, quod mater sua filius Chlodomeris, quos supra memoravimus, unico affectu diligeret, invidia ductus ac metuens, ne favente regine admitterentur in regno, misit clam ad fratrem suum Chlothacharium regem, dicens: 'Mater nostra filius fratris nostri secum retinet et vult eos regno donari; debes velociter adesse Parisius, et habito communi consilio, pertractare oportet, quid de his fieri debeat, utrum incisa caesariae ut reliqua plebs habeantur, an certe his interfectis regnum germani nostri inter nosmet ipsus aequalitate habita dividatur'. De quibus ille verbis valde gavisus, Parisius venit. Iactaverat enim Childebertus verbum in populo, ob hoc hos coniungi regis, quasi parvolus illos elevaturus in regno. Coniuncti autem miserunt ad reginam, quae tunc in ipsa urbe morabatur, dicentes: 'Dirige parvolus ad nos, ut sublimentur in regno'. Ad illa gavisa, nesciens dolum illorum, dato pueris esu putuque, direxit eos, dicens: 'Non me puto amisisse filium, si vos videam in eius regno substitui'. Qui abeuntes, adpraehensi sunt statim, ac separati a pueris et nutritoribus suis, costodiebantur utrique, seursum pueri et seursum hi parvoli. Tunc Childeberthus atque Chlothacharius miserunt Archadium, cui supra meminimus, ad reginam cum forcipe evaginatoque gladio. Qui veniens, ostendit reginae utraque, dicens: 'Voluntatem tuam, o gloriosissima regina, fili tui domini nostri expetunt, quid de pueris agendum censeas, utrum incisis crinibus eos vivere iubeas, an utrumque iugulare'. At illa exterrita nuntio et nimium felle commota, praecipue cum gladium cerneret evaginatum ac forcipem, amaritudinem praeventa, ignorans in ipso dolore quid diceret, ait simpliciter: 'Satius mihi enim est, si ad regnum non ereguntur, mortuos eos videre quam tonsus'. At ille parum admirans dolorem eius, nec scrutans, quid deinceps plenius pertractaret, venit celeriter, nuntians ac dicens: 'Favente regina opus coeptum perficite; ipsa enim vult explere consilium vestrum'. Nec mora, adpraehensum Chlothacharius puerum seniorem brachium elesit in terra, defixumque cultrum in ascella, crudiliter interfecit. Quo vociferante, frater eius ad pedes Childeberthi prosternitur, adpraehensaque eius genua, agebat cum lacrimis: 'Succurre, piissime pater, ne et ego peream sicut frater meus'. Tunc Childeberthus, lacrimis respersa facie, ait: 'Rogo, dulcissime frater, ut huius mihi vitam tua largitate concedas, et quae iusseris pro eius animam conferam, tantum ne interficiatur'. At ille convitiis actum ait: 'Aut eiece eum a te, aut certe pro eo morieris. Tu', inquid, 'es incestatur huius causae, et tam velociter de fide risillis?' Haec ille audiens, repulsum a se puerum proiecit ad eum; ipse vero excipiens, transfixum cultro in latere, sicut fratrem prius fecerat, iugulavit; deinde pueros cum nutriciis peremerunt. Quibus interfectis, Chlothacharius, ascensis equitibus, abscessit, parvi pendens de interfectione nepotum; sed et Childeberthus in suburbana concessit. Regina vero, conpositis corpusculis feretro, cum magno sallentio inmensoque luctu usque ad basilicam sancti Petri prosecuta, utrumque pariter tumulavit. Quorum unus decim annorum, alius vero septuennis erat. Tertium vero Chlodovaldum conpraehendere non potuerunt, quia per auxilium virorum fortium liberatus est. His, postpositum regnum terrenum, ad Dominum transiit, et sibi manupropria capillos incidens, clericus factus est, bonisque operibus insistens, presbiter ab hoc mundomigravit. Hiquoque regnum Chlodomeris inter se aequa lance diviserunt. Chrodigildis vero regina talem se tantamque exhibuit, ut ab omnibus honoraretur; assidua in elymosinis, pernox in vigiliis, in castitate atque omni honestate puram se semper exhibuit; praedia eclesiis, monastyriis vel quibuscumque locis sanctis necessaria praevidit, larga ac prona voluntate distribuit, ut putaretur eo tempore non regina, sed propria Dei ancilla ipsi sedolo deservire, quam non regnum filiorum, non ambitio saeculi nec facultas extulit ad ruinam, sed humilitas evexit ad gratiam.

## 19. De sancto Gregorio et situm Divioninsis castri.

<a id="hist-03-la-020"></a>
[**[020]**](#hist-03-en-020) Erat enim tunc et beatus Gregorius apud urbem Lingonicam magnus Dei sacerdus, signis et virtutibus clarus. Sed quia huius pontificis meminimus, gratum arbitratus sum, ut situm loci Divionensis, in quo maxime erat assiduus, huic inseram lectione. Est autem castrum firmissimis muris in media planitiae et satis iocunda conpositum, terras valde fertiles atque fecundas, ita ut, arvis semel scissis vomere, semina iaceantur, et magna fructuum opulentia subsequatur. A meridie habet Oscarum fluvium piscibus valde praedivitem, ab aquilone vero alius fluviolus venit, qui per portam Ingrediens ac sub pontem decurrens, per aliam rursum portam egreditur, totum monitionis locum placida unda circumfluens, ante portam autem molinas mira velocitate divertit. Quattuor portae a quattuor plagis mundi sunt positae, totumque aedificium triginta tres torres exornant, murus vero illius de quadris lapidibus usque in viginti pedes desuper a minuto lapide aedificatum habetur, habens in altum pedes triginta, in lato pedes quindecim. Qui cur non civitas dicta sit, ignoro. Habet enim in circuitu praetiosus fontes; a parte autem occidentes montes sunt uberrimi viniisque repleti, qui tam nobile incolis falernum porregunt, ut respuant Scalonum. Nam veteres ferunt ab Auriliano hoc imperatore fuisse aedificatum.

## 20. Quod Theudoberthus Visigardem disponsavit.

<a id="hist-03-la-021"></a>
[**[021]**](#hist-03-en-021) Theudoricus autem filio suo Theudoberto Wisigardem, cuiusdam regis filiam, disponsaverat.

## 21. Quod Theudoberthus in Provincia abiit.

<a id="hist-03-la-022"></a>
[**[022]**](#hist-03-en-022) Gothi vero cum post Chlodovechi mortem multa de id quae ille adquesierat pervasissent, Theudoricus Theudobertum, Chlothacharius vero Guntharium, seniorem filium suum, ad haec requirenda transmittunt, Sed Gunthecharius usque Rutinus accedens, nescio qua faciente causa, regressus est; Theudobertus vero usque ad Biterrensim civitatem abiens, Dehas castrum obtinuit atque in praedam deripuit. Deinde ad alium castrum nomen Caprariam legatus mittit, dicens, nisi se ille subdant, omne loco illud incendio concremandum, eosque qui ibidem resedent captivandus.

## 22. Quod postea Deoteriam accepit.

<a id="hist-03-la-023"></a>
[**[023]**](#hist-03-en-023) Erat autem ibidem tunc matrona Deoteria nomen utilis valde atque sapiens, cuius vir aput Biterris urbem concesserat. Quae misit nuntius ad regem, dicens: 'Nullus tibi, domne piissime, resistere potest. Cognuscemus dominum nostrum; veni et quod bene placitum fuerit in oculis tuis facito'. Theudobertus autem ad castrum veniens, cum pace ingressus est, subditumque sibi cernens populum, nihil inibi male gessit. Deoteria vero ad occursum eius venit; at ille speciosam eam cernens, amore eius capitur, suoque eam copulavit stratu.

## 23. De interitu Sigivaldi [et fuga Sigivaldi].

<a id="hist-03-la-024"></a>
[**[024]**](#hist-03-en-024) In illis diebus Theudoricus parentem suum Sigivaldum occidit gladio, mittens oeculte ad Theudobertum, ut et ille Sigivaldum, filium eius, neci daret, quem tunc secum habebat. Sed quia eum de sacro fonte exciperat, perdere noluit. Litteras vero, quas ei pater transmiserat, ipsi ad legendum dedit, dicens: 'Fuge hinc, quia patris mei praeceptum accipi, ut te interficiam; si vero ille defunctus fuerit et me regnare audieris, tunc securus ad me reverteris'. Quod audiens, gratias agens et vale dicens, abscessit. Arelatensim enim tunc urbem Gothi pervaserant, de qua Theudobertus obsedes retenebat; ad eam Sigivaldus confugit. Sed parum se ibidem cernens esse munitum, Latium petiit ibique et latuit. Dum haec agerentur, nuntiatur Theodoberto, patrem suum graviter egrotare, et ad quem nisi velocius properaret, ut eum inveniret vivum, a patruis suis excluderetur et ultra illuc non rediret. At ille haec audiens, cuncta postposita, illuc dirigit, Deoteria cum filia sua Arverno relictam. Cumque abissit, Theudoricus non post multos dies obiit vicinsimo tertio regni sui anno. Consurgentes autem Childeberthus et Chlothacharius contra Theudobertum, regnum eius auferre voluerunt, sed ille muneribus placatis a leodibus suis defensatus est et in regnum stabilitus. Mittens postea Arvernum, Deoteriam exinde arcessivit eamque sibi in matrimonio sociavit.

## 24. Quod Childeberthus Theudobertho muneravit.

<a id="hist-03-la-025"></a>
[**[025]**](#hist-03-en-025) Videns autem Childeberthus, quod ei praevalere non potuit, legationem ad eum misit et ad se venire praecepit, dicens: 'Filios non habeo, te tamquam filium habere desidero'. Quo veniente, tantis eum muneribus ditavit, ut ab omnibus miraretur. Nam de rebus bonis, tam de armis quam de vestibus vel reliquis ornamentis, quod regem habere decet, terna ei paria condonavit, similiter et de equitibus atque catinis. Haec audiens Sigivaldus, quod scilicet Theudoberthus regnum patris obtenuisset, ad eum de Italia rediit. Quem ille congaudens ac deosculans, tertiam partem ei de muneribus, quae a patruo acceperat, est largitus; et omnia, quae in fisco suo pater posuerat de rebus Sigivaldi, patri eius, ipsi reddi praecipit.

## 25. De bonitate Theudoberthi.

<a id="hist-03-la-026"></a>
[**[026]**](#hist-03-en-026) At ille in regno firmatus, magnum se atque in omni bonitate praecipuum reddidit. Erat enim regnum cum iustitia regens, sacerdotes venerans, eclesias munerans, pauperes relevans et multa multis beneficia pia ac dulcissima accommodans voluntate. Omne tributo, quod in fisco suo ab eclesiis in Arvernum sitis reddebebatur, clementer indulsit.

## 26. De interitu filiae Deoteriae.

<a id="hist-03-la-027"></a>
[**[027]**](#hist-03-en-027) Deuteria vero cernens filiam suam valde adultam esse, timens, ne eam concupiscens rex sibi adsumeret, in basterna posita, indomitis bubus coniunctis, eam de ponte praecipitavit; quae in ipso flumine spiritum reddidit. Hoc apud Viridunum civitatem actum est.

## 27. Quod Theudoberthus Visigardem accepit.

<a id="hist-03-la-028"></a>
[**[028]**](#hist-03-en-028) Cumque iam septimus annus esset, quod Wisigardem disponsatam haberet et eam propter Deuteriam accipere nollet, cuniuncti Franci contra eum valde scandalizabantur, quare sponsam suam relinqueret. Tunc commotus, relicta Deuteria, de qua parvolum filium habebat Theodobaldum nomen, Wisigardem duxit uxorem. Quam nec multo tempore habens, defuncta illa, aliam accepit. Verum tamen Deuteriam ultra non habuit.

## 28. Quod Childeberthus contra Chlothacharium abiit.

<a id="hist-03-la-029"></a>
[**[029]**](#hist-03-en-029) Childeberthus autem et Theodoberthus commoventes exercitum, contra Chlothacharium ire disponunt. Ille autem haec audiens, aestimans, se horum exercitum non sustenire, in silva confugit et concides magnas in silvas illas fecit, totamque spem suam in Dei pietate transfundens. Sed et Chrodichildis regina haec audiens, beati Martini sepulchrum adiit, ibique in oratione prosternitur et tota nocte vigilat, orans, ne inter filios suos bellum civile consurgeret. Cumque hi venientes cum exercitibus suis eum obsederent, tractantes illum die sequenti interficere, mane facto, in loco, quo erant congregati, orta tempestas tentoria dissicit, res diripit et cuncta subvertit; inmixtaque fulgora cum tonitruis ac lapidibus super eos discendunt. Ipse quoque super infectam grandine humum in facie proruunt et a lapidibus decedentibus graviter verberantur – nullum enim eis tegumen remanserat nisi parmae tantum–, hoc maxime metuentes, ne ab ignibus caelestibus cremarentur. Sed et equites eorum ita dispersi sunt, ut vix in vicinsimo quoque repperirentur stadio; multi enim ex eis prorsus non sunt inventi. Tunc illi a lapidibus, ut diximus, caesi et humo prostrati, paenitentiam agebant ac veniam praecabantur Deo, quod ista contra sanguinem suum agere voluissent. Super Chlothacharium vero neque una quidem pluviae gutta decidit aut aliquis sonitus tonitrui est auditus, sed nec anilitum ullius venti in illo loco sinserunt. Hi quoque mittentes nuntius ad eum, pacem et concordiam petierunt. Qua data, ad propria sunt regressi. Quod nullus ambigat, hanc per obtentum reginae beati Martini fuisse virtutem.

## 29. Quod Childeberthus in Hispaniis abierunt.

<a id="hist-03-la-030"></a>
[**[030]**](#hist-03-en-030) Post haec Childeberthus rex in Hispaniam abiit. Qua ingressus cum Chlothachario, Caesaragustanam civitatem cum exercitu vallant atque obsedent. At ille in tanta humilitate ad Deum conversi sunt, ut induti ciliciis, abstinentis a cibis et poculis, cum tonica beati Vincenti martiris muros civitatis psallendo circuirent; mulieres quoque amictae nigris palleis, dissoluta caesariae, superposito cinere, ut eas putares virorum funeribus deservire, plangendo sequebantur. Et ita totam spem locus ille ad Domini misericordiam rettulit, ut diceretur ibidem Ninivitarum ieiunium caelebrari, nec aestimaretur aliud posse fieri, nisi eorum praecibus divina misericordia flectiretur. Hii autem qui obsedebant, nescientes quid obsessi agerent, cum viderent sic murum circuire, putabant, eos aliquid agere malefitii. Tunc adpraehensum unum de civitate rusticum, ipse interrogant, quid hoc esset quod agerent. Qui ait: 'Tonicam beati Vincenti deportant et cum ipsa, ut eis Dominus misereatur, exorant'. Quod illi timentes, se ab ea civitate removerunt. Tamen adquisitam maximam Hispaniae partem, cum magnis spoliis in Galliis redierunt.

## 30. De regibus Hispanorum.

<a id="hist-03-la-031"></a>
[**[031]**](#hist-03-en-031) Post Amalaricum vero Theuda rex ordinatus est in Hispaniis. Quem interfectum, Theudegisilum levaverunt regem. His dum ad caenam cum amicis suis aepularet et esset valde laetus, caereis subito extinctis, in recubitu ab inimicis gladio percussus, interiit. Post quem Agila regnum accepit. Sumpserant enim Gothi hanc detestabilem consuetudinem, ut, si quis eis de regibus non placuisset, gladio eum adpeterent, et qui libuisset animo, hunc sibi statuerent regem.

## 31. De filia Theudorici regis Italici.

<a id="hist-03-la-032"></a>
[**[032]**](#hist-03-en-032) Et quia Theudoricus Italiae Chlodovechi regis sororem in matrimonio habuit, mortuus parvolam filiam cum uxore reliquid. Hic autem cum adulta facta esset, per levitatem animi sui, relicto matris consilio, quae ei regis filium providebat, servum suum Traguilanem nomen accepit et cum eum ad civitatem, qua defensare possit, aufugit. Cumque mater eius contra eam valde frenderet petiretque ab ea, ne humiliaret diutius nobile genus, sed, demisso servo, similem sibi de genere regio, quem mater providerat, deberet accipere, nullatinus voluit adquiescere. Tunc mater eius contra eam frendens, exercitum commovit. At illi venientes super eos, Traguilanem interfecerunt gladio, ipsam quoque caedentes, in domo matris reduxerunt. Erant autem sub Arriana secta viventes, et quia consuetudo eorum est, ut ad altarium venientes de alio calice reges accepiant et ex alio populus minor, veninum in calice illo posuit, de quo mater commonicatura erat. Quod illa hausto, protinus mortua est. Non enim dubium est, tale maleficium esse de parte diabuli. Quid contra haec miseri heretici respondebunt, ut in sanctam eorum locum habeat inimicus? Nos vero Trinitate in una aequalitate pariter et omnipotentia confitentes, etiam si mortiferum bibamus, in nomine Patres et Filii et Spiritus sancti, veri atque incorruptibilis Dei, nihil nos nocebit. Indignantes ergo Itali contra hanc mulierem, Theodadum regem Tusciae invitantes, super se regem statuunt. Hic vero cum dedicisset, quae meretrix ista commiserat, qualiter propter servum, quem acceperat, in matrem extiterit parricida, succensum vehementer balneum, eam in eodem cum una puella includi praecepit. Quae nec mora inter arduos vapores ingressa, in pavimento conruens, mortua atque consumpta est. Quod cognuscentes hi regis Childeberthus et Chlothacharius, consubrini eius, necnon et Theudoberthus, quod scilicet tam turpi fuerit interfecta supplicio, ad Theodadum legationem dirigunt, exprobrantes de morte eius atque dicentes: 'Si haec quae egisti nobiscum non conposueris, regnum tuum auferimus et simile te poena damnabimus'. Tunc ille timens, quinquagina eis milia aureorum transmisit. Childeberthus autem, ut erat semper contra Chlothacharium regem invidus atque versutus, cum Theudoberto, nepote suo, coniunctus, divisum inter se hoc aurum, nihil exinde dare regi Chlothachario voluerunt. At ille super thesauros Chlodomeris adgressus, multum illis amplius, quam hi fraudaverant, abstulit.

## 32. Quod Theudoberthus in Italiam abiit.

<a id="hist-03-la-033"></a>
[**[033]**](#hist-03-en-033) Theudobertus vero in Italia abiit et exinde multum adquisivit. Sed quia loca illa, ut fertur, morbida sunt, exercitus eius in diversis febribus corruens vexabatur; multi enim ex his in illis locis mortui sunt. Quod videns Theudobertus ex ea reversus est, multa secum expolia ipse vel sui deferentes. Dicitur tamen tunc temporis usque Ticinum accessisse civitatem, in qua Buccelenum rursum dirixit. Qui, minorem illam Italiam captam atque in ditionibus regis antedicti redactam, maiorem petiit; in qua contra Belsuarium multis vicibus pugnans, victuriam obtenuit. Cumque imperator vidisset, quodBelsuarius crebrius vinceretur, amoto eo, Narsitem in eius loco statuit; Belsuarium vero comitem stabuli quasi pro humilitate, quod prius fuerat, posuit. Buccelenus vero contra Narsitem magna certamina gessit. Captam omnem Italiam, usque in mare terminum dilatavit; thesauros vero magnus ad Theudobertum de Italia dirixit. Quod cum Narsis imperatori posuisset in notitiam, imperator, conductis praetio gentibus, Narsiti solatium mittit, confligensque postea victus abscessit. Deinceps vero Buccelenus Siciliam occupavit; de qua etiam tributa exigens, regi transmisit. Magna enim ei felicitas in his conditionibus fuit.

## 33. De Asteriolo et Secundino.

<a id="hist-03-la-034"></a>
[**[034]**](#hist-03-en-034) Asteriolus tunc et Secundinus magni cum rege habebantur; erat autem uterque sapiens et retoricis inbutus litteris. Sed Secundinus plerumque legationem imperatori a rege missus intulit, et ob hoc iactantia sumpserat ac nonnulla contra rationem exercebat. Qua de causa factum est, ut inter illum atque Asteriolum lis saeva consurgeret, quae usque ad hoc proficit, ut, oblitis verborum obiectionibus, propriis se manibus verberarent. Cumque haec per regem pacificata fuissent et Secundinus adhuc de sua caede tumeret, nata est inter eos rursum intentio; et rex suscipiens Secundini causam, Asteriolum in eius potestatem dedidit. Qui valde humiliatus est et ab honore depositus; sed per Wisigardem reginam iterum est restitutus. Mortua autem illa, consurgens iterum Secundinus, eum interfecit. Nam hic moriens filium dereliquid. Qui cum crevisset et esset adultus, coepit patris sui velle iniuriam vindecare. Tunc Secundinus timore perterritus, dum de villa in villam ante eum fugiret, cum se iam videret eo inminente non posse evadere, ne in manus inimici conruerit, venino se, ut dicitur, interfecit.

## 34. De munere Theudoberthi circa Virdunensis cives.

<a id="hist-03-la-035"></a>
[**[035]**](#hist-03-en-035) Desideratus autem Viredunensis episcopus, cui Theudoricus rex multas inrogavit iniurias, cum post multa exitia, damna atque erumnas ad libertatem propriam, Domino iubente, redisset et episcopatum, ut diximus, apud Viredunensim urbem potiretur, videns habitatoris eius valde pauperes atque distitutus, dolebat super eos; et cum ipse per Theudoricum de rebus suis remansisset extraneus nec haberet de proprio, qualiter eos consolaretur, bonitatem et clementiam circa omnes Theudoberthi regis cernens, misit ad eum legationem, dicens: 'Fama bonitatis tuae in universam terram vulgatur, cum tanta sit tua largitas, ut etiam non petentibus opem praestis. Rogo, si pietas tua habet alequid de pecunia, nobis commodis, qua cives nostros relevare valeamus; cumque hi negutium exercentes responsum in civitate nostra, sicut reliquae habent, praestiterint, pecuniam tuam cum usuris legitimis reddimus'. Tunc ille pietate commotus, septim ei milia aureorum pristitit, qua ille accipiens per cives suos erogavit. At illi negutia exercentes divites per hoc effecti sunt et usque hodie magni habentur. Cumque antedictus episcopus debitam pecuniam obtulisset regi, respondit rex: 'Non habeo necessarium hoc recipere; illud mihi sufficit, si dispensatione tua pauperes, qui oppraemebantur inopia, per tuam suggestionem vel per meam largitatem sunt relevati'. Et nihil exigens, antedictus cives divites fecit.

## 35. De interitu Sirivuldi.

<a id="hist-03-la-036"></a>
[**[036]**](#hist-03-en-036) Defuncto autem apud urbem supradictam memoratum antestitem, Agiricus quidam e civibus in eius est cathedram subrogatus. Siacrius autem, filius eius, reminiscens iniuriam patris, qualiter a Sirivuldo ad regem Theudoricum incusatus, non solum spoliatus, verum etiam suppliciis adfectus fuisset, oppraessum cum armata manu Sirivuldum taliter interfecit. Mane facto, cum nebula esset condensa et vix, adhuc disrumpentibus tenebris, alequid quis possit discernere, venit ad villam eius in Divionensi territurio cui nomen est Floriacum; egressoque domo uno amicorum, putantes, ipsum Sirivuldum esse, interfecerunt eum, et revertentibus, quasi victuriam obtenuissent de inimico, indicat eis unus ex familia, non eos dominum interfecisse, sed subditum. At illi regressi, requirentes eum, cellulam, in qua dormire solitus erat, repertam adgrediuntur. Ad cuius osteum diutissime pugnantes, nihil ei poterant praevalere. Dehinc erutum ab uno latere parietem, ingredientes, gladium eum interemunt. Post mortem enim Theudorici hic interfectus est.

## 36. De obitu Theudoberthi et de interitu Partheni.

<a id="hist-03-la-037"></a>
[**[037]**](#hist-03-en-037) His denique gestis, Theodoberthus rex aegrotare coepit. Ad quem medici multa studia inpenderunt; sed nihil valuit, quiaeum iam Dominus vocare iubebat. Ergo cum diutissime aegrotasset, ab ipsa infirmitate deficiens, reddidit spiritum. Franci vero cum Parthenium in odio magno haberent, pro eo quod eis tributa antedicti regis tempore inflixisset, eum persequi coeperunt. Ille vero in periculum se positum cernens, confugium ab urbe facit ac duobus episcopis suppliciter exorat, ut eum ad urbem Treverecam deducentes, populi saevientes seditionem sua praedicatione conpraemerent. Quibus euntibus, nocte, dum in strato suo decumberet, subito per somnium vocemmagnam emittit, dicens: 'Heu! heu! Succurrite qui adestis et auxilium ferte pereunte'. A quo clamore expergefacti qui aderant, interrogant, quid hoc esset. Respondit ille: 'Ausanius amicus meus cum Papianilla coniuge, quos olim interfeci, ad iudicium me arcessibant, dicentes: „Veni ad respondendum, quia causaturus es nobiscum coram Domino"'. Zelo enim ductus, ante annos aliquos coniugem innocentem amicumque perimerat. Igitur accedentibus episcopis ad antedictam urbem, cum strepentes populi seditionem ferre non possint, eum in eclesia abdire voluerunt, scilicet ponentes eum in arca et desuper sternentes vestimenta, quae erant ad usum aeclesiae. Populus autem ingressus perscrutatusque universus eclesiae angulos, cum nihil repperissent, frendens egrediebatur. Tunc unus ex suspicione locutus, ait: 'En arcam, in qua non est inquisitus adversarius noster'. Dicentibus vero costodibus, nihil in ea aliud nisi ornamenta eclesiae contenere, illi clavem postolant, aientes: 'Nisi reseraveritis velocius, ipsi eam sponte confringemus'. Denique, reserata arca, amotis lintiaminibus, inventum extrahunt, plaudentes atque dicentes: 'Tradidit Deus inimicum nostrum in manibus nostris'. Tunc caedentes eum pugnis sputisque perurguentes, vinctis postergum manibus, ad colomnam lapidibus obruerunt. Fuit autem in cibis valde vorax, sed quae sumebat, quo caelerius ad manducandum commoveretur, sumpto aloae, velociter digerebat; sed et strepidus ventris absque ulla auditorum reverentia in publico emittebat. Hoc ergo exitu consummatus interiit.

## 37. De hieme gravi.

<a id="hist-03-la-038"></a>
[**[038]**](#hist-03-en-038) Gravem eo anno et solito asperiorem hiemem fecit, ita ut torrentes concatiniti gelu pervium populis tamquam reliqua humus praeberet. Aves quoque rigore adfectae vel fame, absque ullo hominum dolo, cum magnae essent nives, manu capiebantur. A transitu igitur Chlodovechi usque in transitum Theudoberthi conputantur anni 37. Mortuo ergo Theudoberto quarto decimo regni sui anno, regnavit Theudoaldus, filius eius, pro eo. EXPLICIT LIBER TERTIUS.
