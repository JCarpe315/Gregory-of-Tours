### 1. How Childebert passed over to Chilperic, and of the flight of Mummolus

<a id="hist-06-en-001"></a>
[**[001]**](#hist-06-la-001) In the sixth year of his reign, King Childebert rejected his peace with King Guntram and allied himself with Chilperic. Not long afterward Gogo died, and Wandelenus was appointed in his place. Mummolus escaped by flight from King Guntram’s kingdom and shut himself within the protecting walls of Avignon. A synod of bishops assembled at Lyons, considered various disputed cases, and condemned the negligent by its judgment. The synod returned to the king, discussing at length the flight of Duke Mummolus and, to some extent, the discords between the kings.

### 2. Of the legates of Chilperic returned from the East

<a id="hist-06-en-002"></a>
[**[002]**](#hist-06-la-002) Meanwhile King Chilperic’s envoys, who had gone to the Emperor Tiberius three years earlier, returned, though not without severe loss and hardship. Because the kings were at odds, they had not dared to enter the port of Marseille and instead came to the city of Agde, situated in the kingdom of the Goths. Before they reached shore, their ship was driven by the wind, struck the land, and broke to pieces. Seeing themselves and their servants in danger, the envoys seized planks and with difficulty reached the bank. Many servants were lost, but most escaped. The inhabitants seized the goods that the waves washed ashore, though the envoys recovered the better items and carried them back to King Chilperic. The people of Agde kept many of them for themselves.

I had then gone to the villa of Novigentum to meet the king. There he showed me a great ceremonial dish that he had caused to be made of gold and gems, weighing fifty pounds, and said, “I made this to adorn and ennoble the Frankish people. If life accompanies me, I will make many more.” He also displayed gold pieces, each weighing a pound, sent by the emperor. On one side they bore a likeness of the emperor and the circular inscription TIBERIUS CONSTANTINE, PERPETUAL AUGUSTUS; on the other, a four-horse chariot with its driver and the inscription GLORY OF THE ROMANS. He showed me many other ornaments presented by the envoys as well.

### 3. Of the legates of Childebert to Chilperic

<a id="hist-06-en-003"></a>
[**[003]**](#hist-06-la-003) While Chilperic was staying at the same villa, Egidius, bishop of Rheims, came with King Childebert’s leading nobles as envoys to King Chilperic. After they had discussed joining together in peace and taking away King Guntram’s kingdom, King Chilperic said, “Because my sins increased, no sons remain to me, and I now have no other heir except my brother Sigibert’s son, King Childebert. Therefore let him be heir to everything over which I have been able to labor; only let me hold everything without question or dispute for as long as I live.” They thanked him, confirmed their statements in written agreements, and returned to Childebert with rich gifts. When they departed, King Chilperic sent Bishop Leudovald with the leading men of his kingdom. Having given and received oaths of peace and confirmed the agreements, they returned laden with gifts.

### 4. How Lupus was driven from Childebert's kingdom

<a id="hist-06-en-004"></a>
[**[004]**](#hist-06-la-004) Duke Lupus of Champagne had long been harassed and continually plundered by various men, especially Ursio and Berthefred. At last they made an agreement to kill him and raised an army against him. Queen Brunhilda, seeing this and grieving over the unjust persecution of her faithful servant, girded herself like a man and burst into the middle of the hostile squadrons, saying, “Do not, men, do not do this evil. Do not persecute an innocent man. Do not join battle over one man and thereby destroy the strength of the region.” Ursio answered her, “Withdraw from me, woman. It was enough for you to hold the kingdom while subject to your husband. Now your son reigns, and his kingdom is preserved not by your protection but by mine. Withdraw from me, or my horses’ hooves will trample you into the earth.” After they had exchanged these and other words at great length, the queen’s intervention prevented them from fighting. Nevertheless, on leaving that place they attacked Lupus’s houses, seized all his resources, and carried them to their own homes while pretending to deposit them in the king’s treasury. They threatened Lupus, saying, “You will not escape my power alive.” Seeing himself in danger, Lupus placed his wife safely within the walls of Lyon-la-Clavate and fled to King Guntram. Guntram received him kindly, and Lupus remained hidden with him, waiting for Childebert to reach lawful age.

### 5. The altercation with the Jew

<a id="hist-06-en-005"></a>
[**[005]**](#hist-06-la-005) While King Chilperic was still staying at the villa mentioned above, he ordered the baggage to be moved and prepared to come to Paris. When I approached him to take my leave, a certain Jew named Priscus, who was on familiar terms with him as a purchaser of goods, arrived. The king gently took hold of Priscus’s hair and said to me, “Come, priest of God, and lay your hand on him.” When Priscus resisted, the king said, “O hard mind and ever unbelieving generation! You do not understand that God’s Son was promised to you by the voices of the prophets, nor do you understand that the mysteries of the Church were prefigured in your sacrifices.”

The Jew answered, “God has not entered into marriage, is not endowed with offspring, and permits no one to share his kingdom. Through Moses he says, ‘See, see that I am the Lord, and there is no God besides me. I will kill and I will make alive; I will strike and I will heal.’” The king replied, “God begot his eternal Son from a spiritual womb, not younger in age or lesser in power. Of him God himself says, ‘From the womb, before the morning star, I begot you.’ In the last ages he sent into the world as healer this Son born before the ages, as your prophet says, ‘He sent his Word and healed them.’ As for your assertion that God does not beget, hear your prophet speaking in the Lord’s voice: ‘Shall I who make others give birth not give birth myself?’ He says this concerning the people who are reborn in him through faith.”

The Jew answered, “Could God become man, be born of a woman, be subjected to blows, and be condemned to death?” When the king fell silent, I joined the discussion and said, “That God’s Son, as God, became man was required not by his necessity but by mine. He could not redeem man, who was captive to sin and subjected to the devil’s servitude, without assuming human nature. I will offer testimony not from the Gospels and the apostle, which you do not believe, but from your own books, and pierce you with your own sword, just as David is recorded once to have slaughtered Goliath.

“Hear your prophet saying that God was to become man: ‘He is God and Man, and who has known him?’ And elsewhere: ‘This is our God, and no other can be compared with him. He found the whole way of knowledge and gave it to Jacob his servant and Israel his beloved. Afterward he was seen on earth and conversed with men.’ Hear your prophet likewise saying that he is born of a virgin: ‘Behold, a virgin shall conceive in her womb and bear a son, and his name shall be called Emmanuel, which interpreted means God with us.’ Another prophet says that he was to be subjected to blows, fastened with nails, and exposed to other injuries: ‘They pierced my hands and my feet; they divided my garments among them,’ and so forth. And again: ‘They gave me gall for food, and in my thirst they gave me vinegar to drink.’ David also says that through the gibbet of the cross he restored to his kingdom the fallen world subject to the devil’s dominion: ‘The Lord reigned from the tree.’ This does not mean that he had not previously reigned with the Father, but that he began a new reign over the people whom he had freed from servitude to the devil.”

The Jew answered, “What necessity compelled God to suffer these things?” I replied, “I have already told you: God created man innocent, but man was ensnared by the serpent’s cunning and became a transgressor of the commandment. Cast out of paradise, he was therefore consigned to worldly labors. Through the death of Christ, God’s only-begotten Son, he was reconciled to God the Father.”

The Jew said, “Could God not have sent prophets or apostles to call man back to the way of salvation without humbling himself in the flesh?” I answered, “From the beginning God always loved the human race, but neither the submersion of the flood, nor the burning of Sodom, nor the plagues of Egypt, nor the miracle of the divided sea and Jordan ever terrified it. It always resisted God’s law and did not believe the prophets; indeed, it not only disbelieved but killed those very preachers of repentance. Therefore no other could have accomplished this if he himself had not descended to redeem it. Reborn through his birth, washed by baptism, healed by his wound, raised by his resurrection, we are glorified by his ascension. Your prophet said that he would come to heal my diseases: ‘By his bruise we have been healed.’ Elsewhere: ‘He himself will bear my sins and pray for transgressors.’ And again: ‘He was led like a sheep to the slaughter, and like a lamb dumb before its shearer, so he did not open his mouth. In his humiliation his judgment was taken away. Who shall declare his generation? His name is the Lord of hosts.’

“Jacob too, from whose race you boast to have come, spoke as though addressing Christ himself, God’s Son, in his blessing upon his son Judah: ‘The sons of your father shall worship you. Judah is a lion’s whelp. From the shoot, my son, you have ascended. Stooping down, you slept like a lion, like a lion’s whelp. Who will rouse him? His eyes are more beautiful than wine, and his teeth whiter than milk.’ Who, Jacob asks, will rouse him? Although Christ himself said, ‘I have power to lay down my life, and I have power to take it up again,’ the apostle Paul nevertheless says, ‘Whoever does not believe that God raised him from the dead cannot be saved.’”

Despite these and other words, the wretched man was never moved to believe. When the king saw that he was silent but not moved by my discourse, he turned to me and asked that I give him a blessing and depart. He said, “I will say to you, priest, what Jacob said to the angel who spoke with him: ‘I will not let you go unless you bless me.’” He ordered water to be brought for my hands. When I had washed and prayed, I took bread, gave thanks to God, ate some myself, and offered it to the king. After drinking unmixed wine and taking my leave, I departed. The king mounted his horse and returned to Paris with his wife, daughter, and entire household.

### 6. Of Saint Hospitius the recluse, his abstinence and miracles

<a id="hist-06-en-006"></a>
[**[006]**](#hist-06-la-006) At that time there lived in the city of Nice a recluse named Hospitius, a man of great abstinence. His pure body was bound with iron chains, over which he wore a hair shirt, and he ate nothing but plain bread with a few dates. During the days of Lent he sustained himself on the roots of Egyptian herbs used by hermits, which merchants brought him. At first he drank the broth in which they had been cooked, and afterward ate the roots themselves. The Lord deigned to work great miracles through him.

On one occasion, with the Holy Spirit revealing it to him, he foretold the coming of the Lombards into Gaul: “The Lombards will come into Gaul and devastate seven cities, because their malice has increased in the Lord’s sight. There is no one who understands and no one who does good to appease God’s wrath. The whole people is faithless, given to perjury, liable to theft, and prone to murder, and no fruit of justice springs from these things. Tithes are not paid, the poor are not fed, the naked are not clothed, and strangers are not received in hospitality or satisfied with sufficient food. Therefore this blow is coming. Now I tell you: gather all your substance within the circuit of your walls so that it may not be plundered by the Lombards, and fortify yourselves in the strongest places.” All were astonished at his words, took their leave, and returned home in great wonder.

He also told the monks, “Depart from this place too, taking your possessions with you. Behold, the people I foretold is approaching.” When they answered, “We will not leave you, most holy father,” he said, “Do not fear for me. They will inflict injuries on me, but they will not kill me.” After the monks departed, that people came. While devastating everything they found, they reached the place where God’s holy recluse lived. He showed himself to them through the tower window. They circled the tower but could find no entrance by which to reach him. Two of them climbed up, removed the roof, and saw him bound in chains and wearing a hair shirt. They said, “This man is a criminal and has committed murder; that is why he is kept bound in these fetters.” Summoning an interpreter, they asked him what evil he had done to be confined in such punishment. He confessed that he was a murderer and guilty of every crime.

One Lombard drew his sword to strike off his head, but his right hand stiffened in the very act of striking, and he could not draw it back. He dropped the sword and it fell to the ground. Seeing this, his companions raised a cry to heaven, begging the saint to tell them mercifully what they should do. He made the sign of salvation and restored the arm to health. The man was converted in that very place, received the tonsure, and is now regarded as a most faithful monk. Two commanders who listened to Hospitius returned safely to their own country, but those who despised his command died miserably in that province. Many of them were seized by demons and cried, “Why, most holy and blessed man, do you torture and burn us like this?” He laid his hand on them and cleansed them.

After this there was a man living in Angers who had lost both speech and hearing through a severe fever. When the fever subsided, he remained deaf and mute. A deacon from that province was sent to Rome to obtain tokens of the blessed apostles and other saints who protect that city. When the sick man’s relatives learned of this, they asked the deacon to take him as a traveling companion, confident that if he visited the tombs of the most blessed apostles he would immediately obtain healing. On their journey they came to the place where blessed Hospitius lived. After greeting and kissing him, the deacon explained the reason for his journey, said that he was going to Rome, and asked Hospitius to commend him to his friends among the shipmasters. While the deacon was still there, the holy man perceived through the Lord’s spirit that divine power was present. He said to the deacon, “I beg you to bring before me the sick man who is now your traveling companion.” The deacon went quickly to his lodging without delay and found the sick man full of fever, indicating by signs that his ears were ringing. He took him and led him to the man of God. Hospitius grasped his hair and drew his head to the window. Taking oil sanctified by a blessing, he held the man’s tongue with his left hand and poured the oil into his mouth and over the top of his head, saying, “In the name of my Lord Jesus Christ, may your ears be opened, and may that power which once drove the harmful demon from a deaf and mute man unlock your mouth.” Then he asked his name, and the man answered clearly and told him.

Seeing this, the deacon said, “I give immeasurable thanks to you, Christ, for deigning to reveal such things through your servant. I was seeking Peter, Paul, Laurence, and the others who glorify Rome with their own blood; here I have found them all, here I have discovered every one.” As he spoke with tears and great wonder, the man of God, avoiding vainglory with all his resolution, said, “Be silent, be silent, dearest brother. It is not I who do these things, but he who created the world from nothing, who for our sake assumed humanity, gives sight to the blind, hearing to the deaf, and speech to the mute, restores their former skin to lepers, life to the dead, and abundant healing to all the sick.” The deacon departed rejoicing, taking leave and going with his companions.

After they had gone, a man named Dominic, blind from birth, came to test the power of this miracle-worker. He stayed in the monastery for two or three months, devoting himself to prayer and fasting. At length the man of God summoned him and asked, “Do you wish to receive your sight?” Dominic answered, “My desire was to learn what was unknown to me. I do not know what light is. I know only that everyone praises it, but from the beginning of my life until now I have not been privileged to see.” Hospitius made the holy cross over his eyes with blessed oil and said, “In the name of Jesus Christ my Redeemer, let your eyes be opened.” His eyes were immediately opened, and he stood in amazement, beholding the mighty works of God visible in this world.

Next a woman who, by her own account, had three demons was brought to him. After he had blessed her with his sacred touch and made the sign of the cross on her forehead with holy oil, the demons were cast out and she departed cleansed. He also healed a girl tormented by an unclean spirit through his blessing.

When the day of his death was approaching, he summoned the monastery’s superior and said, “Bring tools, break through the wall, and send messengers to the bishop of the city to come and bury me. On the third day I will depart from this world and enter the appointed rest that the Lord has promised me.” The superior sent messengers to the bishop of Nice with this news. Afterward a man named Crescens came to the window and, seeing him bound in chains and full of worms, said, “My lord, how can you endure such severe torments so courageously?” Hospitius answered, “He for whose name I suffer these things strengthens me. I tell you that I am now being released from these bonds and entering my rest.”

On the third day he removed the chains with which he had been bound and prostrated himself in prayer. After praying with tears for a very long time, he sat on a stool, stretched out his feet, raised his hands to heaven, gave thanks to God, and yielded up his spirit. Immediately all the worms that had been boring into his holy limbs vanished. Bishop Austadius came and committed his blessed body to burial with the greatest care. I learned all these things from the lips of the man whom I described above as healed by him from muteness and deafness. He told me many other things about Hospitius’s powers, but the fact that I have heard his life has been written by many prevents me from relating them.

### 7. Of the death of Ferreolus, bishop of Uzès

<a id="hist-06-en-007"></a>
[**[007]**](#hist-06-la-007) At that time Ferreolus, bishop of Uzès, a man of great holiness, died full of wisdom and understanding. Following Sidonius, he had composed several books of letters. After his death Albinus, formerly a prefect, obtained the episcopate through Dynamius, governor of Provence, without the king’s consent. He held it for no more than three months and died when proceedings had been initiated to remove him. Jovinus, also a former governor of Provence, then received a royal command granting him the episcopate. But the deacon Marcellus, son of the senator Felix, forestalled him: he summoned his fellow provincials and was ordained bishop on Dynamius’s advice. When Jovinus subsequently pressed for his removal, Marcellus shut himself in the city and tried to defend himself by force; unable to succeed, he prevailed by gifts.

### 8. Of Eparchius the recluse of the city of Angoulême

<a id="hist-06-en-008"></a>
[**[008]**](#hist-06-la-008) Eparchius, the recluse of Angoulême, also died. He was a man of magnificent holiness, through whom God displayed many miracles; omitting most of them, I will touch upon only a few. He was an inhabitant of Périgueux. After his conversion he became a cleric, came to Angoulême, and built himself a cell. Gathering a few monks there, he remained constantly at prayer. Any gold or silver offered to him he distributed for the needs of the poor or the redemption of captives. No bread was ever baked in that cell during his lifetime; devoted people brought it in when necessity required. He redeemed a great host of people with the offerings of the devout, often suppressed the poison of malignant pustules with the sign of the cross, and drove demons from possessed bodies by prayer. He frequently commanded judges with overflowing gentleness, rather than merely asking them, to pardon the guilty. His speech was so gentle that they could not refuse when he begged for indulgence.

On one occasion a man was being led away to be hanged for theft. The inhabitants also accused him of many other crimes, including theft and murder. When Eparchius was told, he sent one of his monks to beg the judge to spare the culprit’s life. But the crowd jeered and shouted that neither the region nor the judge would be safe if the man were released, and the petition was refused. The prisoner was stretched on the rack, beaten with rods and clubs, and condemned to the gallows. When the monk sorrowfully reported this to the abbot, Eparchius said, “Go some distance away. Know that the Lord will restore by his gift the man whom a human being refused to hand back. When you see him fall, seize him at once and bring him to the monastery.” While the monk carried out these instructions, Eparchius prostrated himself in prayer and poured out petitions to God with tears until the hanged man’s bolt and chains broke and he was restored to the ground. The monk seized him and presented him unharmed before the abbot.

Eparchius gave thanks to God and ordered the count to be summoned. He said, “Dearest son, you were always accustomed to hear me with a kindly spirit. Why have you hardened yourself today and refused to release the man for whose life I asked?” The count replied, “I willingly listen to you, holy bishop, but when the crowd rose up I could do nothing else, fearing that a rebellion would be stirred against me.” Eparchius said, “You did not listen to me, but God deigned to hear me, and the man you handed over to death he has restored to life. Behold, he stands before you unharmed.” As he spoke, the man prostrated himself at the feet of the astonished count, who saw alive the man he had left in the destruction of death. I learned this from the count’s own lips. Eparchius performed many other deeds that I have considered too lengthy to recount. After forty-four years of enclosure he was afflicted by a slight fever and yielded up his spirit. He was carried from his cell and committed to burial, and, as I have said, a great company of those he had redeemed joined his funeral procession.

### 9. Of Domnolus, bishop of Le Mans

<a id="hist-06-en-009"></a>
[**[009]**](#hist-06-la-009) Domnolus, bishop of Le Mans, fell ill. In King Chlothar’s time he had presided over the monastic community at the basilica of Saint Laurence in Paris. Because he had always remained faithful to King Chlothar while Childebert the Elder was alive, and had often concealed messengers sent to spy on the king, the king was waiting for an opportunity to give him episcopal honor. When the bishop of Avignon died, Chlothar intended to appoint him there. But blessed Domnolus heard of this and went to the basilica of Saint Martin, where King Chlothar had then come to pray. Keeping vigil all night, he made a petition to the king through the leading men present, asking not to be removed like a captive from the king’s sight and not to have his simplicity wearied among sophisticated senators and philosophical judges. He asserted that such a position would mean humiliation rather than honor for him. The king consented and, when Innocentius, bishop of Le Mans, died, appointed Domnolus bishop of that church.

Once he had obtained the episcopate, he showed himself so great and so holy that, raised to the summit of supreme holiness, he restored the use of their steps to the lame and sight to the blind. After twenty-two years as bishop, seeing himself gravely afflicted by jaundice and gallstones, he chose Abbot Theodulf to take his place. The king assented to his wish, but not long afterward changed his mind, and the choice was transferred to Badegisil, mayor of the royal palace. Badegisil received the tonsure and ascended through the grades allotted to clerics; forty days later, when the bishop died, he succeeded him.

### 10. Of the breaking into the basilica of Saint Martin

<a id="hist-06-en-010"></a>
[**[010]**](#hist-06-la-010) In these days Saint Martin’s basilica was broken into by thieves. They placed against a window of the apse a grating that stood over a dead man’s tomb, climbed up it, broke the glass, and entered. They carried off a large quantity of gold, silver, and all-silk palls, not fearing to set foot on the holy tomb where I scarcely presume even to touch my lips. But the saint’s power chose to reveal their rashness with terrible judgment. After committing the crime they came to the city of Bordeaux, where a quarrel arose and one killed the other. Their deed was thus exposed and the theft discovered; broken silver and palls were taken from their lodging. When this was reported to King Chilperic, he ordered them to be bound in chains and brought before him. Fearing that men would die on account of one who, while living in the body, had often prayed for the lives of the lost, I sent the king a letter of petition, asking that they not be killed since I, to whom the prosecution belonged, was not accusing them. He received this kindly and spared their lives. He carefully reassembled the valuables that had been scattered and ordered them to be restored to the holy place.

### 11. Of Bishop Theodore and Dynamius

<a id="hist-06-en-011"></a>
[**[011]**](#hist-06-la-011) In the city of Marseille, Dynamius, governor of Provence, began to lay grave snares for Bishop Theodore. When Theodore prepared to hasten to the king, Dynamius seized him, detained him in the middle of the city, and treated him outrageously before finally releasing him. The clergy of Marseille were plotting with Dynamius to expel him from the episcopate. But while Dynamius was traveling to King Childebert, King Guntram ordered him and the former prefect Jovinus to be detained. When the clergy of Marseille heard this, they were filled with great joy that he was now detained and assigned to exile, and that matters had reached the point that he would never return to Marseille. They seized the church houses, inventoried the furnishings, opened the store-chests, stripped the storerooms, and took possession of all the church property as though the bishop were already dead, uttering various charges against him which, with Christ’s guidance, were later proved false.

After Childebert made peace with Chilperic, he sent envoys to King Guntram demanding the return of the half of Marseille that Guntram had given him after his father’s death. If Guntram refused, he was to know that he would lose much by retaining this portion. When Guntram refused to return it, he ordered the roads closed so that no one would have passage through his kingdom. Seeing this, Childebert promoted Gundulf from household officer to duke, a man of senatorial family, and sent him to Marseille. Not daring to travel through Guntram’s kingdom, Gundulf came to Tours. I received him kindly, recognized him as my mother’s great-uncle, kept him with me for five days, supplied his needs, and allowed him to depart.

When he proceeded farther, he was unable to enter Marseille because Dynamius resisted him. Nor was the bishop, who had by then joined Gundulf, received back into his church. Dynamius and the clergy barred the city gates, jeering at and despising both bishop and duke. At last Dynamius was invited to a conference with the duke and came to the basilica of blessed Stephen near the city. Doorkeepers guarded the entrance so that the doors would be shut immediately after Dynamius entered. This was done, and the crowds of armed men following Dynamius were excluded and could not enter. Unaware of this, he discussed various matters with Gundulf at the altar. They then left the altar and entered the reception room. When Dynamius entered with them, now stripped of his companions’ support, they sternly rebuked him. The armed attendants who were clamoring around after he had been led away were put to flight. The duke and bishop then assembled the senior citizens so that they might enter the city. Seeing all this, Dynamius begged pardon, gave the duke many gifts, and swore oaths that he would henceforth be faithful to the bishop and king, whereupon his clothing was restored to him. The gates of the city and the doors of the sacred buildings were opened, and both duke and bishop entered with standards, acclamations, and various honorary banners. The clergy involved in this crime, whose leaders were Abbot Anastasius and the priest Proculus, fled beneath Dynamius’s roof, seeking refuge from the very man who had incited them. Many of them were released on suitable sureties and ordered to go to the king.

Meanwhile Gundulf returned to King Childebert after subjecting the city to Childebert’s rule and restoring the bishop to his place. But Dynamius forgot the faith he had promised King Childebert and sent messengers to King Guntram, saying that through the bishop he would lose the portion of the city due to him and would never possess Marseille under his own dominion unless Theodore were expelled. Moved to anger, Guntram ordered, contrary to religious law, that the bishop of the supreme God be brought before him confined in chains, saying, “Let the enemy of my kingdom be thrust into exile so that he can no longer harm me.”

Because the bishop suspected these designs and could not easily be dragged from the city, his enemies waited for the dedication festival of an oratory in the suburban countryside. As he left the city and hastened to the feast, armed men suddenly poured from a hidden ambush with a great uproar and surrounded the holy bishop. They threw him from his horse, scattered all his companions, bound his servants, beat his clergy, placed him on a miserable hack, and led him to the king’s presence without allowing any of his own people to follow. As they passed through the city of Aix, Bishop Pientius, grieving for his brother bishop, supplied clergy to assist him and provided necessities before allowing him to proceed.

While this was happening, the clergy of Marseille again opened the church houses, ransacked private places, inventoried some property, and carried other property to their own homes. The bishop was brought before the king, found innocent, and permitted to return to his city, where the citizens received him with great acclamation. Grave enmity consequently arose between King Guntram and his nephew Childebert; their treaty was broken, and they began laying snares for one another.

### 12. Of the army moved against Bourges

<a id="hist-06-en-012"></a>
[**[012]**](#hist-06-la-012) King Chilperic, seeing these discords springing up between his brother and nephew, summoned Duke Desiderius and ordered him to inflict some injury on his brother. Desiderius raised an army, put Duke Ragnovald to flight, and invaded Périgueux. After exacting oaths he proceeded to Agen. When Ragnovald’s wife heard that her husband had been put to flight and these cities were being brought under King Chilperic’s power, she sought refuge in the basilica of the holy martyr Caprasius. She was dragged out, stripped of her property and her servants’ support, and sent to Toulouse after giving sureties. There she again entered the basilica of Saint Saturninus and remained. Desiderius took all the cities in that region belonging to King Guntram and subjected them to King Chilperic’s rule. Duke Berulf, hearing that the men of Bourges were murmuring that they would enter the territory of Tours, raised an army and stationed himself on their borders. The districts of Isiodore and Berrave in the city of Tours were then severely devastated. Those who could not be present at this siege were afterward cruelly condemned. Duke Bladast went into Gascony and lost the greater part of his army.

### 13. Of Lupus and Ambrose, citizens of Tours, slain

<a id="hist-06-en-013"></a>
[**[013]**](#hist-06-la-013) Lupus, a citizen of Tours, wished to enter the clergy after losing his wife and children, but his brother Ambrosius prevented him, fearing that if Lupus joined the Church he would make God’s Church his heir. His misguiding brother provided him with another wife and appointed a day on which they would be joined and the betrothal gifts presented. The brothers then came together to the fortress of Chinon, where they had lodging. Ambrosius’s wife, an adulteress who loved another man with harlot’s passion and hated her husband, laid a snare for him. After the brothers had feasted together and soaked themselves in wine until drunk late into the night, they lay down together on one bed. During the night, while everyone was sleeping heavily with wine, Ambrosius’s wife’s lover arrived. He lit a fire of straw to see what he was doing, drew his sword, and struck Ambrosius on the head so violently that the sword descended through his eyes and split his head.

Awakened by the blow and finding himself wallowing in blood, Lupus cried loudly, “Alas, alas! Help! My brother has been killed!” The adulterer, who was already leaving after committing the crime, heard this, returned to the bed, and attacked Lupus. Lupus resisted, but the adulterer overwhelmed him, mangled him with many wounds, and left him half alive with a mortal injury. No one in the household noticed. In the morning everyone was appalled by so great a crime. Lupus was found still alive and, after relating what had happened, breathed his last. The harlot did not allow herself even a short period of mourning; after a few days she joined her lover and departed.

### 14. Of the prodigies that appeared

<a id="hist-06-en-014"></a>
[**[014]**](#hist-06-la-014) In the seventh year of King Childebert’s reign, the twenty-first year of Chilperic and Guntram, there were heavy rains, lightning, and severe thunderstorms in January, and flowers appeared on the trees. The star I earlier called a comet appeared with a great darkness around it; it shone through the darkness as though set in an opening, sparkling and spreading its hair. A ray of marvelous size issued from it, looking from a distance like the great smoke of a conflagration. It was seen in the west at the beginning of the first watch of the night.

On the holy day of Easter the sky appeared to be on fire at the city of Soissons, so that two conflagrations seemed visible, one larger and the other smaller. After two hours they joined together, formed a great pyramid, and vanished. In the territory of Paris real blood flowed from a cloud and fell upon many people’s garments, staining them so corruptly that they themselves rejected their own clothing in horror. This portent appeared in three places within the territory of that city. In the territory of Senlis a man’s house was found spattered with blood inside when he rose in the morning.

There was a great pestilence among the people that year: various illnesses and eruptive fevers with pustules and blisters afflicted many people to death, though many who took care escaped. I heard that the inguinal plague was raging severely in the city of Narbonne that year, so that once a man had been seized by it there was no interval before death.

### 15. Of the death of Felix the bishop

<a id="hist-06-en-015"></a>
[**[015]**](#hist-06-la-015) Felix, bishop of the city of Nantes, succumbed to this illness and became gravely sick. He summoned the neighboring bishops and begged them to confirm with their subscriptions the consent he had given to his nephew Burgundio. When this had been done, they sent Burgundio to me. He was then about twenty-five years old. He came and asked me to go to Nantes, tonsure him, and consecrate him bishop in place of his uncle, who was still alive. I refused because I knew this was contrary to the canons. But I offered advice: “It is written in the canons, my son, that no one may enter the episcopate without first receiving the ecclesiastical grades in due order. Return there, dearest son, and ask the man who chose you to tonsure you himself. When you have received the honor of the presbyterate, attend church assiduously; and when God wills Felix to depart, you will easily ascend to the episcopal grade.”

He returned but neglected to follow the advice he had received, because Bishop Felix appeared to be improving. After the fever departed, however, pustules caused by the humor broke out on his shins. He applied an excessively strong plaster of Spanish flies; his shins putrefied, and he ended his life in the thirty-third year of his episcopate, at the age of seventy. His cousin Nonnichius succeeded him by royal appointment.

### 16. How Pappolenus received back his wife

<a id="hist-06-en-016"></a>
[**[016]**](#hist-06-la-016) When Pappolenus heard of Felix’s death, he took back Felix’s niece, from whom he had been separated. Before this he had been betrothed to her, but while Bishop Felix was delaying the marriage, Pappolenus came with a large band, dragged the girl from an oratory, and took refuge in the basilica of blessed Albinus. Bishop Felix, moved to anger, deceived the girl and separated her from her husband. He changed her clothing and placed her in a monastery in the city of Bazas. But Pappolenus secretly sent servants to rescue her from the place where she had been put and bring her to him. He did not refuse; he took her from the monastery and joined her to himself in marriage. Protected by royal decrees, he had no reason to fear her relatives’ threats.

### 17. Of the Jews converted by King Chilperic

<a id="hist-06-en-017"></a>
[**[017]**](#hist-06-la-017) King Chilperic ordered many Jews to be baptized that year and received several of them himself from the holy font. Some, however, were washed only in body, not in heart. They lied to God and returned to their former perfidy, appearing to observe the Sabbath and honor the Lord’s day at the same time. Priscus could by no means be deflected into acknowledging the truth. The angry king ordered him to be committed to custody, intending to compel the man who would not believe voluntarily to hear and believe even against his will. Priscus offered certain gifts and asked for time until his son could marry a Hebrew woman at Marseille, falsely promising that he would then fulfill the king’s command.

Meanwhile a quarrel arose between Priscus and Phatir, a convert from Judaism who was now the king’s godson through baptism. On the Sabbath Priscus girded himself with a prayer shawl and, carrying no iron weapon in his hand as though observing the Mosaic law, entered a secluded place. Phatir suddenly arrived and killed Priscus and the companions who were with him with a sword. After killing them, Phatir fled with his servants, who were in a nearby street, to the basilica of Saint Julian. While they were there, they heard that the king, hoping to save Phatir’s life, would order the servants to be dragged from the basilica and killed as criminals. One of them drew his sword after his master had escaped and killed his companions. He then left the basilica with his sword, but the crowd rushed upon him and cruelly killed him. Phatir obtained permission and returned to King Guntram’s kingdom, from which he had come, but not many days later he was killed by Priscus’s relatives.

### 18. Of the legates of King Chilperic returned from Spain

<a id="hist-06-en-018"></a>
[**[018]**](#hist-06-la-018) King Chilperic’s envoys Ansovald and Domegisel, who had been sent to Spain to inspect the dowry, returned. In those days King Leovigild was encamped with his army against his son Hermenegild and had taken the city of Mérida from him. I have already explained above how Hermenegild allied himself with the Emperor Tiberius’s commanders. This affair also delayed the envoys and caused them to return later. On seeing them, I was anxious to know how faith in Christ was flourishing among the few Christians remaining in that region. Ansovald replied, “The Christians now living in Spain preserve the Catholic faith intact, but the king is now using a new device to drive it out. He deceitfully pretends to pray both at the martyrs’ tombs and in the churches of our religion. He says, ‘I have clearly recognized that Christ the Son of God is equal to the Father, but I do not believe at all that the Holy Spirit is God, because in no book is he described as God.’” Alas, what an iniquitous sentence, what a venomous understanding, what a perverse mind! Where is the Lord’s saying, “God is spirit”? Where is Peter’s saying to Ananias, “Why did you think to tempt the Holy Spirit? You have not lied to men, but to God”? And where is Paul’s statement, when recounting the mystical gifts, “One and the same Spirit works all these things, distributing to each as he wills”? One who works what he wills is known to be subject to no one. When Ansovald reached King Chilperic, a Spanish embassy followed; it proceeded from Chilperic to Childebert and then returned to Spain.

### 19. Of the men of King Chilperic at the bridge of the Oureq

<a id="hist-06-en-019"></a>
[**[019]**](#hist-06-la-019) King Chilperic had posted guards at the bridge of Urbia in the city of Paris to keep plotters from his brother’s kingdom from causing harm. The former duke Asclipius learned of this in advance, attacked at night, killed them all, and severely devastated the district nearest the bridge. When this was reported to King Chilperic, he sent messengers to his counts, dukes, and other officers, directing them to assemble an army and invade his brother’s kingdom. But good counselors restrained him, saying, “They acted wickedly; you act wisely. Send messengers to your brother. If he is willing to make amends for your injury, do no evil; if he refuses, you can then consider what course to follow.” Accepting this reasoning, he stopped the army and sent an embassy to his brother. Guntram made amends for everything and sought his brother’s undiminished affection.

### 20. Of the death of Duke Chrodinus

<a id="hist-06-en-020"></a>
[**[020]**](#hist-06-la-020) Chrodinus died that year. He was a man of magnificent goodness and piety, very generous in almsgiving and a refresher of the poor, a lavish benefactor of churches and a nourisher of clergy. He often founded new estates, planted vineyards, built houses, and established farms. He would then invite bishops whose resources were small, give them a banquet, and generously distribute those houses with their farms and cultivated fields, together with silver, bedding, utensils, servants, and attendants. He would say, “Let these things be given to the Church so that, when the poor are refreshed from them, they may obtain pardon for me before God.” I heard many other good things about this man, which would take too long to recount. He died at the age of seventy.

### 21. Of the signs shown

<a id="hist-06-en-021"></a>
[**[021]**](#hist-06-la-021) Repeated signs appeared that year. The moon suffered an eclipse; real blood flowed from a broken loaf within the territory of Tours; the walls of the city of Soissons collapsed; the earth shook in the city of Angers; wolves entered within the walls of the town of Bordeaux and devoured dogs without any fear of man; and fire was seen running across the sky. The city of Bazas was also consumed by fire, so that churches and ecclesiastical houses were destroyed, though I learned that all the furnishings were rescued.

### 22. Of the bishop Cartherius

<a id="hist-06-en-022"></a>
[**[022]**](#hist-06-la-022) After King Chilperic had seized his brother’s cities, he appointed new counts and ordered all the tributes of the cities to be brought to him. I learned that this was carried out. In those days two men were arrested by Nunnichius, count of the city of Limoges, carrying letters in the name of Cartherius, bishop of Périgueux, which contained many insults against the king. Among other things the bishop was made to complain that he had descended from paradise into hell, meaning that he had been transferred from King Guntram’s kingdom to Chilperic’s rule. The count sent these letters with the men to the king under close guard. The king patiently, for the bishop’s sake, sent men to bring Cartherius before him, intending to examine whether the charges were true. When the bishop arrived, the king presented the men with the letters and asked whether the bishop had sent them. He denied sending them. The men were asked from whom they had received them and named the deacon Frontonius. The bishop was questioned about the deacon and answered that Frontonius was his particular enemy and that there could be no doubt the wickedness was his, since he had often stirred up unjust proceedings against him. The deacon was brought without delay and questioned by the king. He confessed against the bishop, saying, “I dictated this letter at the bishop’s command.” The bishop protested that Frontonius had often sought devices to cast him from the episcopate. The king, moved to mercy and commending his own cause to God, yielded to both: he gently begged the bishop on the deacon’s behalf and asked the bishop to pray for him. Cartherius was then sent back to his city with honor. Two months later Count Nunnichius, who had sown this scandal, was stricken with hemorrhage and died; because he had no children, the king granted his property to various persons.

### 23. That a Son Was Born to King Chilperic

<a id="hist-06-en-023"></a>
[**[023]**](#hist-06-la-023) After the many deaths of his sons, a son was born to King Chilperic. The king therefore ordered all prisons opened, the bound released, and the composition payments owed to the treasury by negligent persons not to be exacted at all. This infant later committed a great outrage.

### 24. Again of the snares of Bishop Theodore, and of Gundovald

<a id="hist-06-en-024"></a>
[**[024]**](#hist-06-la-024) Fresh hostilities again arose against Bishop Theodore. Gundovald, who claimed to be King Chlothar’s son, came from Constantinople and landed at Marseille. I wish briefly to record something of his origin. He was born in Gaul and reared with the diligent care customary among those kings, with locks of hair hanging down his back, and was instructed in letters. His mother presented him to King Childebert, saying, “Behold your nephew, King Chlothar’s son. Because his father dislikes him, receive him, for he is your own flesh.” Childebert, having no sons, accepted him and kept him with him. When this was reported to King Chlothar, he sent messengers to his brother, saying, “Send the boy to me.” Childebert sent the youth without delay. On seeing him, Chlothar ordered the hair of his head to be cut, saying, “I did not beget him.”

After King Chlothar’s death, Gundovald was received by King Charibert. King Sigibert summoned him, again cut off his hair, and sent him to the city of Agrippina, now called Cologne. He escaped from there, again let his hair grow, and went to Narses, who then governed Italy. There he married, begot sons, and went to Constantinople. It is said that after a long time someone invited him to return to Gaul. He landed at Marseille and was received by Bishop Theodore. Theodore also supplied him with horses, and he joined Duke Mummolus, who was then in the city of Avignon, as I mentioned above.

Duke Guntram Boso seized Bishop Theodore and thrust him into custody for this affair, demanding to know why he had brought a foreigner into Gaul and sought through these actions to subject the kingdom of the Franks to imperial rule. Theodore produced a letter said to be subscribed by the hand of King Childebert’s leading men and said, “I did nothing on my own initiative except what my lords and seniors commanded me.” The bishop was guarded in a cell and not permitted to approach the church. One night, while he was praying earnestly to the Lord, the cell shone with overwhelming splendor, so that the count guarding him was seized with immense terror. A globe of immense light was seen above him for two hours. In the morning the count recounted this to the others with him.

Theodore was afterward brought before King Guntram with Bishop Epiphanius, who had fled from the Lombards and was then staying at Marseille because he too was alleged to have known of this affair. The king examined them and found no crime in either, but nevertheless ordered them to remain in custody. After many torments in custody, Bishop Epiphanius died. Gundovald withdrew to an island in the sea to await the outcome. Duke Guntram Boso, together with King Guntram’s duke, divided Gundovald’s property and carried to Auvergne an immense weight of silver, gold, and other things, as it is said.

### 25. Of the signs

<a id="hist-06-en-025"></a>
[**[025]**](#hist-06-la-025) In the eighth year of King Childebert’s reign, on the day before the Kalends of February, the signal for matins sounded on a Sunday in the city of Tours and the people rose and gathered at church. Though the sky was cloudy and rainy, a great globe of fire fell from heaven and ran through the air over a great distance. It gave so much light that everything could be seen as though it were midday. When the cloud received it again, night returned. The waters also rose beyond their usual strength: the Seine and Marne caused such inundations around Paris that shipwrecks often occurred between the city and the basilica of Saint Laurence.

### 26. Of Gunthchramn and Mummolus

<a id="hist-06-en-026"></a>
[**[026]**](#hist-06-la-026) Duke Guntram Boso returned to Auvergne with the treasures mentioned above and went to King Childebert. When he came back from there with his wife and daughters, King Guntram seized and detained him, saying, “Your invitation brought Gundovald into Gaul, and that is why you went to Constantinople years ago.” Guntram Boso answered, “Your own Duke Mummolus received him and kept him with him in Avignon. Now permit me to bring Mummolus himself to you, and then I will be free from the charges made against me.” The king replied, “I will not permit you to go unless you pay fitting penalties for what you have done.” Seeing death near, Guntram Boso said, “Behold my son. Take him as a hostage for what I promise my lord the king. If I do not bring Mummolus to you, let me lose my little child.” The king allowed him to depart but retained his infant son.

Taking with him men from Auvergne and the Vellavi, Guntram Boso went to Avignon. Mummolus treacherously had unsound boats prepared on the Rhône. Guntram’s men boarded unsuspectingly, and when they reached the middle of the river the boats filled with water and sank. In this danger some escaped by swimming, others seized planks from the boats and reached shore, but many less resourceful men drowned in the river.

Duke Guntram reached Avignon. After entering that city’s walls, Mummolus had observed that a small part was not protected by the Rhône. He had diverted a branch of the river so that the whole place was protected by its floodwater. There he dug very deep ditches, and running water concealed the snares he had prepared. When Guntram arrived, Mummolus called from the wall, “If good faith is intact, let him come from one side of the bank and I from the other, and let him say what he wishes.” When this was agreed, Guntram said from the opposite side—for a branch of the river lay between them—“If I may, I will cross, for there are things we should discuss more privately.” Mummolus replied, “Come; do not be afraid.” Guntram entered with one friend, weighed down by the weight of his coat of mail. As soon as his friend reached the river ditch, he sank beneath the water and was never seen again. Guntram too was sinking and being carried away by the swift current when one of the bystanders held out his spear-shaft and brought him back to the bank. After exchanging insults, Guntram and Mummolus departed.

While Guntram was besieging the city with King Guntram’s army, these events were reported to Childebert. Angry that this had been done without his command, he sent the aforementioned Gundulf there. Gundulf lifted the siege and brought Mummolus to Auvergne, but Mummolus returned to Avignon a few days later.

### 27. How King Chilperic entered Paris

<a id="hist-06-en-027"></a>
[**[027]**](#hist-06-la-027) King Chilperic went to Paris on the day before Easter. So that he might escape the curse written into his pact with his brothers, forbidding any of them to enter Paris without another’s consent, he entered the city with the relics of many saints borne before him. He celebrated Easter with great rejoicing and presented his son for baptism. Ragnemod, bishop of that city, received him from the holy font and ordered him to be named Theuderic.

### 28. Of Marcus the Referendary

<a id="hist-06-en-028"></a>
[**[028]**](#hist-06-la-028) The referendary Mark, whom I mentioned above, was suddenly seized by a pain in his side after amassing treasures from the unjust tax assessments. He beat his head, received penance, and breathed his last, and his property was surrendered to the treasury. Great treasures of gold, silver, and many kinds of valuables were found there. He carried nothing away with him except the injury to his soul.

### 29. Of the girls of the monastery of Poitiers

<a id="hist-06-en-029"></a>
[**[029]**](#hist-06-la-029) The envoys returning from Spain reported nothing certain because Leovigild was encamped with his army against his elder son. In the monastery of blessed Radegund a girl named Disciola, niece of the blessed Salvius, bishop of Albi, died in the following manner. When she fell ill, the other sisters attended her assiduously. The day came for her departure from the body, and about the ninth hour she said to the sisters, “Behold, I already feel easier; behold, I feel no pain. There is now no need for your solicitude to expend further care on me. Rather, withdraw from me so that I may relax more easily in sleep.” On hearing this, her sisters withdrew from the cell for a short time and then returned. Standing before her, they waited to hear what she would say. She stretched out her hands, begging a blessing from someone unseen, and said, “Bless me, holy handmaidens of God on high. Behold, you are already being troubled for the third time today on my account. Why, holy ones, do you endure repeated trouble for a weak little woman?” When the sisters asked to whom she was speaking, she gave no answer. After a short interval she gave a loud cry with a laugh and yielded up her spirit.

Behold, a certain demoniac who had come to be cleansed for the feast of the blessed Cross seized his own hair and dashed himself to the ground, saying, “Alas, alas for me, who have suffered such a loss! If only I might have investigated the matter beforehand; then this soul would not have been taken from my power.” When those present asked what he meant, he replied, “Behold, the angel Michael has received the girl’s soul and raised her to the heavens. My prince, whom you call the devil, has no share in her.”

Afterward her body was washed with water and shone with such snowy whiteness that the abbess could find no linen in the store that appeared whiter than the body. She was clothed in clean linens and committed to burial.

Another girl of this monastery also had a vision, which she related to the sisters. She thought she was making a journey and desired to reach a living fountain. Not knowing the way, she met a man who said, “If you wish to reach the living fountain, I will guide your journey.” Thanking him, she followed as he led. Walking together, they came to a great fountain whose waters shone like gold, while the herbs around it gleamed with verdant light like various gems. The man said to her, “Behold the living fountain you sought with such labor. Drink your fill of its streams, so that it may become in you a fountain of living water springing up into eternal life.” As she eagerly drank, the abbess approached from the other side and clothed the naked girl in a royal robe shining with such light, gold, and necklaces that one could scarcely look at it. The abbess said, “Your bridegroom sends you these gifts.”

When the girl saw this vision, she was stricken at heart. A few days later she asked the abbess to prepare a cell in which she could be enclosed. The abbess quickly completed it and said, “Behold the cell. What do you now desire?” The girl asked to be enclosed. When this was granted, the virgins gathered with loud psalmody and burning lamps. Blessed Radegund herself took the girl by the hand and led her to the place. She bade everyone farewell, kissed each one, and was enclosed. The entrance by which she had gone in was walled up, and there she now devotes herself to prayer and reading.

### 30. Of the death of the emperor Tiberius

<a id="hist-06-en-030"></a>
[**[030]**](#hist-06-la-030) The Emperor Tiberius departed from this world that year, leaving the people in deep mourning at his death. He was supremely good, ready in almsgiving, just in judgments, and most cautious in judging. He despised no one but embraced everyone with good will. Loving all, he was loved by all. When he fell ill and despaired of living, he summoned the Augusta Sophia and said, “Behold, I feel that the time of my life is fulfilled. Now, on your advice, I will choose who should preside over the state. I must choose an energetic man to exercise this power.” Sophia chose a certain Maurice, saying, “This man is very energetic and sagacious. He has often fought the enemies of the state and gained victories.” She said this because, once Tiberius had departed, she hoped to be joined in marriage to Maurice. But when Tiberius learned of the Augusta’s consent to this choice, he ordered his daughter to be adorned with imperial ornaments, summoned Maurice, and said, “Behold, with the Augusta Sophia’s consent you have been chosen for the empire. To make you more secure in it, I will give you my daughter.” The girl approached, and her father gave her to Maurice, saying, “Let my empire be granted to you together with this girl. Enjoy it prosperously, always remembering to delight in equity and justice.” Maurice took the girl and led her to his house. When the wedding ceremony had been completed, Tiberius died. After the funeral rites, Maurice was clothed in the diadem and purple, proceeded to the circus, received acclamations and praises, distributed gifts to the people, and was confirmed in the empire.

### 31. Of the many evils which King Chilperic commanded to be done in his brother's cities, or did himself

<a id="hist-06-en-031"></a>
[**[031]**](#hist-06-la-031) King Chilperic received envoys from his nephew Childebert, the foremost of whom was Egidius, bishop of Rheims. When admitted to the king, they presented their proposal: “My lord, your nephew, asks you to preserve in every way the peace you made with him. He cannot have peace with your brother, because after his father’s death Guntram took from him his part of Marseille, retains his fugitives, and refuses to return them. Your nephew Childebert therefore wishes to preserve intact the affection he now maintains with you.” Chilperic answered, “My brother has been culpable in many respects. If my son Childebert examines the order of events, he will immediately discover that his father was killed through Guntram’s collusion.” Bishop Egidius then said, “If you join with your nephew and he joins with you, I can raise an army and swiftly inflict upon Guntram the vengeance that is owed.” When they had confirmed this by oath and exchanged hostages, they departed.

Trusting their promises, Chilperic raised the army of his kingdom and came to Paris, where his stay caused the inhabitants great losses. Duke Berulf came with the men of Tours, Poitiers, Angers, and Nantes to the border of Berry. Desiderius and Bladast, with the entire army of the province entrusted to them, surrounded Bourges from the other side, devastating the regions through which they passed. Chilperic ordered the army that had joined him to pass through Paris. After it crossed, he crossed himself and went to the fortress of Meudon, burning and devastating everything. Although his nephew’s army had not joined him, Childebert’s commanders and envoys were with him. He sent messengers to the dukes mentioned above, saying, “Enter Berry, advance to the city, and exact oaths of fidelity in my name.”

The men of Bourges assembled fifteen thousand strong at the fortress of Mediolanum and fought against Duke Desiderius. The slaughter was so great that more than seven thousand men fell from each army. The dukes and the rest of the people reached the city, plundering and devastating everything. The depredation there was such as had never been heard of in former times: no house, vineyard, or tree remained; they cut down, burned, and destroyed everything. They also took the sacred vessels from the churches and burned the churches themselves.

King Guntram advanced against his brother with an army, placing all his hope in God’s judgment. One evening he sent out his army and killed the greater part of his brother’s forces. In the morning envoys met, and the kings made peace, each promising the other that whichever party had transgressed the law would make amends as the bishops and leading men of the people judged. They then departed in peace. King Chilperic could not restrain his army from plundering and struck down the count of Rouen with a sword. He returned to Paris, abandoning all the booty and releasing the captives. The forces besieging Bourges received orders to return home and carried off such quantities of booty that the whole region from which they had come seemed utterly emptied of both men and cattle.

The army of Desiderius and Bladast also passed through the Touraine, causing fires, plunder, and murders on the scale usually inflicted on enemies. They led away captives, many of whom they later released after stripping them. A cattle plague followed this destruction, so that scarcely any stock remained, and it was a novelty to see a bullock or heifer.

While these things were happening, King Childebert remained encamped with his army in one place. One night the common soldiers in the army were stirred up, raised a great murmur against Bishop Egidius and the king’s dukes, and began to cry out publicly: “Remove from the king’s presence those who sell his kingdom, subject his cities to another’s dominion, and deliver his people into the rule of another prince!” As they shouted these and similar words, morning came. They seized their weapons and rushed to the king’s tent, intending to seize the bishop and leading men, overwhelm them by force, beat them, and cut them with swords. Discovering this, the bishop fled on horseback toward his own city. The people pursued him with shouts, throwing stones and hurling insults. He was protected by the fact that they had no horses ready. Although his companions’ horses were exhausted, the bishop pressed on alone, so terrified that he did not bother to recover a boot that slipped from his foot. Reaching the city, he shut himself within the walls of Rheims.

### 32. Of the death of Leudast

<a id="hist-06-en-032"></a>
[**[032]**](#hist-06-la-032) A few months earlier Leudast had come to the Touraine with a royal order to take back his wife and reside there. He also brought me a letter subscribed by the bishops directing that he be received into communion. But because I saw no letter from the queen, on whose account he had chiefly been excluded from communion, I deferred receiving him and said, “When I receive the queen’s command, I will not delay to receive him.” Meanwhile I wrote to her. She sent back a written reply: “Pressed by many people, I could do nothing except permit him to depart. Now I ask that he not obtain your peace or receive the blessed bread from your hand until I have considered more fully what ought to be done.” Reading this, I feared that he would be killed. I summoned his father-in-law, disclosed the letter, and begged him to keep Leudast safe until the queen’s mind softened. Still hostile to me, he received my simple, God-fearing advice deceitfully and refused to do what I recommended. The proverb I once heard an old man relate was fulfilled: “Always give good counsel to friend and enemy alike, because the friend accepts it and the enemy rejects it.”

Spurning this advice, Leudast went to the king, who was then with the army in the district of Meudon. He begged the people to intercede with the king so that he might be admitted to his presence. When all the people interceded, the king allowed himself to be seen. Leudast prostrated himself at his feet and begged pardon. The king said, “Keep yourself safe for a little while until I have seen the queen and considered how you may return to her favor, since you are found greatly culpable toward her.” But incautious and fickle, Leudast relied on having been admitted to the king’s presence. When the king returned to Paris, Leudast prostrated himself at the queen’s feet in the holy church on Sunday, begging pardon. Gnashing her teeth and abhorring the sight of him, she drove him away and said through her tears, “Since none of my sons survives to investigate the charges against me, I commit them to you, Lord Jesus, to investigate.” Prostrating herself at the king’s feet, she added, “Woe is me! I see my enemy and can do nothing against him.” Leudast was driven from the holy place, and the solemn Mass was celebrated.

When the king and queen returned from church, Leudast followed as far as the street, not suspecting what would happen to him. He went around the merchants’ houses, examined their goods, weighed silver, and inspected various ornaments, saying, “I will buy this and that, for I still have much gold and silver.” As he spoke, the queen’s servants suddenly arrived and tried to bind him in chains. He drew his sword and struck one of them. The others, inflamed with anger, seized shields and swords and rushed at him. One aimed a blow that stripped most of the hair and skin from his head. As he fled across the city bridge, his foot slipped between two planks forming the bridge, and his shin broke. His hands were bound behind his back and he was committed to custody. The king ordered physicians to treat him so that, once healed of these wounds, he could be tortured with prolonged punishment. He was taken to a royal villa, and when his wounds putrefied and he was at the point of death, the queen ordered him to be thrown on his back on the ground. An immense bar was placed against his neck, and another man struck his throat. Thus, having always lived a perfidious life, he ended it with a deserved death.

### 33. Of the locusts, diseases, and prodigies

<a id="hist-06-en-033"></a>
[**[033]**](#hist-06-la-033) In the ninth year of King Childebert’s reign, King Guntram restored his part of Marseille to his nephew. King Chilperic’s envoys returned from Spain and reported that the province of Carpetania had been severely devastated by locusts: no tree, vine, forest, fruit, or green thing remained untouched by their destruction. They also said that the hostility between Leovigild and his son was increasing violently. Pestilence was devastating various places, most severely the city of Narbonne, where it was now raging for the third year since it had first taken hold there and then subsided. People returning from flight were again consumed by the disease. The city of Albi also suffered greatly from this affliction. In those days many brilliantly shining rays appeared in the north at midnight; they came together and separated again until they vanished. The sky from the northern quarter shone so brightly that it seemed to be producing dawn.

### 34. Of the death of Chilperic's son, whom he called Theuderic

<a id="hist-06-en-034"></a>
[**[034]**](#hist-06-la-034) Envoys again came from Spain bearing gifts and seeking an agreement with King Chilperic that, in accordance with the earlier arrangement, he should give his daughter in marriage to King Leovigild’s son. When the agreement had been made and everything discussed, the envoy returned. But after King Chilperic left Paris for the district of Soissons, fresh grief arrived. His son, whom he had washed in holy baptism the previous year, was seized by dysentery and breathed his last. The fiery globe that I mentioned falling from a cloud above had foreshadowed this. Returning to Paris amid immense lamentation, they buried the boy. Chilperic sent after the envoy to recall him so that he might prolong the agreement he had made, saying, “Behold, I am enduring mourning in my house; how can I celebrate my daughter’s marriage?” He then wished to send another daughter, born of Audovera, whom he had placed in the monastery at Poitiers. But he abandoned this plan chiefly because blessed Radegund resisted, saying, “It is not right for a girl dedicated to Christ to return again to the pleasures of the world.”

### 35. Of the death of the prefect Mummolus and of the women slain

<a id="hist-06-en-035"></a>
[**[035]**](#hist-06-la-035) While this was happening, the queen was told that the boy who had died had been carried off by sorcery and incantations, and that the prefect Mummolus, whom she had long detested, had been involved. It happened that while Mummolus was feasting in his house, one of the king’s courtiers was lamenting a beloved servant who had been seized by dysentery. The prefect replied, “I have in my store an herb with which a dysentery sufferer may drink and be immediately healed, however desperate his condition.” When these words were reported to the queen, she burned with greater fury. She seized women of the city of Paris, subjected them to torture, and compelled them by blows to confess what they knew. They confessed that they were sorceresses and testified that they had caused many deaths, adding a statement I can by no means bring myself to believe: “We gave your son, O queen, in exchange for the prefect Mummolus’s life.” The queen subjected the women to still more severe tortures: she killed some, burned others, and bound others to wheels after breaking their bones.

She then withdrew with the king to the villa of Compiègne and revealed to him everything she had heard about the prefect. The king sent servants to summon Mummolus. After examining him, they loaded him with chains and subjected him to torture. His hands were bound behind his back and he was hung from a beam while being questioned about the sorcery he knew. He confessed nothing of the matters described above, but he did admit that he had often received ointments and potions from these women to secure the favor of the king and queen. When taken down from torture, he summoned a reader and said, “Tell my lord the king that I feel no harm from what has been inflicted.” On hearing this, the king asked, “Is it true that he is a sorcerer if he has suffered no injury from these tortures?” Mummolus was then stretched on the rack and beaten with triple thongs until the torturers themselves grew tired. Afterward they drove spikes through the nails of his hands and feet. When the sword was ready to cut off his head, the queen obtained his life, but the humiliation that followed was no less than death. He was placed on a wagon and sent to the city of Bordeaux, where he had been born, after all his property had been taken away. On the journey he suffered a hemorrhage and could scarcely reach his appointed destination. Not long afterward he breathed his last.

The queen then seized the little boy’s treasure. She burned his garments and all his other valuables, whether silk or any kind of wool that could be found; it is said that four wagonloads were consumed. She melted down the gold and silver in a furnace and stored it away so that nothing intact might remain to recall the memory of her son and renew her mourning.

### 36. Of the bishop Aetherius

<a id="hist-06-en-036"></a>
[**[036]**](#hist-06-la-036) Aetherius, bishop of Lisieux, whom I mentioned above, was expelled from his city and later restored in the following manner. There was a certain cleric from the city of Le Mans, exceedingly dissolute, a lover of women and gluttony, and wholly given over to fornication and every impurity. Having frequently committed adultery with a certain harlot, he cut off her hair, changed her woman’s clothing for a man’s, and took her with him to another city, so that when they arrived among strangers no suspicion of adultery would arise. The woman was freeborn and came from good parents. Many days later her relatives discovered what had happened and hastened to avenge the humiliation of their family. They found the cleric, bound him, and committed him to custody, but burned the woman with fire. Then, driven by the accursed hunger for gold, they arranged to sell the cleric for a price, intending either that someone should ransom him or that he should be condemned to death. When this was reported to Bishop Aetherius, he was moved to mercy and rescued him from imminent destruction by paying twenty gold pieces.

After his life had been spared, the cleric declared himself a teacher of letters and promised the bishop that, if boys were entrusted to him, he would make them fully proficient. Delighted to hear this, the bishop gathered boys of the city and entrusted them to his instruction. The cleric was honored by the citizens, and the bishop gave him some land and vineyards. But when invited to the house of the parents of one of his pupils, he returned to his vomit and, forgetting the earlier injury, lusted after the boy’s mother. When the chaste woman disclosed this, her relatives joined together, subjected the cleric to the severest tortures, and wished to kill him. Once more the bishop, moved to mercy, rescued him, chastised him with gentle words, and restored him to honor. But the perverse mind could never be inclined to goodness; instead, he became the enemy of the man by whom he had repeatedly been redeemed from death.

He joined the city’s archdeacon, declared himself worthy of the episcopate, and plotted to kill the bishop. They hired a cleric to strike him with an axe. The conspirators hurried everywhere, whispered together, secretly formed friendships, and offered rewards so that, if the bishop died, the cleric might succeed him. But the Lord’s mercy forestalled their wretchedness and swiftly repressed the cruelty of these wicked men in his compassion. On a day when the bishop had gathered workers in a field to plough, the cleric mentioned above followed him with an axe, while the bishop knew nothing of the plot. At last Aetherius noticed and asked, “Why are you following me so closely with that axe?” Terrified, the cleric fell at the man’s knees and said, “Be strong, priest of God. Know that I was sent by the archdeacon and my teacher to strike you with this axe. Whenever I tried to do so and raised my right hand for the blow, my eyes were covered with darkness, my ears were stopped, my whole body shook with trembling, and my hands were strengthless, so that I could not accomplish what I desired. But when I lowered my hands, I felt no harm at all. I know that the Lord is with you, because I have been unable to injure you.” The bishop wept at these words and enjoined silence on the cleric. He returned home, reclined at dinner, and afterward lay down on his bed, with many little beds for clergy around him.

Distrusting this cleric, the conspirators planned to carry out the crime themselves and devised new schemes either to destroy the bishop by force or to impute a crime that would deprive him of the priesthood. While everyone slept, around midnight they burst into the bishop’s bedroom, crying aloud that they had seen a woman leaving it and had let her go because they were hastening to the bishop. It was insane and a device of the devil to impute such a crime to a bishop who was nearly seventy years old. Without delay they again joined the cleric mentioned above, and the bishop was bound in chains by the hands of the man from whose neck he had often struck chains, and placed under close guard by the man whom he had frequently rescued from foul prisons. Recognizing that his enemies had become overwhelmingly powerful, Aetherius, bound in fetters, tearfully implored the Lord’s mercy. Soon the guards were overcome by sleep, his bonds were divinely loosed, and the innocent man who had so often freed the guilty left his custody. He escaped and crossed into King Guntram’s kingdom.

After his departure, the united accomplices went more confidently to King Chilperic to seek the episcopate, alleging many crimes against the bishop and adding, “You may know that what I say is true, most glorious king, because he feared death for his crimes and crossed into your brother’s kingdom.” Chilperic did not believe them and ordered them to return to the city. Meanwhile the citizens, grieving at their pastor’s absence and recognizing that everything done against him sprang from envy and greed, seized the archdeacon and his accomplice, punished them, and petitioned the king to restore their bishop. The king sent envoys to his brother asserting that he had found no crime in the bishop. King Guntram, being kindly and readily moved to compassion, gave Aetherius many gifts and supplied letters to all the bishops of his kingdom, asking them to console the exile in some way for God’s sake. As he traveled through the cities, God’s bishops gave him so much clothing and gold that he could scarcely carry it to the city that received him. The apostle’s words were fulfilled: “All things work together for good to those who love God.” His exile brought him riches, and his banishment brought him great wealth. When he returned, the citizens received him with great honor, weeping for joy and blessing God, who had at last restored so great a bishop to the Church.

### 37. Of the slaying of the abbot Lupentius of Javols

<a id="hist-06-en-037"></a>
[**[037]**](#hist-06-la-037) Lupentius, abbot of the basilica of the holy martyr Privatus in the city of Javols, was summoned by Queen Brunhilda and came before her. It is said that Innocentius, count of that city, had accused him of uttering something profane against the queen. The case was examined, and since he was found guilty of no treason, he was ordered to depart. But when he began his journey, he was again seized by the count, taken to the villa of Pontico, and subjected to many tortures. Released once more to return home, he pitched his tent above the river Aisne, where his enemy again attacked him. Overpowering him by force, Innocentius cut off his head, placed it in a sack weighted with stones, and cast it into the river; he tied the rest of the body to a rock and plunged it into the depths.

A few days later Lupentius appeared to certain shepherds. His body was thus drawn from the river and committed to burial. While the funeral necessities were being prepared and no one among the people knew whose body it was, especially since the severed head could not be found, an eagle suddenly arrived, lifted the sack from the riverbed, and placed it on the bank. The astonished bystanders seized the sack and carefully examined its contents, finding the severed head. It was then buried with the rest of his limbs. It is said that a divinely sent light still appears there, and if a sick person faithfully prays at his tomb, he departs restored to health.

### 38. Of the death of Bishop Theodosius and of his successor

<a id="hist-06-en-038"></a>
[**[038]**](#hist-06-la-038) Theodosius, bishop of Rodez, who had succeeded Saint Dalmatius, died. Such intense rivalries and scandals arose over the episcopate in that church that it was almost stripped of its sacred liturgical vessels and all its better property. The priest Transobadus was rejected, and Innocentius, count of Gévaudan, was chosen for the episcopate with Queen Brunhilda’s support. Immediately after obtaining the episcopate, he began to attack Ursicinus, bishop of Cahors, claiming that Ursicinus retained dioceses belonging to the church of Rodez. The dispute continued to grow for several years until Innocentius, sitting with his metropolitan and his fellow provincial bishops in the city of Clermont, issued a judgment that he should recover the parishes which the church of Rodez was not remembered ever to have held. This was done.

### 39. Of the death of Bishop Remigius and his successor

<a id="hist-06-en-039"></a>
[**[039]**](#hist-06-la-039) Remigius, bishop of Bourges, died. After his death the greater part of the city was consumed in a severe fire, and the buildings that had survived the hostilities perished there. Sulpicius was then chosen for the episcopate in that city with King Guntram’s favor. When many seekers of the episcopate offered gifts, the king is said to have answered, “It is not the custom of my rule to sell the priesthood for a price, nor is it yours to purchase it with rewards. Let me not be branded with the infamy of shameful gain, and let you not be compared with Simon Magus. By God’s foreknowledge Sulpicius will be your bishop.” Sulpicius was accordingly admitted to the clergy and received the episcopate of the church mentioned above. He was a man of very noble birth, one of the foremost senators of Gaul, well instructed in rhetoric and second to none in the metrical arts. He caused the synod I mentioned above to be held concerning the parishes of Cahors.

### 40. Our altercation with the heretic

<a id="hist-06-en-040"></a>
[**[040]**](#hist-06-la-040) An envoy named Oppila arrived from Spain bearing many gifts for King Chilperic. The king of Spain feared that Childebert would raise an army to avenge his sister’s injury, because Leovigild had seized and imprisoned his son Hermenegild, who had married Childebert’s sister, leaving the woman with the Greeks. When this envoy came to Tours on the holy day of Easter, I asked whether he belonged to my religion. He replied that he believed what Catholics believe. He then proceeded with me to church and attended the solemn Mass, but neither exchanged the kiss of peace with my people nor communicated in the sacred offerings. It was thus recognized that he had lied in claiming to be Catholic. Nevertheless, he accepted an invitation to dinner.

When I anxiously asked what he believed, he answered, “I believe that the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit are of one power.” I replied, “If you believe this as you assert, what prevented you from communicating in the offerings I make to God?” He said, “Because you do not render glory correctly. Following the apostle Paul, I say, ‘Glory to God the Father through the Son,’ whereas you say, ‘Glory to the Father and to the Son and to the Holy Spirit,’ although the teachers of the churches teach that the Father was proclaimed to the world through the Son. As Paul himself says, ‘To the King of the ages, immortal and invisible, the only God, be honor and glory forever and ever, through Jesus Christ our Lord.’”

I answered, “I think no Catholic is unaware that the Father was proclaimed through the Son. But the Son so preached the Father in the world that he also revealed himself as God by his miracles. God the Father needed to send the Son to earth to reveal God because the world had not believed the prophets and patriarchs or even the Lawgiver himself, so that it might at least believe the Son. Glory must therefore be given to God with the persons distinctly signified. I say, ‘Glory to God the Father, who sent the Son; glory to God the Son, who redeemed the world with his blood; glory to God the Holy Spirit, who sanctifies man now redeemed.’ You who say, ‘Glory to the Father through the Son,’ take glory away from the Son, as though he were not glorious with the Father, because he proclaimed the Father to the world.

“The Son, as I have said, proclaimed the Father in the world, but many did not believe. As John the Evangelist says, ‘He came to his own, and his own did not receive him. But to all who received him he gave power to become children of God, to those who believe in his name.’ You disparage the apostle Paul and do not understand his meaning. Observe how cautiously he speaks, according to each person’s capacity, and how he preaches among unbelieving peoples so as not to appear to impose a heavy burden. To some he says, ‘I gave you milk to drink, not solid food, for you were not yet able, nor are you able even now. Solid food belongs to the perfect.’ To others he says, ‘I preached nothing to you except Christ, and him crucified.’ Now, heretic, do you wish, because Paul preached only Christ crucified, to make me doubt that he rose again? Observe instead his caution and see the skill in what he says to others whom he saw to be stronger in faith: ‘Even if I have known Christ crucified, now I know him so no longer.’ Therefore, you accuser of Paul, deny, if you have become so demented in mind, that Christ was even crucified. But I beg you, abandon these notions and hear better counsel. Apply salve to your sore eyes and receive the light of apostolic preaching. Paul spoke more humbly to men in order to raise them to the heights of a loftier faith, as he says elsewhere, ‘I have become all things to all men, so that I might gain all.’ Shall mortal man not give glory to the Son, whom the Father himself glorified from heaven not once but twice and three times?

“Hear what he speaks from heaven when the Son was baptized beneath John’s hand and the Holy Spirit descended: ‘This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased.’ But if your ears are stopped so that you cannot hear this, believe what the apostles heard on the mountain when Jesus was transfigured in glory and spoke with Moses and Elijah. From the shining cloud the Father said, ‘This is my most beloved Son; hear him.’”

The heretic answered, “In none of these testimonies does the Father speak of the Son’s glory; he only shows that he is the Son.” I replied, “If you understand them in this way, I will offer another testimony in which the Father rendered the Son glorious. When the Lord approached his Passion and said, ‘Father, glorify your Son, so that your Son may glorify you,’ what did the Father answer from heaven? Did he not say, ‘I have both glorified him and will glorify him again’? Behold, the Father glorifies him with his own voice, and you try to take his glory away. You show the will, but you have no power to accomplish it. Since you are an accuser of the apostle Paul, hear Paul himself—or rather Christ speaking in him: ‘Let every tongue confess that the Lord Jesus Christ is in the glory of God the Father.’ If his glory is now shared with the Father and he dwells in the same glory in which the Father dwells, how do you dishonor him as though without glory? Why should glory not be rendered among men to him who reigns in heaven with glory equal to the Father’s? I therefore confess Christ, the Son of God, as true God; because the Godhead is one, the glory also is one.”

After this they fell silent and the argument ceased. Oppila went to King Chilperic, presented the gifts sent by the king of Spain, and returned to Spain.

### 41. How King Chilperic went with his treasures to Cambrai

<a id="hist-06-en-041"></a>
[**[041]**](#hist-06-la-041) When King Chilperic learned that his brother Guntram had made peace with his nephew Childebert and that they intended together to take from him the cities he had violently seized, he departed with all his treasures for the city of Cambrai, taking with him all his best possessions. He sent messengers to the dukes and counts of the cities directing them to repair the city walls, enclose their property, wives, and children within their protecting walls, and, if necessary, resist manfully so that the opposing party might not harm them. He added, “If you lose anything, you will acquire greater things when I take vengeance on my enemies,” not knowing that the accomplishment of victories rests in God’s hand. He repeatedly raised an army and then ordered it to rest within its own boundaries. In those days a son had been born to him, whom he ordered to be reared at the villa of Victoriacum, saying, “Lest perchance, if he is seen publicly, some evil befall him and he die!”

### 42. How Childebert went into Italy

<a id="hist-06-en-042"></a>
[**[042]**](#hist-06-la-042) King Childebert went into Italy. When the Lombards heard this, they feared being slaughtered by his army, submitted to his rule, gave him many gifts, and promised to be faithful and subject members of his party. Having accomplished everything he wished with them, the king returned to Gaul. He ordered an army to be raised and directed it to go into Spain, but the campaign was abandoned. Some years earlier he had received fifty thousand solidi from the Emperor Maurice to drive the Lombards out of Italy. When the emperor heard that Childebert had made peace with them, he demanded the money back; but Childebert, confident in his own resources, refused even to answer the demand.

### 43. Of the kings of Galicia

<a id="hist-06-en-043"></a>
[**[043]**](#hist-06-la-043) Events also occurred in Galicia that will be mentioned later. When Hermenegild, as I said above, was hostile to his father and living with his wife in a Spanish city, relying on the support of the emperor and of Mir, king of Galicia, he learned that his father was approaching with an army. He took counsel how to repel or kill him as he came, not knowing in his wretchedness that divine judgment hung over him for plotting such things against his father, however heretical his father was. After deliberation he selected three hundred armed men from many thousands and enclosed them in the fortress of Osser, in whose church springs are divinely filled. He intended that his father, terrified and exhausted by their first attack, might be more easily overcome by the larger force of inferior troops. When King Leovigild discovered these snares, he was greatly troubled in thought. “If I go there with my whole army,” he said, “the enemy forces will mass together and I will be cruelly wounded by their javelins. But if I go with a few, I cannot overcome this band of strong men. Nevertheless, I will go with all my forces.” He approached the place, overthrew the men, and burned the fortress, as I have already mentioned.

After gaining the victory, Leovigild learned that King Mir was encamped against him with an army. He surrounded Mir and exacted oaths that he would be faithful in the future. They exchanged gifts and each returned home. After Mir returned to his own country, he took to his bed a few days later and died, having been weakened by the bad waters and unhealthy air of Spain. On his death his son Eurich sought King Leovigild’s friendship, gave oaths as his father had done, and received the kingdom of Galicia. That same year his kinsman Audica, who was betrothed to Eurich’s sister, came with an army. He seized Eurich, made him a cleric, and ordered him to receive the offices of deacon and presbyter. Audica then took his father-in-law’s wife and obtained the kingdom of Galicia. Leovigild captured his son Hermenegild and brought him in chains to Toledo, condemning him to exile, but he could not rescue Hermenegild’s wife from the Greeks.

### 44. Of divers signs

<a id="hist-06-en-044"></a>
[**[044]**](#hist-06-la-044) The locusts that had devastated the province of Carpetania for five years advanced that year into another neighboring province, occupying the public road. The area they covered extended a hundred and fifty miles in length and a hundred miles in width. Many portents appeared in Gaul that year, and the people suffered many disasters. Roses were seen in January. A great circle of mixed colors appeared around the sun, like the heavenly rainbow usually displayed when rain descends. Frost severely scorched the vineyards, and a subsequent storm devastated vines and crops in many places. Immense drought consumed what the hail had left. Some vineyards showed little fruit and others none, so that men, angry with God, opened the approaches to their vineyards and admitted cattle and beasts of burden, wretchedly mingling curses with their prayers and saying, “May no vine ever grow in these vineyards again!” Trees that had produced apples in July bore other fruit in September. The cattle plague again grew stronger, so that scarcely anything remained.

### 45. Of the nuptials of Rigunth, Chilperic's daughter

<a id="hist-06-en-045"></a>
[**[045]**](#hist-06-la-045) Meanwhile, when the Kalends of September arrived, a great embassy of the Goths approached King Chilperic. He had already returned to Paris and ordered many families to be taken from the royal estates and placed in wagons. He also ordered many who wept and refused to go to be thrust into custody so that he could send them with his daughter more easily. It is said that many, in their bitterness and fear of being taken from their own parents, took their lives with a noose. Son was separated from father and mother from daughter, and they departed with deep groaning and curses. The lamentation in the city of Paris was comparable to the mourning of Egypt. Many people of high rank who were compelled by force to go made wills, assigning their property to churches and asking that, as soon as the girl entered Spain, these wills be opened as though they were already buried.

Meanwhile King Childebert’s envoys arrived in Paris and solemnly adjured King Chilperic to take nothing from the cities of his father’s kingdom that he held, not to bestow any of Childebert’s treasures upon his daughter, and not to dare touch any slaves, horses, teams of oxen, or anything of the kind belonging to those cities. It is said that one of these envoys was secretly killed, though by whom is unknown; suspicion nevertheless fell upon the king. King Chilperic promised not to touch any of these things, summoned the leading Franks and his remaining faithful men, and celebrated his daughter’s marriage. He handed her over to the Gothic envoys and gave her a great treasure. Her mother also produced an immense weight of gold, silver, and garments, so that the king, seeing it, thought nothing had been left to him. Seeing the king disturbed, the queen turned to the Franks and said, “Do not think, men, that anything here comes from the treasures of former kings. Everything you see has been offered from my own property. The most glorious king bestowed much upon me; I gathered some by my own labor; and from the houses granted to me I acquired much in both produce and tribute. You also frequently enriched me with your gifts, which are the source of these things you now see before you. Nothing here comes from the public treasures.” The king’s mind was thus deceived. So great was the quantity of goods that fifty wagons carried the gold, silver, and other ornaments. The Franks offered many gifts: some gold, others silver, some horses, and many garments; each gave a donation as he could.

After tears and kisses, as the girl took her leave and passed through the gate, one of her carriage’s axles broke, and everyone cried, “An evil hour!” Some took this as an omen. When she had set out from Paris, she ordered tents to be pitched eight miles from the city. During the night fifty men rose, seized a hundred excellent horses, the same number of golden bridles, and two great chains, and escaped by flight to King Childebert. Throughout the journey anyone who could slip away fled, taking whatever he could seize. A great store of provisions was gathered for the journey from various cities; the king ordered nothing to be supplied from his own treasury, but everything to be exacted from the poor.

Because the king suspected that his brother or nephew might prepare ambushes for the girl on the road, he ordered her to travel protected by an army. With her were the magnificent Duke Bobo, son of Mummolinus, and his wife, acting as escort, Domegisel and Ansovald, and the mayor of the palace Waddo, who had formerly governed the county of Saintes; the rest of the multitude numbered more than four thousand. The other dukes and chamberlains who had set out with her turned back at Poitiers, while these continued on their journey as best they could. Along the road such plunder and pillage occurred that it can scarcely be described. They stripped the poor people’s little dwellings and devastated the vineyards, cutting down the vines and carrying them off with their grapes. They took cattle and whatever else they could find, leaving nothing along the road they traveled. The words spoken through the prophet Joel were fulfilled: “What the locust left, the creeping locust ate; what the creeping locust left, the young locust ate; and what the young locust left, the blight ate.” So it was at this time: the storm destroyed what the frost left, the drought burned what the storm left, and the army carried off what the drought left.

### 46. Of the death of King Chilperic

<a id="hist-06-en-046"></a>
[**[046]**](#hist-06-la-046) While the wedding party was proceeding with this plunder, Chilperic, the Nero and Herod of my time, went to the villa of Chelles, about a hundred stades from the city of Paris, and engaged in hunting. One day, returning from the hunt after nightfall, he was dismounting from his horse and resting one hand on a servant’s shoulder when a man approached and struck him with a knife beneath the armpit. With a second blow the assassin pierced his belly. A great quantity of blood immediately poured from both his mouth and the opening of the wound, and he poured out his wicked spirit. The preceding narrative shows what malice he practiced. He repeatedly devastated and burned many regions, feeling no grief over them but rather rejoicing, as Nero once sang tragedies amid the burning of the palace. He very often punished men unjustly for their property. In his time few clerics in any way obtained the episcopate.

He was devoted to gluttony, and his god was his belly. He asserted that no one was wiser than himself. He composed two books in verse, as though imitating Sedulius, but their feeble lines cannot stand on any metrical feet. In his ignorance he placed short syllables where long ones belonged and established long syllables where short ones belonged. He also wrote other little works, hymns, and Masses that cannot be accepted by any standard of reason. He detested the causes of the poor. He continually blasphemed the Lord’s priests, and in private he ridiculed and joked about no one more than the bishops of the churches. He called one fickle, another proud, one extravagant, another dissolute, one elated, and another swollen with arrogance. He hated nothing more than the churches. He often said, “Behold, my treasury has remained poor; behold, my riches have been transferred to the churches. No one reigns except the bishops alone. My honor will perish and has been transferred to the bishops of the cities.” Acting in this spirit, he often annulled wills written in favor of churches and frequently trampled upon his own father’s ordinances, thinking that no one remained to preserve his father’s wishes. No lust or luxury could be conceived in thought that he had not perpetrated in deed, and he was always devising new means of injuring the people. If he found anyone guilty at this time, he ordered his eyes to be torn out. In the instructions he sent to judges for his own advantage he added, “If anyone despises my commands, let him be punished by the plucking out of his eyes.” He never loved anyone sincerely and was loved by no one; therefore, when he breathed his last, all his own people abandoned him.

Mallulf, bishop of Senlis, who had already been sitting in his tent for three days without being able to see Chilperic, came when he heard that the king had been killed. He washed the body, clothed it in better garments, spent the night in hymns, placed it on a boat, and buried it in the basilica of Saint Vincent at Paris, leaving Queen Fredegund in the church.

*INCIPIT LIBER VI., INCHOATUS AB ANNO SEXTO CHILDEBERTHI REGIS.*

## 1. Quod Childeberthus ad Chilpericum transiit, et de fuga Mummoli.

<a id="hist-06-la-001"></a>
[**[001]**](#hist-06-en-001) Anno igitur sexto regni sui Childeberthus rex, reiectam pacem Gunthchramni regis, cum Chilperico coniunctus est. Non post multum tempus Gogo moritur; in cuius locum Wandelenus subrogatur. Mummolus a regno Guntchramni fuga dilabitur et se infra murorum Avennicorum monitione concludit. Apud Lugdunum sinodus episcoporum coniungitur, diversarum causarum altercationis incidens neglegentioresque iudicio damnans. Sinodus ad regem revertitur, multa de fuga Mummoli ducis, nonnulla de discordiis tractans.

## 2. De legatis Chilperici ab Oriente reversis.

<a id="hist-06-la-002"></a>
[**[002]**](#hist-06-en-002) Interea legati Chilperici regis, qui ante triennium ad Tiberium imperatorem abierant, regressi sunt non sine grave damno atque labore. Nam cum Massiliensim portum propter regum discordias adire ausi non essent, Agathae urbem, quae in Gothorum regno sita est, advenerunt. Sed priusquam litus attingerent, navis acta vento, inpulsa terris in frustra minuitur. Legati vero cum pueris se in pericolo cernentes, arreptis tabulis, vix ripae relati sunt, multis puerorum amissis; sed plurimi evaserunt. Res autem, quas unda litoris invexerat, incolae rapuerunt; ex quibus quae melius fuit recipientes, ad Chilpericum regem retulerunt. Multa tamen ex his Agathensis secum retenuerunt. Tunc ego Novigentum villa ad occursum regis abieram; ibique nobis rex missurium magnum, quod ex auro gemmisque fabricaverat in quinquagenta librarum pondere, ostendit, dicens: 'Ego haec ad exornandam atque nobilitandam Francorum gentem feci. Sed et plurima adhuc, si vita comis fuerit, faciam'. Aureus etiam singularum librarum pondere, quos imperatur misit, ostendit, habentes ab una parte iconicam imperatoris pictam et scriptum in circulo: TIBERII CONSTANTINI PERPETUI AUGUSTI; ab alia vero parte habentes quadrigam et ascensorem contenentesque scriptum: GLORIA ROMANORUM. Multa enim et alia ornamenta, quae a legatis sunt exhibita, ostendit.

## 3. De legatis Childeberthi ad Chilpericum.

<a id="hist-06-la-003"></a>
[**[003]**](#hist-06-en-003) Denique cum apud eandem villam commoraretur, Egidius Remensis episcopus cum primis Childeberthi proceribus in legationem ad Chilpericum regem venit; ibique conlocutione facta, ut, ablato Gunthchramni regis regno, hi se coniungere debeant in pace, ait Chilpericus rex: 'Filii mihi, peccatis increscentibus, non remanserunt, nec mihi nunc alius superest heres nisi fratris mei Sygiberthi filius, id est Childeberthus rex, ideoque in omnibus quae laborare potuero hic heres existat; tantum dum advixero liceat mihi sine scrupulo aut disceptatione cuncta tenere'. At illi gratias agentes, pactionibus subscriptis, ea quae locuti fuerant firmaverunt et ad Childeberthum cum magnis muneribus sunt regressi. Quibus discedentibus, Chilpericus rex Leudovaldum episcopum cum primis regni sui dirixit. Qui, data susceptaque de pace sacramenta pactionibusque firmatis, mune ati regressi sunt.

## 4. Qualiter Lupus a regno Childeberthi fugatus est.

<a id="hist-06-la-004"></a>
[**[004]**](#hist-06-en-004) Lupus vero dux Campanensis, cum iam diu a diversis fategaretur et spoliaretur assiduae et praesertim ab Ursione et Berthefredo, ad extremum conventione facta ut occideretur, commoverunt exercitum contra eum. Quod cernens Brunichildis regina, condolens fidelis sui insecutiones iniustas, praecingens se viriliter, inrupit medios hostium cuneos, dicens: 'Nolite, o viri, nolite malum hoc facere, nolite persequere innocentem; nolite pro uno hominem committere proelium, quo solatium regionis intereat'. Haec illa loquente, respondit Ursio: 'Recede a nobis, o mulier. Sufficiat tibi sub viro tenuisse regnum; nunc autem filius tuus regnat, regnumque eius non tua, sed nostra tuitione salvatur. Tu vero recede a nobis, ne te ungulae equorum nostrorum cum terra confodiant'. Haec et alia cum diutissime inter se protulissent, obtenuit reginae industria, ne pugnarent. Tamen ab illo loco discedentes, inruerunt in domibus Lupi, et derepto omne praesidio, fingentes se illud in thesauro regis recondere, suis eum domibus intulerunts, intendentes Lupo minas atque dicentes: 'Vivus virtute nostrae non evadit'. At ille cernens se in discrimine, tutatam infra urbis Lugduni Clavati murus coniugem suam, ad Guntchramnum regem confugit. A quo benigne susceptus, cum eo latuit, expectans, ut Childeberthus ad legitimam perveniret aetatem.

## 5. Altercatio cum Iudaeo.

<a id="hist-06-la-005"></a>
[**[005]**](#hist-06-en-005) Igitur Chilpericus rex, cum adhuc apud supradictam villam moraretur, inpedimenta movere praecipiens, Parisius venire disponet. Ad quem cum iam vale dicturus acciderem, Iudaeus quidam Priscus nomen, qui ei ad species quoemendas familiaris erat, advenit. Cuius caesariem rex blande adpraehensa manu, ait ad me, dicens: 'Veni, sacerdus Dei, et inponi manum super eum'. Illo quoque renitenti, ait rex: 'O mens dura et generatio semper incredula, quae non intellegit Dei filium sibi prophetarum vocibus repromissum, non intellegit eclesiastica mystiria in suis sacrificiis figurata'. Haec eo dicente, Iudaeus ait: 'Deus non egit coniugium neque prole ditatur neque ullum consortem regni habere patitur, qui per Moysen ait: Videte, videte, quia ego sum Dominus, et absque me non est Deus. Ego occidam et ego vivere faciam; percutiam, et ego sanabo'. Ad haec rex ait: 'Deus ab spiritali utero Filium genuit sempiternum, non aetati iuniori, non potestati minori, de quo ipse ait: Ex utero ante lucifero genui te. Hunc ergo ante saecula natum in novissimis saeculis mundo misit sanatorem, sicut ait propheta tuus: Misit Verbum suum, et sanavit eos. Quod autem ais, quia ipse non generit, audi prophetam tuum dicentem ex voce dominica: Numquid ego, qui alius parere facio, ipse non paream? Haec enim de populo, qui in eum per fidem renascitur, ait'. Ad haec Iudaeus respondit: 'Numquid Deus homo fieri potuit aut de mulieri nasci, verberibus subdi, morte damnare?' Ad haec rege tacentem, in medio me ingerens, dixi: 'Ut Deus, Dei filius, homo fierit, non suae, sed nostrae necessitatis extetit causa. Nam captivum peccato homine et diaboli servitute subiectum redemere non potuerat, nisi hominem adsumpsisset. Ego vero non de euangeliis et apostolo, quae non credis, sed de tuis libris testimonia praebens, proprio te mucrune confodiam, sicut quondam David Goliam legitur trucidasse. Igitur quod Deus homo futurus esset, audi prophetam tuum: Et Deus, inquid, et Homo, et quis cognovit eum? Et alibi: Hic Deus noster, et non repotabitur alius praeter eum; qui invenit omnem viam scientiae et dedit illam Iacob puero suo et Israhel dilecto suo. Post haec in terris visus est et cum hominibus conversatus est. Quod autem de virgine nascitur, audi similiter prophetam tuum dicentem: Ecce virgo in utero concipiet et pariet filium, et vocabitur nomen eius Emmanuhel, quod est interpraetatum: Nobiscum Deus. Quod verberibus subdi, quod clavis adfigi deberit et aliis quoque iniuriis vel subiacere vel adfici, alius propheta ait: Foderunt manus meas et pedes meos; diviserunt sibi vestimenta mea, et citera. Et iterum: Dederunt in esca mea fel, et in siti mea potaverunt me aceto. Et quod per ipsum crucis patibulum labentem mundum et diaboli dicione subiectum restituerit in regno suo, idem David ait: Dominus regnavit a ligno. Non quod antea non regnaverit apud Patrem, sed super populum, quem a diabuli servituti liberaverat, rudem regnum accepit'. Iudaeos ad haec respondit: 'Quae Deo fuit necessitas, ut ista pateretur?' Cui ego: 'Iam dixi tibi, Deus hominem creavit innoxium, sed astu serpentis circumscriptus, praevaricatur praecepti factus est; et ideo a side paradisi eiectus, mundanis laboribus deputatus est. Qui mortem unigeniti Dei Christi Deo reconciliatus est Patri'. Iudaeos dixit: 'Non poterat Deus mittere prophetas aut apostulos, qui eum ad viam revocarent salutis, nisi ipse humiliatus fuisset in carne?' Ad haec ego: 'A principio genus semper diliquit humanum, quem numquam terruit nec submersio diluvii nec incendium Sodomae nec plagae Aegypti nec miraculum maris Iordanisque divisi; qui semper legi Dei restitit, prophetis non crededit, et non solum non crededit, verum etiam ipsos praedicatores paenitentiae interemit. Ideo nisi ipse eum discendisset redemere, haec explere non potuerat alter. Cuius nos nativitati renati, baptismo abluti, vulnere curati, resurrectione erecti, ascensione glorificati sumus. Quod autem morbis nostris medere venturus erat, propheta tuus ait: Livore eius sanati sumus; et alibi: Ipse peccata nostra portabit et orabit pro transgressoribus; et iterum: Sicut oves ad occisionem ductus est; et quasi agnus coram tundente se sine voce, sic non aperuit os suum. In humiliatione iudicium eius sublatum est. Generationem eius quis enarravit? Dominus exercituum nomen eius. De hoc et Iacob ille, de cuius te iactas venisse generationem, in illa filii sui Iudae benedictione, quasi ad ipsum Christum, filium Dei, loquens, ait: Adorabunt te filii patris tui. Catulus leonis Iuda. De germine, fili mi, ascendisti. Recubans dormisti quasi leo, quasi catulus leonis. Qui suscitavit eum? Pulchriores oculi eius vino et dentes eius lacte candidiores. Quis?, inquid, suscitabit eum? Et licet ipse dixerit: Potestatem habeo ponendi animam meam, et potestatem habeo iterum sumendi eam, tamen Paulus apostulus ait: Qui non credederit, quod Deus illum suscitavit a mortuis, salvus esse non poterit'. Haec et alia nobis dicentibus, numquam conpunctus est miser ad credendum. Tunc rex, silenti illo, cum viderit eum his sermonibus non conpungi, ad me conversus, postulat, ut, accepta benedictione, discederit. Ait enim: 'Dicam', inquid, tibi, o sacerdus, quod Iacob dixit ad angelum, qui ei loquebatur: Non demittam te, nisi benedixeris mihi'. Et haec dicens, aquam manibus porregi iubet. Quibus ablutis, facta oratione, accepto pane, gratias Deo agentes, et ipsi accepimus et regi porreximus; austoque mero, vale dicentes discessimus. Rex vero, ascenso equite, Parisius est regressus cum coniuge et filia vel omni familia sua.

## 6. De sancto Hospicio reclauso et abstinentia vel miraculis eius.

<a id="hist-06-la-006"></a>
[**[006]**](#hist-06-en-006) Fuit autem apud urbem Nicensim eo tempore Hospicius reclausus magnae abstinentiae, qui constrictus catenis ad purum corpus ferreis, induto desuper cilicio, nihil aliud quam purum panem cum paucis dactalis comedebat. In diebus autem quadraginsimae de radicibus herbarum Aegyptiarum, quas heremitae utuntur, exhibentibus sibi negotiatoribus, alibatui. Et primum quidem ius in quo coxerant auriens, ipsas sumebat in posterum. Magnas enim per eum Dominus virtutes dignatus est operare. Nam quodam tempore, revelante sibi Spiritu sancto, adventum Langobardorum in Galleis hoc modo praedixit: 'Venient', inquid, 'Langobardi in Galleis et devastabunt civitates septem, eo quod increverit malitia eorum in conspectu Domini, quia nullus est intellegens, nullus est qui faciat bonum, quc ira Dei placetur. Est enim omnes populus infidelis, periuriis deditus, furtis obnoxius, in homicidiis prumptus, ad quibus nullus iustitiae fructus ullatenus gliscit. Non decimae dantur, non pauper alitur, non tegitur nudus, non peregrinus hospitio recipitur aut cibo sufficiente sacietur. Ideo haec plaga supervenit. Nunc autem dico vobis: Congerete omnem substantiam vestram infra murorum septa, ne a Langobardis deripiatur, et vos ipsos in locis firmissimis cummonite'. Haec eo loquente, omnes obstupefacti et vale dicentes, cum magna admiratione ad propria sunt regressi. Monachis quoque dixit: 'Abscidite et vos a loco, auferentes vobiscum quae habetis. Ecce enim adpropinquat gens quam praedixi!' Dicentibus autem illis: 'Non relinquemus te, sanctissime pater', ait: 'Nolite timere pro me; futurum est enim, ut inferant mihi iniurias, sed non nocebunt usque ad mortem'. Discedentibus autem monachis, venit gens illa; et dum cuncta quae repperit vastat, pervenit ad locum ubi sanctus Dei reclausus erat. At ille per fenestram turris ostendit se eis. Ille vero circumeuntes turrem, aditum, per quem ingrederentur ad eum, invenire non poterant. Tunc duo ascendentes, detexerunt tectum, et videntes eum vinctum catenis indutumque cilicio, dicunt: 'Hic malefactor est et homicidium fecit, ideo in his legaminibus vinctus tenitur'. Vocatumque interpraetem, sciscitantur ab eo, quid male ficerit, ut tale supplitio artaretur. At ille fatetur, se homicidam esse omnesque criminis reum. Tunc unus, extracto gladio, ut caput eius libraret, dextera in ipso ictu suspensa diriguit, nec eam ad se potuit revocare. Tunc gladium laxans, terrae deiecit. Haec videntes socii eius, clamorem in caelo dederunt, flagitantes a sancto, ut, quid agere poterent, clementer insinuaret. Ipse vero inposito salutis signo brachium sanitati restituit. Ille autem in eodem loco conversus, tonsorato capite, fidelissimus monachus nunc habetur. Duo vero duces, qui eum audierunt, incolomes patriae redditi sunt; qui vero contempserunt praeceptum eius, miserabiliter in ipsa provintia sunt defuncti. Multi autem ex ipsis a daemoniis correpti, clamabant: 'Cur nos, sancte beatissime, sic crucias et incendis?' Sed inpositam eis manum, mundabat eos. Post haec homo erat Andecavensis incola, qui per nimiam febrem eloquium pariter auditumque perdederat; et cum febrae convaluisset, surdus permanebat ac mutus. Igitur diaconus ex provintia illa Romam directus est, ut beatorum apostolorum pignora vel reliquorum sanctorum, qui urbem illam muniunt, exhiberet. Quod cum ad parentes infirmi illius pervenisset, rogant, ut eum sibi comitem iteneris sumere dignaretur, confisi, quod, si beatissimorum apostolorum adiret sepulchra, protinus possit adsequi medicinam. Euntibus autem illis, venerunt ad locum, ubi beatus Hospicius habitabat. Quo salutato ac deoscolato, causas iteneris diaconus pandit ac proficisci se Romam indecat seque his qui sancto viro de naucleris amici essent commendare deposcit. Cumque ibi adhuc moraretur, sensit vir beatus per spiritum Domini adesse virtutem; et ait diacono: 'Infirmum, qui comes tui nunc est iteneris, rogo ut meis conspectibus repraesentis'. At ille nihil moratus, velociter ad metatum vadit invenitque infirmum febre plenum, qui per nutum aures suas dare tinnitum indecabat; adpraehensumque ducit ad sanctum Dei. At ille adpraehensa manu caesariem, adtraxit capud illius in finestram, adsumptumque oleum benedictione sanctificatum, tenens manu sinistram linguam eius, ori verticique capitis infudit, dicens: 'In nomine domini mei Iesu Christi aperiantur aures tuae, reseretque os tuum virtus illa, qui quondam ab homine surdo et muto noxium eiecit daemonium'. Et haec dicens, interrogat nomen. Ille vero clara voce ait: 'Sic dicor'. Cum haec vidisset diaconus, ait: 'Gratias tibi inmensas refero, Christi, qui talia per servum tuum dignaris ostendere. Quaerebam Petrum, quaerebam Paulum Laurentiumque vel reliquos, qui Romam proprio cruore inlustrant; hic omnes repperi, hic cunctos inveni'. Haec eo cum maximo fletu et admiratione dicente, vir Dei omni intentione vanam vitans gloriam, ait: 'Sile, sile, dilectissime frater, non haec ego facio, sed ille qui mundum ex nihilo condedit, qui, pro nobis hominem suscipiens, caecis visum, surdis auditum, mutis praestat eloquium; qui leprosis cutem pristinam, mortuis vitam et omnibus infirmis adfluentem medicinam indulget'. Tunc diaconus gaudens et vale dicens, abscessit cum comitibus suis. Quibus discedentibus, homo quidam Dominicus, – sic enim erat viro nomen – a nativitate caecus, advenit ad istius miraculi virtutem probandam. Qui dum in monastirio duobus aut tribus mensibus resederet, oratione ac ieiuniis vacans, tandem vocat eum ad se vir Dei et ait: 'Vis recipere visum?' Cui ille ait: 'Voluntas', inquid, 'mea erat ignota cognuscere. Nam quae sit lux ignoro. Unum tantum scio, quod ab omnibus conlaudatur; ego autem ab inicio aetatis meae usque nunc videre non merui'. Tunc cum oleo benedicto super oculus eius crucem sanctam faciens, ait: 'In nomine Iesu Christi redemptores nostri aperiantur oculi tui'. Et statim aperti sunt oculi eius, et erat admirans cernensque magnalia Dei, quae in hoc mundo videbat. Dehinc mulier quaedam, quae, ut ipsa declamabat, tria habens daemonia, ad eum adducta est. Quam cum tactu sacro benedixisset atque ex oleo sancto crucem fronte eius inposuisset, eiectis daemonibus, purgata discessit. Sed et aliam puellam, ab spiritu inmundo vexatam, benedictione sanavit. Cum autem iam dies obitus eius adpropinquaret, vocavit ad se praepositum monastirii, dicens: 'Exibe ferramentum et inrumpe parietem et mitte nuntius ad episcopum civitatis, ut veniat ad me sepeliendum. Die enim tertia ab hoc egredior mundo et vado in requiem distinatam, quam mihi Dominus repromisit'. Haec eo dicente, misit praepositus ad episcopum civitatis Nicensis, qui ei haec nuntiarent. Post haec Crescens quidam venit ad fenestram, et videns eum catenis vinctum, vermibus plenum, ait: 'O domine mi, qualiter tam valida turmenta tollerare tam fortiter potes?' Cui ille ait: 'Confortat me ille, pro cuius nomine haec patior. Dico autem tibi, quia iam absolvor ab his vinculis et vado in requiem meam'. Adveniente autem die tertia, deposuit catenas, quibus vinctus erat, prostravit se in orationem; et cum diutissime cum lacrimis orasset, conlocans se super scamnum, extensis pedibus elevatisque ad caelum manibus, gratias agens Deo, tradedit spiritum. Et statim omnes vermes ille, qui sanctos artos perforabant, evanuerunt. Adveniens autem Austadius episcopus, beatum corpus studiosissime sepulturae mandavit. Haec omnia ab ipsius ore cognovi, quem superius mutum et surdum ab eo sanatum exposui. Qui multa mihi et alia de eius virtutibus narravit, sed prohibuit me res illa loqui, quia audivi vitam ipsius a multis fuisse conscriptam.

## 7. De transitu Ferreoli Ucecensis episcopi.

<a id="hist-06-la-007"></a>
[**[007]**](#hist-06-en-007) Eo tempore Ferreolus Ucecensis episcopus, magnae vir sanctitatis, obiit, plenus sapientia et intellectu; qui libros aliquos epistolarum, quasi Sidonium secutus, conposuit. Post cuius obitum Albinus ex praefecto per Dinamium rectorem Provinciae extra regis consilium suscepit episcopatum; quem non amplius quam tribus utens mensibus, cum ad hoc causa restitisset, ut removeretur, defunctus est. Iovinus iterum, qui quondam Provinciae rector fuerat, regium de episcopatum praeceptum accipit. Sed praevenit eum Marcellus diaconus, Felicis senatoris filius. Qui, convocatis conprovincialibus, per consilium Dinami episcopus ordinatus est. Sed et ipse inpulsatus deinceps a Iovino, ut removeretur, conclusus in civitate, virtute se defensare nitebatur; sed cum non valeret, muneribus vicit.

## 8. De Eparchio reclauso Aecolinensis urbis.

<a id="hist-06-la-008"></a>
[**[008]**](#hist-06-en-008) Obiit et Eparchius reclausus Ecolesinensis, vir magnificae sanctitatis, per quem Deus multa miracula ostendit; de quibus, relictis plurimis, pauca perstringam. Petrocoricae urbis incola fuit; sed post conversionem clericus factus, Ecolesinam veniens, cellulam sibi aedificavit. In qua, collectis paucis monachis, in oratione morabatur assidue; et si ei aliquid auri argentique offerebatur, aut in necessitatibus pauperum aut in redemptione captivorum distribuebat. Panes in cellula illa, eo vivente, coctus numquam fuit, sed a devotis, cum necessitas exegisset, inferebatur. Magnam enim catervam populorum de oblationibus devotorum redemit; pusularum malarum venenum crucis signum saepe compressit, daemonas de obsessis corporibus oratione abegit et iudicibus plerumque, ut culpabilibus ignoscerent, dulcedine profusa imperavit potius quam rogavit. Nam ita erat dulcis alloquio, ut ei negare non possent, cum fuisset indulgentiam deprecatus. Quodam vero tempore, dum pro furtum quis ad adpendendum deduceretur, qui et in alia multa scelera, tam in furtis quam in homicidiis, accusabatur ab incolis criminosus, et haec ei nuntiata fuissent, misit monachum suum ad deprecandum iudici, ut scilicet culpabilis ille vitae concederetur. Sed insultante vulgo atque vociferante, quod, si hic dimitteretur, neque regioni neque iudici possit esse consultum, dimitti non potuit. Interea extenditur ad trocleas, virgis ac fustibus caeditur et patibulo condemnatur. Cumque mestus monachus abbati renuntiasset: 'Vade', inquid, 'a longe, quia scito, quod, quem homo reddere noluit, Dominus suo munere redonabit. Tu vero, cum eum cadere videris, protinus adprehensum adducito in monasterium'. Monacho vero iussa complente, ille prosternitur in oratione et tam diu in lacrimis ad Deum fudit preces, quoadusque, disruptum obice cum catenis, terrae restitueretur adpensus. Tunc monachus adprehensum eum abbatis conspectibus incolomem repraesentat. At ille gratias Deo agens, comitem arcessiri iubet, dicens: 'Semper me benigno animo solitus eras audire, fili dilectissime; et cur hodie induratus hominem, pro cuius vita rogaveram, non laxasti?' Et ille: 'Libenter te', inquid, 'audio, sancte sacerdos; sed, insurgente vulgo, aliud facere non potui, timens super me seditionem moveri'. Et ille: 'Tu', inquid, 'me non audisti; Deus autem audire me dignatus est, et quem tu tradidisti morti, ille vitae restituit. En', inquid, 'coram te adstat sanus Haec eo dicente, prosternitur ad pedes comitis stupentis, quod videbat vivere quem in mortis interitu reliquisset. Haec ego ab ipsius comitis ore cognovi. Sed et alia multa fecit, quae insequi longum putavi. Post XLIIII vero annos reclusionis suae parumper febre pulsatus tradidit spiritum; protractusque a cellula, sepulturae mandatus est. Magnus autem conventus, ut diximus, de redemptis in eius processit exsequiis.

## 9. De Domnolo Cinomannorum episcopo.

<a id="hist-06-la-009"></a>
[**[009]**](#hist-06-en-009) Domnolus vero Cinomannorum episcopus aegrotare coepit. Tempore enim Chlothari regis apud Parisius ad basilicam sancti Laurenti gregi monasteriali praefuerat. Sed quoniam, Childeberto seniore vivente, semper Chlothario regi fidelis extitit et nuntius illius ad speculandum missus crebrius occulebat, praestolabatur rex locum, in quo pontificatus honorem acciperet. Migrante autem Avenniensis civitatis pontifice, istum illuc dare deliberaverat. Sed beatus Domnolus haec audiens, ad basilicam sancti Martini antistitis, ubi tunc Chlotharius rex ad orationem venerat, accessit, et nocte tota in vigiliis excubans, per priores qui aderant regi suggessionem intulit, ut non quasi captivus ab eius elongaretur aspectu, nec permitteret, simplicitatem illius inter senatores sophisticos ac iudices philosophicos fatigari, adserens, hunc locum humilitatis sibi esse potius quam honoris. Ad haec rex annuens, migrante Innocentio Cinomannorum episcopo, ipsum ecclesiae illi antistitem destinavit. Iam adsumpto episcopatu, talem se tantumque praebuit, ut in summae sanctitatis culmen evectus debili usum gressuum, caeco restituerit visum. Qui post XXII episcopati annos, dum se cerneret morbo regio calculoque gravissime fatigari, Theodulfum abbatem in loco suo praeelegit. Cuius assensum rex praebuit voluntatem, sed non multum post tempus, mutata sententia, in Badegisilum domus regiae maiorem transfertur electio. Qui tonsoratus, gradus quos clerici sortiuntur ascendens, post quadraginta diebus, migrante sacerdote, successit.

## 10. De basilica sancti Martini effracta.

<a id="hist-06-la-010"></a>
[**[010]**](#hist-06-en-010) His diebus basilica sancti Martini a furibus effracta fuit. Qui ponentes ad fenestram absidae cancellum, quod super tumulum cuiusdam defuncti erat, ascendentes per eum, effracta vitrea, sunt ingressi; auferentesque multum auri argentique vel palleorum olosericorum, abierunt, non metuentes super sanctum sepulchrum pedem ponere, ubi vix vel os applicare praesumimus. Sed virtus sancti voluit hanc temeritatem etiam cum iudicio manifestare terribili. Nam hi, perpetrato scelere, ade Burdegalensim civitatem venientes, orto scandalo, unus alterum interemit; sicque patefacto opere, furtum repertum est, ac de hospitale eorum argentum comminutum vel pallea sunt extracta. Quod cum regi Chilperico nuntiatum fuisset, iussit eos alligari vinculis et suo conspectui praesentari. Tunc ego metuens, ne ob illius causam homines morerentur, qui vivens in corpore pro perditorum vita saepius deprecatus est, epistolam regi precationis transmisi, ne, nostris non accusantibus, ad quos persecutio pertinebat, hi interficerentur. Quod ille benigne suscipiens, vitae restituit. Species vero, quae dissipatae fuerant, studiosissime componens, loco sancto reddi praecepit.

## 11. De Theodoro episcopo et Dinamio.

<a id="hist-06-la-011"></a>
[**[011]**](#hist-06-en-011) Apud Massiliense vero urbe Dinamius rector Provinciae graviter insidiari Theodoro episcopo coepit. At ille ad regem properare disponens, conprehensus ab eo, in medium civitatis tenetur, et graviter iniuriatus, tandem laxatus est. Clerici autem Massiliensis dolum cum Dinamio moliebantur, ut ab episcopatum eiceretur. Sed dum ad regem Childebertum ambularet, cum Iovino ex praefectum a Gunthramno rege deteneri iubetur. Quod audientes Massiliensis clerici, gaudio magno repleti, quod iam detineretur, iam deputaretur exilio, quod iam in hoc res perstitisset, ut numquam Massilia reverteretur, domos ecclesiae adprehendunt, ministeria describunt, regesturia reserant, prumptuaria expoliant omnesque res ecclesiae, tamquam si iam mortuus esset episcopus, pervadunt, diversa crimina de pontifice proloquentes, quae falsa Christo auspice deprehendi. Childebertus vero, postquam cum Chilperico pacificatus est, legatos ad Gunthramnum regem mittit, ut medietatem Massiliae, quam ei post obitum patris sui dederat, reddere deberet. Quod si nollet, noverit, se multa perditurum pro partis istius retentatione. Sed ille cum haec reddere nollet, vias claudi praecepit, ut nulli per regnum eius transeundi aditus panderetur. Haec cernens Childebertus, Gundulfum ex domestico duce facto, de genere senatorio, Massiliam dirigit. Qui cum non auderet ambulare iam per Guntchramni regnum, Turonus venit. Quem benigne susceptum recognosco matris meae avunculum esse, retentumque mecum quinque diebus, inpositisque necessariis, abire permisi. Ille vero progressus, Massiliam ingredi, obsistente Dinamio, non valebat. Sed nec episcopus, qui iam tunc cum Gundulfo iunxerat, in ecclesia sua recipiebatur. Dinamius autem una cum clericis portas obserat urbis, insultans pariter ac utrumque despiciens, episcopum scilicet et Gundulfum. Tandem ad colloquium ducis adscitus, in basilicam beati Stephani, quae urbe est proxima, venit. Ostiarii enim custodiebant aedis ingressum, ut, introeunte Dinamio, valvae protinus clauderentur. Quo facto, exclusae armatorum turbae post Dinamium ingredi nequiverunt. Quo non intellegente, dum diversa inter se super altarium conferunt, recedentes ab altario, salutatorium ingrediuntur. Introeunte cum liis Dinamio nudumque iam a suorum solatio terribiliter increpant; fugatisque satellitibus, qui cum armis, eo abducto, circumstrepebant, seniores civium ad se dux una cum episcopo collegit, ut civitatem ingrederetur. Tunc Dinamius haec omnia cernens, veniam petens, datis duci multis muneribus, reddita etiam sacramenta, se fidelem episcopo deinceps regique futurum, suis induitur indumentis. Tunc reseratis tam portarum quam sacrarum aedium valvas, ingrediuntur utrique civitatem, dux scilicet et episcopus, cum signis et laudibus diversisque honorum vexillis. Clerici autem, qui sceleri huic mixti fuerant, quorum caput Anastasius abba et Proculus presbiter erant, infra Dinami tecta confugiunt, petentes ab eo opem refugii, a quo fuerant incitati. Multi tamen eorum per idoneos fideiussores dimissi, ad regem iussi sunt ambulare. Interea Gundulfus, subiugatam civitatem in Childeberthi regis dicione restitutoque in loco suo antistite, ad regem Childeberthum regressus est. Sed Dinamius inmemor fidei, quam Childeberto regi promiserat, ad Guntchramnum regem nuntios dirigit, dicens, quod partem sibi debitam civitatis per episcopum perderet nec umquam Massiliensem urbem suo potiretur dominio, nisi hic evellatur ab ea. At ille ira commotus, iubet contra fas religionis, ut pontifex summi Dei artatus vinculis sibi exhiberetur, dicens: 'Trudatur exilio inimicus regni nostri, ne nobis nocere amplius valeat'. Sed cum episcopus de his suspectus esset nec facile posset ab urbe erui, advenit festivitas dedicationis oratorii ruris suburbani. Cumque ad haec festa, egressus civitatem, properaret, subito armati cum magno fremitu ab occultis insidiis scatentes, sanctum vallant antistitem; deiectumque ab equo, fugant omnes comites eius, servientes alligant, clericos caedunt, ipsumque super miserabilem inponentes caballum, nullum de suis seque permittentes, ad regis deducunt praesentiam. Cumque per Aquensim praeterirent urbem, Pientius episcopus loci, condolens fratri, datis clericis ad solatium inpositisque necessariis, abire permisit. Dum haec agerentur, clerici iterum Massiliensis domus ecclesiae reserant, archana rimantur et alia discribunt, alia suis domibus inferunt. Episcopus vero ad regem deductus nec culpabilis inventus, ad civitatem suam redire permissus, cum grandi est a civibus laude susceptus. Ex hoc enim gravis inimicitia inter Guntchramnum regem et Childeberthum nepotem suum exoritur, disruptumque foedus, sibi invicem insidiabantur.

## 12. De exercitu contra Biturigas commoto.

<a id="hist-06-la-012"></a>
[**[012]**](#hist-06-en-012) Igitur Chilpericus rex cernens has discordias inter fratrem ac nepotem suum pullulare, Desiderium ducem evocat iobetque, ut aliquid nequitiae inferat fratri. At ille, commotos exercitu, Ragnovaldo duce fugato, Petrogoricum pervadit, exactaque sacramenta, Aginnum pergit. Haec audiens uxor Ragnovaldi, quod scilicet, fugato viro suo, haec civitates in potestate regis Chilperici redegerentur, basilicam sancti marthiris Caprasi expetiit. Sed extracta exinde et spoliata a facultate ac solatio famolorum, datis fideiussoribus, Tholosae diregitur, ibique iterum in basilica sancti Saturnini ingressa resedebat. Desiderius vero cunctas civitates, quae in parte illa ad regem Gunthchramnum aspiciebant, abstulit et dicionibus regis Chilperici subegit. Berulfus vero dux, cum Bitorigus musitare, quod Toronicum terminum ingrederentur, audisset, exercitum commovet et se in ipsos fines statuit. Graviter tunc pagi Isiodorensis ac Berravensis urbis Toronicae devastati sunt. Sed et postea crudiliter, qui in hac obsidione adesse non poterant, sunt damnati. Bladastis vero dux in Vasconiam abiit maximamquae partem exercitus sui amisit.

## 13. De Lupo et Ambrosio Turonicis civibus interfectis.

<a id="hist-06-la-013"></a>
[**[013]**](#hist-06-en-013) Lupus urbis Turonicae civis, cum, uxore perdita ac liberis, clericatum expeteret, a fratre Ambrosio prohibitus est, timens, ne heredem institueret Dei ecclesiam, si ei coniungeretur. Rursumque illi uxorem providit et diem, in quo ad disponsalia donanda coniungerent', malesuadus frater indicit. Dehinc ad Cainonensem castrum, ubi hospitium habebant, pariter advenerunt. Sed uxor Ambrosii, cum esset adultera et alium in amore lupanario, exoso marito, diligeret, insidias viro tetendit. Cumque hi germani pariter epulantes et nocte usque ad ebrietatem vino maduissent, in uno strato pariter quieverunt. Tunc moechus uxoris Ambrosii nocte veniens, quiescentibus cunctis et vino depressis, accensis igne paleis, ut videret quid ageret, extracto gladio, Ambrosium in capite librat, ita ut descendens per oculos gladius cervical capitis amputaret. In quo ictu expergefactus Lupus et se in sanguinem volutari decernens, exclamat voce magna, dicens: 'Heu, heu, succurrite, frater meus interfectus est!' Moechus vero, qui iam perpetrato scelere discedebat, haec audiens, regressus ad lectum, Lupum adiit. Quo repugnante, multis plagis laceratum oppressit et mortali ictu sauciatum semivivum reliquid. Sed nullus de familia sensit. Mane autem facto, stupebant omnes de tanto scelere. Lupus tamen adhuc vivens nanctus, sicut actum fuerat referens, spiritum exalavit. Sed nec longum meretrix lugendi sumpsit spatium; sed paucis diebus interpositis, coniuncta moecho, discessit.

## 14. De portentis quae apparuerunt.

<a id="hist-06-la-014"></a>
[**[014]**](#hist-06-en-014) Anno igitur septimo Childeberthi regis, qui erat Chilperici et Gunthchramni vicensimus et primus, mense Ianuario pluviae, coruscationes atque tonitrua gravia fuerunt; flores in arboribus ostensi sunt. Stilla, quem comitem superius nominavi, apparuit, ita ut in circuitu eius magna nigrido esset; et illa, tamquam se in foramen aliquod posita, ita inter tenebras relucebat, scintillans spargensque comas. Prodebat autem ex ea radius mirae magnitudinis, qui tamquam fumus magnus incendii apparebat a longe. Visa est autem a partem occidentis in ora noctis prima. In die autem sanctum paschae apud Sessionas civitatem caelum ardere visum est, ita ut duo apparerent incendia; et unum erat maior, aliud vero minor. Post duarum vero horarum spatio coniuncta sunt simul, factamque pharum magnam, evanuerunt. In Parisiaco vero terminum verus sanguis ex nube defluxit et super vestimenta multorum hominum caecidit et ita tabe maculavit, ut ipsi propria indumenta horrentes abnuerunt. Tribus enim locis in termino civitatis illius hoc prodigium apparuit. In Silvanectinse vero terreturio hominis cuiusdam domus, cum ille mane surgerit, sanguine respersa ab intus apparuit. Magna tamen eo anno lues in populo fuit; valitudinis variae, milinae cum pusulis et vissicis, quae multum populum adficerunt mortem. Multi tamen, adhibentes studium, evaserunt. Audivimus enim eo anno in Narbonensem urbem inguinarium morbum graviter desevire, ita ut nullum esset spatium, cum homo correptus fuisset ab eo.

## 15. De obitu Felicis episcopi.

<a id="hist-06-la-015"></a>
[**[015]**](#hist-06-en-015) Felix vero episcopus Namneticae civitatis in hac valitudine corruens, graviter aegrotare coepit. Tunc vocatis ad se episcopis, qui propinqui erant, supplicat, ut consensum, quem in Burgundione nepote suo fecerat, suis subscriptionibus roborarent. Quod cum factum fuisset, eum ad me dirigunt. Erat tunc temporis Burgundio quasi annorum XXV. Qui veniens rogat, ut, accedens usque Namnetas, episcopum eum in locum avunculi, qui adhuc superstes erat, tonsoratum consecrare deberem. Quod ego abnui, quia canonibus non congruere cognovi. Consilium tamen piaebui, dicens: 'Habemus scriptum in canonibus, fili, non posse quemquam ad episcopatum accedere, nisi prius ecclesiasticus gradus regulariter sortiatur. Tu ergo, dilectissime, revertere illuc et pete, ut ipse te qui elegit debeat tonsorare. Cumque presbiterii honorem acciperis, ad ecclesiam adsiduus esto; et cum eum Deus migrare voluerit, tunc tu facile episcopale gradum ascendes'. At ille regressus, consilium acceptum adimplere dissimulavit, eo quod Felix episcopus ab incommodo levius agere videretur. Sed postquam febris discessit, tibiae eius ab humore pusulas emerserunt. Tunc cantaredarum cataplasmam nimium validam ponens, conputrescentibus tibiis, anno episcopatus sui XXXIII, aetate septuagenaria vitam finivit. Cui Nonnichius consobrinus, rege ordinante, successit.

## 16. Quod Pappolenus uxorem suam recepit.

<a id="hist-06-la-016"></a>
[**[016]**](#hist-06-en-016) Audiens autem Pappolenus eius obitum, neptem illius, de qua separatus fuerat, recepit. Ante hoc autem tempus disponsatam eam habuerat; sed dissimolante de nuptiis Filici episcopo, hic cum magna cohortem veniens, ab oraturio puellam abstraxit et in basilica beati Albini confugit. Tunc Filex episcopus ira commotus, circumventam puellam dolis a marito separavit, mutataque veste, apud Vasatensem urbem in monastirio posuit. Sed illa occultos pueros nuntius dirigit, ut scilicet eam ereptam a loco, in quo posita erat, acciperet. Quod ille non abnuens, adsumptam de monastirio puellam suo coniugio copolavit, regalibusque munitus praeceptionibus, timere parentum distulit moenas.

## 17. De Iudaeis per Chilpericum regem conversis.

<a id="hist-06-la-017"></a>
[**[017]**](#hist-06-en-017) Rex vero Chilpericus multos Iudaeorum eo anno baptizare praecipit, ex quibus pluris excipit a sancto lavacro. Nonnulli tamen eorum, corpore tantum, non corde abluti, ad ipsam quam prius perfidiam habuerant, Deo mentiti, regressi sunt, ita ut et sabbatum observare et diem dominicum honorare vidiantur. Priscus vero ad cognuscendam veritatem nulla penitus potuit ratione deflecti Tunc iratus rex iussit eum custodiae mancipare, scilicet ut, quem credere voluntariae non poterat, audire et credere faceret vel invitum. Sed ille, datis quibusdam muneribus, spatium postulat, donec filius eius Massiliensim Hebraeam accipiat; pollicitui dolosae, se deinceps quae rex iusserat impleturum. Interea oritur intentio inter illum et Pathiren ex Iudaeo conversum, qui iam regis filius erat ex lavacro. Cumque die sabbati Priscus praecinctus orario, nullum in manu ferens ferramentum, Moysaicas legis quasi impleturus, secretiora conpetiret, subito Pathir adveniens, ipsumque gladio cum sociis qui aderant iugulavit. Quibus interfectis, ad basilecam sancti Iuliani cum pueris suis, qui ad propinquam plateam erat, confugit. Cumque ibidem resederent, audiunt, quod rex, dominum vitae cessum, famolos tamquam malefactores a basileca tractos iuberet interfici. Tunc unus ex his, evaginato gladio, domino iam fugato, socios suosinterficit, ipse postmodum cum gladio de basileca egressus; sed inruente super se populo, crudilitere interfectus est. Pathir autem, accepta licentia, ad regnum Guntchramni, unde venerat, est regressus; sed non post multos dies a parentibus Prisci interfectus est.

## 18. De legatis Chilperici regis ab Hispania reversis.

<a id="hist-06-la-018"></a>
[**[018]**](#hist-06-en-018) Igitur legati Chilperici regis, id est Ansovaldus et Domegiselus, qui ad conspiciendam dotem in Hispaniis fuerant missi, regressi sunt. His diebus Leuvichildus rex in exercitu contra Herminichildum, filium suum, resedebat, cui et Meritam civitatem abstulit. Nam hic qualiter cum ducibus imperatoris Tiberii fuerit coniunctus, iam superius exposuimus. Nam et legatis haec causa innexuit moras, ut tardius regredirentur. Quibus visus, ego sollicitus eram, qualiter in ipsis christianis, qui pauci in eo loco remanserant, fides Christi ferveret. Cui haec Ansovaldus respondit: 'Christiani, qui nunc apud Hispanias conmorantur, catholicam fidem integre servant. Sed rex novo nunc ingenio eam nititur exturbare, dum dolose et ad sepulchra martirum et in eclesiis relegionis nostrae orare confingit. Dicit enim: „Manefeste cognovi, esse Christum filium Dei aequalem Patri; sed Spiritum sanctum Deum penitus esse non credo, eo quod in nullis legatur codicibus Deus esse".' Heu, heu, quam iniquam sententiam, quam venenosum sensum, quam prava mente! Et ubi est illud quod Dominus ait: Spiritus Deus est; et illud Petri, quod Annaniam ait: Quod tibi visum est temptare Spiritum sanctum? Non es hominibus mentitus, sed Deo. Ubi est et illud quod Paulus, mistica dona commemorans, ait: Haec enim operatur unus atque idem Spiritus, dividens unicuique, prout vult? Qui enim operatur quod vult, nulli cognuscetur esse subiectus. Accedente autem Ansovaldo ad Chilpericum regem, legatio Hispaniorum est subsecuta, quae de Chilperico ad Childeberthum accedens, in Hispaniis est regressa.

## 19. De hominibus Chilperici regis apud Urbiam fluvium.

<a id="hist-06-la-019"></a>
[**[019]**](#hist-06-en-019) Apud pontem vero Urbiensim civitatis Parisiacae Chilpericus rex custodes posuerat, ut insidiatores de regno fratris sui, ne nocerent aliquid, arcerentur. Quod Asclipius ex duce praecognito, nocte inruens, interfecit omnes pagumque ponte proximum graviter depopulatus est. Cumque haec regi Chilperico nuntiatum fuisset, misit nuntios comitibus ducibusque vel reliquos agentibus, ut, collecto exercitu, in regno germani sui inruerent. Sed prohibitus est consilio bonorum, ne faceret, dicentibus sibi: 'Illi perverse aegerunt, tu vero sapienter age. Mitte fratri nuntios, et si iniuriam tuam emendare voluerit, nihil mali geris; si vero noluerit, tractabis deinceps, quid sequaris'. Et sic ratione accepta, prohibetu exercitu, legationem fratri dirigit. Sed ille cuncta emendans, fratris quaesivit integrae caritatem.

## 20. De obito Chrodini ducis.

<a id="hist-06-la-020"></a>
[**[020]**](#hist-06-en-020) Eo anno Chrodinus obiit, vir magnifice bonitatis et pietatis, aelimosinarius valde pauperumque refector, profluus ditatur eclesiarum, clericorum nutritur. Nam sepe a novo fundans villas, ponens vinias, aedificans domus, culturas eregens, vocatis episcopis, quorum erat parva facultas, dato epulo, ipsas domus cum culturibus et culturis, cum argento, parastromatibus, utensilibus, ministris et famolis benigne distribuebat, dicens: 'Sint haec aeclesiae data, ut, dum de his pauperes reficiuntur, mihi veniam obteneant apud Deum'. Multa enim et alia bona de hoc viro audivimus, quae insequi longum est. Transiit autem aetate septuagenaria.

## 21. De signis ostensis.

<a id="hist-06-la-021"></a>
[**[021]**](#hist-06-en-021) Haec in hoc anno iteratis signa apparuerunt: luna eclypsim passa est; infra Toronicum territurium verus de fracto pane sanguis effluxit; muri urbis Sessionicae conruerunt; apud Andecavam urbem terra tremuit; infra muros vero Burdegalensis oppidi ingressi lupi canes deforaverunt, nequaquam hominem metuentes; per caelum ignis discurrere visus est. Sed et Vasatensis civitas incendio concremata est, ita ut eclesiae vel domus aeclesiasticae vastarentur. Ministerium tamen omne ereptum fuisse cognovimus.

## 22. De Cartherio episcopo.

<a id="hist-06-la-022"></a>
[**[022]**](#hist-06-en-022) Igitur pervasis Chilpericus rex civitatibus fratris sui novos comites ordinat et cuncta iubet sibi urbium tributa deferri. Quod ita impletum fuisse cognovimus. His diebus adprehensi sunt duo homines a Nunnichio Lemovicinae urbis comite, deferentes ex nomine Charteri Petrogoricae urbis episcopi litteras, quae multa inproperia loquebantur in regem; in quibus inter reliqua erat insertum, quasi quereretur sacerdos, se a paradiso ad inferos descendisse, scilicet quod a regno Guntchramni in Chilperici fuerit dicionibus commutatus. Has litteras cum his hominibus iam dictus comes sub ardua custodia regi direxit. Rex vero patienter propter episcopum mittit, qui eum suo conspectui praesentarent, discussurus utique, si vera essent quae ei opponebantur, an non. Adveniente vero episcopo, rex homines illos cum litteris repraesentat. Interrogat sacerdoti, si ab eo directae fuerint. Negat ille a se directas. Interrogantur vero homines, a quo eas acceperint. Frontonium diaconum proferunt. Interrogatur de diacono sacerdos. Respondit, sibi eum esse praecipuum inimicum, nec dubitari debere, ipsius esse nequitias, qui contra eum saepius causas commovisset iniquas. Adducitur diaconus sine mora; interrogatur a rege; confitetur super episcopum, dicens: 'Ego hanc epistolam episcopo iubente dictavi'. Proclamante vero episcopo et dicente, quod saepius hic ingenia quaereret, qualiter eum ab episcopatu deiceret, rex misericordia motus, commendans Deo causam suam, cessit utrisque, deprecans clementer episcopum pro diacono, et supplicans, ut pro se sacerdos oraret. Et sic cum honore urbi remissus est. Post duos vero menses Nonnichius comes, qui hoc scandalum seminavit, sanguine percussus interiit, resque eius, quia absque liberis erat, diversis a rege concessae sunt.

## 23. Quod Chilperico rege filius natus est.

<a id="hist-06-la-023"></a>
[**[023]**](#hist-06-en-023) Dehinc Chilperico regi post multa funera filiorum filius nascitur. Ex hoc iubet rex omnes custodias relaxaris, vinctos absolvi conpositionesque neglegentum fisco debitas praecipit omnino non exigi. Sed magnum deinceps dolum hic intulit infans.

## 24. Item de insidiis Theudori episcopi et de Gundovaldo.

<a id="hist-06-la-024"></a>
[**[024]**](#hist-06-en-024) Nova iterum contra Theodorum episcopum bella consurgunt. Nam Gundovaldus, qui se filium Chlothari regis esse dicebat, de Constantinopoli veniens, Massilia est advectus. De cuius origine quaedam strictim libuit memorare. Hic cum natus esset in Galliis et diligenti cura nutritus, ut regum istorum mos est, crinium flagellis per terga dimissis, litteris eruditus, Childebertho rege a matre repraesentatur, dicente ea: 'Ecce', inquid, nepotem tuum, Chlothari regis filium; et quia invisus habetur patri, suscipe eum, quia caro tua est'. Quem ille, eo quod ei fili non essent, accipiens, retenibat secum. Nuntiantur haec regi Chlothario, misitquae fratri nuntius, dicens: 'Dimitte puerum, ut veniat ad me'. Ne moratus ille iuvenem fratri direxit. Quo viso, Chlotharius iussit tundi comam capitis eius, dicens: 'Hunc ego non generavi'. Igitur post Chlothari regis obitum a Charibertho rege susceptus est. Quem Sigyberthus arcessitum iterum amputavit comam capitis eius et misit eum in Agripinensim civitatem, quae nunc Colonia dicitur. Ille quoque ab eo loco dilapsus, dimissis iterum capillis, ad Narsitem abiit, qui tunc Aetaliae praeerat. Ibi accepta uxore, filios procreavit et ad Constantinopolim accessit. Inde, ut ferunt, post multa tempora a quodam invitatus, ut veniret in Galliis, Massilia adpulsus, a Theodoro episcopo susceptus est. Ab eodem etiam acceptis aequitibus, Mummolo duci coniunctus est. Erat autem tunc Mummolus in civitate Avennica, sicut supra iam diximus. Gunthchramnus vero dux adpraehensum Theodorum episcopum in custodia pro hac causa detrusit, repotans, cur hominem extraneum intromisissit in Galliis voluissetque Francorum regnum imperialibus per haec subdere ditionibus. At ille epistolam, ut aiunt, manu maiorum Childeberthi regis subscriptam protulit, dicens: 'Nihil per me feci, nisi quae mihi a domnis nostris et senioribus imperata sunt. Custodiebatur igitur sacerdus in cellula nec permittebatur eclesiae propinquare. Quadam vero nocte, dum adtentius oraret ad Dominum, refulsit cellula nimio splendore, ita ut comes, qui erat custus eius, ingente pavore terreretur; visusque est super eum lucis inmense globus per duarum horarum spatium. Mane autem facto, narrabat haec comes ille citeris, qui cum eo erant. Post haec autem ductus est ad Gunthchramnum regem cum Epyfanio episcopo, qui tunc a Langobardus fugiens Massilia morabatur, scilicet quod et ipse conscius huius causae fuisset. Discussi igitur a rege, in nullo inventi sunt crimine. Rex tamen iussit eos sub custodia degere. in qua post multa supplicia Epyfanius episcopus obiit. Gundovaldus vero in insola maris secessit, expectans eventum rei. Gunthchramnus vero dux cum duce Gunthchramni regis res Gundovaldi divisit et sicum Arverno detulit inmensum, ut ferunt, argenti pondus et auri vel reliquarum rerum.

## 25. De signis.

<a id="hist-06-la-025"></a>
[**[025]**](#hist-06-en-025) Anno octavo Childeberthi regis pridiae Kal. Februarias, cum die dominico apud urbem Toronicam ad matutinus signum conmutum fuisset et populus surgens ad eclesiam conveniret, caelo nubilo, cum pluvia globus magnus ignis de caelo dilapsus, in spatio multo cucurrit in aera, qui tantam lucem dedit, ut tamquam media diae omnia cernerentur. Quo iterum in nube suscepto, nox successit. Aquae vero extra solitu invaluerunt; nam tantum inundationes Sygona Matronaque circa Parisius intulerunt, ut inter civitatem et basilicam sancti Laurenti naufragia saepe contingerent.

## 26. De Guntchramno et Mummolo.

<a id="hist-06-la-026"></a>
[**[026]**](#hist-06-en-026) Gunthchramnus quoque dux Arvernum cum supradictis thesauris reversus, ad Childeberthum regem abiit. Cumque exinde regrederetur cum uxore et filiabus, a Gunthchramnum regem conpraehensus retenebatur, dicente: sibi regem:'Tua invitatio Gundovaldum adduxit in Galliis, et ob hoc ante hos annus abisti Constantinopoli'. Cui illi: 'Mummolus', inquid, 'dux tuus ipse suscepit eum et in Avennionem secum retenuit. Nunc autem permitte me, ut adducam ipsum tibi, et tunc inmunis ero ab his quae repotantur mihi'. Cui rex ait: 'Non permittam te abire, nisi dignas luas poenas pro his quae commisisti'. At ille cernens se mortem propinquam, ait: 'Ecce filium meum! Suscipe illum, et sit obses pro his quae promitto domino meo rege; et nisi Mummolum adducam tibi, perdam parvo]um meum'. Tunc rex permisit eum abire, retentum secum eius infantulo. At ille, adsumptos secum Arvernis atque Villavis, Avennione abiit. Sed asto Mummoli navis in Rodano infirmae paratae sunt; ascendentesque simpliciter, ut in medio amnis venerunt, impletis navibus mergebantur. Tunc in periculo positi, alii natando evaserunt, nonnulli vero, arreptis ipsarum navium tabulis, attigerunt litus. Plerique autem, quorum minor fuit astutia, in amne dimersi sunt. Gunthchramnus vero dux advenit Avennione. Providerat enim Mummolus, postquam intra murus urbis illius est ingressus, ut quia pars parva resedebat, quae non vallabatur a Rhodano, ut, eductam ex eo partem, locus ille totus ex hoc alluvio muneretur; in quo loco fossas magnae profunditudinis fodit, praeparatusque dolos aqua decurrens operuit. Tunc, adveniente Gunthchramno, ait ex muroy Mummolus: 'Si fides est integra, veniat ille ab una parte ripae et ego ex alia, et quod voluerit eloquatur'. Quod cum convenisset, ait Gunthchramnus econtra, – hoc enim brachium fluminis inter utrumque erat positum –: 'Si licit', inquid, 'vadam, quia sunt aliqua quae inter nos secretius conferantur'. Cui ille: 'Veni', ait, 'ne timeas'. Ingressus cum uno amicorum suorum – et erat luricae pondere adgravatus – ilico amicus ille, ut foveam amnis attigit, sub aquis dimersus nusquam conparuit. Gunthchramnus vero cum demergeretur atque portaretur ab unda veloci, unus de adstantibus, porrectam manu eius astam, eum litori reddidit. Et tunc, inlatis sibi convitiis, ipse vel Mummolus discesserunt. Obsedente quoque Gunthchramno ipsam urbem cum exercitu Gunthchramni regis, nuntiata sunt haec Childebertho. At ille ira commotus, cur haec non iussus ageret, Gundulfum superius dictum illuc direxit. Qui, amota obsidione, Mummolum Arvernus adduxit. Sed post paucos dies Avennione regressus est.

## 27. Quod Chilpericus rex Parisius est ingressus.

<a id="hist-06-la-027"></a>
[**[027]**](#hist-06-en-027) Chilpericus rex pridie quam pascha celebraretur, Parisius abiit. Et ut maledictum, quod in pactione sua vel fratrum suorum conscriptum erat, ut nullus eorum Parisius sine alterius voluntate ingrederetur, carere possit, reliquias sanctorum multorum praecidentibus, urbem ingressus est diesque paschae cum multa iocunditate tenuit filiumque suum baptismo tradedit, quem Ragnemodus ipsius urbis sacerdus de lavacro sancto suscepit ipsumque Theodoricum vocitare praecepit.

## 28. De Marco refrendario.

<a id="hist-06-la-028"></a>
[**[028]**](#hist-06-en-028) Marcus quoque refrendarius, cui supra meminimus, post congregatus de iniquis discriptionibus thesauros, subito latere dolore detentus, capud totundit, atque paenetentiam accipiens, spiritum exalavit, resque eius fisco conlatae sunt. Nam magni ibidem thesauri ex auro argentoque et multarum specierum reperti sunt, nihil exinde secum aliud portans nisi animae detrimentum.

## 29. De puellis monasterii Pictavensis.

<a id="hist-06-la-029"></a>
[**[029]**](#hist-06-en-029) Legati de Hispaniis reversi nihil certi renuntiaverunt, eo quod Leuvichildus contra filium suum seniorem in exercitu resederet. In monastirio autem beatae Radegunde puella quaedam nomen Disciola, quae beati Salvii Albigensis episcopi neptis erat, obiit hoc modo. Cum egrotare coepisset et ei assiduae sorores aliae deservirent, venit dies ille, quo migraret a corpore, et circa horam nonam ait sororibus: 'Ecce iam leviorem me sentio; ecce nihil doleo! Nunc autem non est necesse sollicitudine vestrae, ut mihi curae aliquid inpendatis; sed potius discedite a me, quo facilius sopore relaxer'. Haec audientes sorores eius, recesserunt parumper cellola, et post paulolum advenerunt. Denique stantibus illis coram ea, expectabant, quid ab illa elocutionis audirent. Ipsa autem, expansis manibus, benedictionem a nescio quo afflagitans, ait: 'Benedic' inquid, 'mihi, sanctae ac famulae Dei excelsi; ecce enim iam tertio fatigaris hodiae mei causa! Et cur, sanctae, pro infirma muliercula crebras iniurias sustenis?' Interrogantibus vero illis, ad quem haec verba proferret, penitus non est effata. Tunc facto modico intervallo, emisit voce magna cum riso; et sic tradedit spiritum. Et ecce quidam inerguminus, qui tunc ad beati Crucis gloriam mundandus advenerat, adreptam manibus caesariem, conlisit se in terram, dicens: 'Heu, heu, heu nobis, qui tale damno perpessi sumus! Vel licuisset prius causas inquirere, et sic de potestate nostra fuisset ablata haec anima'. Inquirentibus vero his qui aderant, quod esset hoc verbum, quod loquebatur, respondit: 'Ecce anima puellae Michahel angelus suscepit, et ipsi eam ad caelos evexit. Princeps vero noster, quem vos diabolum nominatis, nihil in ea participatur'. Post haec corpus aquis ablutum ita candore niveo refulgebat, ut nullum lenteum repperire abbatissa potuisset in prumptu, quod corpore candidior cerneretur; induta tamen lenteis mundis, sepulturae mandata est. Nam et alia puella huius monastirii visum vidit, quod sororibus retulit. Potabat, inquid, se iter aliquod conficere; et erat ei votum, ut ad fontem vivum gradiens perveniret. Cumque viam nesciret, vir quidam se obviam obtulit, dicens: 'Si', inquid, 'vis ad fontem vivum accedere, ego ero praevius iteneris tui'. At illa gratias agens, sequebatur praecedentem. Quibus ambolantibus, pervenerunt ad fontem magnum, cuius aquae tamquam aurum splendebant, herbae vero in modum diversarum gemmarum vernante luce radiabant. Et ait vir ad eam: "Ecce fonte vivo, quem multo labore quaesisti! Satiare nunc ab eius fluentis, ut fiat tibi fons aquae vivae salientis in vitam aeternam'. Cumque illa avide ex his aquis auriret, ecce ab alia parte veniebat abbatissa et, denudatam puellam, induit eam vestem regiak, quae tanta luce auroque et munilibus refulgebat, ut vix possit intendi, dicente sibi abbatissa: 'Sponsus enim tuus mittit tibi haec munera'. Haec cum puella vidisset, conpuncta est corde, et post dies paucus rogavit abbatissam, ut sibi in qua inclauderetur cellolam praepararet. At illa velociter perfectam, ait: 'Ecce', inquid, 'cellolam! Quid nunc desideras?' Puella vero petiit, ut recludi permitteretur. Quod cum ei praestitum fuisset, congregatis virginibus cum magno psallentio, accensis lampadibus, tenente sibi beata Radegunde manu, ad locum usque perducitur. Et sic vale faciens omnibus et osculans singulas quasque, reclausa est; structoque aditu, per quem ingressa fuerat, ibi nunc oratione ac lectione vacat.

## 30. De obitu Tiberii imperatores.

<a id="hist-06-la-030"></a>
[**[030]**](#hist-06-en-030) Hoc anno Tiberius imperator migravit a saeculo, magnum luctum relinquens populis de obito suo. Erat enim summe bonitatis, in aelimosinis prumptus, in iudiciis iustus, in iudicando cautissimus; nullum dispiciens, sed omnes in bona voluntate conplectens. Omnes diligens, ipse quoque diligebatur ab omnibus. Hic cum egrotare coepisset et se iam vivere disperaret, vocavit Sophiam agustam, dicens: 'Ecce! iam impleto sentio tempus vitae meae; nunc consilio tuo elegam, qui rei publicae praeesse debeat. Oportit enim strinuum elegi, quae praesit huic potestati'. At illa Mauricium quendam elegit, dicens: 'Valde strinuus et sacax vir isti. Nam et sepius contra inimicos rei publicae demicans, victurias obtenuit'. Haec enim dicebat, ut, isto transeunte, huius coniugio necteretur. Sed Tiberius, postquam consensum cognovit agustae de huius electione, iussit exornare filiam suam ornamentis imperialibus, et vocato Mauritio, ait: 'Ecce! cum consensu Sophiae agustae ad imperium elegeris; in quo ut firmior sis, filiam meam tradam tibi'. Et accedentem puella, tradedit eam pater Mauritio, dicens: 'Sit tibi imperium meum cum haci puella concessum. Utire eum felix, memor semper, ut aequitate et iustitiam delecteris'. At ille, acceptam puellam, duxit eam ad domum suam; et transacta solemnitate nuptiarum, Tiberius obiit. Igitur caelebrato iustitio, Mauricius, indutus diademate et purpora, ad circum processit, adclamatisque sibi laudibus, largita populo munera, in imperio confirmatur.

## 31. De multis malis.

<a id="hist-06-la-031"></a>
[**[031]**](#hist-06-en-031) Denique Chilpericus rex legatus nepotis sui Childeberthi suscepit, inter quos primus erat Egidius Remensis episcopus. Quibus intromissis ad regem, data suggestione, dixerunt: 'Pacem, quam cum domino nostro, nepote tuo, fecisti, petit a te omnimodis conservare; cum fratri vero tuo pacem habere non potest, quia partem Massiliae ei post mortem abstulit patris fugacesque suos retenet nec eos vult ei remittere. Ideo Childeberthus. nepus tuus, caretatem, quam nunc tecum retenit, integre vult servare'. Et ille: 'ln multis', inquid, frater meus accessit culpabilis. Nam si ordinem rationis filius meus Childeberthus inquirat, cognuscit protinus, quod huius conludio pater eius est interfectus'. Haec eo dicente, Egidius episcopus ait: 'Si cum nepote tuo coniungeris et ipse coniungitur tibi, commoto exercitu, ultio quae debetur super eum velocius infertur'. Quod cum iuramento firmassent obsedesque inter se dedissent, discesserunt. Igitur fidens in promissis eorum Chilpericus, commoto regni sui exercitum, Parisius venit. Ubi cum resedisset, magnum dispendium rerum incolis intulit. Berulfus vero dux cum Toronicis, Pectavis Andecavisque atque Namneticis ad terminum Bitoricum venit. Desiderius vero et Bladastis cum omni exercitu provintiae sibi commissae- ab alia parte Betoricum vallant, multum vastantes per quas venerunt regiones. Chilpericus vero iussit exercitum, qui ad eum accessit, per Parisius transire. Quo transeunte, et ipse transiit atque ad Mecledonensem castrum abiit, cuncta incendio tradens atque devastans. Et licet exercitus nepotis sui ad eum non venisset, tamen duces et legati eius cum ipso erant. Tunc misit nuntius ad supradictus duces, dicens: 'Ingrediemini Beturigum, et accedentes usque ad civitatem, sacramenta fidelitatis exegite de nomine nostro'. Biturigi vero cum quindecim milibus ad Mediolanensim castrum confluunt ibique contra Desiderium ducem confligunt; factaque est ibi stragis magna, ita ut de utroque exercitu amplius quam septim milia caecidissent. Duces quoque cum reliqua parte populi ad civitatem pervenerunt, cuncta deripientes vel devastantes; talisque depopulatio inibi acta est, qualis nec antiquitus est audita fuisse, ut nec domus remaneret nec vinea nec arbores, sed cuncta succiderent, incenderent, debellarent. Nam et ab eclesiis auferentis sacra ministeria, ipsas incendio cremabant. Gunthchramnus vero rex cum exercitu contra fratrem suum advenit, totam spem in Dei iudicio conlocans. Qui die una iam vespere, misso exercitu, maximam partem a germani sui exercitu interficit. Mane autem concurrentibus legatis, pacem fecerunt, pollicentes alter ab alterutrum, ut quicquid sacerdotes vel seniores populi iudicarent, pars parte conponerent, quae terminum legis excesserat; et sic pacifici discesserunt. Chilpericus vero rex, cum exercitu suo a praedis arcere non possit, Rhodomaginsem comitem gladio trucidavit; et sic Parisius rediit, omnem relinquens praedam captivosque relaxans. Ad isti qui Bitoricas obsedebant, accepto mandato, ut reverterentur ad propriam, tantas praedas secum sustulerunt, ut omnes regio illa, unde egressi sunt, valde potaretur evacuata vel de hominibus vel de ipsis pecoribus. Ingressus quoque exercitus Desiderii atque Bladastis per Toronicam, incendia, praedas et homicidia tanta fecerunt, sicut solet contra inimicos fieri; nam et captivus adduxerunt, de quibus spoliatus plurimus postea demiserunt. Subsecutus est morbus pecorum hanc cladem, ita ut vix vel initium remaneret novumque esset, si aliquis aut iuvencum viderit aut cerneret buculam. Sed dum haec agerentur, Childeberthus rex cum exercito suo uno in loco resedebat. Nocte autem quadam commutus exercitus, magnum murmor contra Egidium episcopum et ducibus regis minor populus elevavit ac vociferare coepit et publicae proclamare: 'Tollantur a faciae regis, qui regnum eius venundant, civitates illius dominatione alteri subdunt, populus ipsius principis alterius dicionibus tradunt'. Dum haec et his similia vociferando proferrent, facto mane, adpraehenso armorum apparato, ad tenturium regis properant, scilicet ut adpraehensis episcopum vel senioribus vi obpraemerent, verberibus adficerent, gladiis lacerarent. Quod conperto, sacerdus fugam iniit, ascensoque equitae, ad urbem propria tendit. Ad populus ille cum clamore sequebatur, proiciens post eum lapides evomensque convitia. Fuitque tunc ei haec causa praesidium, quod his paratus equites non habebant. Attamen lassatis sociorum equis, solus pertendit episcopus, tanto timore perterritus, ut unam caligam de pede elapsam collegare non curaret. Et sic usque civitatem veniens, se infra murorum Rimensium septa conclusit.

## 32. De interitu Leudastis.

<a id="hist-06-la-032"></a>
[**[032]**](#hist-06-en-032) Ante paucus autem mensis Leudastis in Toronico cum praecepto regis advenit, ut uxorem reciperet ibique commoraretur. Sed et nobis epistolam sacerdotum manu subscriptam detulit, ut in communione reciperitur. Sed quoniam litteras reginae non videmus, cuius causa maximae a communione remotus fuerat, ipsum recipere distuli, dicens: Cum reginae mandatum suscepero, tunc eum recipere non morabor'. Interea ad eam dirigo; qui mihi scripta remisit, dicens: 'Conpraessa a multis aliud facere non potui, nisi ut eum abire permitterem. Nunc autem rogo, ut pacem tuam non mereatur neque eologias de manu tua suscipiat, donec a nobis quid agi debeat plenius pertractatur'. At ego haec scripta relegens, timui, ne interficeretur; arcessitoque socero eius, haec innotui, obsecrans, ut se cautum redderet, donec reginae animus leneretur. Sed ille consilium meum, quod pro Dei intuitu simpliciter insinuavi, dolose suscipiens, cum adhuc nobis esset inimicus, noluit agere quae mandavi; impletumque est autem illud proverbium, quod quendam senem narrantem audivi: 'Amico inimicoque bonum semper praebe consilium, quia amicus accepit, inimicus spernit'. Spraeto ergo hoc consilio, ad regem dirigit, qui tunc cum exercitu in pago Megledonense degebat; depraecatusque est populum, ut regi praeces funderet, ut eius praesentia mereretur. Depraecante igitur omni populo, rex se videndum ei praebuit. Prostratusque pedibus eius, veniam flagitavit. Cui rex: 'Cautum', inquid, 'te redde paulisper, donec, visa regina, conveniat, qualiter ad eius gratiam revertaris, cui multum inveniris esse culpabilis'. At ille, ut erat incautus et levis, in hoc fidens, quod regis praesentiam meruisset, rege Parisius revertente, die dominico in eclesia sancta reginae pedibus provolvitur, veniam depraecans. At illa frendens et execrans aspectum eius, a se repulit, fusisque lacrimis, ait: 'Et quia non extat de filiis, qui criminis mei causas inquerat, tibi eas, Iesu domine, inquerendas committo'. Prostrataque pedibus regis, adiecit: 'Vae mihi, quae video inimicum meum, et nihil ei praevaleo'. Tunc repulso eo a loco sancto, missarum solemnia celebrata sunt. Igitur regresso rege cum regina de eclesiam sanctam, Leudastis usque ad plateam est prosecutus, inopinans, quid ei accederit; domusque negutiantum circumiens, species rimatur, argentum pensat atque diversa ornamenta prospicit, dicens: 'Haec et haec conparabo, quia multum mihi aurum argentumque residit'. Ista illo dicente, subito advenientes reginae pueri, voluerunt eum vinci catenis. Ille quoque, evaginato gladio, unum verberat; exinde succensi felle, adpraehensis parmis et gladiis, super eum ruunt. Ex quibus unus librans ictum, maximam partem capitis eius a capillis et cute detexit. Cumque per pontem urbis fugiret, elapso inter duos axes, quae ponte faciunt, pede, effracta oppressus est tibia, legatisque postergum manibus, custodiae mancipatur. Iussitque rex, ut studeretur a medicis, quodadusque ab his ictibus sanatus, diuturno supplicium cruciaretur. Sed cum ad villam fiscalem ductus fuisset et, conputriscentibus plagis, extremam ageret vitam, iussu reginae in terram proiecitur resupinus; posito ad cervicem eius vecte inmenso, ab alio ei gulam verberant. Sicque semper perfidam agens vitam, iusta morte finivit.

## 33. De locustis, morbis prodigiisque.

<a id="hist-06-la-033"></a>
[**[033]**](#hist-06-en-033) Anno nono Childeberthi regis partem Massiliae Gunthchramnus rex ipse nepote suo refudit. Legati principis Chilperici de Hispaniis regressi, nuntiaverunt, provintiam Carpitaniam graviter a locustis fuisse vastatam, ita ut non arbor, non vinea, non silva, non fructus aliqui aut quicquam viride remaneret, qui non a locustis everteretur. Agebant enim, inimicitias illas, quae inter Leuvichildum et filium suum pullulaverant, vehementer augere. Per loca enim lues vastabat, sed maximae apud urbem Narbonensim validius desaeviebat, et iam tertio anno, quod ibidem adpraehenderat et requieverat; populique revertentes a fuga, iterum morbo consumpti sunt. Nam et Albigensis civitas maximae ab hoc inquomodo laborabat. His diebus apparuerunt a parte aquilonis nocte media radii multi fulgore nimio relucentis, qui ad se venientes iterum separabantur, usquequod evanuerunt. Sed et caelum ab ipsa septemtrionali plaga ita resplenduit, ut potaretur aurora producere.

## 34. De obitu fili Chilperici, quem Theodoricum vocavit.

<a id="hist-06-la-034"></a>
[**[034]**](#hist-06-en-034) Legati iterum ab Hispania venerunt, deferentes munera et placitum accipientes cum Chilperico rege, ut filiam suam secundum convenentiam anteriorem filio regis Leuvichildi tradere deberet in matrimonio. Denique dato placito et omnia pertractata, legatus ille reversus est. Sed Chilperico rege egresso de Parisius, ut in pago Sessionico accederet, novus luctus advenit. Filius enim eius, quem anno superiore sacro baptismate abluerat, a desinteria correptus, spiritum exalavit. Hoc enim fulgor ille, quod superius ex nube dilapsum memoravimus, figuravit. Tunc cum inmenso fletu regressi Parisius, sepelierunt puerum, mittentes post legatum, ut reverteretur, scilicet ut placitum quod posuerat prolongaret, dicente rege: 'Ecce planctum in domo sustineo, et qualiter nuptias filiae celebrabo?' Voluit enim tunc aliam filiam illuc dirigere, quam de Audovera habebat, et eam in monasterium Pictavensi posuerat. Sed illa distulit, resistente praecipue beata Radegunde et dicente: 'Non est enim dignum, ut puella Christo dedicata iterum in saeculi voluptatibus revertatur'.

## 35. De interitu Mummoli praefecti et mulieribus interfectis.

<a id="hist-06-la-035"></a>
[**[035]**](#hist-06-en-035) Dum autem haec agerentur, nuntiantur reginae, puerum, qui mortuus fuerat, maleficiis et incantationibus fuisse subductum ibique Mummolum praefectum, quem iam diu regina invisum habebat, conscium esse. Unde factum est, ut, epolante eo in domo sua, quidam de aulicis regis puerum dilectum sibi, qui a desenteria correptus fuerat, lamentaret. Cui praefectus respondit: 'Habetur mihi herba in prumptu, de qua se desentiricus auriat, quamlibet desperatus sit, mox sanatur'. Nuntiatis his reginae, maiore furore succenditur. Interea adpraehensis mulieribus urbis Parisiacae tormentis adplicat ac verberibus cogit fatere quae noverant. At ille confitentur se maleficas esse, et multos occumbere leto se fecisse testatae sunt, addentes illud, quod nulla ratione credi patior: 'Filium', aiunt, 'tuum, o regina, pro Mummoli praefecti vita donavimus'. Tunc regina, tormentis gravioribus mulieribus affectis, alias enegat, alias incendio tradit, alias rotis, ossibus confractis, innectit. Et sic Conpendio villam una cum rege secessit ibique universa regi quae de praefecto audierat revelavit. Rex vero, missis pueris, iussit eum arcessire; discussumque catenis onerant et suppliciis subdunt. Trabi postergum revinctis manibus, adpenditur et ibi, quid maleficii noverit, interrogatur; sed nihil de his quae superius memoravimus confetetur. Hoc tamen y protulit, saepius se inunctionis et potionis, quae ei regis reginaequae gratiam praeberent, ab his mulieribus suscipisse. Depositus igitur de poena, vocat ad se lectorem, dicens: 'Nuntia domino meo regi, quia nihil mali sentio de his quae inlata sunt'. His auditis, rex: 'Verumne est', inquid, 'hunc esse maleficum, se de his nihil est laesus poenis?' Tunc extensum ad trocleas, tam diu loris triplicibus caesus est, quoadusque ipse lassarentur tortures; post haec sudis ungulis manuum pedumque difigunt. Cumque in hoc causa ageretur, ut ad dicidendam cervicem eius gladius inminerit, regina vitam obtenuit; sed non fuit minur morti humilitas subsecuta. Nam inpositus plaustro, ad Burdigalensim urbem, in qua ortus fuerat, ablata omni facultate, transmittitur; in via vero ictuatus sanguine, vix accedere quo iussus est valuit. Sed non post multum tempus spiritum exalavit. Post haec regina, adpraehenso pueroli thesauro, tam vestimenta quam reliquas species, vel ex sirico aut quocumque vellere invenire potuit, igne consumpsit; quod ferunt quattuor plaustra levasse. Aurum veroy vel argentum fornace conflatum reposuit, ne aliquid integrum remanerit, quod ei planctum fili in memoriam revocaret.

## 36. De Aetherio episcopo.

<a id="hist-06-la-036"></a>
[**[036]**](#hist-06-en-036) Aetherius vero Lixoensis episcopus, cui supra meminimus, hoc ordine a civitate sua vel expulsus est vel receptus. Clericus quidam extitit ex Cinomannica urbe, luxuriosus nimis amatorque mulierum et gulae ac fornicationis omnique immunditiae valde deditus. Hic mulieri cuidam saepius scorto commixtus, comam capitis totondit, mutatoque virili habitu, secum in alia civitate deduxit, ut suspicio auferetur adulterii, cum inter incognitos devenisset. Erat enim mulier ingenua genere et de bonis orta parentibus. Conperto autem post dies multos propinqui eius quae acta fuerant, ad ulciscendam humilitatem generis sui velocius properant, repertumque elericum vinctum custodiae mancipant, mulierem vero ignem consumunt. Et, sicut cogit auri sacra famis, elericum sub pretio venundari procurant, ea videlicet ratione, ut aut esset qui redimeret, aut certe morti addiceretur obnoxius. Cumque haec Aetherio episcopo delata fuissent, misericordia motus, datis XX aureis, eum ab imminenti exemit interitu. Igitur postquam vitae donatus est, profert se litterarum esse doctorem, promittens sacerdoti, quod, si ei pueros delegaret, perfectos hic in litteris redderet. Gavisus auditu sacerdos, pueros civitatis collegit ipsique delegat ad docendum. Denique cum iam honoraretur a civibus et pontifex ei aliquid terrae vinearumque largitus fuisset ac domum parentum eorum quos erudiebat invitaretur, reversus ad vomitum, unius pueruli matrem immemor anterioris iniuriae concupiscit. Quod eum pudica mulier declarasset, coniuncti parentes eius gravissimis elericum tormentis subdentes, interimere voluerunt. Quem sacerdos iterum misericordia motus castigatum verbis lenibus liberavit honorique restituit. Sed mens laeva numquam ad bonitatem potuit inclinari, sed potius factus est eius inimicus, a quo saepius fuerat de morte redemptus. Coniunctus est enim archidiacono civitatis, et se episcopatu dignum proferens, episcopum molitur occidere. Locatumque clericum, qui eum bipenne percuteret, ipsi ubique discurrunt, musitant, amicitias clam inligant, proferunt praemia, ut, si sacerdos obiret, ipse succederet. Sed misericordia Domini anticipavit eorum miseriam crudelitatemque iniquorum hominum veloci pietate repressit. Die vero, quo sacerdos operarios in agro adgregaverat ad sulcandum, clericus antedictus cum secure prosequitur sacerdotem, nihil de his penitus aliquid scientem. Tandem igitur haec advertens: 'Quid tu', inquid, 'me attentius cum hac bipenne prosequeris?' At ille timore perterritus, ad genua viri provolvitur, dicens: 'Fortis esto, sacerdos Dei. Nam scias me emissum ab archidiacono ac praeceptore, ut te secure percuterem. Quod cum saepius facere voluissem et ictum dextera suspensa librarem, tegebantur tenebris oculi mei, et aures obserabantur, totumque corpus tremore quatiebatur, sed et manus absque virtute erant, et quae optabam implere non poteram; cum vero manus deposuissem, nihil mali sentiebam omnino. Cognovi enim, quoniam tecum est Dominus, eo quod non potui aliquid te nocere'. Haec eo dicente, flevit sacerdos, imponens silentium clerico, reversusque domum, caenae discubuit. Qua exacta, in stratum suum quievit, habens circa lectum suum multos lectulos clerieorum. Denique diffisi hi de clerico, per se nefas perficere cogitantes, nova argumenta machinantur, per qua aut eum vi extinguerent aut certe crimen, quo a sacerdotio divelleretur, inponerent. Interea, quiescentibus cunctis, media fere nocte, cubiculum sacerdotis inrumpunt, exclamantes voce magna atque dicentes, vidisse se mulierem a cubiculo egredi ipsamque ob hoc dimisisse, dum ad episcopum festinassent. Et insanie pars haec et consilium diaboli fuit, in tali aetate crimen inponere sacerdoti, qui erat fere LXX annorum. Nec mora, coniuncto secum iterum antedicto clerico, alligatur sacerdos catenis ab eius manibus, de cuius collo saepius vincla discusserat, et arduae custodiae mancipatur ab eo, quem de cenosis carceribus plerumque liberavit. At ille cognoscens, inimicos sibi vehementer invaluisse, Domini misericordiam cum lacrimis in vincla compactus exorat. Mox opprimuntur somno custodes, solutisque divinitus vinclis, de custodia procedit innoxius, noxiorum frequentissimus liberator. Deinde dilapsus, ad regnum Gunthramni regis transiit, Quo discedente, liberius iam coniuncti satellites ad regem Chilpericum properant pro episcopatu petendo, multa crimina de episcopo proloquentes, addentes ista: 'In hoc cognosce, rex gloriosissime, vera esse quae dicimus, quia mortem pro sceleribus timens ad fratris tui transiit regnum'. Quod ille non credens, hos ad civitatem redire iubet. Dum haec agerentur, mesti cives de pastoris absentia, cognoscentes omnia quae de eo acta fuerant per invidia et avaritia perpetrata, adpraehensum cum satellite archidiaconum iniuriae subdentes, ad regem petierunt, ut reciperent sacerdotem suum. At rex legatos fratri suo dirigit, adserens, nihil se criminis in episcopo repperisse. Tunc rex Guntchramnus, ut erat benignus et profluus ad miserandum, multa ei munera contulit, dans etiam epistolas per omnes episcopos regni sui, ut peregrinum aliquid pro Dei intuitu consolarentur. Tunc circumiens civitates, tanta ei a sacerdotibus Dei conlata sunt tam in vestibus quam in auro, ut vix civitati quae meruerat possit inferri; impletumque est illud quod ait apostolus: Quia diligentibus Deum omnia concununt in bonum. Nam huic peregrinatio divitias attulit, et exilium opes multas invexit. Post haec regrediens, a civibus cum grandi honore susceptus est, ut prae gaudio flerent et benedicerent Deum, qui tandem eclesiae tantum restituit sacerdotem.

## 37. De nece Lupenti abbatis Gaballitani.

<a id="hist-06-la-037"></a>
[**[037]**](#hist-06-en-037) Lupentius vero abba basilicae sancti Privati martyris urbis Gabalitanae, a Brunichilde regina arcessitus, advenit. Incusatus enim, ut ferunt, fuerat ab Innocentio supradictae urbis comite, quod profanum aliquid effatus de regina fuisset. Sed discussis causis, cum nihil de crimine maiestatis conscius esset inventus, discedere iussus est. Verum ubi via carpere coepit, iterum ab antedicto comite captus et ad Ponticonem villam deductus, multis suppliciis est adfectus; dimissusque iterum, ut rediret, cum super Axonam fluvium tentorium tetendisset; iterum inruit super eum inimicus eius. Quem vi oppressum, amputatum caput in culleum oneratum lapidibus posuit et flumini dedit; reliquum vero corpus vinctum cum saxo inmersit gurgiti. Post dies vero paucos apparuit quibusdam pastoribus, et sic extractum a flumine sepulturae mandatum est. Sed dum necessitates in funere pararentur et ignoraretur, quis esset e populo, praesertim cum caput truncati non inveniretur, subito adveniens aquila levavit culleum a hindere fluminis et ripae deposuit. Admirantesque qui aderant, adpraehensum culleum, dum sollicite, quid contineret, inquirunt, caput truncati repperiunt, et sic cum reliquis artubus est sepultum. Nam ferunt nunc et lumen ibi divinitus apparere; et si infirmus ad hoc tumulum fideliter deprecatus fuerit, accepta sospitate recedit.

## 38. De obitu Theodosii episcopi et de successore eius.

<a id="hist-06-la-038"></a>
[**[038]**](#hist-06-en-038) Theodosius Rutenorum episcopus, qui sancto Dalmatio successerat, diem obiit. In qua ecclesia in tantum pro episcopatu intentiones et scandala orta convaluerunt, ut paene sacris ministeriorum vasis et omni facultate meliori nudaretur. Verumtamen Transobadus presbiter reiecitur, et Innocentius Gabalitanorum comis eligitur ad episcopatum, opitulante Brunichilde regina. Sed, adsumpto episcopatu, confestim Ursicinum Cadurcinae urbis episcopum lacessire coepit, dicens, quia diocesis Rutinae ecclesiae debitas retineret. Unde factum est, ut, diuturna intentione gliscente, post aliquot annos coniunctus metropolis cum suis provincialibus apud urbem Arvernum resedens iudicium emanaret, scilicet ut parrochias, quas numquam Rutina ecclesia tenuisse recolebatur, reciperet. Quod ita factum est.

## 39. De obitu Remedi episcopi et successore eius.

<a id="hist-06-la-039"></a>
[**[039]**](#hist-06-en-039) Remigius Biturigum episcopus obiit. Cuius post transitum gravi incendio pars maxima civitatis cremata est, ibique illa quae hostilitati restiterant perierunt. Post haec Sulpicius in ipsa urbe ad sacerdotium, Guntchramno rege favente, praeelegitur. Nam cum multi munera offerrent, haec rex episcopatum quaerentibus respondisse fertur: 'Non est principatus nostri consuetudo sacerdotium venumdare sub pretio, sed nec vestrum eum praemiis comparare, ne et nos turpis lucri infamio notemur et vos mago Simoni comparamini. Sed iuxta Dei praescientia Sulpicius vobis erit episcopus'. Et sic ad clericatum deductus, episcopatum ecclesiae supradictae suscepit. Est enim vir valde nobilis et de primis senatoribus Galliarum, in litteris bene eruditus rethoricis, in metricis vero artibus nulli secundus. Hic senodum illam, cui supra meminimus, pro parrochiis Cadurcinis fieri commonuit.

## 40. De altercatione nostra cum heretico.

<a id="hist-06-la-040"></a>
[**[040]**](#hist-06-en-040) Legatus vero Oppila nomen de Hispaniis advenit, multa munera Chilperico rege deferens. Timebat enim rex Hispanorum, ne Childeberthus exercitum ad ulciscendam sororis suae iniuriam commoverit, quia Leuvichildus adpraehensum filium suum Herminichildum, qui sororem Childeberthi regis acciperat, retruserat in custodia, ipsa mulierem cum Grecis relictam. Igitur cum die sancto paschae hic legatus Toronus advenisset, sciscitati sumus, utrum nostrae relegionis esset. Respondit ipse, se hoc credere quod catholici credunt. Exinde procedenss nobiscum ad eclesiam, missarum sollemnia tenuit; sed neque pacem cum nostris fecit neque de sacrificiis sacris communicavit. Cognitumque est, mendum esse, quod dixerat se esse catholicum. Nihilhominus ad convivium invitatus adfuit. Cumque ego sollicitus requirirem, quid crederit, respondit: 'Credo Patrem et Filium et Spiritum sanctum unius esse virtutes'. Cui ego respondi: 'Si haec, ut adseris, credis, quae obstetit causa, ut de sacrificiis, quae Deo offerimus, communicare deferris?' Et ille: 'Quia', inquid, gloriam non recte responditis; nam iuxta Paulum apostolum nos dicimus: „Gloria Deo Patri per Filium"; vos autem dicitis: „Gloria Patri et Filio et Spiritu sancto", cum doctores eclesiarum doceant, Patrem per Filium nuntiatum fuisse in mundum, sicut ipse Paulus ait: Regi autem saeculorum inmortali et invisibili, soli Deo honor et gloria in saecula saeculorum, per Iesum Christum dominum nostrum'. Et ego respondi: 'Patrem per Filium adnuntiatum nulli catholicorum esse incognitum, reor; sed sic praedicavit Patrem in saeculo, ut et se virtutibus ostenderit Deum. Deo autem Patri haec necessitas fuit Filium mittendi ad terras, ut ostenderit Deum, ut, quia mundus prophetis et patriarchis atque ipse Latori legis non crediderat, saltim vel Filio crederit. Ideoque necesse est, ut sub significatione personarum gloria detur Deo. Dicimus ergo: "Gloria Deo Patri, qui misit Filium; gloria Deo Filio, qui sanguine suo redemit mundum; gloria Deo Spiritui sancto, qui sanctificat hominem iam redemptum". Nam tu, qui dicis: "Gloria Patri per Filium", ademis gloriam Filio, quasi ipse non sit gloriosus cum Patre, propter quod eum adnuntiavit in mundum. Nuntiavit, ut diximus, Filius Patrem in mundo; sed multi non crediderunt, dicente Iohanne euangelista: In sua propria venit, et sui eum non reciperunt. Quodquod autem reciperunt eum, dedit eis potestatem filius Dei fieri, his qui credunt in nomine eius. Nam tu, qui Paulo apostolo derogas et sensum eius non intellegis, percipe, quam caute loquitur et iuxta ut recepere quis potest; adverte, qualiter praedicat inter gentes incredulas, ut nullo onus grave videatur inponere, sicut quibusdam dicit: Lac vobis potum dedi, non escam; nondum enim poteratis, sed nec adhuc quidem potestis. Perfectorum est enim solidus cibus. Sed et aliis dicit: Nihil vobis praedicavi nisi Christum, et hunc crucifixum. Nunc autem quid vis, o tu heretice, quia Paulus Christum tantum crucifixum praedicavit, resurrexisse tu dubitas? Adverte potius cautelam eius et vide astutiam, quid aliis dicit, quos robustiores videbat in fide: Et si, inquid, novimus Christum crucifixum, nunc autem iam non novimus. Nega ergo, tu accusatur Pauli, si tantum mente dementia coepit, quia nec crucifixus est. Sed, quaeso, relinque ista et audi consilium meliorem, adhibe culiria oculis lippis et lucem praedicationis apostolicae percipe. Secundum homines enim loquibatur Paulus humilius, ut eos ad celsioris fidei fastigia sublevaret, sicut alibi ait: Omnibus omnia factus sum, ut omnes lucri facerem. Numquid homo mortalis non est daturus gloria Filio, quem ipse Pater non semel, sed bis et tertio glorificavit e caelo? Absculta, quid de caelis loquitur, cum idem Filius, discendente Spiritu sancto, sub Iohannis manu baptizaretur: Hic est, ait, Filius meus dilectus, in quo bene conplacui. Certe si oppilatas habeas aures, ut ista non audias, crede apostolis, quid in monte audierunt, cum transfiguratus Iesus in gloria loqueretur cumMoysi et Helia; nempe de nubi splendida Pater ait: Hic est Filius meus carissimus, ipsum audite'. Ad haec hereticus respondit: 'Nihil in his testimoniis Pater de gloria loquitur Fili, nisi tantum ipsum Filium monstrat'. Et ego: 'Si enim ista sic recipis, proferam tibi aliud testimonium, in quo Pater reddidit Filium gloriosum. Veniente autem Domino ad passionem, cum ille diceret: Pater, glorifica Filium tuum, ut Filius tuus glorificet te, quid ei respondit Pater e caelo? Nonne ait: Et glorificavi et iterum glorificabo? Ecce enim Pater glorificat eum propria voce, et tu ei gloriam conaris ademere? Voluntatem quidem ostendis, sed potestas nulla subpeditat. Nam qui Pauli apostoli accusatur exsistis, audi ipsum, immo Christum in ipso loquentem: Omnes lingua confiteatur, quia dominus Iesus Christus in gloria est Dei Patris. Quod si nunc communis cum Patre gloria et in ipsa qua nunc Pater est gloria commoratur, qualiter eum tu quasi inglorium exhonoras? Aut cur? non erit ei reddenda gloria inter hominis, qui pari gloria cum Patri regnat in caelis? Confitemur ergo Christum, filium Dei, Deum verum: ideoque quia Deitas una, una erit et gloria'. Post haec, dato selentio, ab altercatione cessatum est. Ille quoque ad Chilpericum regem accedens, oblatis muneribus quae rex Hispanorum miserat, in Hispania est regressus.

## 41. Quod in Camaracense abiit.

<a id="hist-06-la-041"></a>
[**[041]**](#hist-06-en-041) Conperto autem Chilpericus rex, quod Gunthchramnus, frater eius, cum Childebertho, nepote suo, pacem fecerat et civitates, quas violenter invaserat, ei simul vellent auferre, cum omnibus thesauris suis in Camaracense urbe discessit et omnia quae melius habere potuerat secum tulit. Misitque ad duces et comites civitatum nuntius, ut murus conponerent urbium resque suas cum uxoribus et filiis infra murorum monimenta concluderent atque ipsi, si necessitas exigerit, repugnarent viriliter, ne his pars adversa nocerit, illud addens: 'Et si aliquid perdideritis, cum de inimicis ulciscimur, maiora conquiretis', nesciens, patrationem victuriarum in manu Dei consistere. Deinde sepius exercitu commovit et iterum infra terminum requiescere iobet. His diebus ei filius natus fuerat, quem in villa Victuriacense nutrire praecepit, dicens: 'Ne forte, dum publice videtur, aliquid male incurrat et moriatur!'

## 42. Quod Childeberthus in Italia abiit.

<a id="hist-06-la-042"></a>
[**[042]**](#hist-06-en-042) Childeberthus vero rex in Italia abiit. Quod cum audissent Langobardi, timentis, ne ab eius exercitu caederintur, subdedirunt se dicioni eius, multa ei dantes munera ac promittentes se parte eius esse fidelis atque subiectus. Patratisque cum his omnibus quae voluit, rex in Galliis est regressus atque exercitu commovere praecepit, quem in Hispania dirige iussit; sed quievit. Ab imperatore autem Mauricio ante hos annos quinquaginta milia soledorum acceperat, ut Langobardus de Italia extruderit. Audito autem imperator, quod cum his in pace coniunctus est, pecuniam repetibat; sed hic fidus a solatiis nec responsum quidem pro hac re voluit reddere.

## 43. De Galliciensibus regibus.

<a id="hist-06-la-043"></a>
[**[043]**](#hist-06-en-043) In Gallitia quoque novae res actae sunt, quae de superius memorabuntur. Igitur cum Herminichildus, sicut supra diximus, patri infensus esset et in civitate quadam Hispaniae cum coniuge resediret, de imperatoris solatio fretus atque Mironis Galliciensis regis, patrem ad se cum exercitum venire cognovit consiliumque iniit, qualiter venientem aut repelleret aut negaret, nesciens miser, iudicium sibi inminere divinum, qui contra genitorem, quamlibet hereticum, talia cogitaret. Habitu ergo tractatu, de multis virorum milibus trecentos veros elegit armatus et infra castrum Osser, in cuius eclesia fontes divinitus conplentur, includit, ut scilicet primo impetu ab his pater territus ac lassatus, facilius ab inferiore manu, quae erat plurima, vinceretur. Denique his dolis Leuvichildus rex cognitis, cogitatione maxima fatigatur. 'Si', inquid, 'illuc cum omni exercitu abiero, conglobatis in unum exercitus adversorum iaculis crudilissime sauciatur. Se vero cum paucis vadam, virorum fortium manum nequeo superare. Tamen cum omnibus ibo'. Et accedens ad locum, viros proteruit castrumque conbusit, sicut iam superius memoratum est. Patrata quoque victuria, cognovit, Mironem regem contra se cum exercitu resedere. Quo circumdato, sacramenta exigit, sibi in posterum fore fedilem. Et sic, datis sibi invicem muneribus, unusquisque ad propria est regressus. Sed Miro postquam in patria rediit, non multos post dies conversus ad lectulum, obiit. Infirmatus enim ab aquis Hispaniae fuerat malis aeribusque incommodis. Quo defuncto, filius eius Eurichus Leuvichildi regis amicitias expetiit, dataque, ut pater fecerat, sacramenta, regnum Galliciensim suscepit. Hoc vero anno cognatus eius Audica, qui sororem illius disponsatamto habebat, cum exercitu venit; adpraehensumque clericum facit ac diaconatus sibi praesbiterii ei inponi honorem iobet. Ipse quoque, acceptam soceri sui uxorem, Galliciensim regnum obtenuit. Leuvichildus vero filium suum Herminichildum coepit et sicum usque Toletum adduxit, condemnans eum exilio; uxorem tamen eius a Grecis erepere non potuit.

## 44. De diversis signis.

<a id="hist-06-la-044"></a>
[**[044]**](#hist-06-en-044) Locustae quoque de Carpitania provintia, quam per quinque vastaverant annos, hoc anno progressae ageremque publicum tenentes, ad aliam se provinciam, quae huic vicina erat provinciae, contulerunt. Quarum spatium in centum quinquaginta extenditur milibus longitudo, latitudo vero in centum milibus terminatur. Hoc anno multa prodigia apparuerunt in Galliis, vastationisque multae fuerunt in populo. Nam mense Ianuario rosae visae sunt; circa solem quoque circulus magnus apparuit, diversis coloribus mixtus, ut solet in illo caelestis iris ambitu, pluvia discendente, monstrari. Proina graviter vineas exussit; tempestas etiam subsecuta vineas segetesque per plurima loca vastavit; residuum quoque grandinis siccitas inmensa consumpsit, exiguusque fructus in aliquibus viniis visus, in aliis vero nullus, ita ut irati contra Deum homines, patifactis aditibus viniarum, pecora vel iumenta intromitterent, noxias sibi immixcentes miseri praeces atque dicentes: 'Numquam in his viniis palmis nascatur in sempeternum!' Arboris vero, quae mense Iulio poma protulerant, mense Septembre fructus alios ediderunt. Morbus pecorum iteratis invaluit, ita ut vix quicquam remaneret.

## 45. De nuptiis Rigunthae, filiae Chilperici.

<a id="hist-06-la-045"></a>
[**[045]**](#hist-06-en-045) Interim advenientibus Kalendis Septembribus, Gothorum magna legatio ad regem Chilpericum accedit. Ipse vero iam regressus Parisius, familias multas de domibus fiscalibus auferre praecepit et in plaustris conponi; multus quoque flentes et nolentes abire in custodia retrudi iussit, ut eos facilius cum filia transmittere possit. Nam ferunt, multos sibi ob hanc amaritudinem vitam laqueo extorsisse, dum de parentibus propriis auferre metuebant. Separabatur autem filius a patre, mater a filia, et cum gravi gemitu ac maledictionibus discedebant; tantusque planctus in urbe Parisiaca erat, ut plancto conpararetui Aegyptio. Multi vero meliores natu, qui vi conpellebantur abire, testamenta condiderunt, resque suas eclesiis depotantes atque petentes, ut, cum in Hispaniis puella introisset, statim testamenta illa, tamquam si iam essent sepulti, reserarentur. Interea legati regis Childeberthi Parisius advenerunt, contestantes Chilperico rege, ut nihil de civitatibus, quas de regno patris sui tenebat, auferret aut de thesauris eius in aliquo filiam muneraret ac non mancipia, non equites, non iuga bovum neque aliquid huiuscemodi de his auderet adtingere. De quibus legatis unum ferunt clam interemptum, sed nescitur a quo; suspicio tamen vertebatur ad regem. Promittens vero Chilpericus rex nihil de his contingere, convocatis melioribus Francis reliquisque fidelibus, nuptias celebravit filiae suae. Traditamque legatis Gothorum, magnus ei thesaurus dedit. Sed et mater eius inmensum pondus auri argentique sive vestimentorum protulit, ita ut videns haec rex nihil sibi remansisse potaret. Quem cernens regina commotum, conversa ad Francus, ita ait: 'Ne potitis, viri, quicquam hic de thesauris anteriorum regum habere; omnia enim quae cernetis de mea proprietate oblata sunt, quia mihi gloriosissimus rex multa largitus est, et ego nonnulla de proprio congregavi labore et de domibus mihi concessis tam de fructibus quam tributis plurima reparavi. Sed et vos plerumque me muneribus vestris ditastis, de quibus sunt ista quae nunc coram videtis; nam hic de thesauris publicis nihil habetur'. Et sic animus regis dilusus est. Nam tanta fuit multitudo rerum, ut aurum argentumque vel reliqua ornamenta quinquaginta plaustra levarent. Franci vero multa munera obtulerunt, alii aurum, alii argentum, nonnulli equites, plerique vestimenta, et unusquisque ut potuit donativum dedit. Iam vero vale faciens puella post lacrimas et oscola, cum de porta egrederetur, uno carrucae effracto axe, omnes 'Mala hora' dixerunt; quod a quibusdam pro auspicio susceptum est. Denique haec de Parisius progressa, octavo ab urbe miliario tenturia figi praecepit. Surgentes enim quinquaginta viri de nocte, adpraehensis centum equitibus optimis totidemque frenis aureis ac duobus catenis magnis, ad Childeberthum regem fuga dilapsi abierunt. Sed et per totum iter cum labi quis potuisset, effugiebat, ferens secum quae arripere potuisset. Apparatus quoque magnus expense de diversis civitatibus in itenere congregatus est; in quo nihil de fisco suo rex dare praecepit, nisi omnia de pauperum coniectures. Sed quoniam suspicio erat regi, ne frater aut nepus aliquas insidias puellae in via pararent, vallatam ab exercitu pergere iussit. Erant autem cum ea viri magnifici Bobo dux, filius Mummolini, cum uxore, quasi paranymphus, Domigysilus et Ansovaldus, maior domus autem Waddo, qui olim Sanctonicum rexerat comitatum; reliquum vero vulgus super quattuor milia erat. Ceteri autem duces et camararii, qui cum ea properaverant, de Pectavo regressi sunt; isti vero iter conficientes, pergebant ut poterant. Per quam via tanta spolia tantaequae praedae factae sunt, ut vix valeant enarrare. Nam hospiciola pauperum spoliabant, vineas devastabant, ita ut incisis codicibus cum uvis auferrent, levantes pecora vel quicquid invenire potuissent, nihil per viam quam gradiebantur relinquentes; impletumque est quod dictum est per Iohel propheta: Residuum locustae comedit eruga, et residuum erucae comedit brucus, et residuum bruci comedit rubigo. Ita et hoc actum est tempore, ut residuum proinae proteriret tempestas et residuum tempestatis exuriret siccitas et residuum siccitatis auferret hostilitas.

## 46. De interitu Chilperici regis.

<a id="hist-06-la-046"></a>
[**[046]**](#hist-06-en-046) His itaque cum haec praeda pergentibus, Chilpericus, Nero nostri temporis et Herodis, ad villam Calensim, quae distat ab urbe Parisiaca quasi centum stadiis, accedit ibique venationes exercit. Quadam vero die regressus de venatione iam sub obscura nocte, dum de equo susceperitur et unam manu super scapulam pueri reteniret, adveniens quidam eum cultro percutit sub ascellam iteratoque ictu ventrem eius perforat; statimque profluente cupia sanguinis tam per os quam per aditum vulneris, iniquum fudit spiritum. Quam vero malitiam gesserit, superior lectio docet. Nam regiones plurimas sepius devastavit atque succendit; de quibus nihil doloris, sed letitia magis habebat, sicut quondam Nero, cum inter incendia palatii tragidias decantaret. Persaepe hominis pro facultatibus eorum iniuste punivit. In cuius tempore pauci quodammodo episcopatum clerici meruerunt. Erat enimP gulae deditus, cuius deus venter fuit. Nullumque sibi adserebat esse prudentiorem. Conficitque duos libros, quasi Sidulium meditatus, quorum versiculi debilis nullis pedibus subsistere possunt, in quibus, dum non intellegebat, pro longis sillabas breves posuit et pro breves longas statuebat, et alia opuscula vel ymnus sive missas, quae nulla ratione suscipi possunt. Causas pauperum exosas habebat. Sacerdotes Domini assiduae blasphemabat, nec aliunde magis, dum secricius esset, exercebat ridicola vel iocos quam de eclesiarum episcopis. Illum ferebat levem, alium superbum, illum habundantem, istum luxoriosum; illum adserebat elatum, hunc tumidum, nullum plus odio quam eclesias habens. Aiebat enim plerumque: 'Ecce pauper remansit fiscus noster, ecce divitiae nostrae ad eclesias sunt translatae; nulli penitus nisi soli episcopi regnant; periet honor noster et translatus est ad episcopus civitatum'. Haec agens, adsiduae testamenta, quae in eclesias conscripta erant, plerumque disrupit, ipsasque patris sui praeceptiones, potans, quod non remanerit qui voluntatem eius servaret, saepe calcavit. Iam de libidine atque luxoria non potest repperire in cogitatione, quod non perpetrasset in opere, novaquae semper ad ledendum populum ingenia perquaerebat; nam, si quos hoc tempore culpabilis repperisset, oculos eis iobebat erui. Et in praeceptionibus, quas ad iudicis pro suis utilitatibus dirigebat, haec addebat: 'Si quis praecepta nostra contempserit, oculorum avulsione multetur'. Nullum umquam pure dilexit, a nullo dilectus est, ideoque, cum spiritum exalasset, omnes eum reliquerunt sui. Mallulfus autem Silvanectensis episcopus, qui iam tertia die in tenturio resedebat et ipsum videre non poterat, ut eum interemptum audivit, advenit; ablutumque vestimentis melioribus induit, noctem in hymnis deductam, in nave levavit et in basilica sancti Vincenti, quae est Parisius, sepelivit, Fredegunde regina in ecclesia derelicta.
